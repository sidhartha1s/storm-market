# Part Three, Playbook 3 — Extreme Rainfall and Flash Flood

Target: 10 pages · draft 1, 2026-07-29

---

## The event

Flash flooding is the hazard this book is most confident it can save a reader from, because the deaths
are concentrated in a single decision that takes two seconds and is almost always avoidable.

On the morning of 4 July 2022 — and in a dozen comparable events every year — water rose in a
drainage that had been dry the previous evening, at a rate no one standing beside it would have
predicted, in a place where the rain fell miles upstream and never touched the people it killed.

That last clause is the whole hazard. **Flash flooding does not require rain where you are.** It
requires rain in your watershed. A canyon, a wash, a creek, an urban storm drain, a burn scar — all of
them collect water from an area far larger than what you can see, and deliver it as a moving front
rather than as a rising level.

The other reason to open here: rainfall extremes are the part of the weather picture changing fastest,
and the flooding maps most households rely on describe where water has historically gone rather than
where this decade's rain sends it.

## What actually kills, and what actually costs

**Killed in vehicles.** This is the dominant pattern and it holds year after year: the majority of
flash flood deaths happen to people in cars, driving into water on a road.
«CITE: NWS statistics on flood fatalities by location, specifically the vehicle share.»

The physics are worth knowing precisely, because "it looked shallow" is the recurring last judgment.
Six inches of moving water takes a person off their feet. A foot of moving water can float a typical car
— and once it floats, it steers nowhere. Two feet moves most vehicles, including large ones. And the
roadbed under moving water frequently is not there; the water has already scoured it out, so the depth
you can see is not the depth you will drop into.
«CITE: NWS or USGS figures for the depths at which vehicles float and are swept, and the "Turn Around,
Don't Drown" campaign source.»

Then the secondary pattern: **at night**, when depth is unreadable, and **in basements and low-lying
apartments**, where water arrives through the door, the drain and the window well faster than people
believe.

**Costs:** this is the uninsured loss category. A few inches through a finished ground floor is a
five-figure repair, and if the water came from outside, the homeowner policy does not pay. If it came up
through the drains, it does not pay either — unless the sewer backup endorsement was bought. Two cheap
protections (103 and the endorsement noted in 102) sit between a household and this.

## Before

- **92 — know your local gauge**, and know the name of the creek, wash or drainage that concerns you.
  There is a river gauge upstream of most people, published live, and it is a better predictor of your
  next hour than anything else available.
- **103 — flood insurance**, with the thirty-day clock. Outside a high-risk zone the premium is
  materially lower, and a large share of claims come from exactly there.
- **102 — the sewer backup endorsement**, checked for specifically. It is usually inexpensive and it is
  usually absent.
- **82 — a sewer backwater valve**, which is the physical version of the same protection.
- **78, 79 — gutter capacity and grading**, the cheapest flood mitigation that exists: get roof water
  discharged well away from the foundation and get the ground sloping away from the building.
- **36 — a sump pump with battery backup**, because the flood and the power cut arrive together and a
  mains-only sump is a decoration during the event it exists for.
- **81 — elevate the mechanicals**, and **80 — flood openings** where wet floodproofing is the right
  approach.
- **85, 86 — two alert paths and county enrollment**, because flash flood warnings are frequently the
  fastest-issued products in the system and are often the only notice you get.
- **114 — the vertical evacuation rule**, understood in advance: in a flash flood you go up inside a
  solid structure rather than out into a vehicle.
- **100 — the video inventory**, especially of the basement and ground floor, which is what gets
  destroyed.

**The walk to do once.** Go outside during heavy rain, in daylight, and watch where the water actually
goes. Where does it pond? Where does it run against the house? Which drain backs up? Which window well
fills? Every household discovers something in ten minutes of looking that they would otherwise discover
at two in the morning.

**Then find out what is below you.** If you have a basement or a below-grade apartment, know that it is
the room the water reaches first and that the exit may be above the water. Do not sleep down there
during a flood watch, and do not store the irreplaceable things there at all.

## During

**Flood watch** — conditions are favourable. Move vehicles to higher ground now, because a car parked in
a dip is a total loss for no reason. Move valuables and documents up a floor. Charge everything. Check
the gauge.

**Flash flood warning** — flooding is imminent or occurring. **Move to higher ground immediately.** Not
in a minute. This warning has the shortest useful lead time of any product the weather service issues,
and it is frequently issued when water is already moving.

**Flash flood emergency** — the rarest and most serious wording, reserved for a severe threat to life.
If you see it, the response is immediate and vertical.

**The four rules, in the order you will need them:**

**Do not drive into water.** Turn around. There is no exception for a familiar road, a large vehicle, or
water that looks shallow. Roads have been removed underneath.

**Do not walk into water.** Six inches moving is enough, and you cannot see the manhole cover that is
gone.

**Do not go into a basement or a below-grade space** once water is moving. Water pressure holds doors
shut, and stairwells fill.

**Go up, not out.** Inside a solid structure, upstairs beats the car. If you go into an attic, take
something that gets you through the roof (Protection 114) — an attic with no exit is a sealed box.

**If your vehicle is in rising water:** out immediately, through a window, and to higher ground. Do not
stay with the car and do not try the doors. This is in Protection 115 and it deserves saying out loud to
everyone who drives.

**If you are outdoors and caught:** away from the channel, uphill, immediately, and do not attempt to
cross moving water to reach somewhere better. Wait for the level to drop; flash floods peak and recede
quickly, which is the one merciful property they have.

## After

**The water is contaminated.** Sewage, fuel, pesticides, whatever was in the neighbourhood. Boots,
gloves, eye protection. Wash after every contact. Any wound that has been in floodwater gets treated
properly and watched (Protection 65).

**The electrics.** Do not enter standing water in a building until you know the power to it is off, and
do not switch anything on that has been wet. Anything that was submerged — outlets, switches, the panel,
the furnace, the water heater — needs an electrician's or technician's judgment, not yours.

**Discard without negotiation:** food that touched floodwater, including sealed containers where the
seal or the lid was submerged (Protection 21); porous materials that absorbed it — carpet and underlay,
mattresses, upholstery, particleboard, wet insulation, and wallboard up to above the water line.

**Then move fast on drying,** because mould begins within a day or two. Water out, air moving,
dehumidifiers running, cavities opened. Speed here changes whether you have a repair or a gut job.

**The sewer backup case** deserves its own note because it is the household version of this hazard and
it is common: water coming *up* through drains, toilets and floor wastes rather than in through the
door. Treat it as raw sewage — full protective equipment, professional cleaning for anything porous, and
disinfection of every hard surface. Then buy the endorsement and cost the backwater valve, because it
recurs. A household that has had one sewer backup will have another.

**The claim.** Photograph the water line before it dries and becomes invisible — it is the single most
useful piece of evidence you will produce. Photograph everything before you remove it, keep every
receipt including the dehumidifier hire, and know which of your three possible policies is responding.

**The burn scar case.** If you live below ground that burned in the last few years, your flash flood
risk is elevated for years afterwards and the water arrives carrying mud, rock and trees. That is a
debris flow, it behaves differently, and it is covered in Playbook 5.
