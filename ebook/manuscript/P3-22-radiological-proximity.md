# Part Three, Playbook 22 — Radiological Proximity

Target: 6 pages · draft 1, 2026-07-29

---

## The event

On 11 March 2011 a magnitude 9.0 earthquake struck about 231 miles northeast of Tokyo. The NRC's account
of what followed is a clean lesson in how a nuclear accident actually happens: **eleven reactors at four
sites shut down automatically** as designed. Fukushima Dai-ichi then **lost all grid power**, and its
**diesel generators ran for about 40 minutes** — at which point an estimated **45-foot (14-metre) tsunami**
hit the site and damaged many of them, along with some of the battery backup. **Four of the six reactors
lost all generator power.** Units 1, 2 and 3 were operating; their steam-driven and battery-powered safety
systems held for several hours, and in some cases more than a day, and then failed. **All three reactors
overheated.**
«SRC: US Nuclear Regulatory Commission, "Backgrounder on NRC Response to Lessons Learned from Fukushima" —
9.0 earthquake 231 miles northeast of Tokyo; eleven reactors at four sites shut down automatically;
Dai-ichi lost grid power with diesels running about 40 minutes before an estimated 45-foot tsunami; four
of six units lost all generator power; Units 1, 2 and 3 overheated. |
https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/fact-sheets/japan-events.html | retrieved 2026-07-29»

Read that sequence and notice that the reactors did the right thing and the *support systems* failed, in
order, over hours and days. Nothing about it was instant. Which is the reason this playbook's instruction
is about following official direction over a period of days rather than reacting in a minute.

The finding that matters most for this playbook is uncomfortable, and half of it is very well documented.

**The radiation did not produce a mass casualty event.** The United Nations Scientific Committee on the
Effects of Atomic Radiation spent two years assessing the accident, and the main findings of its 2013
report were: **cancer rates to remain stable; a theoretical increased risk of thyroid cancer among the
most exposed children; no impact on birth defects or hereditary effects; no discernible increase in
cancer rates for workers.** UNSCEAR later reported *increasing evidence that some of the public doses in
that 2013 report had been overestimated* — ingestion doses significantly so. Tens of thousands of people
were evacuated.
«SRC: UNSCEAR, "The Fukushima-Daiichi Nuclear Power Station Accident: an overview," summarising the
UNSCEAR 2013 Report main findings and the subsequent finding that 2013 public dose estimates were
overestimated. | https://www.unscear.org/unscear/en/fukushima.html | retrieved 2026-07-29»

**The other half — that the evacuation itself killed people — this book will not quantify**, because the
figures in circulation could not be traced to an official assessment of the kind above. What can be said
without a number is the thing that is true of every evacuation in this book: moving frail, elderly and
hospitalised people at speed carries its own mortality, and the authorities ordering an evacuation are
weighing that against the exposure. That is precisely why the instruction here is to **follow official
direction rather than improvise** — including when the direction is to stay put, which is the harder one
to obey.

That is not an argument for complacency. It is the reason this chapter is the shortest hazard playbook in the
book and the reason its instruction is unusually simple: **the plan is not yours to write. Follow the
official instruction, precisely, and do not improvise.**

Three Mile Island in 1979 is the American reference. TMI-2 partially melted down on 28 March 1979 —
the most serious accident in US commercial nuclear history — and the NRC's own account is that the
small radioactive releases *"had no detectable health effects on plant workers or the public."* The
roughly two million people around the plant are estimated to have received an average dose of **about
1 millirem above normal background.** A chest X-ray is about 6 millirem, and natural background in
that area is 100 to 125 millirem a year. The maximum dose at the site boundary would have been under
100 millirem above background.
«SRC: US Nuclear Regulatory Commission, "Backgrounder on the Three Mile Island Accident." |
https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/fact-sheets/3mile-isle.html | retrieved 2026-07-29»

What the accident did change was everything about the response: emergency planning, operator training,
radiation protection, and regulatory oversight were all rebuilt afterwards. The lesson for a household
is not that radiological events are harmless. It is that the American event with the frightening
pictures did its real damage through a chaotic public-information period, not through dose.

## What actually kills, and what actually costs

**For a household outside a plant's immediate vicinity, radiation exposure is not the main risk.** Saying
otherwise would be dishonest, and dishonesty here is what the preparedness industry sells.

**What actually causes harm**, in the order it has historically done so:

**A badly executed evacuation**, especially of medically fragile people. Moving a hospital ward or a care
home in an emergency has a measurable mortality cost.

**Interrupted medical care**, in a displacement that lasts months or years rather than days.

**Psychological and social harm** — prolonged displacement, uncertainty, stigma, and the loss of community —
which in Fukushima's aftermath is the best-documented health consequence of the whole event.

**Radioactive iodine uptake by the thyroid**, which is genuinely preventable and genuinely worth preventing,
particularly in children. That is the entire purpose of Protection 127, and it is a narrow intervention with
a narrow timing window.

**Contaminated food and water supply**, which is why planning zones extend much further than the direct-
exposure zone: the outer zone exists to control milk, produce, water and livestock feed, not to protect people
from a plume.

**Costs:** displacement, potentially long-term. Property value effects. Agricultural losses.

## Before

- **125 — the proximity lookup.** Find out whether you are inside a planning zone, and which. The inner
  zone — the **plume exposure pathway**, about **ten miles** in radius — is where sheltering, evacuation and
  potassium iodide are planned. The outer zone — the **ingestion exposure pathway**, about **fifty miles** —
  exists to control contaminated food, milk and water rather than to protect you from a plume.
  «SRC: NRC, "Emergency Planning Zones" — the plume exposure pathway EPZ extends about **10 miles** in radius (sheltering, evacuation, KI where appropriate); the ingestion exposure pathway EPZ extends about **50 miles** and governs bans on contaminated food and water. Page updated 28 Aug 2024. https://www.nrc.gov/about-nrc/emerg-preparedness/about-emerg-preparedness/planning-zones.html · retrieved 2026-07-29»
- **If you are inside a zone, read your county's actual plan once.** It names your reception centre, your
  route, your notification method and your school's arrangements. It is specific, it is public, and it
  replaces every generic instruction in this book. Households inside the zone are typically enrolled in a
  notification scheme, and frequently in a potassium iodide distribution programme.
- **127 — potassium iodide**, if you are in a zone. Free from many states. Read the dosing by age group on
  the packet, note the expiry, note the contraindications, and put it with the medication supply. Then leave
  it alone until officials say to take it.
- **126 — shelter-in-place**, which is the same protocol as the hazmat playbook: go in, stay in, tune in.
- **85, 86 — two alert paths**, plus whatever the plant-specific notification system is where you live —
  frequently sirens with a defined meaning and a tone-alert radio.
- **107, 108 — go-bags and the five- and thirty-minute lists**, because if an evacuation is ordered it will
  be with short notice and it may be for a long time. This is the hazard where the thirty-minute list should
  genuinely include the things you would grieve, because you may not come back.
- **42, 59, 61 — the medical dependencies**, and this is the protection this playbook most wants to
  emphasise. Given that the documented harm is displacement harm, the households at real risk are the ones
  with someone on dialysis, oxygen, insulin or complex medication. Their plan needs to be specific, agreed
  with clinicians, and written down.
- **112 — the out-of-area contact and the school arrangement**, because schools inside a zone have their own
  protective action plans and you need to know whether the children are sheltered in place or bussed to a
  named reception point.

**If you are not inside a planning zone, this playbook is finished** and you can go to the next one. That is
most readers, and saying so is part of being honest about proportion.

## During

**Everything in this section defers to the official instruction**, which will be specific, local and better
informed than any general advice. The value of reading it in advance is that you recognise the terms when
they are used.

**Get information from the official channel** — the county, the state, the plant's notification system, the
emergency broadcast — and not from social media, because this is the hazard with the widest gap between what
is happening and what is being said about it.

**If told to shelter in place:** everyone in, windows and doors shut, **HVAC off entirely**, fireplace
damper closed, into the interior room, seal what you were told to seal, and listen. Distance, shielding and
time are the three things that reduce dose, and a sealed interior room provides two of them. Take water,
medication, phones and the radio.

**If told to evacuate:** go, on the route given, to the reception centre named. Take the go-bags, the
documents, the medication and the pets. Reception centres for a radiological event typically include
monitoring and, if needed, decontamination, which is a good reason to go where you are sent rather than to a
relative's house first.

**If told to take potassium iodide, take it — then, at that dose, for that person.** Not before, because
timing is what makes it work. Not for everyone if the guidance specifies age groups. Not a double dose.
Not instead of evacuating or sheltering, because it protects one gland and nothing else.

**Do not eat or drink anything that was exposed** — produce from the garden, milk from a local animal, water
from an open tank or a rain butt. Stored sealed food and bottled water are fine, and this is where Protection
1 earns its place in a playbook that otherwise has very little for you to do.

**Bring pets and livestock in** where you can, with covered water and stored feed rather than pasture.

**If you were outdoors during a release:** remove outer clothing before entering the house and bag it, which
removes the great majority of any contamination; shower with soap and water, gently, without scrubbing skin
raw; and wash hair with shampoo rather than conditioner, which can bind material to hair.

**Do not take iodine supplements, iodised salt, or any product marketed as a radiation remedy.** They do not
work, several are harmful in the doses people take, and the only relevant product is the specific potassium
iodide dose that officials direct.

## After

**Follow the guidance on food, water, agriculture and re-entry**, and expect it to be issued in stages over
weeks. The ingestion pathway is where most of the practical restrictions land: milk, leafy vegetables,
locally slaughtered meat, well water and garden produce.

**Get monitored if it is offered**, and get the result recorded. Documentation of a measured dose — including
a reassuring one — is worth having for the rest of your life, and the absence of it is what makes later
health questions unanswerable.

**Register with any health monitoring programme** established for the affected population, and keep the
household's records: where you were, when, what you were told, what you took, what you were monitored at.

**Expect the displacement to be the real problem**, and treat it as one. If your household is out of its home
for months, the things that will actually damage you are interrupted medical care, disrupted schooling,
insecure housing, lost income and the psychological load. Protection 66 — the section on the mind after the
event — was written with this playbook in view, and getting help for that early is more consequential than
anything radiological.

**And be ready for the information environment**, which in this hazard is the worst in the book. Expect
confident claims in both directions: people telling you it is nothing and people telling you a region is
uninhabitable, neither with evidence. The official measured data, published by the agency doing the
monitoring, is the only usable source, and your own monitoring result is the only one about you.
