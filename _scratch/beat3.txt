Sakurajima sits inside Kagoshima Bay, directly across the water from Kagoshima city, on Kyushu. On September thirteenth, the ash plume from the summit crater climbed to two thousand four hundred meters. The agency's forecast that day had the ash drifting northwest, over the city's northern districts. Moderately heavy ashfall was expected to reach Kagoshima city by eleven that night. A lighter dusting was expected as far as a coastal city up the shore. One day later, the wind direction changed. On September fourteenth, the plume climbed higher, to two thousand five hundred meters. This time the agency's forecast had the ash drifting due north instead of northwest. Moderately heavy ashfall was again expected in Kagoshima city, but earlier, by eight that evening. The lighter fall on the fourteenth reached in a different direction entirely, as far as a town across the prefecture line. The volcano's output barely changed between the two days. Plume height, crater, and the city sitting under it all stayed roughly the same. What changed was the wind. It shifted from northwest to north, and the predicted light ashfall shifted with it. On the thirteenth it reached a coastal city on one side of Kagoshima. On the fourteenth it reached a town on the far side of the prefecture, one day apart. For Kagoshima city itself, both forecasts agreed on moderately heavy ashfall either way. Only the timing moved, from eleven at night to eight in the evening. The bay does not decide who gets dusted. The wind above it does, and that wind changed direction within twenty four hours.
