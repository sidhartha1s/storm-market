The agency on record is the Japan Meteorological Agency, JMA, the government body responsible for tracking every active volcano in Japan, including Sakurajima. Its bulletins are technical activity reports, not press statements, built from seismographs, gas sensors, and cameras trained on the crater around the clock, issued on a set schedule. The most recent bulletin, timed sixteen hundred Japan Standard Time on September fourteenth, closes with this line from the agency itself. Volcanic gas discharge remains generally abundant. Magma continues accumulating beneath the caldera under the bay. Eruptive activity is expected to continue. That assessment does not describe a single day's weather. It describes a system still filling from below, with the eruptions at the surface treated as the release, not the whole event. The agency is not forecasting an ending to this activity. It is describing a process it expects to keep running past this bulletin and past the next one, for as long as the deep supply keeps arriving beneath the bay.
