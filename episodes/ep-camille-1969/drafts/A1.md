### A1-S6 · narration [RS]
**Picture:** [VE] the counter climbs, then jams at "11+" and blinks. Beige cite card, the survey row's own words: "Nelson County · WB
Recorder · 11+ · Gage overflowed" · USGS Open-File Report 70-51, Table 1. A second row: "11+ · 11" bucket · Bucket overflowed before 2 a.m.".
Then three quick rows from the same table: "Barrel", "Street trash container", "Plastic garbage container". On the last three sentences,
two dots on the map less than 5 miles apart, one labelled "20+ in", the other "under 6 in"; chip "4 TIMES"; source line "Weather Bureau
survey team, reported in USGS Professional Paper 804".
> I read the survey table. A Weather Bureau recording gauge in Nelson County, the kind that logs rain as it falls, stopped at eleven inches, because it overflowed. Another bucket in the county overflowed before two in the morning. So most of what we know about that night came out of barrels and garbage cans that happened to be sitting outside. And they didn't agree. The survey team found more than twenty inches in some spots. A few miles away, the same team found less than six. That happened four separate times, which means one family's night could look nothing like the next hollow's.
