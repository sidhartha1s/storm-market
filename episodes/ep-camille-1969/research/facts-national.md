### Virginia death toll
113 (S3, S6, 13 Nov 1969 count, dead only); 113 + 39 missing = 152; 124 (S14 headline); 150 (S8 1973, S9 1999); 153 (S10 NWS Virginia page, S15, S16). Missing: 39 (S3), "never found" count not in any primary source.

### Virginia damage
over $116M (S3, S14); $113-$140M (S10); $140M (S15).

### Central pressure
905 mb = lowest measured, Gulf, 0016 UTC 17 Aug (S1 citing Simpson et al. 1970); 901 mb = rejected dropsonde 1815 UTC 17 Aug (S1); 909 mb = original landfall value (Bay St. Louis barometer; S4, S5, S6); 904 mb = second Breath reading, same eye passage (S1); 900 mb = reanalysis landfall (S1, S2, S7); 919 mb = reanalysed dropsonde value (S1).

### Winds
190 mph old sustained (S2); 175 mph reanalysis (S2, S7); "estimates at the coast are near 200 mph" (S6); gusts "at least 190 mph" (S3).

### Rain duration in Virginia
8 hours from about 7 p.m. (S3, S8 "8-hour deluge of about 28 inches", S9); "3 to 5 hours" (S6, S11 NWS later summaries). Peak amount: 27-28 in (S3), 28 in (S8), 27 in (S11, S14), 31 in unconfirmed (S3, S9).

The "Virginia is the largest single part" claim is supported only if the 153 figure is paired with a Gulf count of 143 or less, or by the unsourced AOML sentence. The primary NWS split (143 vs 113) makes the Gulf coast the larger part. Safe wording: "Virginia's flood killed more than a hundred people, close to or about as many as the Gulf coast, depending on whose count" and avoid "the deadliest part".
