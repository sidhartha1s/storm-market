## A2-S1 (marker: "a single house")
Claim 1, "up Davis Creek near Woods Mill ... small hollows that fold back off the creek": CONFIRMED.
- PP 804 p. 16 (pdf p. 24), RAW: "A number of missing houses, particularly in the Davis Creek basin, originally stood in small hollows which were scoured by avalanches."
- Place: OFR 70-51 fig. 4 caption, list of illustrations p. 4 (RAW): "Landslide at Davis Creek, 5 miles north of Lovingston". Woods Mill and Davis Creek are the same spot on OpenStreetMap (Nominatim: hamlet Woods Mill 37.8449 N, 78.8222 W; stream "Davis Creek, Woods Mill, Nelson County" 37.8435 N, 78.8239 W). OSM is secondary, but it agrees with the two USGS texts. "Near Woods Mill" holds.
Claim 2, "Some of those hollows had a single house in them": UNVERIFIED. No source on disk, and none found. Searched the full text of PP 804 and OFR 70-51 for "single house", "one house", "lone house": zero hits. The only house count for a Davis Creek hollow is the opposite: OFR 70-51 p. 22, "only two of 25 houses remained ... from this one hollow" (S11 itself uses it). Cut the sentence.
Replacement spoken line (S1 close): "That same night, up Davis Creek near Woods Mill, families were living in the small hollows that fold back off the creek into the mountain." (Drop "where they always had": not in any source. PP 804 says houses "originally stood in small hollows".)

## A2-S6 Williams and Guy, scar characteristics: CONFIRMED (two small wording fixes)
- PP 804 abstract p. 1 (pdf p. 9), RAW: "(1) they usually followed pre-existing depressions on hillsides and occurred on slopes greater than 35 percent, ... (3) hillsides facing north, northeast and east were more susceptible to avalanching than slopes facing other directions".
- Body p. 17 (pdf p. 25): "Based on 12 typical scars that were measured in detail". Authors: title page "By GARNETT P. WILLIAMS and HAROLD P. GUY" and OFR 70-51 p. 41 "SEDIMENT ASPECTS OF THE FLOOD By Garnett P. Williams and Harold P. Guy". Aspect body: "slopes facing north, northeast, and east experienced several times as many avalanches as slopes facing most other directions" (table 4 text).
- 35 percent = 0.35 ft per ft = about 1 ft up per 2.9 ft across: "about a foot up for every three feet across" holds. The paper also says typical scar gradients run 0.30 to 0.80, most commonly 0.50 to 0.60, so "steeper than thirty-five percent" is the abstract's wording and fair for "usually".
- Fix 1: "They began on slopes steeper than thirty-five percent" -> "They usually came off slopes steeper than thirty-five percent" (the abstract says they "occurred on" such slopes; the start point is a separate finding).
- Fix 2: "old hollows already worn into the hillside" -> "dips already in the hillside" (source word: "pre-existing depressions"; "worn" is invented).
- "Went back and measured": supported (12 scars measured in detail, 186 scars plotted from maps, PP 804 p. 41). "I read their paper" is the narrator's own line.

## A2-S7 Scar size: CORRECTED (one source for the numbers)
- OFR 70-51 p. 41 (pdf p. 53), Williams and Guy chapter, RAW: "The scars are generally restricted to a 40-squaremile area in the middle of Nelson County, just south of Horseshoe Mountain ... A typical scar is about 1 to 3 feet deep, 25-75 feet wide (gradually widening in the down-slope direction) and 300-600 feet long (fig. 21)."
- PP 804 p. 17, RAW: "Typical avalanche scars range from about 200-800 feet in horizontal length, 25 to 75 feet in width and 1 to 3 feet in depth. Some scars are as short as 20 feet or as long as 1,000 feet, as wide as 200 feet, and as deep as 15 or 20 feet."
- Width 25 to 75 and depth 1 to 3 agree. Length does not: 300-600 (OFR) vs 200-800 (PP 804). "One or two football fields" (300 to 600 ft) matches OFR 70-51 only; PP 804's 200 to 800 would be "about one to three". The picture says the photograph is from PP 804, so the on-screen source line must say OFR 70-51 for the length, or the label must change.
- Boulders: PP 804 p. 14 (pdf p. 22), RAW: "The Nelson County avalanches included soil, trees and other plants, water, and usually rocks ranging from gravel to boulders up to 10 feet in intermediate diameter." Holds.
- "Strips": PP 804 p. 14 "downslope-trending strips on the hillsides" and Sharpe quote "long and relatively narrow track". Holds.
- "Forty square miles in the middle of Nelson County, just south of Horseshoe Mountain" holds, OFR 70-51 only. "Most of those scars sit packed into" is the writer's sweep; the source says "generally restricted to". Replace "most of those scars sit packed into" with "those scars are mostly confined to".
Replacement spoken line: "Each scar is a long strip down the slope. A typical one runs twenty-five to seventy-five feet wide, three to six hundred feet long, which is one or two football fields, and only one to three feet deep. ... And those scars are mostly confined to about forty square miles in the middle of Nelson County, just south of Horseshoe Mountain." On screen: "300 to 600 ft long · USGS OFR 70-51" (not PP 804). The "ten-foot boulder" shape is sourced (PP 804 p. 14).
