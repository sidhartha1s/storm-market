## 1. "Hurricane Camille (1969) - The Original Monster Storm | Documentary" (Rex Jones)
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mOLSKRBhcGM . Channel 2,020 subscribers. A University of Mississippi (Southern Documentary Project)
film re-posted on the director's own channel.
- **Lead:** NOT QUOTABLE. The video has no captions and no auto-captions (`yt-dlp --list-subs`: "has no automatic captions", "has no subtitles"),
  and no media may be downloaded. The first two chapter titles, from the video's own chapter list, are "Living with the Gulf" (0:00) and
  "Unprecedented intensity" (0:00). The description opens: "one of the most powerful storms ever to strike the Mississippi Gulf Coast".
- **Minutes on Virginia vs coast:** 0 vs 56, from the 18 chapter titles (none names Virginia, Nelson County or inland flooding) and the
  description (no "Virginia", no "Nelson"). The most-liked "you missed Virginia" comment (127 likes) confirms it. Treat as UNVERIFIED
  until the film is watched.
- **Numbers stated:** none readable. Chapters hint at what it covers: "The Richelieu myth" (22:00), "The Seabees recovery effort" (18:00),
  "The 'Faith, Hope, Charity' graves" (43:00).

## 2. "HURRICANE CAMILLE 1969: The Category 5 Storm Too Strong to Measure" (FAULT LINES)
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nKvUuTK2piQ . Channel 13,300 subscribers. Narrated with a synthetic-sounding voice (viewers say so).
- **Lead (0:00):** "August 17th, 1969. Half past 11:00 at night on the Mississippi Gulf Coast. The most powerful hurricane ever
  measured on approach to the United States is coming ashore in darkness."
- **Teaser at 1:30, a Virginia hook inside the first two minutes:** "the deadliest hours of this disaster will not happen here and not
  tonight. They will happen two days later, 900 miles away in mountains where no one was ever warned at all."
- **Minutes on Virginia vs coast:** Virginia 21:00 to 25:30 (about 4.5 min, 15 %); the rest is the coast, the recon flights, the surge, the
  Richelieu, and the Saffir-Simpson scale (26:00 to 29:30). Camille's one-sentence thesis is the scale: "the reason a category scale exists".
- **Numbers stated:** pressure 901 mb at the last recon flight, about 900 mb at landfall; winds "near 190 mph" estimated, "about 175 mph at
  landfall" after reanalysis; surge "24 and a half feet" and "24.6 ft above normal" at Pass Christian; Gulf Coast "143 people died";
  Nelson County "113 of them, roughly 1% of everyone in the county"; "Some of the missing were never found" (no count); rain "about 27 inches"
  in "roughly 8 hours", gauges "more than 30"; "Add Virginia to the coast and Camille's toll passes 250". Richelieu: "about two dozen people stayed...
  most did not survive". Also says the "hurricane party" story is a legend.
- **Internal contradiction to note:** the teaser says the deadliest hours happen in Virginia, but its own figures are 143 on the coast and
  113 in Nelson County. "Deadliest" in our angle depends on which Virginia count the primary sources support.

## 3. "The Storm That Cut an Island in Half - Camille 1969" (Dark Horizon)
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QNkW1ndzsag . Channel 49,100 subscribers. Description sells flood-preparedness gear.
- **Lead (0:00):** "August 17th, 1969. Just before midnight, a category 5 hurricane slams into the Mississippi coast and starts erasing it.
  An estimated 175 mph. A storm surge 24 ft tall. 900 millibars."
- **Virginia hook by 1:00, and in the description:** "Two days later, the storm is officially dead... That night, it kills more than 100 people in the
  mountains of Virginia without one warning." Description: "That night it killed 124 people in the mountains of Virginia, and no flash flood warning
  was ever issued." This is the same shape as our angle draft.
- **Minutes on Virginia vs coast:** Virginia 9:30 to 14:30 (about 4.5 min, 26 %) after 9 min of coast; closing 2.5 min on Katrina.
- **Numbers stated:** 175 mph, 900 mb (908 and 905 on earlier flights), surge 24.6 ft "for the next 36 years, the highest"; Gulf Coast 143 dead;
  Nelson County "124 people die... One out of every 100 residents"; "33 of Virginia's dead were never found"; "over 250 in all"; rain "At least 27 inches
  of rain fall in roughly 5 hours", "Government meteorologists later classify that downpour as close to the maximum rainfall physically possible at that
  location"; "The local Weather Bureau office receives exactly one phone call before the lines go down. No flash flood warning is ever issued";
  "more than a hundred of its bridges are gone"; $1.4 billion damage. Richelieu: "Eight of the people inside are killed" (FAULT LINES and the 1969
  film say most or 23 of 24: the videos disagree).
- This is the closest competitor to our angle: it already carries 124, the never-found count (33), the "probable maximum" idea and the no-warning claim.

## 4. "Storm Stories: Hurricane Camille Part I" (The Weather Channel)
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=okXBaOIvkQ4 . Channel 1.39 million subscribers. A re-upload of the Jim Cantore series episode.
- **Lead (0:00):** a survivor sound bite, then the show open: "never in my lifetime will I ever experience anything as severe as Hurricane Camille...
  this coast was gone as we knew it and it was like you were looking at Hiroshima or Nagasaki after the bomb blast". At 1:00 the setup is the date:
  "August 17th 1969 a weekend music festival called Woodstock is making national headlines but on the Mississippi Gulf Coast the big story is Hurricane Camille".
- **Minutes on Virginia vs coast:** 0 vs 22. Follows a reporters' night at the Buena Vista Hotel in Biloxi. Part II was not pulled.
- **Numbers stated:** "132 lives in Mississippi alone"; surge "20 feet, 8 feet higher than any previous hurricane"; gusts "150 miles an hour" then "nearing 200";
  a ship log "anemometer fails at 200 knots"; "nearly 500 businesses"; "100 boats". No Virginia figure.

## 5. "Living in Virginia: Remembering Hurricane Camille" (VPM)
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dT_UVZDOhWw . Channel 45,700 subscribers. A 1999 public-TV episode ("30 years ago this summer"), put on YouTube in 2014.
Captions are all caps.
- **Lead (0:06):** "30 YEARS AGO THIS SUMMER, VIRGINIA EXPERIENCED ITS DEADLIEST STORM IN MEMORY." Then a survivor sound bite: "ROBERT WAS LOST. HIS WIFE.
  THREE DAUGHTERS. AND THE GRANDDAUGHTER."
- **Minutes on Virginia vs coast:** about 25 vs 1.5 (the Gulf shows as a clip at 2:00 to 3:30, then Tennessee and Kentucky, then Nelson County). It follows
  the Raines brothers (Warren, 14, and Carl), Massies Mill, Davis Creek, the helicopter search, the Mennonite disaster service, and what changed in the county.
- **Numbers stated:** "more than two feet of rain" in "SIX HOURS OR SO", "a storm Virginia hadn't seen for perhaps 1000 years"; Massies Mill on-air clip
  "TOTAL COUNT, 23 GONE"; Davis Creek "50 PEOPLE... 20 SHARED THE SAME LAST NAME"; "Two days after the flood the death toll stood at about 45, the number of
  missing twice that number"; final toll "IMPOSSIBLE TO SAY. MOST AGREE IT WAS ABOUT 130. THE FIGURE FOR ALL OF VIRGINIA WAS ONLY SLIGHTLY HIGHER";
  "DOZENS OF BRIDGES"; winds "200 MILES PER HOUR" at the coast. Two names given as never found: Donna Fay Wood and her brother Gary, 4.

## Ruled out as our lead (per references/angle.md "Rule out the default")

What the top videos open with, so we do not:
1. **The Mississippi landfall at about 11:30 p.m. on 17 Aug with the records.** Fault Lines and Dark Horizon both open on it (900 mb, 175 mph, a 24.6 ft
   surge); The Weather Channel opens on a survivor's "never in my lifetime" line and the same night; Rex Jones's first chapters are the Gulf and
   "Unprecedented intensity".
2. **Strongest-hurricane / Category 5 / "the storm that made the scale".** Fault Lines builds the whole video on it ("the reason a category scale exists").
   The Weather Channel ends on the Saffir-Simpson line.
3. **The Richelieu "hurricane party".** A lead or turning point in Dark Horizon (11:30), Fault Lines (16:00), the 1969 film, and a chapter in Rex Jones
   ("The Richelieu myth", 22:00). It is also what viewers argue about in the comments.
4. **The coast's evacuation or reporters' night in a hotel** (Fault Lines 8:00; The Weather Channel's whole episode).
5. **The "deadliest part was inland, in Virginia" teaser as the opening beat.** Used twice already within the first 90 s: Fault Lines (1:30, "the deadliest hours...
   will not happen here") and Dark Horizon (0:30 to 1:00 and in its description, "124 people... no flash flood warning"). A cold open that reveals the same
   twist is not new; the surprise is spent for anyone who watched either.
6. **The 27 inches in about 5 to 8 hours and "the probable maximum" line, the one-phone-call line, and "no warning" as the headline fact.** Dark Horizon already says
   all of these in one minute (10:00 to 11:00).
7. **The Raines brothers as the entry point** (VPM's centrepiece; Dark Horizon uses them at 12:30). Strong, but the people-story is taken.

What no video leads with, or covers in depth (observation from the transcripts, not a locked angle):
- No video of the 5 general ones starts in Nelson County. The Virginia-led videos (VPM, Jamey Singleton) are local public-TV material that does not mention
  the coast, the records or any of the national numbers.
- The never-found people are a number in one video (Dark Horizon: 33) and two named people in another (VPM: Donna Fay Wood, Gary). Nobody works through how
  the missing count moved over the weeks (Jamey Singleton's tape has 101 missing on 24 Aug, 88 on 26 Aug, "32... probably never be found" about 1 Sep).
- Every competitor stated a different Virginia death toll: 106 (1969 film), 113 (Fault Lines), "about 130" (VPM), 124 (Dark Horizon), 153 (a commenter), 39 (a commenter).
  The count itself is a gap; the primary-source check decides which, and the script must say what it counts.
- The inland-flood warning gap is stated once (Dark Horizon's "exactly one phone call") and not sourced on screen.
