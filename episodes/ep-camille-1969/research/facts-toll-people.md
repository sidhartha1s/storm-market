## Human stories

Living people: only those a published on-record source names are listed. No private details beyond what the source prints.

1. Raines family, Massies Mill (Tye River). On 19-20 Aug 1969 the family of seven plus four neighbor children (Wood family) left their home in a station wagon; the Tye River stopped the car and they went on foot. Five of the seven Raines died (parents and three siblings). Warren Raines, 14 (Radio IQ), held a tree until morning; his brother Carl survived in a willow tree; rescuers in boats took them at daybreak (WTKR). Sister Ginger, about 7, was found two weeks later, 20 miles away (WTKR). Johanna Raines, 18, died (C-VILLE). Donna Fay Wood and Gary Wood, in the car with them, were never recovered (C-VILLE). Quotes: Warren Raines, "My mother said, 'Let go. We'll catch you.' I let go of it. And I got there, and they were gone." (WTKR 2 Sep 2019) and "Lost our childhood in one night. Became orphans in just minutes," (same); "I saw entire houses floating by, a lot of automobiles, cattle, logs, telephone poles, just all kinds of things were floating by." (Radio IQ 19 Aug 2019). Sources: P31, P28, P17, WHSV Part 1 (P18). Family buried together at Jonesboro Cemetery (WTKR). Inconsistent detail: one outlet says Carl was 16 (search summary only, UNVERIFIED).
2. Davis Creek and the Huffman family. 52 died in Davis Creek, 18 of them Huffmans (WDBJ 2019, C-VILLE); Tiffany Spencer, a Huffman relative, says 23 in the Huffman household (WSET 2016). Deborah Harvey, 8 in 1969, lived on higher ground: "You started realizing that all of these people who rode the school bus with you, went to church with you, were gone," and "It's not something that people can understand who haven't lived through it," (Tim Saunders, WDBJ, 19 Aug 2019, https://www.wdbj7.com/content/news/Camille-survivor-recalls-devastation-along-Nelson-Countys-Davis-Creek-554647111.html). Her father spent a week building a jeep-passable road. USGS says the 50 Davis Creek deaths could mostly have been avoided with a warning (P6).
3. Milton Harris, 17, and sister Iris, 6 (Muddy Creek, Route 29). Family of seven went to reach their church, the car stalled on Route 29, they stayed in it all night as water rose; all survived. Harris: "My father kept saying we need to keep an eye on the creek," Iris: "it was raining so hard you couldn't breathe," (Urbanowicz, WHSV, 19 Aug 2019, P18 URL). Ages as given by the tool; confirm.
4. Dick Whitehead and Sheriff Bill Whitehead. Whitehead, about to start college, was with his father the sheriff that night. Sheriff cut off in the west; radio call relayed by Augusta County to Richmond civil defense (P29). Whitehead: "By the first night, it was obvious it was not a rescue." (P30). 30 in the Nelson school system died, per Whitehead (C-VILLE).
5. Ed Tinsley, State Trooper in Amherst County, drove county roads, worked Nelson about three weeks: "It took me about 8 hours to do that and there were no main, secondary roads in to Nelson County." (WHSV Part 2, 20 Aug 2019). Plus P38. WTKR: "There is no comparison to anything I worked on,"
6. Nelson voices on the night (Radio IQ, 19 Aug 2019): Ed Rothgeb (late) "My house is on a hill, and when I stepped out into my yard I had water resting in my yard around my ankles"; Cliff Wood (late) "Trees broke loose from the ground, and the earth and the rocks tumbled -- rocks as big as automobiles."; Audrey Diane Evans "It was raining like crazy when we came back, and if you were out in it it was almost like you couldn't breathe". Source P26 URL.
7. Medical examiner side: Dr. Robert Raynor assisted chief medical examiner Dr. James Gamble; said in 2009 that people either escaped injury entirely "or they died." (C-VILLE, tool paraphrase of a 2009 statement; outlet of the 2009 statement not given). Pair with P7: blunt-force injuries.
8. Leads not opened: Radford University Appalachian Folklife Archive, oral histories "Night of Terror: Hurricane Camille hits Nelson County" (Heather D. Branch, 2000, four interviewees; transcript 200.446.07.pdf) and "Stratton's Survival: the Nelson County Flood of 1969" (Robert Stratton, interviewed by Lindsay Woodson; family lived a month on canned food and a woodstove). Pages are JavaScript-only; transcripts not read. Best next step for dated, on-record quotes.

## Wording check for the angle
"Killed about 124, and some were never found": supported for Nelson County by local sources and the NWS state page range (123 to 126). "Deadliest part of a famous coast hurricane" fits as Virginia 150-153 against Gulf Coast counts (outside this slice). Do not say "124 in Virginia": the federal and NWS Virginia totals are 150-153, and Encyclopedia Virginia's 124 is the outlier use. "Some were never found": about 32 to 33 (one in four of Nelson's dead); no primary final number.
