## B. SEGMENT BLOCKS

### A3-S1 (cut off, radio call, Augusta relay)
- Claim: Whitehead stuck in the west, got a distress call out by radio; Augusta County sheriff's office heard it and passed it to civil defense in Richmond. Verdict: CONFIRMED (read raw).
- Raw (C-VILLE, Lisa Provence, 14 Aug 2019, RAW page text): "Nelson Sheriff Bill Whitehead lived in the western district of the county, near Massies Mill and Tyro, and he couldn't get to Lovingston, says his son, Dick Whitehead... Like others, he was trapped by washed out bridges. But he was able to make a radio distress call that was heard by the sheriff's department in Augusta County, which alerted the civil defense office in Richmond."
- URL: https://c-ville.com/a-flood-in-the-mountains-50-years-later-nelson-county-remembers-camilles-devastation/ (curl now returns Cloudflare 403; the page was read from the raw HTML copy saved earlier today in the scratch folder, `fc\cville.html`).
- Draft says "the sheriff's office over in Augusta County": C-VILLE says "sheriff's department". Use "sheriff's department".
- Geography: CONFIRMED. Wikipedia "Nelson County, Virginia" (RAW): "The Blue Ridge Mountains form the northwest boundary of the county"; adjacent counties "Augusta County - northwest", "Amherst County - southwest". Augusta sits across the ridge, to the northwest, not simply "west". Spoken "on the far side of the Blue Ridge" holds.
- "phone lines were down, roads and bridges gone": WHSV Part 2 (RAW): "all telephone and radio communication was lost." WTKR (RAW), Tinsley: "One thing we didn't have for about 36 hours was communications." Holds.
- "By morning, Nelson County was cut off": no source says "cut off" in those words; WHSV: "there were no main, secondary roads in to Nelson County" (Tinsley) and "The only way to get help into the county was by helicopter." Holds in substance.
- Spoken fix: "the sheriff's department over in Augusta County".

### A3-S2 (nuclear plan, Tinsley)
- "civil defense plan had been written for a nuclear disaster": CORRECTED. C-VILLE raw: "The county's civil defense team had been preparing for a nuclear disaster, not a natural disaster." That is preparing, not a written plan. Replacement: "The county's civil defense team had been preparing for a nuclear disaster, not a flood."
- "just getting into Nelson took him about eight hours": CORRECTED. WHSV Part 2 (Aubrey Urbanowicz, 20 Aug 2019, https://www.whsv.com/content/news/Camilles-Destruction-Part-2-557629921.html, RAW): "On the morning of August 20th, Trooper Tinsley was called into work. His job was to drive the county to see what roads were open. 'It took me about 8 hours to do that and there were no main, secondary roads in to Nelson County.'" The eight hours is the drive to see what roads were open, and the county driven is not named in the text (Tinsley was an Amherst County trooper). It is not "getting into Nelson". Replacement: "Ed Tinsley was a state trooper next door in Amherst County. That morning he was sent out to check which roads were open, and it took him about eight hours. There was no main or secondary road into Nelson County."
- Cite card quote: "It took me about 8 hours to do that and there were no main, secondary roads in to Nelson County." CONFIRMED verbatim, speaker Ed Tinsley, WHSV 20 Aug 2019. Card should say "State Police, Amherst County" (WHSV: "State Trooper in Amherst county in 1969"). If the card shows the quote alone, it must not be read as 8 hours to reach Nelson.
- "worked there about three weeks": CONFIRMED. WHSV raw: "Tinsley was in Nelson county for about three weeks after the storm." WTKR raw (2 Sep 2019): "Retired State Trooper Ed Tinsley labored 27 straight days helping the county recover."
- "lack of communication was the biggest thing": CONFIRMED. WHSV raw: Tinsley "The lack of communication was the biggest thing". Draft's [verify P38] fine: P38 also has "Human loss was the big thing..." which is a different quote, do not blend.

### A3-S10 (later counts include the 1969 missing) [verify]
- UNVERIFIED as a statement of fact. What exists: NWS Sterling (RAW, from factcheck-A) "At least 153 Virginians lost their lives in Camille". USGS OFR 99-518 (RAW, factcheck-A) "killed 150 people". USGS PP 804 (Williams and Guy 1973, RAW text of the scan): "About 150 people, 125 of them in Nelson County, died in the storm and flood." Cardinal News 21 Aug 2024 (RAW): "at least 124 people were killed in Virginia, with some accounts as high as 153". Encyclopedia Virginia: "Virginia counted 124 deaths" in one place and "113 deaths ... thirty-nine missing and presumed dead" in another. None of these says how 150 or 153 was built, or that it includes the missing. WTKR (RAW) even writes "1 percent of Nelson's population, more than 150 people, were killed or missing" for Nelson alone, which shows how loosely totals mix.
- 113 + 39 = 152 is arithmetic, not a sourced build, and PP 804's own "125 in Nelson" is Nelson-only inside the 150. The line "Now add the thirty-nine missing ... That comes to a hundred fifty-two. The Weather Service's later count is a hundred fifty-three" implies the later counts were built that way. No source supports it.
- Replacement that is true as sourced (orchestrator can keep the card idea): "The hundred thirteen counts only people who were found. Thirty-nine more were still missing. Later counts run to a hundred fifty or a hundred fifty-three, and none of the sources says how they added the missing in. The hundred twenty-four you'll see in a lot of places is Nelson County alone." Drop "That comes to a hundred fifty-two", or keep it only as the narrator's own sum with "close to" and no claim about the later counts; the card "152 vs 153" is the same implication and should go.
- "113 only counts the people who were found": supported by USGS OFR 70-51 wording "113 dead ... 39 persons still missing" (factcheck-B).
