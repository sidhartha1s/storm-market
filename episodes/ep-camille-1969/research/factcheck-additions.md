## A1-S13: CORRECTED (small)
Line: "Right at Massies Mill, the USGS later worked out the Tye's peak from the marks the water left behind. It came to about seventy thousand cubic feet of water every second. That's from a valley that drains only about sixty-six square miles."
Raw, USGS Open-File Report 70-51 (Camp and Miller, 1970), pdftotext of https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1970/0051/report.pdf (cached `ofr7051.txt` line 6450 to 6453, printed p. 100 to 101), RAW: "02-0264.00 Tye River at Massies Mill, Va. (Miscellaneous site). Location.--... at bridge on State Highway 56, 0.7 mile southeast of Massies Mill, Nelson County, and 0.9 mile downstream from Rocky Run. Drainage area.--66 sq mi. Maximum.--August 1969: Discharge 70,000 cfs Aug. 20 from slope-area measurement."
70,000 cfs and 66 sq mi: CONFIRMED RAW (V20 was FETCH-flagged; now RAW).
"From the marks the water left behind": the OFR names the method (slope-area) but does not describe it in the text I read. USGS Fact Sheet 2012-3112 (https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2012/3112/fs2012-3112.pdf, pdftotext, cached `add-fs3112.txt`), RAW: "The slope-area method is a technique for estimating the peak discharge of a flood after the water has receded ... it relies on evidence left behind by the flood, such as high-water marks (HWMs) on trees or buildings." So the phrase is a true description of the method.
"Right at Massies Mill": the measurement site is 0.7 mile southeast of the village, at the Route 56 bridge. The station is titled "at Massies Mill", but "right at" overstates.
Replacement spoken line: "Near Massies Mill, the USGS later worked out the Tye's peak from the marks the water left behind. It came to about seventy thousand cubic feet of water every second. That's from a valley that drains only about sixty-six square miles."
