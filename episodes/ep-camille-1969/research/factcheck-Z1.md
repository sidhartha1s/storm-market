## Z1-S1 · "about 2:50 AM cloudburst cut lights and phones": CORRECTED (one word)
- Claim: "the hardest burst of the storm hit, and for many people in Nelson County it cut the lights and the phones."
- Source, RAW: USGS Professional Paper 804 (Williams and Guy 1973), https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0804/report.pdf, printed page 5 (text starts under the "THE FLOOD 5" header): "A particularly strong cloudburst struck about 0250, cutting off electricity and telephones for many of the inhabitants." Same page 4: "The storm lasted about 7 or 8 hours, from around 2030 hours August 19 until 0330 or 0400 August 20. Except for a brief lull at about 2330 or 2400 hours, the rainfall, thunder, and lightning were intense during the entire storm."
- Time (0250 = "ten to three", "about") holds; "lights and phones for many" holds. "Hardest burst of the storm" does not: PP 804 says "particularly strong", not strongest, and calls the whole storm intense. No gauge series in the paper ranks the bursts.
- Replacement spoken line: "At about ten to three that morning, a particularly strong cloudburst hit, and for many people in Nelson County it cut the lights and the phones."
- Picture/popfig "about 2:50 AM · Aug 20" and source line "USGS Professional Paper 804, pp. 4 to 5": fine (the cloudburst sentence itself is p. 5; the storm-duration sentence is p. 4). PP 804 gives no time zone for 0250; the popfig does not need one.

## Z1-S2 · slides in the dark until about 4 AM: CORRECTED (sequence, not facts)
- Claim: "So when the slopes above the hollows let go, many families were in the dark with no way to call anyone. The slides went on until about four."
- Source, RAW, PP 804 p. 15: "Observations during the present storm were obscured because of the darkness." p. 16: "The accounts of local residents suggest that the avalanching was more frequent toward the end of the storm. Some slides occurred after the rain had abated to a sprinkle or mist, at about 0400 hours on August 20." p. 16 (Erosion section): "Some big slides were heard after the thunder and lightning had stopped around 0400 August 20."
- Facts hold: slides ran to about 0400 (the paper says some came after 0400, so "until about four" is a slight undershoot, not wrong); power and phones were out for many from 0250 (S1 quote).
- Problem: "So when the slopes ... let go, [families] were in the dark" reads as if the slides began after the 0250 outage. PP 804 gives no clock time for the first slides and says avalanching ran through the storm, more often toward the end. Slides before 0250 happened with power on for most. The paper does not say the outage preceded any particular slide.
- Replacement spoken line: "By then, many families were in the dark with no way to call anyone, and the slopes above the hollows were still letting go. The slides went on until about four."
- Picture "slides one, two, three then clock runs to about 4 AM": fine under the replacement.

So 6 Rockfish, 2 James: the two-river wording holds.
- Same list, RAW: "Of the unidentified dead, the adults were believed to be traveling on U.S. 29 when they were swept away in the floodwaters." No source I read says creeks carried them. PP 804 says nothing on where bodies were found.
- Replacement spoken line: "The flood swept people down into the Rockfish and the James, and as I'm telling you this, none of the eight those rivers gave back has a name yet." ("they gave back" is ambiguous: a listener can read "they" as the state. "Those rivers" removes that.)
- Source line "Virginia Department of Health · August 2026 · WHRO, 13 Oct 2026": fine. Eight markers "along both rivers, no ages": fine; keep the single James marker above the Rockfish mouth on the James, not on the Rockfish.

## Z1-S4 · USGS OFR 99-518, Davis Creek, warning, Oak Hill Church: CORRECTED (scope of "went back over Davis Creek")
- RAW: https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1999/ofr-99-0518/ofr-99-0518.html, section "MITIGATION OF DEBRIS FLOW HAZARDS" (HTML, no page numbers). Title: "Inventory of debris flows and floods in the Lovingston and Horseshoe Mountain, VA, 7.5' quadrangles, from the August 19/20, 1969, storm in Nelson County, Virginia", B. A. Morgan, G. Iovine, P. Chirico, G. F. Wieczorek, Open-File Report 99-518, 1999 ("preliminary").
- Full sentences, verbatim: "The third measure involves early warning based on predicted rainfall and upstream measurement of intense rainfalls exceeding 2 inches/per hour for more than 4 hours. If both evacuation plans and a warning system had been in place in Nelson County during 1969, the citizens along Davis Creek would have been advised just before midnight of August 19 to seek refuge at a nearby structure not in the path of streams draining steep slopes, such as the Oak Hill Church. Had all the citizens heard and heeded this warning, most or all of the 50 fatalities in that one area could have been avoided."
- Hypothetical, not what happened: the report states both sentences as conditionals ("If both ... had been in place", "would have been advised", "Had all ... heeded", "could have been avoided"). The script presents it as the report's hypothetical: S4 "With a warning system and a plan, it says people there would have been told"; S5 "If everyone had heard it and gone, the report says ... could have lived". Both attribute to "it says"/"the report says" and keep the conditional. PASS on that point.
- Problems:
  1. S4 "the US Geological Survey went back over Davis Creek": the report is a two-quadrangle inventory of the whole storm in Nelson County; Davis Creek is one example in the Mitigation section and elsewhere ("Davis Creek and Freshwater Cove experienced many debris flows ... run-out distances exceeding 2 miles"). "Thirty years later" holds (1999 - 1969).
  2. The warning trigger is the authors' construct (rainfall above 2 in/hr for 4+ hours, plus forecast). "Just before midnight" is their estimate, not a recorded event.
  3. Cite card elides without a mark: "just before midnight of August 19 ... such as the Oak Hill Church" skips "to seek refuge at a nearby structure not in the path of streams draining steep slopes,". Add the ellipsis or quote the clause whole.
  4. The report gives no location for Oak Hill Church; the church marker on our Davis Creek map must come from another source, or stay unplaced.
- Replacement spoken line: "Thirty years later, the US Geological Survey went back over the storm's damage in Nelson County. With a warning system and an evacuation plan, its report says, people along Davis Creek would have been told, just before midnight, to get to a building out of the creek's path, like the Oak Hill Church."
- Card text: "... a warning system ... just before midnight of August 19 ... to seek refuge at a nearby structure not in the path of streams draining steep slopes, such as the Oak Hill Church" · USGS Open-File Report 99-518, 1999.
