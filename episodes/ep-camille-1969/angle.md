# ep-camille-1969 · angle

Subject: Hurricane Camille, August 1969, the night flood in Nelson County, Virginia (19 to 20 August 1969).

## Status: the owner's tick, and why it needs one more word from him

**The owner's tick (21 Oct 26, relayed by the coordinator session, not his own words in this session):** he ticked the third
documentary slot, where Camille 1969 was the recommended default over Helene 2024. The angle line that came with it:

> "the deadliest part of a famous coast hurricane was in the Virginia mountains: a night flood in Nelson County killed about 124, and some were never found"

**What the fact pass did to that line (21 Oct 26, rows in `research/`):**
- "The deadliest part" does not hold on the primary split. NWS Jackson: "256 deaths (143 on the Gulf Coast and 113 in the
  Virginia floods)" (facts-national N14). Virginia is 44 % of that total. Virginia is the larger part only if a later count
  (153, NWS Wakefield and Sterling) is set against the 1969 Gulf count, and no single source makes that comparison except a NOAA
  staff blog with no breakdown (N16). The safe wording is "nearly half".
- "Killed about 124": 124 is the common local number for Nelson County, not Virginia. Federal Nelson numbers are 123 and 126
  (NWS), the state marker says 114 died and 37 remain missing; Virginia-wide sources say 113 dead plus 39 missing (USGS, Nov 1969),
  150 (USGS 1973 and 1999) or 153 (NWS). "Killed" is a hard flag word (`references/flag-words.md`). Safe wording: "more than a
  hundred twenty people in Nelson County lost their lives".
- "Some were never found": holds. 32 (WHSV 2019) or 33 (C-VILLE, News & Advance); no primary gives the final number.
- The twist is spent. Two 2026 videos already open on it inside 90 s: FAULT LINES (251,635 views, 1:30: "the deadliest hours of
  this disaster will not happen here") and Dark Horizon (157,181 views, its description: "That night it killed 124 people in the
  mountains of Virginia, and no flash flood warning was ever issued"). It fails `references/angle.md` test 1: a viewer who watched
  the top videos already learned it (`research/teardowns.md`).
- New since those videos were planned: on 31 Aug 2026 the Virginia Department of Health asked the public to help identify eight
  people whose remains were recovered around the Rockfish River after the flood (29News/WVIR and WJLA, both read raw on 21 Oct 26).
  No competitor video carries it.

So the meaning moved. Per `references/angle.md` "A changed meaning needs a new lock", candidate 1 below waits for the owner's
words. The hook and intro in `edit_script.md` are drafted on candidate 1 so the gate can run in one pass; if he picks another,
they are thrown away, not adapted.

## Candidates (references/angle.md method, 21 Oct 26)

**Ruled out as the lead** (what the top videos open with, `research/teardowns.md`): the Mississippi landfall near midnight with
the records (900 mb, 175 mph, 24.6 ft storm tide); "the storm that made the Category 5 scale"; the Richelieu "hurricane party";
the coast evacuation or the reporters' hotel night; "the deadliest part was in Virginia, with no warning" as the cold-open twist;
the 27 inches near the "probable maximum" as the headline fact; the Raines brothers as the entry point.

1. **RECOMMENDED: the flood that still is not finished counting.** "Camille's Virginia flood still is not closed: fifty-seven
   years later the state is asking for help to name eight people it recovered from the Rockfish River, and about thirty-two more
   from Nelson County were never found at all."
   - Breaks: the belief that a 1969 disaster is settled history with a known count.
   - Acts it needs: the night (rain, Massies Mill, the Raines family); the mountainsides that came down (debris avalanches,
     blunt-force injuries, why people were carried miles); the count that would not close (101 missing, then 88, then 39, then
     about 32; every source a different total); the eight and the appeal (VDH, 2026). Four acts plus the close.
   - Source: VDH appeal via 29News/WVIR 31 Aug 2026 and WJLA (raw); USGS OFR 70-51 p. 1 and 22 (113 dead, 39 missing; Davis Creek
     27 dead, 22 missing); USGS PP 804 (blunt-force injuries, Dr. Gamble identifying the victims); WSP 2030.
   - Still unverified: the never-found count (32 vs 33 vs 37, no primary); the VDH release itself (news copies only); the eight
     are described as seven people by age group in both news copies (2 adults, 1 older woman, 2 teens, 2 children); WHRO 13 Oct 26 piece
     not opened (DNS).
   - Footage: Library of Virginia aerials of Massies Mill, Tyro, the Tye at Norwood, Lovingston (rights "No known copyright
     restrictions", permission and fee asked: unknown, low risk); VCU reels of the James at Richmond (PD stated); USGS figures;
     our own maps and counters. No PD moving picture of Nelson County found yet.
2. **The mountains came down.** "Most of the people the flood took in the Virginia mountains were not drowned: the hillsides
   above them let go in the dark, and the geologists found broken bones and blunt-force injuries, not water, in most of them."
   - Source: USGS PP 804 abstract, "died from broken bones and other blunt-force injuries, rather than by drowning" (V34);
     survivor descriptions of the slides in PP 804 (V31, V32). No top video leads with it.
   - Fills: the science act, and it explains candidate 1's missing. Alone it is one mechanism, about one and a half acts. Best as an arc.
3. **The rain was already on paper.** "The rain on Nelson County was not beyond imagining: in 1956 the Weather Bureau had worked out
   for the Army Corps of Engineers the most that could fall there, twenty-eight inches in six hours, and a trash barrel caught
   twenty-seven in about eight."
   - Source: USGS OFR 70-51 p. 7 (ESSA-Weather Bureau staff), the 1956 study and the trash-barrel row (N10, V2, V5).
   - Partly spent: Dark Horizon says "close to the maximum rainfall physically possible" in one line. The 1956 study and the barrel
     are new. One act. Best as an arc.
4. **Nearly half came two nights later.** "Nearly half of everyone Camille took, a hundred thirteen of two hundred fifty-six in the
   Weather Service's count, lost their lives two nights after landfall, in the Virginia mountains, from a storm no longer a hurricane."
   - This is the ticked line, corrected to what the sources say. True and sourced (N14), but spent as a cold open by FAULT LINES and
     Dark Horizon. Best as context inside the intro, not the lead.
5. **The warning that came before midnight.** "The record says there was no warning, but the USGS later wrote that most of the fifty
   on Davis Creek could have been saved by a warning issued before midnight."
   - Source: USGS OFR 99-518 (P6), only a partial quote read; contradicts "no warning" in OFR 70-51 (V36) and the NWS pages. No clock
     time for any Virginia warning found in a primary. Not usable until the warning record is found; kept as a lead for the fact lane.

**Recommendation: 1 as the spine, 2 and 3 as arcs inside it, 4 as one line of context in the intro.** 1 is the only candidate no
reference video has, it is current (the appeal is open now), and 2 and 3 give it its mechanism and science acts. It keeps the half of
the ticked line that holds ("some were never found") and drops the half the sources break ("the deadliest part").
