# Palm Sunday 1965: critique of the eight hooks and six intros in `hook-intro-draft.md`

Asked for by the owner, 11 Oct 26: "I think this is not going anywhere. finding it hard to visualize this as the hook or the intro. Find the defects of the stuff you just created. And see if you can fix it somehow. What is missing per hook, how to improve it, what can be better. I like the questions you're asking. But overall, I think something better can be done."

Written by an independent critic (helper p65-hook-critic, read-only), recorded here word for word by the orchestrator. Nothing in the rebuilds is fact-checked yet. IDs: V1 to V3 are the hooks of Versions 1 to 3, H4 to H8 the later hooks, IV1 to IV3 the intros of Versions 1 to 3, IA to IC the later intros.

Biggest defect: no draft gives the viewer a picture; every noun is a metaphor or a citation (desk, hands, word, a study) where a person, a clock or an object should stand.

## V1
Defect: "Stopped one desk short" is a figure of speech that answers the title's "who" with furniture. "First one" and "whose hands" are pronouns to decode.
Missing: a named holder, a stake, anyone unwarned.
Fix: Paul names police and the outcome: "Police headquarters heard the tornado warning and did nothing until it hit." [C2] Put five forty three on a clock graphic.

## V2
Defect: "Somebody" and "the word" dodge the title. The tail is about a study leaving out a name: a hook about a redaction. The date takes a spoken line the label carries.
Missing: who, when, what happened next.
Fix: "police" for "somebody", "where the tornado was" for "the word"; ask about the reason, not the missing name.

## V3
Defect: the best number in the set hangs on "word sat on one desk": passive, no actor. "An Indiana county seat" is nowhere.
Missing: the actor, and the two clock times that make thirty five minutes a running clock (about seven thirty, eight oh seven) [A13, C7].
Fix: "Police were told where the tornado was. About thirty five minutes later it hit the county seat, and they had told nobody."

## H4
Defect: cleanest sentence, but no time or place. The tail drifts to a survey team and "Did anyone fix the police desk?", an Act 6 question.
Missing: who was waiting at the other end.
Fix: keep the sentence, add the people it never reached, ask about that evening.

## H5
Defect: the quote lands before the viewer knows who speaks or about what. "Who knew" is unverified by the writer's own tag.
Missing: the setup that makes six words hit.
Fix: facts first, quote as the last words of Paul's clip, credited to "their chief".

## H6
Defect: right facts, wrong nouns. "Held a tornado's position" is map room talk, and the hook ends on "A 1966 study printed", a citation.
Missing: the request to tell the radio station, which makes silence a choice [C5].
Fix: "Police were told where the tornado was and asked to tell the radio station. Nobody did."

## H7
Defect: opens on a ratio from a study the viewer has never heard of; "forewarning" and "advisories" are report words. The last line leans on A13, where the director did tell police.
Missing: any single event.
Fix: drop as a hook. Seven of eight is an intro graphic: eight unnamed tiles, seven lit.

## H8
Defect: strongest of the eight ("Nobody did."), but "the caller" hides a civil defense director, and the fifteen minute line moves blame onto him.
Missing: a clock and a consequence.
Fix: name his role, end on eight oh seven, hold the fifteen minutes for Act 4.

## IV1
Defect: eleven lines, eleven subjects. "The forecast did not fail" comes second, before anything it could contradict. The researcher's name arrives at line four.
Missing: one person, and a question at the end.
Fix: one clock on screen from eleven forty five to five forty three; source name on a card.

## IV2
Defect: the claim is read out like a memo, then two roster sentences start "There were".
Missing: what any of those seven people did.
Fix: three roles, not seven, each lighting on a chain graphic as it is named.

## IV3
Defect: "never says which is which" tells the viewer the story has no places. A methods note in the first minute.
Missing: something standing where the county should be.
Fix: cut the line, say "one Indiana county", show the clock.

## IA
Defect: the Red Cross and the electric company get the same weight as police. "Did not always pass them on" is a hedge.
Missing: the family, who get one line and no detail.
Fix: write the telephone list on screen and end on a blank row.

## IB
Defect: seven lines of clock times and agencies before the claim. It reads as a log.
Missing: tension before the turn.
Fix: open on "in some places nobody did" [C2], then run the clock back to eleven forty five.

## IC
Defect: the only intro that starts with people, then drops them and runs the day backward.
Missing: what that family was doing: the first nice spring day [C21], "Lassie" on [E], "not a cloud in the sky" [C22].
Fix: stay in the living room for three lines built on those rows, then go to the telephone.

## Overall
- No Picture notes. El Reno writes one per spoken line. Unshowable here: desk, hands, word, position, forewarning, advisories, a study, offices now count, the line, one message, the caller.
- The title asks who. V1 to V3 answer "a desk" or "somebody". H4 to H8 say police but give them no clock, request or consequence inside Paul's clip.
- No unwarned person in any hook. Rows exist: the sheriff's "We didn't have any warning at all" [C6], the station that "couldn't prepare our listeners" [C8], a boy watching "Lassie" [E].
- The toll is a footnote: "Four Weather Service offices now count" puts the citation ahead of the lives. Say the number; source in small text on screen.
- Hidden county names cost the place stamp, map pin, town photograph and local front page. Stand ins: real clock times, roles as cards, the study's sentence as a document card, a chain graphic, named places (South Bend, the Elkhart Truth).
- Paul's extra words: sentence one pays the title (who, did what); sentence two gives the request or the clock in plain nouns, since his face is the only picture for ten seconds. Never the date or source; the label carries those.
- It sounds like a report: source names inside twenty seconds; six intros reshuffle the same facts and end on no question. No recorded sound exists (inventory E3).
- Check before voicing: C2 says police "had heard the State Police message". Read the page image before a line says "heard the tornado warning".

## Questions worth keeping
- What reason did the police chief give? (V2, H6)
- Who had it for those thirty five minutes? (V3)
- Were police the only ones who told nobody? (H7)
- If the announcer never got it, what could a family at home hear? (IV1, IC)
