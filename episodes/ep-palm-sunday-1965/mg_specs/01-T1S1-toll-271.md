**Spoken (T1-S1):** "Two hundred seventy one people lost their lives that day."
**One look:** real wreckage in two named Indiana places, and one number for the whole outbreak: 271, across six states.

## 0. What this graphic claims
271 people lost their lives in the outbreak of April 11, 1965, across six states, by the National Weather Service's count; other counts run from 260 to 266. The wreckage is real and each picture is the place its label names. The number is not Indiana's alone.

## 4. Must be true in the picture
1. The figure is `271`. No other figure in the frame is larger than 22 px; 260 and 266 appear only inside the source line. 137 is nowhere.
2. **The number is the whole outbreak's.** The label under it reads `lives lost across six states`, because both photographs are Indiana places and the number is not Indiana's.
3. The source line names the National Weather Service and says other counts exist. It is on screen for at least 1.5 s.
4. Each photograph is under its own label and no other; the label changes on the same frame as the cut. Both labels end in "Indiana".
5. The words are "lives lost". No "killed", "dead" or "deaths" (`references/flag-words.md`).
6. Never black: frame 0 is P1 at full brightness, straight off Paul's last word.
7. No siren icon, no funnel drawing, no tornado photograph (C1 and C6 are not used here), no third photograph.
8. Design sheet S3, S6, S7.
