# County key: which Indiana counties are "County A", "B", "C" in Brouillette (1966)

Grades: IDENTIFIED (two or more independent details match one county, none conflict), LIKELY (one strong match, nothing against), UNKNOWN.
Result: B = Grant County (Marion), LIKELY. C = Howard County (Kokomo), LIKELY, and only because B is. A = Elkhart County, LIKELY. Nothing reaches IDENTIFIED: the study names no county, and no second source was found that says "County B is X".

STUDY = Brouillette, DRC Working Paper 5, 1966, https://udspace.udel.edu/bitstreams/4195e27e-6820-45b7-aa77-bfbeb1bd8d8e/download , re-fetched this run and read as pdftotext OCR (printed page numbers below). IWX = https://www.weather.gov/iwx/1965_palmsunday_50 . facts.md row numbers refer to `facts.md` in this folder.

## What the study itself gives away (best evidence, read in the STUDY text)

1. The study lists the eight counties it worked in (printed pp. 4-5): "St. Joseph, Elkart, La Grange, Marshall, Grant, Howard, Boone, and Hamilton". County A, B, C are not forced to be among them (C is only "the adjacent county to the west"), but A and B almost surely are, because the study had interviews there.
2. The same list names the organizations interviewed per county. This is the only place the study leaks anything about who exists in each county:
   - Grant: Marion City Police, Sheriff, County Civil Defense, WMRI Radio, WTAF-TV.
   - Howard: Kokomo City Police, Sheriff, three radio stations. No County CD.
   - Boone: Lebanon City Police, Sheriff, County CD. No radio or TV station.
   - Hamilton: Noblesville City Police, Sheriff only. LaGrange: Sheriff only.
   - St. Joseph, Elkhart, Marshall: police, sheriff, CD (St. Joseph and Elkhart also radio).
3. Footnote 16 (printed p. 36 and footnote list) cites the Lebanon Reporter, "Was Adequate Alarm Given to Boone Tornado Victims Sunday?", but only as a general quote: "In general, the alert seemed adequate. But specifically it appeared non-existent." It is not tied to County B. Footnote 17 (Kokomo Tribune) is quoted right after it: "There were warnings on television Sunday evening that Howard was one of the counties threatened". The study does not use either to key a county letter. The old facts.md row F inference (B = Boone) rests on footnote 16 alone and is the weaker reading (see Boone below).

## County B: Grant County, seat Marion. Grade LIKELY (strong on two details, one open alternative)

Matching details:
- "The tornado hit the county seat at 8:07 p.m." (STUDY printed p. 23, facts.md C7). Marion is the seat of Grant. The Russiaville tornado reached "the southern outskirts of Marion" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_Palm_Sunday_tornado_outbreak). IWX: "In reality the storm had hit Marion around 8pm." facts.md F row "Marion tornado": SURVEY hospital clock "stopped at 8:10 PM".
- B's director saw the tornado about 7:30 and the hit came 35 minutes later (STUDY p. 22-23). Same storm, West to East: IWX table "Russiaville to SE of Marion and Greentown", 0020 UTC (7:20 PM EST), counties Clinton, Howard, Grant, 48 miles. Russiaville to Marion in about 35 to 40 minutes is the right speed for that track.
- "adjacent county to the west (County C)" with a tornado "just entering County C from the west" at about 7:15 (STUDY p. 22). The county west of Grant is Howard. The IWX table has the earlier tornado "SE of Lafayette to W of Russiaville", 0007 UTC (7:07 PM), counties Tippecanoe, Clinton, which hands over to the Russiaville tornado at the Clinton to Howard line.
- B's paragraph: the tornado "blew down high voltage power lines as it cut across the county" (STUDY p. 23-24). SURVEY printed p. 45 (facts.md D): "The tornado toppled 31 Indiana and Michigan Electric company transmission towers as it entered the south portion of Marion."
- B has a CD director, a city police chief, a sheriff and "the local radio station" plus "radio-TV stations" (STUDY pp. 22-23). Among the eight counties only Grant has CD, city police, and both radio and TV in the interview list, and was hit that late.
- The sheriff: "We didn't have any warning at all." The study says one of eight counties got no forewarning (p. 16, facts.md C19). Fort Wayne added Grant to its warning list at 8:10 PM (SURVEY p. 43, facts.md A16), after the 8:07 hit.

Details against, or open:
- Boone County (seat Lebanon) also fits "west county has a tornado at 7:15": the Crawfordsville tornado touched down "At twenty after seven" (IWX narrative) and moved "northwest of Lebanon" (IWX); IWX table 0050 UTC. Against Boone as B: Wikipedia's tornado list says that tornado "passed just northwest and north of Lebanon" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tornadoes_in_the_1965_Palm_Sunday_tornado_outbreak), so the seat was not obviously hit; and the study's Boone entry has no radio or TV station, while B has one. The footnote 16 title is the only point for Boone. I could not find a clock time for the storm at Lebanon, so Boone is not excluded by a time.
- A radio-station quote on STUDY p. 25-26 says "our transmitters were hit at 7:45" and "the local police had been notified earlier, that a tornado was headed toward (us)". The study does not say which county. 7:45 is 22 minutes before 8:07, so it is either a transmitter site outside Marion, a Howard station (Kokomo was hit about then), or timing slop. Do not attach this quote to B.
- facts.md row C6 "the sheriff" says TV did not list B as in danger; the Kokomo Tribune (footnote 17) says TV did list Howard. That separates B from Howard. It does not separate B from Boone.
- No source read says "County B is Grant". The reading comes from clock, direction and the interview list.

Next to read to move this to IDENTIFIED: the Marion Chronicle, 21 April 1965, p. 9 (STUDY footnote 10) or any Marion paper of 12 April for "civil defense" and "police chief" and "8:07"; the Lebanon Reporter, 15 April 1965, p. 4; Storm Data April 1965 for the Marion strike time; the DRC original field notes in the Ohio State / Delaware archives.

Safe to say on air: "In one Indiana county the civil defense director saw the tornado, radioed the city police, and the word went no further. The tornado hit the county seat thirty-five minutes later." Do not name Marion or Grant County.

## County C: Howard County, seat Kokomo. Grade LIKELY (follows B)

Matching details:
- It is "the adjacent county to the west" of B (STUDY p. 22). If B is Grant, C is Howard.
- Tornado "just entering County C from the west" at about 7:15 (STUDY p. 22): IWX table has the Russiaville tornado starting 0020 UTC (7:20 PM) and narrative "At 7:30pm" at Russiaville; Wikipedia gives Russiaville about 7:28 PM CDT/00:28 UTC. The earlier Lafayette storm ends "W of Russiaville" (0007 UTC).
- STUDY p. 31: "One radio station in County C used to await up-to-date information concerning tornadoes to come over the Associated Press wire." Howard has three radio stations in the interview list.
- County C has a sheriff who is out recovering a drowning victim (STUDY p. 22). Howard's sheriff is in the interview list.

Against: C could be a county outside the eight (Clinton, if B were Howard). Nothing in the study describes C beyond these lines.

Safe to say on air: "The next county west." No name.

## County A: Elkhart County (seat Goshen), grade LIKELY, single strong match

Matching details:
- "Most organizations and the general public were alerted before another series of tornadoes started cutting a second swath across the county following the same path as the first one", and "many were killed by the second one due to the convergence of people at disaster sites" (STUDY p. 21, facts.md C4). IWX: the Dunlap area took "2nd violent tornado to strike the town of Dunlap in just over an hour"; table: first Dunlap tornado 2315 UTC (6:15 PM, 14 lives), second 0010 UTC (7:10 PM, 36 lives), both in Elkhart County.
- State Police sighting at 5:50 PM and "the tornadoes had already started touching down in the county" (STUDY p. 20): Elkhart's first tornado began 6:15 PM, about 25 minutes later (Wakarusa to Middlebury, table).
- Two city police departments in Elkhart (Elkhart, Goshen) fit "the County's largest city" phrasing, and the interview list gives County CD for Elkhart.

Against / open:
- St. Joseph County (the second Dunlap tornado began "several miles south of South Bend" per IWX narrative) and Marshall County (Lapaz tornado, about 5:45 to 6:00 PM) are not excluded. Neither had a second same-path swath that killed many that I found. The "local WB" in the A paragraph could be South Bend, which sits in St. Joseph.
- No source read says which county's CD director was told "twenty minutes after it hit".

Next to read: the Elkhart Truth or Goshen News of 12 April 1965 for the civil defense director's account; SURVEY South Bend log printed pp. 37-41 (facts.md) for any 5:50 PM State Police entry.

Safe to say on air: "A county in northern Indiana where the first tornado came through, people were told, and a second tornado came down the same path." Do not name the county.
