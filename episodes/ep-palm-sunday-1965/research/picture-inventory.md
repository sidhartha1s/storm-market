# Picture inventory: Palm Sunday outbreak, 11 Apr 1965

Scope: real photographs, film, documents and sound we might use. Nothing was downloaded. All rights lines below were read from the page named in the URL column unless the row says otherwise. Page text was read through a fetch tool that returns excerpts, so quotes are fragments of the page wording, not full licence blocks.

Channel rule applied: US government work and stated public domain or open-licence material is Free. A newspaper, agency or storm-chaser photograph is No unless its owner licenses it. A photograph on a government page is not Free for that reason alone.

Verdicts: Free, Ask (a named owner we could write to), No.

Two kinds of Free, read before using:
- Free (gov): made by the Weather Bureau, NWS, NOAA, Office of Civil Defense or Army. Safe under the rule.
- Free [Commons PD claim]: the item is a newspaper photograph, and Wikimedia Commons says it is public domain because it was "published ... without a copyright notice". That is the uploader's legal argument, not a licence from the owner. It meets "stated public domain" but sits against "a newspaper photograph is not usable". Lead to decide. Rows where I had evidence against the claim are marked Ask.

## Three short lists

### Free items that can carry the warning chain
- A9, the four SELS forecasts (Kansas City desk): https://www.weather.gov/lot/1965_04_11_Palm_Sunday_Tornado_Outbreak
- A10, the Weather Bureau Grand Rapids statements at 520 pm and 615 pm and after: https://www.weather.gov/grr/1965PalmSundayTornado
- A1 to A5, teletype machines and operators of the Weather Bureau (NOAA photo library, rights read on a mirror)
- B3, B4, B5, the 11 Apr 1965 charts that start the day
- B1, the Weather Bureau survey report (not opened by me)
- A13 to A15, Office of Civil Defense films (office and communications scenes, not watched)
- C1, the Huffman twin funnel, as a Commons PD claim (lead to decide)
- C2, C5, C6, C7, Dunlap, Alto, Kokomo and Pittsfield damage, as Commons PD claims

### Gaps where nothing free was found
- A 1960s Weather Bureau office interior in April 1965, and the Kansas City SELS room in 1965 (only the Life photos, A7, which are Ask)
- State police radio room, civil defense office in 1965, 1965 radio or TV studio, transistor radio, a siren of the period
- Newspaper front pages of 12 Apr 1965 (Elkhart Truth and others, none free)
- Downed power lines and steel towers
- Russiaville and Michigan town damage (only Ask items)
- 1965 radar scope pictures
- Any recorded radio, TV, police radio or siren sound from that evening
- April 1965 newsreels and National Archives film of the outbreak or of the 14 Apr 1965 Johnson visit

### Owners worth writing to
- The Elkhart Truth (Paul Huffman family): the twin funnel in C1, D1, D2, D3 and the 12 Apr 1965 front page
- Elkhart County Historical Society / Indiana State Library: the Mel Troyer press photos, C3 and C11
- NWS Northern Indiana (Blake Naftel, Dick Loney): permission trail for C13
- NWS Detroit and NWS Grand Rapids: where the Toledo (C4) and Manitou Beach (C8) pictures came from
- American Meteorological Society: Fujita 1970 paper and figures, B2, C9
- Kokomo Tribune (Kokomo Morning Times archive): Alto, Greentown, Russiaville
- Toledo Blade and Time Inc. / Life: C20 and C10, A7
- WSBT / Sinclair and the Indiana Broadcast History Archive: 1965 TV frames and the WSJV segment, A11, A12
- UPI: the Johnson visit picture, C15
- NOAA Storm Prediction Center history staff: the Kansas City SELS pictures, A8, and Dr. Russell Schneider's collection
