## Dominant question
Why did the United States go twelve years without an EF5, and what did one tornado in a North Dakota field do
to end it?

## Architecture
Explanation with a twist 60 % (the scale, not the storms, was the drought) + Countdown 40 % (11:02 to 11:21 PM,
nineteen minutes).
- **Escalation:** a tanker car thrown 476 feet → three dead on farms nobody was watching → rated EF3, a Friday
  night storm like a hundred others → twelve years of "no EF5s any more" → a paper about throwing train cars →
  the rating changed four months later → every EF4 of the last decade is back in question → what that means
  for the warning on your phone.
- **Twist:** the deciding evidence was not a house. It was freight.
- **US stake by minute two:** the drought covered Mayfield 2021, Rolling Fork 2023, Vilonia 2014, Rochelle 2015.
  The viewer has lived through "EF4" storms that may have been EF5. The scale decides what a warning sounds like.
- **Master map** (returned to at every act seam): the 12.1 mile track, touchdown 3.8 mi south of Enderlin, the
  rail line, the Maple River, Highway 46, dissipation 5 mi southwest of Alice. Width 1.05 mi drawn to scale.
- **Clock device:** 11:02 PM → 11:21 PM CDT, one dial, returned to in A1 and A6.
- **One named person per act:** A1 Marcario and Roberta Lucio · A2 Carl Jones · A3 Tony Lyza · A4 Connell Miller ·
  A5 Carl Jones and the Northern Tornadoes Project · A6 the viewer.
