📘 The book mentioned at 8:23 — The Storm-Ready Home

Every typhoon that has hurt Japan in the last ten years arrived with a central pressure below 960
hPa. Typhoon No. 25 — expected to earn the name Dujuan within hours — is forecast to arrive weaker
than every single one of them, at 975 hPa and 67 mph. That number is milder, on paper, than eight
separate typhoons that killed people, flooded cities, and grounded flights across Japan since 2016.
The track this storm is forecast to fly sits directly over the single most crowded week on Japan's
calendar in eleven years: the first full five-day Silver Week since 2015, with the Asian Games
opening in Nagoya on the same Saturday and Japan Railways' flagship bullet train fully reserved.

This video lines up tonight's live JMA and JTWC numbers against Lionrock (2016), Lan (2017), Jebi and
Trami (2018), Faxai and Hagibis (2019), Nanmadol (2022), and Ampil (2024) — nine storms benchmarked on
one table, peak pressure and peak wind measured over open water, not at landfall. Two open threads
remain tonight: the track, which Japan's own forecast currently ends offshore, and the storm's own
strength, which has not yet reached typhoon status.
