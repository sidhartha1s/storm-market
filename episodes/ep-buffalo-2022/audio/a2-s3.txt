At about a quarter past eight, the air at the airport dropped below freezing. The rain had been soaking the roads all night, at thirty-seven to thirty-nine degrees. When the air behind a front drops that fast, the water on the pavement has no time to drain or dry. It freezes where it lies, and the snow keeps falling on it. Forecasters call that a flash freeze, and it's the rain-to-snow switch the warning had spelled out the day before.
