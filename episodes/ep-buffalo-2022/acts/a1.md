## A1 · Once in a generation
Chapter card: Purpose · prove the warning was out early and loud, product by product, and show what the forecasters said the danger was.
Question · If the weather service saw it coming, what exactly did it tell Buffalo, and when?
Reveal · A careful office used its strongest words three days out, issued a watch almost three days ahead and a Blizzard Warning twenty-seven hours ahead, and its warning text put wind and visibility at the center.
Visual event · the forecast desk timeline building one product card at a time; a pressure dial falling from 1000 to 968 mb; a wind arrow running the length of Lake Erie into Buffalo.
Exit hook · Thursday night it was raining at thirty-eight degrees and Friday was a workday: why were so many people already out when it hit?

### A1-S3 — narration
**Picture:** self-made pressure dial graphic, needle sweeping from 1000 mb "THU EVENING" to 968 mb "FRI EVENING", a red band marking the 24 mb line it clears. Chip "32 MB IN ~24 HRS". Source line: "NWS Buffalo forecast discussion, 20 Dec 2022 (forecast)". [VE] [RS] [PAY]
> Behind that wind was the low itself. The forecast had its pressure falling from about one thousand millibars on Thursday evening to about nine hundred sixty-eight by Friday evening. That's thirty-two millibars in roughly a day. The textbook bar for a storm strengthening that fast is twenty-four in a day, and the note said this one would clear it easily.

Rows: `research/nws.md` Q5 row 5.1 (1000 mb Thu evening to 968 mb Fri evening; "easily meeting the definition of bombogenesis (24mb/24hrs)"; PRIMARY, forecast). The word "bomb" (and "bomb cyclone") is a hard flag word, so the spoken line and chips describe the definition instead of naming it. Observed values (5.4, 5.5) not used.

### A1-S4 — narration
**Picture:** self-made map, Lake Erie from Toledo to Buffalo, the low's forecast track passing north into southern Ontario, then a long arrow running southwest to northeast down the lake's length and landing on Buffalo at the eastern tip. Labels "LAKE ERIE", "BUFFALO", "ONTARIO". Chip "SETTING LOW PRESSURE RECORDS" with source "NWS Buffalo, 21 Dec 2022". [VE] [RS] [Q]
> By Wednesday afternoon, the discussion said the low could start setting low pressure records once it crossed north of the border into Ontario. That track mattered for Buffalo. The wind behind a low passing that way blows from the southwest, straight down the length of Lake Erie, and Buffalo sits right at the end of it. Every mile of open lake is another mile for that wind to pick up moisture and speed.

Rows: `research/nws.md` Q5 row 5.2 (1:24 p.m. 21 Dec, "setting low pressure records once it passes north of the border", 982 mb over southernmost Ontario Friday morning; PRIMARY).
