## A2 · Friday morning
Chapter card: Purpose · walk through Friday morning one airport log row at a time, so the viewer feels how fast the roads changed and how late the order to stay off them came.
Question · If the warning was out a day early, why were people already on the roads when the whiteout arrived?
Reveal · Rain-soaked roads froze under falling snow in a few hours, the state emergency did not keep anyone home in Erie County, and the county ban took effect after the morning commute was over.
Visual event · the airport log card ticking row by row (7:24, 8:06, 8:39, 12:05, 12:54) with a temperature line falling from 38 to 12; a second clock lane for the officials (6:00 state of emergency, 9:30 county ban) laid under it.
Exit hook · at nine thirty the people who left at seven were already out on the ice: where were they?

> Now think about the roads. The rain had been soaking them all night, at thirty-seven to thirty-nine degrees. Forecasters call that a flash freeze, and it's the rain-to-snow switch the warning had spelled out the day before.
