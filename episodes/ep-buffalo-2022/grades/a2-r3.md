## S6 "the drivers already out on those roads had not been told to go home": NOT SUPPORTED as written
What the 8:50 post and the 9:30 ban do support: Erie County's driving ban was posted on the WKBW live blog at 8:50 a.m. (fetched, fact-check row 33) and took effect at 9:30 (WKBW, fetched). So no county driving ban was announced before about 8:50. That is all.
Why the sentence goes further than that:
1. Absence claim. No source says nobody told drivers anything; the record only shows the first county order we found. The act's own Rows line admits it is "framing".
2. Sound of "told". A listener hears "nobody warned them". The episode says otherwise: A1 quotes the warning's "Travel is strongly discouraged" (acts/a1.md line 46), and A1 says the warning was issued 27 hours ahead. "Told to go home" is a narrower thing, but it reads as a contradiction of A1.
3. State action existed. `research/response.md` line 141: "state road closures 6 a.m. Fri". Fact-check row 28: the 22 Dec release says DOT was preparing to close SR 400, SR 219, I-290, I-190 and SR 5 in Erie County from 6 a.m. Friday. Row 27: the Thruway was closed to all traffic at an unstated time. S7 itself says the state announced a truck ban at 6. Expressways closing is a state "stay off" signal for the drivers on exactly those roads.
4. Timing. The line sits in S6 under "By Friday morning" (the governor's outage release, time of day not in the record) so it cannot be pinned before or after the 8:50 post.
5. "Already out" has no count anywhere (S10's commute figure is a typical-year Census number, 75 percent leave before 9:00).
Supported rewrite for the line, if kept: "The county would not post its driving ban until ten to nine." (WKBW 8:50, fetched). But that fact is S8's reveal; saying it in S6 spends it early. Better to cut the sentence and the S6 outage detour (see fix 1).
