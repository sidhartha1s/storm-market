📘 The book mentioned at 10:05

On the night of December 10, 2021, a tornado outbreak crossed the central United States, and the forecast had been right for days. This film follows that week: what each Weather Service office said and when, why a December night could produce tornadoes that stayed on the ground for so long, and what happened at two large workplaces, an Amazon delivery station in Edwardsville, Illinois, and a candle factory in Mayfield, Kentucky. Then it asks a plain question: what rule tells a workplace how to plan for a tornado, and has anyone written the advice since?

Sources and courtesy credits: NWS Joint Regional Service Assessment "The Historic December 10-11, 2021 Tornado Outbreak"; NOAA Storm Prediction Center; NWS Paducah, St. Louis, Memphis and Louisville (forecast discussions via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive); OSHA Hazard Alert Letter to Amazon.com Services LLC (April 26, 2022); NASA Earth Observatory (images by Joshua Stevens); FEMA and DVIDS photographs; U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, Louisville District, and Kentucky National Guard (133rd Mobile Public Affairs Detachment) video.
