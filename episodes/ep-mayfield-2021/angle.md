Claim: The forecast was right days ahead, and nobody had told big workplaces what to do. On 10 December 2021 the outbreak took 89 lives directly with 71 tornadoes across eight states, the deadliest December tornado event on record since 1950, and people lost their lives at work: 6 at the Amazon delivery station in Edwardsville, Illinois, and 8 at the Mayfield Consumer Products candle factory in Kentucky. The Weather Service's own assessment found a gap in its safety outreach for buildings with large footprints, and one of its offices wrote that there was no official guidance on sheltering in the workplace.
Spine / arcs: Spine = the forecast against the workplace. Memphis's forecast discussion five days out (6 Dec) through the Storm Prediction Center outlooks and the warnings that night, set against the two large-footprint buildings where people were still at work. Arc 1 = Edwardsville (the first workplace, earlier in the evening). Arc 2 = the candle factory in Mayfield, on the 165.6 mile track. Close = Service Assessment Finding 6: no NWS guidance for large-footprint buildings.
Source: NWS Service Assessment, The Historic December 10-11, 2021 Tornado Outbreak (p4 toll, p14 the 6 Dec Memphis discussion, p34 Finding 6); NWS Paducah event page; OSHA and the Kentucky Labor Cabinet for the workplace findings.
Ruled out as the lead: the long-track EF4 itself (path length, 190 mph, "the Quad-State tornado"); the live stream from under the factory rubble; the courthouse and downtown Mayfield destroyed; the December rarity on its own. Every top video leads with one of them (teardowns: `research/teardowns.md`).

## What the fact check changed (21 Oct 26; wording, not meaning, so the lock stands; `research/fact-sheet.md`)
- "Five days" is four: the Memphis discussion's first issuance with the line is 3:36 PM CST Monday 6 Dec (SA p14, IEM archive). The Storm Prediction Center first named that Friday a week ahead, on Day 8 (3 Dec, SA p7), without drawing a risk area.
- "Deadliest December" is "the deadliest December tornado event on record since 1950" (SA p4).
- Finding 6 says "a gap in NWS safety outreach and resources related to buildings with large footprints"; the "no guidance" words are the WFO St. Louis after-action review, quoted in the SA p33-34. Attribute each to its own source.
- Amazon's six were contractors working at the building, not Amazon staff (OSHA). The candle plant was cited by Kentucky's state OSHA program: 7 serious violations, $40,000 proposed, all contested, none about a shelter. The 2019 $9,810 settlement is a separate matter. Confirmed: 165.6 miles, 89 / 71 / eight states.

## Legal care that rides with the angle (coordinator brief, 21 Oct 26)
- Script it as "no guidance existed for large buildings", never as blaming a company.
- Claims that workers were threatened with firing are allegations only: attributed to who made them, or left out.
