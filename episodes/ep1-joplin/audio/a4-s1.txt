In the weeks that followed, the National Weather Service did something uncomfortable. It investigated itself. The assessment team went door to door among the survivors, and the answers they brought back changed American weather policy forever. Because the majority of people they surveyed admitted they did not take shelter when the first siren sounded. They waited. They checked a second source, they called a relative, they stepped outside to look at a storm that rain had made invisible. The warning system had delivered its message in time, and the message had bounced off a decade of false alarms.
