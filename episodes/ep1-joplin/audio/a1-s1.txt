To understand what happened at five forty-one that evening, you have to understand what the sirens meant to Joplin. Because on paper, the system worked. Twenty-eight sirens covered the city, and they were loud, and they were tested, and people knew the sound. But the sirens had a habit. They cried for thunderstorms. They cried for hail. They cried, as one resident later told federal investigators, when all it meant was a little more water in the gutter. And every false alarm was a small withdrawal from a bank of trust that Joplin did not know it would need. On May twenty-second, that account came due.
