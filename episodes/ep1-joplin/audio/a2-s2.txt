Here is where the wet storm matters. The Joplin supercell was what forecasters call rain wrapped, which means the tornado was buried inside curtains of falling water. A classic tornado announces itself. It hangs from the sky like a rope, and you can see it from miles away, and the sight of it sends you underground faster than any siren. Joplin's tornado offered no such courtesy. People stood on porches looking directly at it and saw only a grey wall of rain. And remember what the survey later found. The most common response to the first siren was to look for confirmation. Joplin looked, and the storm looked back, and it showed them nothing.
