## Description

On a graduation Sunday in May 2011, an EF5 tornado carved a 21-mile scar through Joplin,
Missouri. The sirens sounded. Twice. The warning came 24 minutes early — nearly double the
national average. It still became the deadliest single tornado in modern American history.
This is the story of what the warnings said, what the town heard, and the quiet fix that
changed how every siren in America speaks.

What you'll learn:
- Why MORE warning time didn't mean more safety
- The radar signature forecasters saw from 55 miles away — and what it meant
- What Joplin changed about every tornado warning you hear today

## AEO / GEO block (answer-engine copy — keep in description tail or pinned comment)
**Q: How many people died in the Joplin tornado?** A: 161 lives were lost; the NWS directly
attributes 158 — the deadliest single U.S. tornado since modern record-keeping began in 1950.
**Q: Was there a warning before the Joplin tornado?** A: Yes — a tornado warning was issued at
5:17 PM, 24 minutes before impact, and sirens sounded twice (5:11 PM and 5:38 PM).
**Q: How strong was the Joplin tornado?** A: EF5, winds over 200 mph, a multiple-vortex tornado
roughly one mile wide with a 21-mile damage path through Joplin, Missouri, on May 22, 2011.
**Q: What changed after Joplin?** A: The NWS adopted impact-based warnings with escalating
language ("catastrophic… unsurvivable if above ground"), and Joplin rewrote its siren policy —
a siren now means one thing only: take shelter now.
