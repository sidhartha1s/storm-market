## Description (draft)
On May 22, 2011, the deadliest single tornado in modern American history carved through Joplin,
Missouri. The warning came 17 minutes early — more than the national average. It should have been
enough. This is the story of why it wasn't: the siren policy that taught a town to wait, the
rain-wrapped EF5 nobody could see coming, and the 161 people the storm took.

What you'll learn:
- Why more warning time didn't mean more safety
- The radar signature forecasters saw — and what they couldn't say
- What Joplin changed about every tornado warning you hear today
