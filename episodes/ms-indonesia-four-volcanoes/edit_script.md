## B5 — WHY SIX AT ONCE (5:30–8:00)  *(tiltmeter line: k1)*

### B5-S1 — narration
> Indonesia has more active volcanoes than any country on Earth, and the reason is under the sea floor. The islands sit where the Indo Australian plate slides beneath the Eurasian plate. The sinking plate carries ocean water down with it. That water lowers the melting point of the rock above, and the rock melts into magma. The magma rises through the crust until it finds a vent. Every volcano along the arc is fed this way, so an active week across the country is not unusual. Six in one day is rare, but it does not need a shared trigger. Six separate chambers were simply full at the same time. [f24, f11]

Visual: subduction cross-section MG (plate sinking, water arrows, melt rising, six vents); label text "INDO-AUSTRALIAN PLATE" · "EURASIAN PLATE" · "MAGMA".

### B5-S2 — narration
> Anak Krakatau is watched more closely than the others because of what it has done before. It grew inside the hole left by the eighteen eighty three eruption of Krakatoa. That eruption killed about thirty six thousand people, most of them by tsunami. Then came the twenty second of December twenty eighteen. At three minutes past nine at night, the southwest side of this new cone slid into the sea. The first tide gauge registered the wave twenty four minutes later, when it was already passing. Four hundred and twenty six people died on the nearby coast. There was no earthquake to warn anyone, and the country's tsunami sirens listen for earthquakes. The cone lost two thirds of its height that night, from three hundred thirty eight meters to one hundred ten. So this month the agencies checked the water first. Japan's Meteorological Agency ran a tsunami evaluation on September fifth and found no unusual sea level at any station. Indonesia's Geological Agency head, Lana Saria, assessed the part of the cone rebuilt since twenty eighteen as relatively small. Too small, in her judgement, to raise a wave on its own. One instrument is worth knowing about. A tiltmeter on the island, which measures whether the ground is swelling, has been rising since early August, according to the monitoring agency. Swelling means magma is moving in. It does not, by itself, say when or whether a slope will give way. The three kilometer exclusion zone around the crater stays in force. Coastal assembly points and evacuation routes are staffed and ready, but no evacuation has been ordered. [h1, h2, f20, f21, f9]

In May nineteen eighty its north flank collapsed, and fifty seven people died. The lesson Indonesia learned in twenty eighteen is the one the Pacific Northwest lives with. The warning does not come from the mountain shaking. It comes from watching the slope. In twenty eighteen Indonesia's disaster agency admitted a hard fact. The country had no early warning system for tsunamis caused by landslides or volcanoes in the sea. The twenty two deep water buoys meant to feel a wave directly had all been out of service since twenty twelve. Some were vandalised, some were used as anchors, some were stolen. What stands between the coast and a repeat today is the exclusion zone and the tiltmeter. And people who know to walk uphill when the sea pulls back. [f9, f18, r4, u1, k3, k4]
