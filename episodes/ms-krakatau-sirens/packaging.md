## The one proposition
A volcano that collapses into the sea makes a tsunami with no earthquake, so an earthquake-based warning system, the
only kind Indonesia had in 2018 and the kind most of the world still has, stays silent. The wave reaches the beach in
under half an hour. The only siren that works is the one you carry: the sea pulling back, the sound, the night sky.
Second premise (at ~45 %): this is not an Indonesian problem. The tallest wave ever recorded was in Alaska in 1958, and
a 500-million-cubic-metre slope above Prince William Sound is being watched right now for the same reason.

## Net-info-gain (facts the outlier videos do not carry; primary sources)
- BNPB spokesman Sutopo Purwo Nugroho, 23 Dec 2018: Indonesia "did not yet have any early warning system for tsunamis
  caused by underwater landslides or volcanoes in the sea." [Inquirer/AFP]
- All 22 of Indonesia's tsunami buoys had been dead since 2012: vandalised, used as fishing moorings, stolen. [ABC AU]
- 2018 timeline: collapse 21:03 WIB; BMKG detects 21:27; waves on the beaches 21:30–21:38; BMKG's first statement was
  "high tide". 426 dead (revised from 437), 14,059 injured, 2,752 houses, 510 boats; Seventeen band: 6 of the party
  killed on stage at Tanjung Lesung; a PLN company outing lost 43 of 199. Run-ups to 13.4 m. [Wikipedia digest of
  BNPB/BMKG; Science Advances 2020]
- Since 2018: 533 seismographs by 2024; the German-Indonesian TsunamiRisk project (22 + 7 institutions, 2021–24);
  new water-level stations that see volcanic disturbances; BMKG: detection "incomplete without underwater sensors". [BMKG, GFZ]
- Lituya Bay, 9 July 1958: M7.5 on the Fairweather fault, 30 million m³ of rock into the bay, 524 m run-up, 180 km/h,
  two minutes between quake and wave; Howard Ulrich and his 8-year-old son survived on the Edrie, the Swansons were
  lifted over the trees on the Badger, Orville and Mickey Wagner died on the Sunmore. Ulrich: "It was not a wave at
  first. It was like an explosion." [Earth magazine / USGS]
- Barry Arm, Alaska: ~500 million m³, flagged 2020, 48 km from Whittier; the 2020 worst case said 300 m at the source
  and 30 m regionally; the 2021 USGS reassessment cut Whittier to about 0.6 m offshore while the fjord itself still sees
  >200 m; monitored by water level (NTWC), seismic, infrasound, ground radar, a deformation camera, InSAR; status
  4 Sept 2026: "no signs of large-scale active deformation". [Alaska DGGS, USGS]
- 1883: Merak wave 46 m; 24 m on Sumatra's south coast, 42 m on Java's west coast; official toll 36,417
  (Banten 21,565, Lampung 12,466, Batavia 2,350). Heard at Perth (3,110 km) and Rodrigues (4,800 km). [Wikipedia digest
  of Verbeek / Royal Society]
- The 1969 film put Krakatoa "East of Java"; the island is west of Java. (Senior-audience wink, C5.)
