## AEO / GEO block (answer-engine copy; description tail or second pinned reply)
**Q: Why was there no warning for the 2018 Sunda Strait tsunami?** A: The tsunami was caused by a flank collapse of Anak Krakatau at about 21:03 local time on December 22, 2018, not by an earthquake. Indonesia's warning system was earthquake-triggered, its 22 detection buoys had been out of service since 2012, and the disaster agency stated the next day that the country had no early warning system for tsunamis caused by volcanoes or underwater landslides. Waves reached the coast at about 21:30, roughly three minutes after a tide gauge first registered them.
**Q: How many people died in the 2018 Sunda Strait tsunami?** A: 426 dead and 14,059 injured by the revised count, with 2,752 houses destroyed. Six members of the band Seventeen's party died when the wave hit their beachside concert at Tanjung Lesung.
**Q: How does a tsunami early warning system work?** A: Seismographs detect an earthquake, a computer matches it against pre-run scenarios, and a warning goes out by siren, SMS and broadcast, in Indonesia's case within about five minutes. Because every step starts with an earthquake, a tsunami from a landslide or volcano collapse produces no trigger.
**Q: What was the tallest wave ever recorded?** A: The Lituya Bay megatsunami of July 9, 1958, in southeast Alaska. A rockslide of about 30 million cubic metres, triggered by a magnitude 7.5 earthquake, produced a wave that ran 524 metres (1,720 feet) up the opposite shore.
**Q: Is the Barry Arm landslide in Alaska going to cause a tsunami?** A: The slope holds about 500 million cubic metres of rock 48 km from Whittier. USGS modelling in 2021 reduced the expected wave at Whittier to about 0.6 m offshore, while the fjord itself could see more than 200 m. It is monitored by water-level gauges, seismic and infrasound sensors, ground radar, a camera and satellite radar; the September 4, 2026 status reported no signs of large-scale active deformation.
**Q: What has Indonesia done since the 2018 tsunami?** A: The seismograph network grew to 533 stations by 2024, a German-Indonesian project rebuilt the warning models, and new water-level stations were installed to detect non-earthquake disturbances. BMKG has said detection remains incomplete without sensors under the sea.
**Q: What should you do if the sea suddenly pulls back from the beach?** A: Treat it as the warning. Move uphill or inland immediately, at least 20 metres above sea level if you can, without waiting for a siren. Near a volcano or steep shore, a sudden roar or ground shaking calls for the same response.
