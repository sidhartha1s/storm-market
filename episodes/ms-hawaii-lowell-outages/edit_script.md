## B5 — WHY THE LAST TWENTY PERCENT TAKES THE LONGEST (5:30–8:00)

### B5-S1 — narration
> The reason power comes back in stages is the shape of the grid. kay-eye-you-see carries electricity across the island on fifteen main transmission lines. Everything else hangs off them. When a transmission line goes down, no house beyond it gets power until that line is standing again. Only then can crews move to the smaller distribution lines that feed individual streets. By last Sunday twelve of the fifteen transmission lines were back. That is what pushed restoration to seventy two percent in one jump. The North Shore sits at the far end of the system, so it waits longest. The terrain makes it slower still. Crews reach downed lines on foot or by helicopter, on ground soaked by more than a foot of rain. [f17, f2, f7]

Visual: schematic MG of the Kauai grid: fifteen lines lit, three dark, the North Shore spur at the end; then helicopter and crew footage; popfigs "15 LINES" · "12 BACK" · "72% IN ONE DAY".

### B5-S2 — narration
> The second constraint is people. Kauai is a small island and its utility does not carry enough line workers to fix damage this widespread in a week. That is why about a hundred outside crews were flown in, some of them on an Air Force C seventeen. Governor Green said he decided the state had to do everything possible to shorten the time to recovery. Partnering with the military and the National Guard, he said, was a major addition to the effort. Some of that equipment was already in Hawaii for a different storm. Hurricane Lala hit the Big Island in the middle of August, and the machines brought in for Lala were still in the state. Green said getting them to Hawaii for Lala is now also helping reduce suffering on Kauai. Two hurricanes in one month is the reason the request to Washington covers both. The storm itself is long gone. The Central Pacific Hurricane Center's final advisory listed Lowell as a post tropical cyclone. Its winds were down to fifty miles per hour, more than eight hundred miles northwest of Lee-HOO-eh. What remains, a week later, is a repair job. [f18, q2, q1, f19]
