Page cites for the Service Assessment (SA) are the document's own page numbers (as in angle.md). FEMA cites use the report's chapter-page numbers (e.g. 6-7). WAF = Weather and Forecasting vol. 17 (June 2002).

## 3. SHELTERS (spine: where people sheltered)

- 35 people in one Grady County storm cellar, all survived; cellar door pulled open; mobile homes destroyed; neighbourhood "primarily mobile homes" | SA p22 | CONFIRMED
- Same story with a name: Darrell Turner and 34 family members and neighbours sheltered in his brother's storm cellar in Grady County after hearing NWS warnings on local TV; the F5 pulled the door off, destroyed their house, everyone survived; "In the same community, eleven others lost their lives." | NOAAREPORT p2 | CONFIRMED (US gov, public domain text)
- NWS Norman meteorologist-in-charge Dennis McCarthy, June 1999: "...some people just didn't have the type of shelter needed to survive this magnitude of storm--such as a reinforced safe room or underground facility." | NOAAREPORT p3 | CONFIRMED (quote, attributed)
- FEMA: "the best means to reduce loss of life ... is to take refuge in specifically designed tornado shelters"; shelters are "the only means of providing individuals with near absolute protection" | FEMA342 exec summary p xvi, p7-? (7.3) | CONFIRMED
- FEMA: both aboveground in-residence shelters and belowground shelters "were successfully utilized ... and were responsible for saving many lives" | FEMA342 §6.1.1 p6-1 | CONFIRMED
- Safe rooms built to FEMA recommendations: "no casualties occurred in them"; one on the outskirts of F4 damage in Midwest City, another on the periphery of F5 damage near Bridge Creek (an insulated-concrete-form shelter, "damage limited to light missile impacts") | D&B02 p614-615; FEMA342 Fig 6-3 | CONFIRMED
- Few shelters existed: "relatively few tornado shelters found within the paths"; below-ground ones had door problems (plywood in metal skin, sliding bolt); BPAT: "Few successes were observed"; successes = engineered shelters | D&B02 p614; FEMA342 exec summary p xvi | CONFIRMED
- Basements are uncommon in the Oklahoma damage areas (slabs) while most Kansas homes have them; a basement is not a shelter unless it has a concrete roof slab | D&B02 p612; FEMA342 §3.3 p3-22, §6.2.1.1 p6-16 | CONFIRMED
- A sheltered 4-wheel-drive pickup wrapped around a utility pole; a car fell through a roof into a house; a vehicle blew into a house, penetrated the first floor and nearly hit the basement slab | SA photo p13; BROOKS-LIST P4; FEMA342 p6-16 | CONFIRMED
- Houses (above ground): 19 of the 36 deaths | BROOKS-LIST | CONFIRMED. But interior rooms mostly worked: BPAT surveyed 89 core remnants (rooms left standing) along the centre of the track; interior bathroom part of the remnant 81 % of the time, closet 75 %, kitchen 16 %; 63 % had both bathroom and closet | FEMA342 §6.2.1.2 p6-20 to 6-22 | CONFIRMED (does not count who was inside)
- D&B02's own core-remnant tally: bathroom 74 yes / 20 no; closet 57 / 19; kitchen 5 / 33 (likelihood of avoiding serious injury, subjective) | D&B02 Table 1 p615 | CONFIRMED (informal, footnote: outcome for occupants not known)
- At F4/F5 damage no interior walls are left, "so occupant survivability is a matter of luck if the occupants cannot reach adequate shelter" | D&B02 p615 | CONFIRMED
- Community shelters: Midwest High School gym (Midwest City), underground under the bleachers, about 500 people sought shelter, capacity 3,500; a similar one at Del City High School | FEMA342 §6.1.2 p6-7 | CONFIRMED
- Westmoore High School (Moore), within 100 yd of the vortex; several hundred students and parents at an awards ceremony moved to a central locker core, others to a reinforced stairwell; gym roof ripped off; no injuries or deaths | FEMA342 §6.2.2 p6-23/24; SA p23; STORMDATA p10 | CONFIRMED
- Three Moore-area schools' designated corridors (Northmoor, Kelly, Sooner Rose) had clerestory windows; Kelly's corridor collapsed; had it been used "numerous injuries or deaths would have occurred" | FEMA342 §6.2.2 p6-24/25 | CONFIRMED
- Capt. John Millhouse (NEXRAD Operational Support Facility): about 30 min warning, sheltered with wife, children aged 6 and 2, cat and dog in the centre hallway of his Moore home; only the walls around them remained; all safe | NOAAREPORT p2-3 | CONFIRMED
- OUN-OVERPASS: one interior room was the only part of a house left in a violent-damage area; 7 people walked away with minor injuries | OUN-OVERPASS | CONFIRMED (talk, photo caption; location not stated)
- Moore only had "3 people in well built structures out of an estimated over 2,000" die | OUN-OVERPASS | CONFIRMED (see 2a)
- Telecommunications firm changed plan to "shelter in place, do not drive home"; the F5 passed within 1 mile of the company | SA p12, p21 | CONFIRMED
- Fleeing by car: "ample NWS warning lead times and live local TV coverage allowed many individuals to escape the path ... via automobile" | SA p22 | CONFIRMED
- No count of "people who survived in shelters" exists in SA, FEMA342 or D&B02 | looked at all three | NOT FOUND (only the 35, about 500 and several hundred figures above)
- Kansas contrast (other tornado, Haysville): 85 workers moved to the basement at Norland Plastics, 13 disabled-adult clients in bathtubs, none injured; a wheelchair user in a Sedgwick Co. mobile home and a man refused entry (with his dog) at a mobile-home-park community shelter both died | SA p31; FEMA342 p6-11; D&B02 p615 | CONFIRMED (not this tornado)

### 4b. Tornado Emergency origin and policy (item 6)

## 5. SCIENCE (wind, structure, radar)

- DOW (Doppler on Wheels) peak ground-relative wind 135 +/- 5 m/s (about 302 mph, range about 291 to 313 mph), measured at 32 m above ground over Bridge Creek at 2354 UTC (6:54 p.m.), in a circulation about 350 m across, over about 100 m; "the strongest ever measured in a tornado" (as of 2007) | WURMAN07 p31-32 (Fig 1 + text) | CONFIRMED. Raw Doppler max 134.0 m/s plus 1.0 m/s for tornado motion = 135.0; spectral width 15-20 m/s gives the +/- 5
- Same paper: Doppler velocities as high as 142 m/s (about 318 mph) were rejected as outliers by the filter | WURMAN07 p32 | CONFIRMED (that 142 m/s = 318 mph is our arithmetic; it is plausibly the origin of "318", not stated by the paper)
- "318 mph" appears in an official source: NWS Norman FAQ: DOW researchers "announced that their radar measured 318-mph winds" near Bridge Creek; "validity ... still open to some question"; "maximum speed actually may be less than 318 mph (although it likely will remain above 300 mph)". F6 does not exist; 318 is still inside Fujita's F5 band (261-318 mph) | OUN-PAGE FAQ; SA App A (F5 261-318 mph) | CONFIRMED
- "301 mph" is NOT in any retrieved primary source; 135 m/s converts to 302 mph. Peak error is +/- 5 m/s (about +/- 11 mph), not +/- 20 mph | WURMAN07 | CONFIRMED (absence). Wikipedia lists 321 mph in a search snippet: no primary support found | SECONDARY, rejected
- Burgess et al. say the maximum wind "likely occurred during the early portion of the tornado's life" and leave it to a later paper | BURGESS p456-457 | CONFIRMED
- OUN-PAGE: the DOW figure is the highest recorded tornado wind but "several other F5 tornadoes have produced damage comparable to, or even more severe", and the May 3 tornado was not the biggest, widest, deadliest, longest-lived or longest-track | OUN-PAGE FAQ | CONFIRMED
- WSR-88D delta-V (rotation) above 45 m/s as the tornado formed, near 70 m/s sustained, 70-85 m/s when it hit SW OKC/Moore; tornado narrowed and weakened 7:05 to 7:15 p.m. then re-intensified 7:20 to 7:30 p.m. | BURGESS p457, Fig 1, section 5 | CONFIRMED
- F-scale caveat: wind-damage ratings "never calibrated"; F rating is the subjective maximum along the path, most of the path is lower | SPEHEGER p372 | CONFIRMED
- Building codes in force protect only to about 90 mph; most damaged homes failed at the load path (roof-to-wall-to-foundation) and garage doors; modest fixes (hurricane clips, anchor bolts) would cut damage outside the F4/F5 core | D&B02 p612, p616; FEMA342 exec summary | CONFIRMED
- Return frequency of such an event: about 1 in 1000 years for any given square mile in central Oklahoma | D&B02 p617 | CONFIRMED
- 1991 Kansas overpass video made people think overpasses are safe; "highway overpasses do not provide good shelter ... one of the worst places to be" (J. Purpura, NWS) | NOAAREPORT p3 | CONFIRMED
- A storm-survey fact useful for a "how do we know" beat: rated and timed by compositing surveys, video, radar; times good to 1 min, worst case 3 min; video clocks differ by up to 10 min; 8 NOAA/university ground-survey teams, 18 meteorologists on 4 May | SPEHEGER p365, p368 | CONFIRMED
- A pre-published A9 timing correction: Storm Data changed the begin time from 1723 CST to 1726 CST | STORMDATA p2 note | CONFIRMED

## 9. OPEN QUESTIONS AND WHAT THE ANGLE CAN SAY
1. "One of the best-warned tornadoes on record": no primary source ranks it. What the sources support: "remarkable 32-minute average" first-warning lead time for the OKC-area counties (SA p9), 65/18/31/13 by county (SA p9), national average 10-15 min (WURMAN07). Looked at SA, FEMA342, B&D02, D&B02, NOAAREPORT, NSSL-PAGE, OUN-PAGE: none say "best". Andra, Quoetone and Bunting 2002 (WAF 17, 559-566, warning decision-making) not retrieved; may hold comparisons. Safe wording: "warned for an hour in Grady County, 31 minutes in Cleveland County, 13 in Oklahoma County".
2. Minute Bridge Creek was hit: now bracketed 6:47 to 6:55 p.m. (section 8). Hook check: at 6:47-6:55 the first warning was already about 2 h old, but the SA gives no text of the warnings covering Bridge Creek between 6:26 and 6:47; App C lists only highlights. "More than an hour of warning at Bridge Creek itself" is NOT proven; the 65 min is to the first tornado in Grady County at 5:52 p.m., 54 min to 63 min before Bridge Creek from that tornado's start. The Tornado Emergency (6:57) was issued after Bridge Creek was hit.
3. Spine nuance: 19 of 36 died in houses, but Moore Emergency Management says over 2,000 people were in the path in Moore and only 5 died (OUN-OVERPASS) and BPAT's core-remnant data favours bathrooms/closets. Both can be true; say "no shelter below ground" carefully, not "houses were not shelters".
4. Mobile-home count 9, 10 or 11 (section 8). Recommend attributing "ten" to the Brooks list or saying "nine to eleven, depending on who counted".
5. Brown et al. 2002 (Okla. State Dept of Health, WAF 17, 343-353): the death-by-location study; not retrieved. Try journals.ametsoc.org / Wayback of an NSSL or OSDH copy. Would settle the 9/10/11.
6. NCEI Storm Events Database page itself (ncdc.noaa.gov/stormevents) is script-rendered and returned an empty shell; STORMDATA is the same underlying NWS text. Event row IDs not captured.
7. Oklahoman day-after reporting, FEMA photo library, NOAA Photo Library: not found (see section 6, 7).
8. NWS policy date for the Tornado Emergency: only a preprint (ARXIV) says 2014 with IBW; NWSI 10-511 and NWS press material not opened.
9. Chronology of the original SA App C table text extraction is scrambled; section 1 reads the page images C-2 and C-3 directly.
10. Not opened: REDCROSS, Texas Tech WISE report (https://www.depts.ttu.edu/nwi/Pubs/ReportsJournals/ReportsJournals/Tornadoes-of-OKC-may-3-1999.pdf), Marshall 2002 (WAF 17, 582-598, damage survey at Moore), SPC "forecasting issues" paper https://www.spc.noaa.gov/publications/edwards/3may_spc.htm.
