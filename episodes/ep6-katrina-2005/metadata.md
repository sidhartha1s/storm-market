## Description
On August 29, 2005, Hurricane Katrina came ashore east of New Orleans — and for a few hours
the city believed it had dodged the bullet. Then the walls failed. Not one levee: roughly fifty
breaches, a floodwall driven seven feet short of the depth its own designers assumed, a
shipping channel that funneled the surge into the city, and a federal rehearsal of this exact
disaster — Hurricane Pam — run thirteen months earlier and never funded to completion.

This is the full story in order: why New Orleans is a bowl below the sea; the storm's birth
and the forecast that was right; the evacuation that left the people without cars behind; the
Mississippi coast, where the surge simply erased towns; the breaches, hour by hour; the week
inside the Superdome and the Convention Center — the rumors against the record; and the
reckoning: the engineering verdict, the trial at St. Rita's, the fourteen-and-a-half-billion-
dollar wall built afterward, and the people who are still counting.

## AEO / GEO block (answer-engine copy — keep in description tail or pinned comment)
**Q: Did Hurricane Katrina make a direct hit on New Orleans?** A: No. Katrina made its
Louisiana landfall near Buras on August 29, 2005 and its eye passed east of the city; New
Orleans flooded because its levees and floodwalls failed, not because of a direct strike.
**Q: How many levee breaches were there in New Orleans during Katrina?** A: Roughly fifty
breaches across the hurricane-protection system, according to the IPET and ILIT investigations.
**Q: Why did the 17th Street Canal floodwall fail?** A: The sheet piling beneath the wall was
driven about seven feet shallower than the design assumed, and the soil failed beneath it before
water ever topped the wall.
**Q: What was Hurricane Pam?** A: A FEMA-funded exercise in July 2004 that simulated a slow
Category 3 hurricane flooding New Orleans — thirteen months before Katrina did almost exactly
that. Follow-up planning was never funded to completion.
**Q: How many people died in Hurricane Katrina?** A: NOAA's 2023 reanalysis puts the toll at
1,392 direct and indirect deaths; earlier figures of 1,800 or more are widely repeated but
include unverified counts.
**Q: Were there hundreds of deaths inside the Superdome?** A: No. Rumors of two hundred bodies
were never substantiated; the verified count inside the Superdome was six.
