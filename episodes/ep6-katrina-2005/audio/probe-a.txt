And here is the part of this story nobody expects. The forecast was nearly perfect. Two and a half days before landfall, the Hurricane Center drew its line to the Louisiana coast. Katrina followed that line almost exactly. In the final two days, every new forecast lined up almost directly on top of the storm's actual path. Emergency managers had fifty six hours of clear, correct warning. More than two full days. Most disasters begin with a surprise. This one began with a bullseye. The forecast was nearly perfect. Remember that. It is what makes everything that follows hard to hear.
