A vortex does not care what it lands on. This one touched down on the western edge of Redding, in a neighborhood called Lake Keswick Estates. Ordinary streets. Ranch houses, fences, parked cars, a school not far away. Into that came a column of fire a thousand feet wide, spinning at highway speed. It did not burn those houses the way a fire burns a house. It tore them apart. Investigators walking the ground afterward found homes not just gone, but scattered. Roofs peeled off and thrown. Cars lifted and set down in yards where they did not belong. A pickup truck wrapped halfway around a tree. This was not the slow, creeping fire the crews had trained for. This was a fire behaving like the inside of a tornado, grinding through a place where families had eaten dinner an hour before. Most of the people on those streets had already run. The evacuation had reached them in time, and that is the only reason the number that follows is as small as it is. Because when a thing like this comes through a neighborhood, the question is never how much it destroys. The question is who could not get out of its way in time.
