## 6. Fair-use reality

Fair use is a **legal defense you can raise after being sued**, not a mechanism that prevents a
claim or strike from happening. YouTube's Content ID system is automated and never evaluates
the four-factor fair-use test — it matches fingerprints and either issues a **claim** (redirects
ad revenue, video stays up, disputable — survivable) or, if the rights holder opts for manual
enforcement instead, a **copyright strike** (can suspend monetization/features; three strikes
terminates the channel — not survivable, much harder to reverse since a human already sided
with the rights holder before any dispute is heard).

Concretely: station/network broadcast material (KFOR/News9/KOCO/CNN/ABC/CBS/TWC) is virtually
certain to be Content-ID claims territory — annoying but survivable, standard for commentary/
documentary channels using short cutaways. Dan Robinson is the opposite risk profile — he has
publicly stated he pursues manual takedowns and litigation, specifically against documentary
producers, which is the strike-and-legal-fee scenario, not the claim scenario. That distinction
— automated claim vs. a rights holder who has told you directly he will manually strike and sue
— is the entire basis for treating broadcast footage as usable-with-attribution-risk and treating
Dan Robinson's footage, and the two CC-BY-tagged-but-chaser-origin Wikimedia videos (§2, flagged
"do not use"), as license-or-don't-touch regardless of any CC tag attached to them.
