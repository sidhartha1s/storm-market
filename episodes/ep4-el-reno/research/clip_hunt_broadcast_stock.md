## 6. Fair-use reality note

Fair use is a **legal defense you can raise after being sued**, not a mechanism that prevents a claim or strike from happening in the first place. YouTube's Content ID system is automated and doesn't evaluate the four-factor fair-use test at all — it just matches audio/video fingerprints against a rights-holder's reference file and either monetizes-for-them (a **claim**, which redirects ad revenue but keeps the video up — survivable, and disputable) or, if the rights holder chooses manual enforcement instead of Content ID, issues a **copyright strike** (which can suspend monetization/features and, at three strikes, terminate the channel — not survivable, and much harder to reverse since a human at the platform already agreed with the rights holder before any dispute is heard).

Concretely for this project: network/station broadcast material (KFOR/News9/KOCO/CNN/ABC/CBS/TWC) is virtually certain to be Content-ID claims territory — annoying but survivable, standard practice for commentary/documentary channels using short broadcast cutaways. Dan Robinson's material is the opposite risk profile — he has publicly stated he pursues **manual takedowns and litigation**, not passive Content ID claims, specifically against documentary producers, which is the strike-and-legal-fee scenario, not the claim scenario. That distinction — automated claim vs. a rights holder who has told you directly he will manually strike and sue — is the entire basis for treating station/network footage as usable-with-attribution-risk and Dan Robinson's footage as license-or-don't-touch.
