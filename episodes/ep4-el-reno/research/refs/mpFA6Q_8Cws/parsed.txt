[1] The 2013 El Reno tornado was a hyper-violent, rain-wrapped supercell storm that struck central Oklahoma on May 31, 2013. It stands as the widest tornado ever recorded in world history, expanding rapidly to a peak width of 2.6 miles (4.2 km).  While it was officially rated an EF3 based on ground structural damage, mobile Doppler radar units tracking the storm measured extreme internal wind speeds reaching up to 302 mph (486 km/h), which is well into EF5 territory. Due to its erratic movement, rapid expansion, and rain-wrapped camouflage, it tragically took the lives of eight people, including four professional storm chasers. ## The Storm Chaser Tragedy The El Reno event permanently changed the storm-chasing community after it claimed the lives of legendary veteran researcher Tim Samaras, his son Paul Samaras, and meteorologist Carl Young. Operating as the highly respected TWISTEX research team, they were attempting to deploy scientific instruments ahead of the storm. They were caught off guard by three critical, unpredictable factors:   * The Invisibility Cloak: Heavy rain completely wrapped around the core, making it look like a featureless wall of water rather than a distinct funnel. * Sudden Acceleration: The tornado abruptly veered and accelerated its forward ground speed from 20 mph to over 55 mph (88 km/h), cutting off their planned escape route. * Invisible Sub-Vortices: A massive, broad 2.6-mile outer wind field suddenly expanded over their vehicle, carrying violent internal mini-vortices spinning at 302 mph.  This tragedy, along with hundreds of amateur chasers becoming trapped in a massive traffic gridlock on local highways, forced a massive industry shift. It established strict new safety mandates prioritizing tactical distance and mobile radar tracking over visual confirmation
