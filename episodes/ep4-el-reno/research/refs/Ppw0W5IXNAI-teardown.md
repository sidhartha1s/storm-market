## Narration Style
- **POV/tense:** third-person, past tense for narrative reconstruction, occasional present tense in the cold open for immediacy ("This is...", "heading straight for").
- **Sentence rhythm:** long, clause-chained sentences ("but" / "as" / "because" connectors), matches our JP-style connective-flow narration approach — rarely short punchy fragments.
- **Est. WPM:** ~5,243 narration words (after word-level dedup of rolling auto-captions) over ~1887s of narrated runtime ≈ **167 wpm** — noticeably faster than our channel's 129-148 wpm target zone. This channel talks fast/dense; we should NOT copy this pace.
- Stats (wind speed, death toll, timing) are dropped inline throughout the march section, not batched — e.g. "296+ mph" lands as both a spoken line and a graphic card simultaneously.
- Emotion is NOT purely end-loaded here — victim vignettes (O'Neal/Bonilla, Bridges/Windemann) are woven into the middle "march," with the emotional weight of chaser deaths (Samaras family, Young, Henderson) concentrated at the very end. This is a hybrid, not our pure "stats-middle/emotion-end" model.

## Footage / Visual Style
- **Footage types:** predominantly real storm-chaser dashcam/handheld footage (grainy, green-tinted storm light), local broadcast news feeds (KFOR NewsChannel 4 bug visible repeatedly, Weather Channel bug), radar/velocity graphics, a custom map graphic with animated chaser-position callouts and colored path lines (red circles = danger zone, colored lines = individual chaser tracks), aftermath drone/ground damage photography, and archival photos of victims.
- **Grading:** minimal/no grading — raw chaser footage kept in native low-contrast, overcast/green storm light; broadcast clips kept as-is with original station bugs and lower-thirds untouched (no attempt to hide source).
- **Text/graphic density:** moderate-high in the map/radar sections (named labels, red hazard circles, colored path lines all on screen simultaneously) but sparse-to-none during raw handheld footage — density is deliberately uneven, concentrated where it aids geographic tracking.
- **Transitions:** **hard cut is default throughout**; the one notable exception is a **fade-to-black + text-card ("1 Week Earlier..")** used specifically to signal a time-jump/flashback — this is a used-sparingly, meaning-bearing transition, not a stylistic tic repeated elsewhere in the sampled frames.
- **Thumbnail/visual identity:** leans entirely on the authenticity of real found footage rather than AI-generated or stock cinematic shots — a directly opposite footage strategy to our channel's PD+AI-b-roll+stock spine, since this creator appears to source directly from chasers/news (raising the licensing risk our CLAUDE.md flags — "storm-chaser footage never without a license").

## Comment-Mined Insights (top 30 by likes, sample of 200 pulled)
- **Praised elements:** none of the top comments are pure praise — engagement here is almost entirely factual dispute/reaction, not compliments on craft.
- **Complaints / friction points:**
  - Repeated dispute over the **EF3 vs EF5 rating** — many viewers insist El Reno should be rated higher (EF5/"EF6"), reacting against the video's own EF3 framing. This is a factual/authority gap the creator didn't pre-empt.
  - Criticism of a **news reporter's routing advice** (told drivers to go south onto an already-congested interstate) — viewer flags this as a real editorial/safety failure that the video doesn't interrogate.
  - Comments note a "reframing" of reckless risk-taking (Samaras team) as heroic "life-saving data" — some viewers push back on the film's framing of storm-chaser risk as unambiguously noble.
  - One commenter flags **found footage (Skip's) being reused without full context** ("didn't hear the part where it was recovered") — a sourcing/attribution nitpick.
- **Unanswered questions / content gaps (differentiation opportunities for us):**
  1. **No clear resolution of the EF3-vs-EF5 rating controversy** — a dedicated explainer segment on *why* the National Weather Service settled on EF3 despite record wind speeds (satellite/mobile radar limitations, damage-indicator methodology) would fill a real, comment-visible curiosity gap and fits our "net-info-gain" NWS-service-assessment differentiator directly.
  2. **No interrogation of the news-media routing failure** — a segment on the broadcast advice that sent traffic into the tornado's path (a systemic/institutional-failure angle) is untouched territory and matches our "at least 2 facts no competitor has" mandate.
  3. **No treatment of the chaser-community ethics debate** (risk-normalization, chase convergence causing traffic entrapment) beyond a passing "safety first" line — a more critical documentary treatment of *why* hundreds of chasers converged and how that congestion contributed to civilian deaths (O'Neal, Bridges, Windemann) would differentiate us from this video's largely uncritical framing.

## Steal / Avoid List
**Steal:**
- Cold open in present tense directly on the disaster moment, zero preamble — matches our own hook doctrine, validated again here at 15.3M views.
- Location-by-location "march" tracked on a labeled map graphic with real chaser names/positions — strong spine structure, directly reusable for our own march segments (we can do this cleaner/less cluttered).
- Weaving victim vignettes into the middle rather than only at the end keeps stakes alive throughout — worth testing against our "emotion at the end" default; a hybrid may perform better.
- Using a real on-air broadcast clip as a mid-narration insert (verbatim chaser/meteorologist dialogue) grounds the "found footage" authenticity our footage rules already prioritize (PD/broadcast, not paraphrase).

**Avoid:**
- Fast ~167 wpm pace — too dense for our target audience/pacing; keep our 129-148 wpm.
- Heavy reliance on unlicensed storm-chaser dashcam/handheld footage as the visual spine — directly the footage class our CLAUDE.md forbids without a license; we cannot replicate this look with the same sourcing.
- Leaving the EF3/EF5 rating controversy and the news-routing failure unaddressed — these are exactly the credibility/content gaps we should close, not repeat.
- Uncritical "safety first" framing of storm-chaser risk-taking without engaging the audience's visible skepticism in comments.
