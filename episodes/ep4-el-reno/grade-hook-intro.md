## Register violations
1. T1-S1: "That night was May thirty first, twenty thirteen, around Oklahoma City." A naming sentence. It repeats the
   popfig that lands in second one.
2. T1-S1: "Twelve of the twenty one had taken shelter from those tornadoes, and the tornadoes are not what took them."
   Riddle order ("are not what took them"), a tense jump from "had taken" to "are", and "took them" is vague to the
   ear. The tail carries 8 number-words in 40 words.
3. I1-S1: "At three minutes past six". Breaks the literal-clock rule; the rule's form is "six oh three PM".
4. I1-S3: "Twelve were sheltering in drainage ditches and storm drains, and eight of those twelve were children aged
   eight or under. The youngest of the twelve was five months old." Twelve, eight, twelve, eight, twelve in two
   sentences, and "eight" now means three things within 35 s (eight in vehicles, eight children, aged eight), beside
   "eighteen" in I1-S2. The ear loses the count.
5. I1-S4: "Nowhere on that table is a house, a store, a school or a storm shelter." Inversion.
6. I1-S4 against I1-S3: "storm shelter" lands one sentence after "sheltering in ... storm drains". Heard aloud, the
   twelve sound like people who were in a shelter.
7. I1-S5: "People later told the weather service that fear from that day led them to do things they normally would
   not." Faithful to the assessment, but the words hold no picture; only the highway shot says what the "things" were.
8. I1-S5: "and none came near the size of this one." "This one" has no named evacuation before it in the segment; the ear cannot tell whether
   Oklahoma City is one of the six; no figure for the size.
9. I1-S8: "Scientists later rebuilt both moments from radar, and the two moments did not look alike." "Moments" twice,
   nothing to see or count, and "Scientists" is unnamed against the named-source rule.
10. I1-S5 to I1-S6: no joining word. The intro moves from the evacuation to the crews as a list item, not with but,
    because or which means.
11. I1-S7: "Tim Samaras, his son Paul and Carl Young". Heard once, "his son Paul and Carl Young" can sound like two sons.

## Overclaims (sentence against the picture note or figure beside it)
1. I1-S4: "That night, the safest place around Oklahoma City was home". The picture holds "0 of 21 inside a building ·
   NWS Service Assessment, Appendix C" through this sentence, so "safest" is heard with the weather service's name on
   screen. The table counts where lives were lost; no source ranks places, and the research file says the claim is not
   that people rode out hits at home. The title's word stays; it should be heard as the film's reading of the count.
2. I1-S4: "and many people had just driven away from theirs". Set right after the 21 pins, it implies the 21 were
   people who fled by car. By the research table, four of the eight vehicle rows read "storm chasing", and the twelve
   went into drains on foot (the family of seven "only a few feet away" from their house). True of the city, not of
   most of the 21. A viewer who knows Samaras was chasing will catch it.
3. I1-S2: "with each person's age and where they were". The script's own tag says the table gives a circumstance, not
   a place. The same gap sits under the 21-pin map in I1-S4.
4. T1-S1: "Twelve of the twenty one had taken shelter from those tornadoes". The table says "Sheltering in drainage
   ditch"; what they were sheltering from is sourced for the family of seven only (News 9). Low risk, worth one check.
5. T1-S1 picture: the counter reads "19 tornadoes · NWS Norman", while the fact check says the nineteen span the
   Norman and Tulsa forecast areas. The spoken "across Oklahoma" is right; the label is not.
6. I1-S5: "none came near the size of this one". Cleared as an overclaim: the fact check quotes the study ("No other
   event ... could compare to the magnitude of this evacuation"). It is marked confirmed from `research/facts.md`, not
   re-pulled, so one re-pull before lock.
7. I1-S8: "the two moments did not look alike". Vague enough to be safe, but it rests on the open tag in its own
   picture note.
