## What this changes outside the intro
The earlier research note and the owner page described "the one that hit the TV crew" and "the one that reached the research car" as if they were two vortices. The paper points to one: racing across US-81 at about 56 mph when it met the S U V, nearly stopped over Reuter Road when it met the car about four minutes later. Acts 2 and 3 are written to that.

## Added after the grade of Acts 4 and 5 (9 Oct 26)
- **Manufactured homes (A5-S5): a safety limit the script must state.** NWS guidance, read from search results of weather.gov pages on 9 Oct 26: NWS Wilmington, "Tornadoes: what you should know" (https://www.weather.gov/media/ilm/Tornadoes_NWSILM.pdf): "Mobile homes and trailers are not safe. Evacuate immediately and seek shelter in a sturdy building."; NWS Birmingham tornado safety rules (https://www.weather.gov/bmx/sps_torsafetyrules): in mobile homes, "leave well in advance of the approaching severe weather and go to a strong building". No source pulled says whether anyone was inside the manufactured homes the El Reno tornado damaged. The script says both things wherever those homes sit beside "the safest place was home".
- **Moore (A5-S7): the limit.** OUN: the May 20, 2013 tornado "killed a total of 24 people". "Survived above ground" is the assessment's finding about residents who used their shelter plans; it is not a claim that everyone in a building survived.
- **The broadcaster's own reply (A4-S12): UNVERIFIED.** A local blog reproduced a Facebook post from that week in which he is said to have written that he felt desperate to help and that the advice to get out of the way turned into a traffic jam. The original post was not found. Usable only with a verify tag; without it the script says he answered the criticism publicly that week and no more.
- **A lead, not a fact:** one search summary says The Oklahoman reported that survivors of a family lost in a drainage tunnel said the broadcast swayed them to leave. Not read, not used.
- **Deep Fork Creek, "four miles" (A4-S4): on file.** N9 (Stein, read directly): "along Deep Fork Creek near Bell Isle Bridge, four miles from where the family took shelter". FOX's "five miles downstream" is the fire department's figure for how far the bodies were carried. Both stand; the script uses the four-mile recovery.
- **Who the three researchers were (A3-S10): CONFIRMED, the tag can go.** SA, read directly: "Three experienced storm chasers associated with past NOAA research projects were killed by the El Reno tornado ... The three chasers killed, part of a research group called TWISTEX, were considered some of the most experienced and safety-minded chasers within the storm chaser community." W14, read directly: "Samaras had extensive experience deploying instrumentation in and near tornadoes, some of which were large, intense, and complex in structure".
