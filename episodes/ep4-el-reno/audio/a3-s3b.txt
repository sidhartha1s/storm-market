The Cobalt was not the only vehicle the storm caught. Minutes earlier, a Weather Channel chase vehicle carrying meteorologist Mike Bettes and his crew was snatched off the road by the circulation's edge. It was thrown rolling across a field, and landed crushed in the grass. Everyone inside survived, the driver with serious injuries, and the crew's own cameras recorded the vehicle's tumbling view of the sky. That footage went around the world within days, because it captured something no viewer had seen before. Professionals, in a reinforced chase vehicle, on live assignment, completely overpowered. Add up the Bettes crash, the TWISTEX deaths, and the dozens of battered vehicles limping off the grid that evening. The message of El Reno was already written before the sun went down. This storm had hunted the people who knew storms best, and it had caught them.
