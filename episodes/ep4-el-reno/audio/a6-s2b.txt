Sit with what that ten to thirty percent gap implies, because it reaches far beyond one storm. Every tornado in the historical record that crossed open country got the same treatment this one did, rated by whatever it happened to hit. Which means the record books are not a catalog of how strong tornadoes are. They are a catalog of how strong tornadoes were where they met something built by humans. Nobody knows how many storms filed away as weak spent their worst minutes over empty fields. El Reno became the case that forced that conversation into the open, because for once, a research radar was standing there taking the measurement the damage could not. The scientists who surveyed it published the radar numbers and the damage numbers side by side. The gap between them is now part of how engineers argue about the scale itself. One tornado, two truths, both correct. The width record was accepted, because width can be measured by radar. The wind record officially does not exist. It blew across grass, and grass does not testify.
