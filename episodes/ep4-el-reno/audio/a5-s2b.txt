Think about what the water asked of people that the wind never does. A tornado warning has one instruction, get low, get underground if you can. A flash flood inverts it completely. The safe place from one hazard is the deadly place for the other. And on this night, the two hazards arrived in sequence, from the same storm, a few hours apart. The federal reviewers found people had been told to get out of the tornado's way. Some of them ended their evening in drainage ditches, in low water crossings, in cars on flooded frontage roads. The exact places the first warning had pushed them toward. There is no villain in that sentence. There is a warning system built to handle one disaster at a time, meeting a storm that refused to be one disaster. The review's recommendation afterward was almost humble. Teach the media, and the public, how to hold two hazards in mind at once. It took twenty one deaths in one night to put that sentence in a federal report.
