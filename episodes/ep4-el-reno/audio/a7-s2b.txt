The evening left marks on the warning system too, and they are quieter than a memorial but just as permanent. The federal review of that night runs to findings and recommendations that read like a confession. Coordinate what broadcasters tell a city that cannot shelter underground. Never let a flood warning drown under tornado coverage. Study how a traumatized population actually behaves, instead of how a pamphlet assumes it will. And years later, when researchers went looking through six American cities for anything resembling Oklahoma City's evening on the highways, they found nothing close. That study exists because this evening demanded an explanation. Every one of those changes was paid for on the thirty first of May. The currency was twenty one lives. Three of them belonged to people who had spent their careers trying to make warnings better.
