At nine minutes past six, the tornado did something that rewrote every escape plan on Highway Eighty One. It turned hard to the left, and as it turned, it accelerated. The federal report is precise about this moment. Forward speed climbed from roughly thirty five miles per hour to more than fifty, and the inflow winds made driving away from it difficult. Every chaser on that road grid had built their position around a storm moving southeast at twenty five miles per hour. That storm no longer existed. In its place was something wider than their escape margin, faster than their vehicles on wet section roads, and completely hidden in rain.
