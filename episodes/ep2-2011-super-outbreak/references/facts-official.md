# 2011 Super Outbreak (April 25–28) — Fact Verification

Primary source used: **NWS Service Assessment, "The Historic Tornadoes of April 2011"** (PDF fetched from https://www.weather.gov/media/publications/assessments/historic_tornadoes.pdf, full text extracted locally, 76 pages). IMPORTANT SCOPE NOTE: this Service Assessment covers ONLY April 27, 2011 in a defined "assessment area" (parts of MS/AL/TN/GA) — it is NOT a 4-day, all-states report. Numbers below are labeled by scope. Secondary sources (Wikipedia's "2011 Super Outbreak" and "List of tornadoes in the 2011 Super Outbreak" articles, themselves sourced to NOAA/NCEI/SPC/NWS regional offices) were used to fill the 4-day/all-states gaps the Service Assessment doesn't cover, and are labeled as such — treat these as secondary until a primary NCEI/SPC document is pulled directly.

## 1. Total outbreak tornado count, Apr 25–28

- **CONFLICTING VALUES — no single authoritative number, all sourced to NOAA/NWS/SPC data but counted differently:**
  - **368 total** (367 confirmed by NWS local Weather Forecast Offices in the US + 1 confirmed by Environment Canada in Ontario) — Wikipedia "List of tornadoes in the 2011 Super Outbreak," sourced to NWS regional office storm reports. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tornadoes_in_the_2011_Super_Outbreak
  - **362 confirmed tornadoes** — NOAA NCEI "On This Day" retrospective. https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/news/2011-tornado-super-outbreak
  - A per-state/per-date breakdown table (Wikipedia, sourced to SPC storm reports) shows partial state totals: Alabama 60, Arkansas 28, Florida 2, Georgia 13, Indiana 3, Kentucky 12, Mississippi 39, Missouri 5, New York 9 (table continues beyond what was captured — not a complete state list here).
- **Competitor claim "368 / 21 states": the 368 figure is defensible** (matches the Wikipedia/NWS-sourced count) but is NOT the NCEI figure (362) — flag both, cite whichever your script uses.
- **States affected: NOT independently confirmed at 21.** One NOAA NCEI source says "affected almost one third of the nation" and lists at least 13 states by name; the per-state table above already shows 9+ states with a longer tail. Do not assert "21 states" without pulling the full state table — I could not confirm the exact count from a primary NWS/NCEI document in this pass.

## 2. Death tolls

- **Outbreak-wide total (Apr 25–28, all causes): 348 killed** — 324 tornado-related deaths across six states + 24 non-tornado thunderstorm-related deaths (downbursts etc.). Source: Wikipedia "2011 Super Outbreak," citing NWS/SPC fatality data. **The "316" and "324" competitor numbers are BOTH real but describe different scopes, not errors:**
  - **316 fatalities = April 27 ONLY**, per the NWS Service Assessment itself (primary source, direct quote): *"With 316 fatalities (31 in Mississippi, 234 in Alabama, 32 in Tennessee, 15 in Georgia, and 4 in Virginia)... more than 2,400 injuries. Damages from this outbreak totaled over $4.2 billion."* This is the single deadliest DAY, not the 4-day outbreak.
  - **324 = tornado-related deaths across the full 4-day outbreak** (six states), per secondary/Wikipedia sourcing.
  - **348 = ALL deaths (tornado + non-tornado severe weather) across the full 4-day outbreak.**
  - **The "352" figure was not found in any source and could not be verified — do not use it.**
- **Alabama, April 27:**
  - **234 fatalities** — NWS Service Assessment (primary source, direct quote above) — this is specifically the April 27 event.
  - **238 tornado-related deaths in Alabama** — secondary source (Wikipedia, citing SPC + Alabama Emergency Management Agency), which appears to be the full-outbreak Alabama tornado death count (not April-27-only). The "252" competitor claim was not found in any source checked — flag as unverified/likely wrong.
  - Recommend using **234 (April 27, NWS Service Assessment, primary)** or **238 (outbreak-wide AL, secondary)** and stating which day/window you mean — do not blend them.
- **Tornado-direct vs. indirect distinction:** The Service Assessment DOES make this distinction for April 27: *"Of the 316 deaths reported, 313 were associated with the afternoon/evening tornadoes."* — meaning 3 of the 316 April-27 deaths were from the pre-dawn morning tornado round, not the main afternoon/evening outbreak. The wider outbreak's tornado-vs-thunderstorm split is the 324-vs-24 figure above (secondary source).

## 3. Total damage

- **NWS Service Assessment (primary, April 27 scope only): "Damages from this outbreak totaled over $4.2 billion."** This is the number for the April 27 event specifically, not the full 4-day outbreak, and not inflation-adjusted (2011 dollars).
- **Full 4-day outbreak total damage:** secondary sourcing (Wikipedia) states **$10.2 billion in 2011 USD**, calling it "the costliest tornado outbreak in United States history." NCEI's retrospective article gives **"roughly $12 billion in damages (2021 dollars)"** — i.e., $10.2B in 2011 dollars inflation-adjusts to ~$12B in 2021 dollars. These are NOT conflicting; they're the same figure in different-year dollars. **The "$15B inflation-adjusted" competitor claim is in the right ballpark for a later inflation year (e.g., 2024-25 dollars) but was not independently confirmed — treat as approximate/unverified.**
- **Alabama-specific $4.2B claim: this matches the NWS Service Assessment's OUTBREAK-WIDE-as-stated figure exactly, but the Assessment's own text attributes the $4.2B to "this outbreak" (its April 27 assessment-area scope), not explicitly to Alabama alone.** The competitor claim of "Alabama $4.2B" is likely a mis-attribution of the Assessment's April-27 total damage figure to Alabama specifically — flag this as probably incorrect/conflated.

## 4. NWS Service Assessment key findings — pre-dawn Apr 27 squall line knocking out power/sirens/NWR

Direct quotes from the Service Assessment (primary source):

- *"A round of severe thunderstorms moved through the western and central portions of the assessment area in the pre-dawn hours of April 27... The early morning storms produced over two dozen tornadoes, with 3 tornado-caused fatalities and more than 40 injuries. Widespread damage occurred across central and northern Mississippi, central and northern Alabama, and southern middle Tennessee. The morning storms caused power outages affecting not only the public, but also several NOAA Weather Radio All Hazards (NWR) transmitters. In some of these areas, power was not restored by the time the more significant afternoon/evening storms moved through the area."*
- **NWR transmitter outage table (Table 2 in the SA), verbatim:**
  | Transmitter | Time of Outage | Time Restored | Reason |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Florence (HUN) | 4/27, 6:44 a.m. | 4/29, 8:00 a.m. | Power outage, generator failed |
  | Winfield (BMX) | 4/27, 8:23 a.m. | 4/27, 5:50 p.m. | Communications failure |
  | Cullman (HUN) | 4/27, 2:45 p.m. | 5/5 (temporary tower) | Struck by tornado, temporary tower set up |
  | Huntsville (HUN) | 4/27, 5:15 p.m. | 5/3 | Power outage, switched to generator, communications failure |
  | Winchester (HUN) | 4/27, 5:25 p.m. | 5/1 | Power outage, switched to generator, communications failure |
  | Fort Payne (HUN) | 4/27, 6:30 p.m. | 5/1 | Power outage |
  → **That's 6 NWR transmitters affected in the north Alabama/Huntsville and Birmingham CWAs**, not a number the SA states as a single headline figure — it's derived by counting the table rows.
- *"The early morning round of severe storms caused initial infrastructure damage, not all of which was repaired in time for the afternoon/evening event. The afternoon storms caused additional damage. In all, more than 100 high-tension power transmission line towers were destroyed, most across northern Alabama."*
- No specific "X customers without power" figure was found in the document — the SA speaks in outage/restoration times per transmitter, not customer counts. Do not fabricate a customer-count number.
- **Competitor claim of "~36 pre-dawn tornadoes before 8am" is NOT supported** — the SA's own language is "over two dozen tornadoes" (i.e., 24+, imprecise), not a specific count of 36. Use "over two dozen" per the primary source, or find the exact SPC storm-report count for the specific pre-8am window if a precise number is required.

## 5. Tornado warning lead times

- **NWS Service Assessment (primary), April 27/assessment-area only: "The mean lead time for tornado warnings in the assessment area was 22.1 minutes. The probability of detection was 89 percent and the false alarm ratio was 49 percent."**
  - This directly contradicts the "24 min" competitor claim for April 27 — the primary-source figure is **22.1 minutes**, not 24.
  - Separately, the SA also reports: *"The lead time, the time from the initial three watch issuances to the first tornadoes in those watches, averaged 2.4 hours. Watch lead times to the first significant tornado in each area ranged from 3–6 hours."* — that's WATCH lead time, a different metric from WARNING lead time; don't conflate them.
- **National average lead time circa 2011 (~13 min): NOT FOUND in the Service Assessment or in the sources checked this pass.** This figure would live in NWS annual verification statistics reports, which weren't pulled in this session — flag as unverified rather than asserting it. Needs a follow-up primary-source check (NWS/NOAA verification stats page) before using it on-camera.

## 6. "Four EF5s in one day" record claim

- **Confirmed: April 27, 2011 produced 4 EF5 tornadoes** — NWS Service Assessment (primary): *"In all, 31 of these tornadoes were rated as EF3 or stronger. Eleven tornadoes were rated EF4 and four were rated EF5."*
- **April 3, 1974 (the 1974 Super Outbreak) produced 7 F5 tornadoes**, per secondary sourcing (Wikipedia, "2011 Super Outbreak" article): *"Four tornadoes were rated EF5 – behind only the 1974 Super Outbreak (7 F5s)."* So the competitor question ("6 or 7") is answered: **7**, not 6.
- **Correct, defensible phrasing:** April 27, 2011 tied the modern (post-Feb-2007, EF-scale-era) single-day record for EF5 tornadoes at 4, but this is fewer than the 7 F5-rated tornadoes recorded on April 3, 1974 (F-scale era). It is accurate to say "the most EF5 tornadoes in a single day since the Enhanced Fujita scale was adopted in 2007" — it is NOT accurate to say "never before or since" in absolute terms, since 1974 had more (under the older F-scale, which rated storms more aggressively than the EF-scale would for the same damage — a known methodological difference, not just a bigger outbreak). Recommend scripting it as: *"More EF5 tornadoes than any day since the Enhanced Fujita scale began in 2007 — though the 1974 Super Outbreak, rated on the older Fujita scale, still holds the all-time single-day record with seven F5s."*

## 7. SPC timeline, April 27

- **High-risk convective outlook: confirmed issued at 7:55 a.m. CDT** — NWS Service Assessment (primary), direct quote: *"...forecast issued at 1255 UTC, 7:55 a.m. CDT, April 27"* and *"The Day 1 outlooks contained an upgrade to a high risk for severe thunderstorms for northeastern Mississippi, central and northern Alabama, southeastern Tennessee, and northeastern Georgia."* Matches the competitor claim exactly.
- **Tornado probability: confirmed 45 percent** — NWS Service Assessment (primary), direct quote: *"The 30 percent chance of tornadoes in the morning outlook, with an upgrade to 45 percent on the midday update, is particularly noteworthy."* So the morning outlook was 30%, upgraded to 45% at midday — both numbers are real, just sequential, not one being wrong.
- **PDS watches: confirmed.** SA (primary): *"From 8:30 a.m. through midnight on April 27, SPC issued 16 tornado watches and 2 severe thunderstorm watches. Four of the tornado watches contained the 'Particularly Dangerous Situation' (PDS) wording... The PDS tornado watches covered Mississippi, central and northern Alabama, northwestern Georgia, middle and eastern Tennessee, and western North and South Carolina."*

## 8. Vilonia, AR (April 25) and April 26 tornado count

- **Vilonia, AR tornado (Apr 25): rated EF2 (high-end), NOT EF3** — 4 fatalities, 16 injuries, per Wikipedia's tornado-event infobox sourced to NWS Little Rock storm survey. Winds ~130 mph. Occurred ~7:30 p.m. CDT April 25 (not the Apr 27 event some competitors may conflate it with). If a competitor claims EF3 for this tornado, that's wrong.
- **April 26 count:** partial per-state table (secondary, SPC-sourced) shows Arkansas had 6 "Late April 26" tornadoes, Kentucky 5, Louisiana 10, plus scattered activity in other states — I did not get a clean single national total isolated to "April 26 only" in this pass; the outbreak's per-day breakdown needs the full SPC storm-reports page (spc.noaa.gov/climo/reports/110426_rpts.html, linked as a reference in Wikipedia but not fetched directly this session) for an authoritative count.
