This is almost certainly a licensed/repackaged TV documentary (interview-driven, real archival broadcast + survivor smartphone footage — not AI-narrated) uploaded to two channels. **Treat as ONE data point for "table stakes" counting, not two.**

## 2. Table stakes (covered by 3+ treatments — must-cover for our episode)

Counting Spark/Wonder as ONE treatment, "3+" means 3 of the remaining 4 independent treatments (Spark/Wonder, Swegle, SkyWhisper, DarkHorizon):

1. **The Hackleburg–Phil Campbell–Mount Hope EF5** — every single source covers this as the outbreak's signature/deadliest tornado, complete with the Wrangler Jeans factory, the concrete shelter that lost its roof but saved all 21 inside, ~71-72 deaths, and 75% of Hackleburg destroyed. This MUST be the episode's centerpiece tornado.
2. **The Tuscaloosa–Birmingham EF4** — universal coverage; John Oldshue's life-saving livestream, the tornado's visibility on live TV/skycam, the 64-death total, and its status as costliest-tornado-on-record-until-Joplin are all near-universal facts.
3. **James Spann as the through-line meteorologist figure** (or at minimum, an on-air meteorologist protagonist) — 3 of 4 use this device explicitly (Spark/Wonder fully, Swegle and DarkHorizon quote his on-air lines as connective tissue even without naming him as a formal protagonist).
4. **The EF scale (0–5) explained**, tied to specific tornadoes' ratings — universal.
5. **Storm-spotter/livestream verification as a narrative device** (John Oldshue in particular) — appears in 3+ treatments as a "second-source confirmation of a tornado's existence" beat.

## 5. Technique bank (deduped, attributed)

- **Flash-forward cold open**: stitch a montage of found-footage/audio clips from LATER in the video, before any date/place context — creates curiosity without spoiling who/where. [Spark/Wonder, SkyWhisper, DarkHorizon all use variants]
- **Bookend callback**: repeat the exact cold-open line/audio again in full context mid-video, rewarding attentive viewers. [Spark/Wonder, DarkHorizon]
- **Ticking-clock timestamps as connective tissue**: anchor nearly every beat to an exact time ("3:05 p.m.," "4:50 p.m.") to compress geography without losing the audience. [SkyWhisper, DarkHorizon]
- **Live broadcast/scanner audio spliced directly into narration** (not just B-roll) — real meteorologist/anchor lines function as story beats, not illustration. [All 4 treatments use this to some degree; Swegle and Spark/Wonder most heavily]
- **Object-scale damage translation instead of adjectives**: convert every big stat into a vivid physical image (jeans found 40mi away, a truck reduced to a bumper, a safe carried 600ft with its door torn off) — the strongest "translate stats to human scale" device observed. [SkyWhisper, DarkHorizon]
- **Repeating catchphrase as structural anchor**: flag a real quote as "a very famous quote from this outbreak" to reinforce memorability and create an intentional callback. [Swegle]
- **The "forgotten tornado" framing device**: acknowledge outbreak fatigue in real time — "any other year this would define a career, but it's overshadowed here" — justifies runtime allocation while adding meta-commentary. [Swegle]
- **Running comparison to a single benchmark disaster (Joplin, May 22 2011)**: used as a yardstick for both death toll and cost, creating continuity/franchise potential. [Swegle, SkyWhisper]
- **Digression-and-return rhythm**: follow every action beat with a short explainer cutaway (warning fatigue stat, EF scale, radar limits, vacuum effect) before snapping back to the crisis — keeps info density high without breaking tension. [Spark/Wonder]
- **Deferred payoff/cliffhanger structure**: cut off a survival scene mid-crisis (e.g., "don't open the door yet") and don't resolve it until deep in the aftermath act. [Spark/Wonder]
- **Understatement as horror**: imply a death via a cut-off text/phone call rather than stating it outright — the silence does the work. [Spark/Wonder]
- **A single "named victim" reserved for the biggest segment**, keeping other death tolls anonymized — makes the one profiled story land harder by contrast. [SkyWhisper — Ashley Harrison]
- **Authority-figure vulnerability at the close**: end on the protagonist meteorologist's own grief/self-criticism rather than a triumphant "warnings saved lives" note. [Spark/Wonder]
- **Escalating superlative stacking**: each new segment claims its own "biggest/deadliest/strongest" record, even when mutually exclusive with a prior claim — keeps every segment feeling like the peak. [SkyWhisper, DarkHorizon]
- **Numeric progression as a structural drumbeat**: close every tornado segment with the same stat cadence — duration → path length → death toll → dollar damage. [SkyWhisper]
- **Deliberate structural rest beat**: insert a smaller/quieter event (April 28 coda) right before the final aggregate numbers, letting the audience breathe between climaxes. [SkyWhisper]
- **Zoom-out act after the ground-level march**: after several town-level tracks, pull back to state/national aggregate stats that recontextualize everything just narrated. [DarkHorizon]
- **Visual proof via Google Earth satellite scar comparison**: show literal tornado-track scarring from space, non-narrated — tangible "wow" proof without relying on stats. [Swegle]
- **A rare/never-before-issued alert as a dramatic beat**: elevate a data point (first-ever Tornado Condition Index 10/10) into a "this had literally never happened" moment. [Swegle]
- **"Show, don't tell" B-roll for chaos**: use a scrolling warnings-ticker/"Local on the 8s"-style graphic to convey warning-density without narrating it. [Swegle]
- **Parallel-thread/parallel-tracking structure**: follow multiple simultaneous survivor storylines across the state at once rather than a single strict linear march, mirroring the real multi-front nature of the day. [Spark/Wonder]
- **Contrast beat before disaster**: insert a warm, ordinary-life scene (college-town color, tailgating culture) immediately before the fatal hit — classic "peace before the storm" juxtaposition. [Spark/Wonder]
- **Second-source verification as suspense device**: frame a meteorological procedure (spotters phoning in to confirm a tornado) as a live tension beat — will they get close enough, will they survive. [Spark/Wonder]
- **Closing on an unresolved quote/note rather than a wrap-up**: end on raw ambiguity or self-critical reflection instead of a tidy "we're safer now" bow. [SkyWhisper, Spark/Wonder]
- **Rhetorical anaphora closer**: "Each one had a name. Each one had a family. Each one had a story..." — cheap, effective eulogy technique for a mass-casualty close. [DarkHorizon]
