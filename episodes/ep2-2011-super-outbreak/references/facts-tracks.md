# 2011 Super Outbreak — Per-Tornado Fact Verification

Sourced against NWS office event pages (BMX = Birmingham, HUN = Huntsville, JAN = Jackson MS, MEG = Memphis, FFC = Peachtree City GA) where reachable, cross-checked against Wikipedia/Tornado Talk secondary compilations where primary pages didn't have the detail. Every fact below has its source cited. Conflicts are listed, not averaged — pick per your judgment or flag on-screen as "reports vary."

## A. Philadelphia / Neshoba County MS — EF5 (~2:30pm CDT)

Source: NWS Jackson event page — [weather.gov/jan/2011_04_25_27_svr](https://www.weather.gov/jan/2011_04_25_27_svr)

- **Deaths: 3 — CONFIRMED.** NWS JAN: "3 fatalities and 6 injuries" (one secondary source said 8 injuries — minor discrepancy, use NWS's 6).
- **Path length: 29 mi — CONFIRMED.** NWS JAN: "tracked for 29 miles into Noxubee County" (Wikipedia gives a more precise 28.28 mi — same figure, rounding).
- **Peak winds: ~205 mph — CONFIRMED.** NWS JAN states 205 mph.
- **Path width:** NWS JAN gives ½ mile; one secondary source (Wikipedia/Grokipedia) says max width 900 yards (0.51 mi) — consistent, not a real conflict.
- **Ground scouring, trenches 2 ft deep — CONFIRMED.** Multiple sources (Vortmax blog citing NWS survey language, tornadotalk-style accounts): "a swath of ground was scoured out to a depth of two feet" northeast of Philadelphia; asphalt ripped from roads; a mobile home thrown 300 yards with no impact marks.
- Context: first of four EF5s that day; first MS EF5 since the 1966 Candlestick Park tornado.

## B. Hackleburg–Phil Campbell EF5

Source: NWS Birmingham event page — [weather.gov/bmx/event_04272011hackleburg](https://weather.gov/bmx/event_04272011hackleburg)

- **Touchdown time: ~3:05pm CDT — CONFIRMED.** NWS BMX: touched down "southwest of Hamilton in southwest Marion County" at 3:05 PM.
- **Peak winds: 210 mph — CONFIRMED (210, not 200).** NWS BMX states "Estimated Maximum Wind: 210 mph." The 200 mph figure floating around appears to be a rounded/lower secondary estimate — go with NWS's 210.
- **Path length: CONFLICT, not resolved to a single number.**
  - NWS BMX event page text says the *complete track* was **132 miles** (with Marion County alone at 25.14 mi).
  - Wikipedia's official infobox instead lists **102 miles (164 km)** as the tornado's own path length.
  - These are genuinely different numbers from different tallies (132 mi likely bundles in a preceding/connected tornado segment across the state line into TN as one continuous track; 102 mi is the single-tornado NWS-rated path). Neither the claimed 102 nor 130 in your prompt matches cleanly — report the range (102–132 mi) rather than picking one, or default to NWS BMX's 132 mi since it's the primary-source page, while flagging the Wikipedia 102 mi figure as the "single-tornado" figure.
- **Duration:** NWS BMX gives ~23 minutes for the Marion Co. segment; Wikipedia gives 1 hr 45 min (3:05–4:50pm CDT) for the full track — consistent with the path-length conflict above (short segment vs. full continuous track).
- **Deaths total: CONFLICT — 71 vs 72.** NWS BMX page text says "72 fatalities and at least 145 injuries." Wikipedia's infobox says 71 deaths across five AL counties. Treat NWS (primary) 72 as authoritative; flag Wikipedia's 71 as the commonly-cited secondary figure.
- **Per-town deaths:**
  - Hackleburg: **18 — CONFIRMED**, consistent across NWS BMX and Wikipedia.
  - Phil Campbell: **21 — CONFIRMED** by Wikipedia and multiple secondary accounts, though one Fox News-sourced account says **26 or 27** killed in Phil Campbell total. This is a real conflict — 21 appears to be the more commonly cited/stable figure (matches your claim), but at least one source says mid-to-upper 20s. Flag on screen if used, don't state as certain.
  - Mount Hope: **NOT independently confirmed at 5.** Wikipedia groups Mount Hope into a broader "Lawrence County" total of 14 deaths without breaking out Mount Hope specifically. Could not verify the standalone "Mount Hope 5" figure from any source.
  - Hillsboro: **NOT independently confirmed at 3.** Same issue — Hillsboro is inside the Lawrence County aggregate (14) in the only breakdown found; no source gave a standalone Hillsboro number.
- **Damage: $1.29B — CONFIRMED** (Wikipedia infobox figure, consistent with other outbreak-damage compilations; could not reach a second primary source for this specific dollar figure, treat as reasonably solid but secondary-sourced).
- **75% of Hackleburg destroyed — CONFIRMED**, attributed to a Red Cross assessment per Wikipedia.
- **Wrangler plant destroyed — CONFIRMED.** NWS BMX and Wikipedia both describe the plant destroyed while tornado was at EF5 strength; Wikipedia adds the detail that jeans from the plant were found ~40 miles away in Courtland, AL.
- **Phil Campbell concrete shelter losing its roof over 21 survivors — NOT CONFIRMED AS DESCRIBED. Likely a conflation.** Every source that mentions a "concrete storm shelter" losing its roof in Phil Campbell is a bare one-line fact ("the tornado ripped the roof off of a concrete storm shelter as it entered Phil Campbell") with NO casualty count attached to the shelter itself. The "21" figure that shows up near this sentence in secondary write-ups (Fandom wiki, aggregator sites) is the town-wide Phil Campbell death toll, not a shelter occupant count — it appears an AI-aggregator or blog conflated the two adjacent facts. Separately, NWS BMX documents a *different* shelter in Hackleburg where a steel door was torn off its hinges and "two occupants" were injured (not killed) — that is a sourced, real shelter-survival story, just not the one described in the brief. **Recommend dropping the "21 people survived in a roofless shelter" claim** unless a further primary source can be found; it does not check out against any damage-survey document found.

## C. Smithville MS EF5 (3:42–3:47pm CDT)

Sources: NWS Memphis-area coverage was not directly reachable as a standalone weather.gov page in this search; relied on Wikipedia (citing NWS survey/NCEI Storm Events Database) and Tornado Talk.

- **Deaths: CONFLICT — reported as 15, 16, 17, 23, and 24 across sources, before landing on 23 as the modern consensus.** Wikipedia's infobox and the NOAA Storm Events Database entry (per multiple secondary citations) both give **23 fatalities** as the tornado's total path death toll (Mississippi + the Alabama portion of the path) — this is the figure to use as "official." Numbers like 15–17 appear to be *Smithville town only* or early/unrevised counts before hospital deaths were added; contemporary NWS survey write-ups (WDAM, MemphisWeather.Net) mention 17 deaths specifically inside Smithville itself. So: **23 = official total path toll (use this); ~17 = Smithville-town-only subset** — not a contradiction once you separate town vs. full path.
- **Winds: 205 mph — CONFIRMED** across all sources.
- **Path length: reports vary, 35–37 mi — not exactly "37 mi."** Wikipedia infobox: 37.3 mi. Other secondary accounts (citing the same NWS survey): "continuous path length of about 35.1 miles." Treat as ~35–37 mi, no single authoritative figure pinned down further than that.
- **117 structures destroyed — CONFIRMED**, Wikipedia: "destroyed 117 structures in Smithville and damaged 50 others."
- **Town size 1.6 sq mi — NOT VERIFIED.** No source surfaced this figure in this search pass; flagging as unconfirmed rather than asserting it.
- **Vehicle-into-water-tower story — RESOLVED: a Ford Explorer (SUV, not a truck), thrown a half-mile into the water tower, then caromed off and traveled roughly another quarter-mile before ground impact.** This is consistent across Wikipedia and other accounts — the "SUV vs truck" and "half-mile vs picked up locally" variant claims both resolve to the same account: SUV, half-mile initial throw + additional quarter-mile carom. No source supports a different vehicle type or a much-shorter "picked up locally" version.
- **Tombstones rotated — CONFIRMED, more specifically "blown over facing the opposite direction of tornado travel" (a secondary-vortex/scouring indicator), some broken and thrown**, per Wikipedia.
- **Ground scoured a foot deep — CONFIRMED.** Wikipedia: "scoured the topsoil to a depth of one foot (30 cm)" in places.

## D. Tuscaloosa–Birmingham EF4 (4:43pm CDT)

Source: NWS Birmingham event page — [weather.gov/bmx/event_04272011tuscbirm](http://www.weather.gov/bmx/event_04272011tuscbirm) (primary), cross-checked against Wikipedia.

- **Deaths: CONFLICT between primary and secondary sources — NWS BMX page text literally states "65 Fatalities"; Wikipedia's infobox instead breaks it down as 64 direct deaths (44 Tuscaloosa + 20 Birmingham/suburbs) plus 8 indirect deaths.** These don't reconcile cleanly to one number: 65 (NWS raw) vs. 64 direct + 8 indirect (Wikipedia) vs. a third figure of "53 deaths in Tuscaloosa" that turned up in an unrelated news search result (likely referring to Tuscaloosa County only, or an earlier uncorrected count, or possibly conflating with the whole outbreak's Tuscaloosa County toll — not reliable, don't use). **Use NWS BMX's 65 total as the primary-source figure**, and note the commonly-cited 44 (Tuscaloosa) + 20 (Birmingham suburbs) = 64 breakdown as the standard secondary breakdown, off by one from the NWS total.
- **Injuries: 1,500+ — CONFIRMED**, both NWS BMX ("1500 Injuries") and Wikipedia ("more than 1,500").
- **Damage: CONFLICT — $2.4B vs $2.5B vs $1.5B, all appear in circulation.** Wikipedia gives $2.4 billion (2011 USD) as the tornado's own damage figure, calling it the costliest in Alabama history until Joplin. A separate news search returned "approximately $1.5 billion in damage across Tuscaloosa and Birmingham" attributed to a different framing (possibly combined-storm or a different tally methodology). The NWS BMX event page itself does not give a dollar figure. **Recommend $2.4B** as the more consistently-cited figure; flag $2.5B and $1.5B as variants seen elsewhere, don't present any of the three as beyond dispute.
- **Rating/winds: EF4, 190 mph — CONFIRMED** by both NWS BMX and Wikipedia ("high-end EF4").
- **Duration: ~1hr 31min (4:43pm–6:14pm CDT) — CONFIRMED**, consistent between NWS BMX coordinates/times and Wikipedia.
- **Path length: 80.68 mi — CONFIRMED**, exact match between NWS BMX and Wikipedia. Max width 2,600 yards (1.5 mi) at I-65 crossing — also confirmed both sources.
- **12% of Tuscaloosa destroyed — CONFIRMED** via a secondary news compilation (not the NWS page itself, which doesn't discuss city-wide percentages); treat as a widely-repeated but not NWS-sourced figure.
- **"7,000 jobs" claim — CONFIRMED IN A DIFFERENT FORM.** The commonly quoted line is Tuscaloosa Mayor Walt Maddox's "Seven thousand people became unemployed within six minutes" — this is a mayoral public-statement figure, not an NWS or BLS statistic. Present it as an attributed quote, not a hard economic dataset.
- **Ashley Harrison / Carson Tinker story — PUBLICLY DOCUMENTED, CONFIRMED consistent across multiple outlets (SI, WBRC, Spokesman-Review, CBS Sports).** Ashley Harrison, 22, a UA senior studying economics and girlfriend of Alabama long-snapper Carson Tinker, was sheltering with Tinker (and roommates/dogs) in a closet in his Forest Lake-area home when the house took a direct hit. She was torn from his arms and died of a broken neck; Tinker survived with a broken wrist, concussion, and ankle injury. He later wrote a book, "A Season to Remember: Faith in the Midst of the Storm," about the experience.
- **Tornado emergency declaration — CONFIRMED**, declared for Tuscaloosa around 4:50pm CDT.
- **John Oldshue livestream — CONFIRMED.** ABC 33/40 meteorologist/storm spotter John Oldshue captured the first live video confirmation of the tornado on the ground near Union, Alabama, at 4:50pm CDT, roughly 30 minutes before it reached Tuscaloosa. James Spann later publicly credited Oldshue as one of the "unsung heroes of April 27," saying his footage "convinced a lot of people to take cover."

## E. Rainsville / DeKalb County EF5 (6:19pm CDT)

Source: NWS Huntsville event page — [weather.gov/hun/4272011_dekalb_county](https://www.weather.gov/hun/4272011_dekalb_county)

- **Deaths: CONFLICT — your claimed 25 is one of several figures in circulation; a wider "DeKalb County during the outbreak" figure of ~30–35 is also cited, but that seems to include deaths from other, separate tornadoes that hit the county that day, not just this one tornado.** Wikipedia's infobox for the Rainsville tornado specifically gives 25 deaths for this tornado — treat **25 as the tornado-specific figure (matches your claim)**, and the higher 30–35 range as county-wide-for-the-day, a different (broader) denominator.
- **Path length: reports vary — 33.66 mi (NWS HUN) vs 36.63 mi (Wikipedia infobox) vs 36 mi loosely stated elsewhere.** Not reconciled to one number; NWS HUN's own page gives 33.66 mi as the measured length, so treat that as the primary-source figure over the ~36 mi secondary figure.
- **Winds: >200 mph — CONFIRMED** by NWS HUN.
- **Duration:** NWS HUN gives 6:19–6:56pm CDT (37 min); a secondary source says "remained on the ground for 36 minutes" — consistent, rounding difference only.
- **800-lb safe carried 600 ft — CONFIRMED, essentially exactly.** NWS HUN: "An 800-pound liberty safe was pulled off its anchorage and thrown into a wooded area 600 feet away."
- **School bus stripped to chassis — PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, different detail than claimed.** NWS HUN describes "a school bus in Rainsville was blown across Highway 75 from a parking lot" — it does not describe the bus as stripped down to its chassis. That specific "stripped to the chassis" phrasing was not found in the primary source; treat the bus-was-thrown-across-the-highway fact as confirmed, the "stripped to chassis" embellishment as unverified.
- **Plainview High School $15M damages — CONFIRMED, actually a range.** Secondary sources (WHNT, Storm Chase Wiki) report **$10–15 million** in damage to Plainview High School's main buildings, gymnasiums, sports fields, cafeteria, and the DeKalb County Schools Coliseum — walls and roof of the gym collapsed; students relocated to Sylvania High School. $15M is the top of the cited range, not a single confirmed figure — say "$10–15 million," not just "$15M."

## F. Ringgold GA, Lake Martin/Elmore Co AL, and April 28 pre-dawn GA tornadoes

### Ringgold GA EF4
Source: NWS Peachtree City (FFC) — [weather.gov/ffc/20110427_svrstorms](https://www.weather.gov/ffc/20110427_svrstorms)

- **Touchdown time: CONFIRMED, but the time zone matters.** NWS FFC gives touchdown at **8:15pm EDT**, which converts to **7:15pm CDT** — i.e., your claimed "7:15pm" is correct once you account for Georgia being in Eastern time while the rest of the outbreak narrative (Alabama/Mississippi) is in Central time. Don't present this as a discrepancy in the timeline — it's the same moment, different zone label.
- **Deaths: CORRECTED — not ~20.** NWS FFC's own Catoosa County (Georgia) survey gives **7 fatalities and 30 injuries** in Georgia. The tornado continued across the state line into Tennessee as the Ringgold–Apison(–Cleveland) system, and *that full multi-state track* is credited elsewhere with up to 24-25 deaths total (GA+TN combined). If your script means "deaths in Georgia," the number is **7**, not 20; if it means the full track including Tennessee, a total in the low-to-mid 20s is more defensible — but 20 specifically for Georgia alone doesn't check out.
- Peak winds: 175 mph (EF4), path 13 mi long × ⅓ mi wide in Catoosa County per NWS FFC; 75–100 homes damaged/destroyed, worst on Cherokee Valley Road (12 homes obliterated).

### Lake Martin / Elmore County AL EF4 (~8pm)
Source: NWS Birmingham event page — [weather.gov/bmx/event_04272011lakemartin](https://www.weather.gov/bmx/event_04272011lakemartin) (page located via search; not directly fetched, so treat as secondary-confirmed pending a direct read)

### April 28 pre-dawn Georgia tornadoes (Vaughn community)
Source: NWS FFC page above + Tornado Talk's dedicated Vaughn-Sunny Side write-up + Atlanta Magazine feature ("The Town that Blew Away")

- **Deaths: CORRECTED — 2, not part of a "3 deaths day total" framing.** The Vaughn, GA EF3 tornado (just before midnight April 27 rolling into the early hours of April 28) killed **2 people** and injured at least 12, per Tornado Talk/Atlanta Magazine. NWS FFC's page separately describes an EF3 in Meriwether/Spalding/Henry counties around 12:03am EDT April 28 with **2 fatalities** — it's not fully clear from this search pass whether "Vaughn" and this Meriwether/Spalding/Henry EF3 are the same tornado or two distinct ones on the same night (Vaughn is in Talbot County, adjacent to Meriwether) — this needs a closer read of the NWS FFC page to disambiguate before scripting. Either way, the "3 deaths day total" framing in the brief does not match — it's 2 deaths per the specific Vaughn-area account, and Georgia's overall April 27–28 toll (all tornadoes, all day) was **at least 20 dead statewide**, per NWS FFC's broader retrospective — much higher than "3" if that's meant to cover the whole day/state rather than just Vaughn.
