## 2. Complete Point Inventory (chronological, exhaustive)

### April 25, 2011
- 3:25 p.m.: SPC issues a PDS (Particularly Dangerous Situation) tornado watch for most of Arkansas + parts of Missouri, Oklahoma, Texas, Louisiana.
- Multiple twisters reported within hours across those states.
- News clip: "deadly mix of tornadoes and flooding in Arkansas overnight."
- **Vilonia, Arkansas** hardest hit — massive tornado destroyed most of the town.
- Up to 12 people reported missing; ~50 homes destroyed.
- Statewide (Arkansas): at least 7 killed by severe weather.
- Survivor audio: "they always say it sounds like a train. It didn't. It was just a... loud wind. Our ears popped."
- Tornado intensity breakdown: mostly EF1, four EF2, one EF3 confirmed.
- Day total: 18 tornadoes in Arkansas alone; 42 across the US.

### April 26, 2011
- Day begins under a "high risk" of severe weather.
- Warning issued for Louisiana, Arkansas, Oklahoma, Texas.
- Tornado count "now over 300"; unofficial/unconfirmed April total over 500 — described as "never happened in recorded history."
- 31 EF0 and 19 EF1 tornadoes.
- Four EF2 tornadoes hit **Greenbrier, Marshall, Tar Hill, and Deadwood, Texas**.
- One long-tracked wedge tornado caused damage in rural Texas/Louisiana.
- 55 total storm/tornado reports for the day.

### April 27, 2011 — Setup
- Powerful cold front stretching Texas → Ozarks → Ohio Valley.
- Warm, moisture-rich air surges into Mississippi, Alabama, Tennessee.
- Overnight severe warnings already posted across the South.
- SPC raises tornado probability to **45%** for the corridor from Meridian, Mississippi to Huntsville, Alabama.
- Embedded safety PSA clip: shelter in lowest-level basement; if none, first floor, small interior room (hall/closet/bathroom), away from windows, near center of house; explicitly debunks the "safe corner" (NE/SW) myth.
- 6:00–9:00 a.m.: several tornadoes touch down across central Alabama, including near **Cullman** — one described live as "a violent, large, dangerous tornado" heading toward a Walmart.
- On-air meteorologist naming **Hackleburg, Phil Campbell, Alabama** as targets ahead of time: "you got to call a strike a strike."
- 2:30 p.m.: supercells explode southwest of northern Alabama — narrator calls this "the scariest round."

### Hackleburg–Phil Campbell EF5 (Alabama)
- 3:05 p.m.: tornado first touches down SW of **Hamilton, Marion County** — initially looks EF1/EF2.
- Near Highway 43 it explodes to EF4; houses swept from foundations.
- Moving parallel to US-43 into **Hackleburg**, expands to violent **EF5**, 3/4-mile wide circulation, winds up to **210 mph**.
- Damage: homes "blew away like matchsticks," asphalt ripped from ground, underground storm shelters wrecked, some bodies thrown hundreds of yards.
- Nearly every vehicle tossed; **75% of Hackleburg destroyed**.
- Deadliest tornado in Alabama history; initially surveyed as EF3, upgraded to EF5 after damage assessment.
- First EF5 to hit Alabama since the **1998 Birmingham tornado**.
- 24 hours later, search crews found **30 people** who did not survive.
- 3:28 p.m.: funnel crosses into **Franklin County**, now nearly 1.25 miles wide, forward speed 69 mph.
- **Phil Campbell**: homes flattened, churches reduced to rubble, mobile-home frames hurled up to 50 yards.
- A concrete storm shelter believed impenetrable has its roof ripped away, exposing all **21 people** inside.
- A 25-foot stretch of road pavement peeled from the ground.
- Named survivor story (unnamed couple): one person in a bathtub blown into the street; a second person thrown from the house, slammed against a vehicle, grabbed a metal pole, was torn loose again and thrown onto a parked Explorer (source of a dent) — "he doesn't even know why he's still alive."
- Passing through **Oak Grove**: peak intensity — vehicles fly hundreds of yards, even deep-anchored brick homes torn apart and scattered "like dust."
- Continued track: **Mountain Hope, Langtown, Tanner**, across **Limestone County**, finally lifting in **Madison County at 4:50 p.m.**
- Total stats: 1 hour 45 minutes active — second longest-lived tornado of the whole outbreak; second deadliest US tornado of 2011 after Joplin; **102-mile path**; **$1.29 billion** in damage.
- Survivor audio: "it felt like an earthquake, the ground was shaking... 45 seconds of just fury... after getting out of there and seeing everything gone, you're really in shock."

### Smithville, Mississippi EF5
- 3:42 p.m.: the same supercell that had earlier produced a long-track EF3 near New Wren spins up another tornado 3 miles WSW of **Smithville**, along the Tennessee-Tombigbee Waterway.
- Within roughly 2 minutes it hits Smithville proper.
- Snaps trees near the Smithville Recreation Area; intensifies into a catastrophic **EF5 in under 3 minutes**.
- 3/4-mile wide; town itself only 1.6 square miles.
- First tornado of this intensity to hit Mississippi since **1966**.
- Crosses Davis Road South, carving a trench deep enough to remain visible in aerial imagery a year later.
- Winds peak at **205 mph**, moves NE along Mississippi Highway 25.
- First fatality: a bed and breakfast obliterated.
- Even well-anchored brick houses swept away.
- An RV hurled **250 yards**, embedded in the ground.
- A truck ripped apart — only its bumper left dangling on the Smithville water tower.
- Plumbing lines ripped from the ground, floor tiles peeled, sill plates torn from foundations.
- Rotational force strong enough to knock granite tombstones in the direction opposite the tornado's travel.
- **117 structures destroyed**: city hall, post office, four churches, police station, water system, plus countless homes/businesses.
- Survivor audio: "people screaming for help everywhere... I walked up on three that had passed away, and then there were 20 or more screaming for help."
- Weakens approaching rural areas NE of town, crosses into **Itawamba County**.
- Continues into Alabama's Marion County, causing EF1 damage near **Bear**.
- Restrengthens to EF3 striking **Shottsville**.
- Dissipates near **Hodges at 4:23 p.m.**
- Totals: 41 minutes of violent winds through communities; **37-mile damage path** across Mississippi and Alabama; **23 fatalities**. Called "one of the most intense tornadoes ever recorded and the strongest of the entire 2011 super outbreak."

### Tuscaloosa–Birmingham EF4
- 4:43 p.m.: a supercell produces a large wedge tornado in rural **Greene County, Alabama**, EF2 intensity, moving adjacent to Tuscaloosa County, uprooting trees, damaging structures.
- Named on-air meteorologist **John Oldshue** captures footage near **Union** — described as the first video evidence the tornado had touched ground, and one of the most widely seen tornadoes in history via live broadcast.
- NWS declares a tornado emergency for Tuscaloosa.
- 5:10 p.m.: storm moves into southern/eastern Tuscaloosa — slams directly into a city of **90,000** (unusual, since most of the outbreak's violent tornadoes hit rural areas).
- Starts at EF4, crosses **35th Street**, guts a cell tower and multiple warehouses in an industrial area.
- Tuscaloosa Police Department HQ spared; the **County Emergency Management Office** takes a direct hit and is wrecked along with equipment/vehicles.
- Named victim: **Ashley Harrison**, days from graduating University of Alabama, staying in a rental home; sheltered inside, home torn apart, hurled **65 yards**; died — described as her parents' only child.
- Moves through **Rosedale** and **Forest Lake**, leveling homes and multiple apartment complexes (some two-story buildings reduced to rubble).
- Live tower-cam footage from atop the Tuscaloosa County Courthouse; radar velocity delta described as ~150 knots.
- Crosses **15th Street & McFarland Boulevard** at EF4.
- Continues through **Alberta City**.
- By **Holt**, widens to a full mile; every tree snapped, even those in ravines.
- Crossing **Hurricane Creek**: shreds a railroad trestle, flings a **34-ton steel truss** more than 100 feet up a hill.
- Marina on **Holt Lake** takes a major hit; tornado weakens slightly to EF3.
- Plows through woodland toward Birmingham.
- Entering **Jefferson County**, regains EF4 strength — reaches peak intensity and width for this track.
- 6:00 p.m.: crosses western/northern Birmingham suburbs — **Concord, Pleasant Grove, McDonald Chapel**, northern Birmingham.
- Radar shows a huge debris ball; storm tracked live for over 30 minutes.
- At a coal yard, a **35-ton railcar** hurled nearly **400 feet**.
- Weakens to EF2 after the coal yard but still destroys pier-and-beam foundation homes and warehouses in McDonald Chapel.
- Moves to **Pratt City**, **Fultondale**, weakens further along **US Route 31**, finally lifts **2 miles north of Tarrant**.
- Totals: active **1 hour 31 minutes**; kills **44 in Tuscaloosa** and **20 in Birmingham** (64 combined); **1,500+ injured**. Described as one of the longest-track tornadoes of the outbreak and second deadliest after Hackleburg–Phil Campbell.

### Rainsville–Sylvania EF5 (DeKalb County, Alabama)
- 5:45 p.m.: supercell in NE Alabama begins intensifying.
- 6:10 p.m.: initial severe thunderstorm warning; tornado warning follows for the **Lakeview** community.
- 6:19 p.m.: funnel touches down in Lakeview, NE of **Geraldine** — initially light tree/building damage.
- Moving toward **Fife**: displays multiple vortices, alternating between horizontal sub-vortices and a solid wedge shape.
- At Fife: intensifies to EF3 — significant tree damage near Beulah Baptist Cemetery, severe home destruction.
- Tracks toward **Shiloh** along Heaton Road, growing wider/stronger.
- At Shiloh: becomes **EF5** — demolishes a metal farm building, uproots multiple homes.
- Exits Shiloh toward **Rainsville**: vortex widens from 50 yards to half a mile; winds around **200 mph**.
- Rainsville: homes, businesses, public facilities erased so completely "not even debris was left behind."
- Vehicles hurled hundreds of yards; a **school bus** flung across a highway, stripped to bare chassis.
- Multi-vortex tornado active through Rainsville for **36 minutes**, carving a **36.63-mile path** (later restated in the summary line as "36-mi").
- Survivor audio: "it was about 3 minutes, and it changed our whole life... from being homeless and out of work... you look up and you can see the sky from a basement."
- **Huddle House** restaurant reduced to rubble, one wall left standing.
- **Plainview High School**: **$15 million** in damages.
- Along **Lingerfelt Road**: a stone house destroyed, its stone pillar uprooted, a section of concrete foundation ripped from the ground, pavement scoured.
- An **800-lb safe** ripped from its anchoring, carried **600 feet** into a wooded area, its steel door torn off.
- Continues toward **county roads 515 and 441**.
- Eastern **Sylvania**: **Mountain View Baptist Church** obliterated; a couple inside a nearby home thrown into an open field — did not survive.
- Crosses SE of **Henagar** and **Ider**: debarks trees, destroys part of **Blake Community Church**.
- Shrinks rapidly crossing into Georgia near **Fox Mountain**.
- Carves 3 more miles through **Rising Fawn**, lifts just outside the community at **6:55 p.m.**
- Called the **4th and final EF5 of the 2011 super outbreak**.
- Totals: active 36 minutes; **36-mile path across DeKalb County**; **25 lives lost**; **~$10 million** in damage.

### April 28, 2011 (coda)
- ~12:00 a.m. (near midnight April 27): an EF3 develops north of Alps Road, ~2.17 miles SW of **Alvaton**; simultaneously an EF2 tracks through southern **Meriwether County**.
- The EF3 barrels through the small community of **Vaughn** — "in less than a minute" the community is devastated; over a dozen homes destroyed, many more damaged.
- Continues NE at EF3 strength, briefly crosses into **Henry County**, lifts 2 miles SE of **Hampton at 12:28 a.m.**
- 10 minutes later: another EF3 touches down **4 miles south of Meansville**, south-central **Pike County**.
- Path crosses **Pike, Lamar, Monroe, and Butts counties**, lifting SE of Butts County at **1:15 a.m.**
- Day total: **47 twisters**; "not as devastating" as April 27 but **3 lives lost**, many injuries.

## 5. Notable Techniques (steal-worthy — this is our formula model)

- **Cold open = future flash-forward, then narrated pull-back.** Opens on the goriest imagery/audio from deep in the video (aftermath, "smell of death") before jumping back 2 days to start the chronological build — creates an open loop that isn't closed until literally the last minute of runtime (the callback line "true scale of destruction was revealed... forced to ignore the dead").
- **Ticking-clock timestamps as the connective tissue.** Nearly every beat is anchored to a specific time ("3:05 p.m.", "3:28 p.m.", "4:50 p.m.") — this creates a documentary/procedural pace and lets the narrator compress geography without losing the audience.
- **Escalating structure via repeated superlative-stacking.** Each EF5 segment ends with a "biggest/deadliest/strongest" claim (Hackleburg = deadliest in Alabama history; Smithville = strongest of entire outbreak; Rainsville = 4th and final EF5) — even though these are mutually exclusive superlatives, they function rhetorically to keep each segment feeling like the peak, right before the next one tops it.
- **Numeric progression as a structural drumbeat.** Each segment closes with the same stat cadence: duration → path length → death toll → dollar damage. This ritualized "stat card" at the end of every tornado story is a reusable template.
- **Physical-object damage details as a horror shorthand instead of narrator adjectives.** Rather than saying "the tornado was extremely powerful," it shows scale via concrete objects: 34-ton steel truss thrown 100+ ft, 35-ton railcar thrown 400 ft, an 800-lb safe carried 600 ft with its door torn off, granite tombstones rotated backward, a truck reduced to a bumper on a water tower. This is a strong technique to copy — object-scale damage details land harder than adjectives or even wind-speed numbers.
- **Live broadcast/scanner audio spliced directly into narration**, not just used as B-roll — meteorologists' real-time calls ("Oh no. No. No.", "this is becoming a large tornado") are treated as story beats in their own right, heightening urgency without added narrator lines.
- **A single "named victim" reserved for the biggest/most relatable segment** (Ashley Harrison in Tuscaloosa) rather than naming a victim in every segment — this makes that one story land harder by contrast with the otherwise anonymized death tolls elsewhere.
- **Deliberate structural rest beat**: the April 28 coda is intentionally smaller in stakes right before the final numbers — gives the audience room to breathe between the four back-to-back EF5 climaxes and the closing statistical gut-punch.
- **Closing on an unresolved quote rather than a wrap-up** — no narrator "in conclusion" line, no rebuilding-and-hope coda. Ends on raw ambiguity, which is unusual and worth testing (risk: some viewers may feel unresolved/unsatisfied, but it's clearly working at 121K views).
