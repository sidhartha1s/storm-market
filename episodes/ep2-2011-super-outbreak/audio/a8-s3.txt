But the numbers were never the real story, and this is the part of it we owe to the people who lived it. Ashley Harrison was twenty-two years old, a senior at the University of Alabama, weeks from graduation, her parents' only child. When the tornado emergency was declared she did what the warnings said to do. She sheltered in a closet, in a house, with her boyfriend holding on beside her. He woke up in the wreckage. She never did. There was nothing careless in how she died, and that is exactly why her story is the one to remember. Multiply it by three hundred and twenty-four. In Tuscaloosa today there is a memorial to that afternoon, honoring the responders who were called by circumstance, burdened by tragedy, and who answered anyway. And in Hackleburg, the town that lost eighteen people and three quarters of its buildings, the Wrangler plant was rebuilt. It reopened in twenty thirteen, with more jobs than it had before the storm. The town chose to exist. That is the other thing April twenty-seventh proved. And it is the thing worth carrying out of this story.
