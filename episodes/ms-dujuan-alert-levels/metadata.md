## Description
https://paulraymond.online
📘 The book mentioned at 7:39 — The Storm-Ready Home

Right now a tropical depression sits near Guam, and Japan's weather agency has already mapped its
path for the next five days. When Japanese officials decide what to tell residents, they will not
reach for a hurricane watch or a hurricane warning. Japan runs its own five-level alert ladder, built
in May 2021 after the old two-tier system confused too many people at the worst possible time.

This explainer covers: today's live numbers for the unnamed tropical depression near the Marianas
(998 hPa, 34 mph sustained, gusts to 51), the two-tier system Japan retired and the 5-level ladder that
replaced it, Level 3 (the elderly and people with disabilities evacuate first — a tier the US has no
federal match for), Level 4 (Evacuation Instruction, backed by JMA's own quoted Urgent Warning
guidance, and the closest match to a US mandatory evacuation order), Level 5 (Emergency Safety
Measures — the point where leaving itself may no longer be safe, compared against the National
Weather Service's Extreme Wind Warning), why America runs three uncoordinated systems (hurricane
watch, hurricane warning, and a separately-timed local evacuation order) where Japan runs one, and
where tonight's storm actually sits on that ladder (still below Level 1, forecast to stay east of
Japan through its five-day window).

Sources: Cabinet Office, Government of Japan, "Evacuation Information (Revised Version)," effective 20
May 2021; Japan Meteorological Agency, "Emergency Warning System"; JMA's live TC2630 advisory (16-17
Sep 2026); NOAA National Hurricane Center / National Weather Service, "Hurricane and Tropical Storm
Watches, Warnings, Advisories and Outlooks."
