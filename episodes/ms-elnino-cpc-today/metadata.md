A very strong El Niño holds across the Pacific tonight. NOAA's Climate Prediction Center keeps its El Niño Advisory in force,
with better than a nine in ten chance that this becomes one of the strongest events on record and a three in four chance of a
historic one. The warmest water sits off South America. The Atlantic is silent at the peak of hurricane season. And the World
Meteorological Organization has called it exceptional, in words it has not used in fifty years.

This update covers: the four Niño regions and where the heat is stacked (weekly to 12 Sep: Niño 1+2 +3.7 °C, Niño 3 +2.8, Niño 3.4 +2.0,
Niño 4 −0.1, all higher than the 10 Sep advisory), the quiet Atlantic (no active storms, one area near Bermuda, activity under 10 percent of normal), the agencies on record
(WMO Secretary-General Celeste Saulo, UN Secretary-General António Guterres, NOAA's Emily Becker), why the surface numbers hold
(subsurface water up to +8 °C, a positive Indian Ocean Dipole), the winter lean for the United States (milder north, wetter south),
and what nobody has said yet: the peak value, the end date, and when the Atlantic wakes up. Next CPC update: 8 October.
