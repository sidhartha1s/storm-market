## STAKES CURVE

Rises correctly H → A1 → A2, then sags twice.

- **H to A1**: numbers become people. Tim Haley carrying 169 and Gus Allen's one sentence are the emotional
  floor the rest of the script is measured against. Correct escalation.
- **A2**: peak human stakes of the first half. The bus on the stage, 34 dead in four minutes, five schools by
  name. This is the strongest act and it is the one that is underwritten by 130 words.
- **A3**: holds on information but **drops on human stake**. Three institutions in a row (the office going
  dark, the helicopter, the courthouse safe) carry two deaths between them on screen. Gilbert saves it, but
  the act is about systems, not people, and the viewer feels it.
- **A4**: back to peak. The rescuers under the second tornado and Becky Todd's two sons are the best-placed
  human beats in the script.
- **A5 is the sag**, roughly 15:45 to 17:30. Stakes score 2. The act opens with a real question, then spends
  240 words on the F-scale ladder and the one-air-mass engine with no one in danger. Pennington's dead wife
  appears inside a graphics explanation of what F5 damage looks like, which spends a death on a definition.
  The microburst reveal at A5-S4 is the best payoff in the script and it arrives after the viewer has already
  been given two lectures.
- **A6** recovers by moving the stake to the present tense (433,000 people on the path), which is the right
  move, but the act asks three questions in 520 words and answers them in order, so curiosity flattens even as
  stakes rise.

Secondary soft spot: the A2 → CTA seam. A2-S5 is 25 words of tease carrying the whole hinge across the CTA.
It works because the hinge ("his own scale was too small") is genuinely good, but there is no margin.

## FACT FLAGS

### Hard errors — must fix before TTS

1. **A6-S1: "On April 27th, 2011, an EF5 tornado came out of Mississippi, crossed Alabama for a hundred and
   thirty two miles."** Wrong. The addendum (§1) gives the Hackleburg–Phil Campbell EF5 a 132-mile path across
   **five Alabama counties — Marion, Franklin, Lawrence, Limestone, Madison**. It began in Marion County,
   Alabama. The Mississippi origin belongs to the Guin F5 of 1974, 37 years earlier. As written the script
   hands the 2011 tornado the 1974 tornado's birthplace, in the act whose whole argument is that the two
   events share ground.
2. **A6-S1b: "Madison County next door, where the second Tanner tornado ended."** Reversed. Fact sheet §1: the
   **first** Tanner F5 "ended in Madison County"; the **second** traveled ~98 miles "before lifting in Coffee
   County, Tennessee."
3. **A4-S1 internal contradiction.** "Twenty eight people died, **most of them in and around the town of
   Tanner**" (first F5) versus, 80 words later, "**Sixteen people died in Limestone County** that night" (both
   F5s combined, per addendum §1). Tanner is in Limestone County. Both numbers are sourced; the framing "most
   of them in and around Tanner" is what breaks. The first Tanner F5's 28 deaths span Lawrence, Limestone and
   Morgan counties.
4. **I: "The first tornado touched down at 2:16 PM over empty ground."** It was the first **F5**, not the first
   tornado of the outbreak — the fact sheet table labels it "First F5 of the outbreak" and lists earlier and
   concurrent tornadoes. A1-S1 gets it right ("The first F5 of the day"), so the script contradicts itself
   across acts and spends A1's own reveal a minute early.
5. **A1-S1: "Ninety minutes later a town with a warning… would lose thirty one people."** Measured: 2:16 PM
   CDT to 3:30 PM CDT = **74 minutes**. The clock graphic is on screen while the line is spoken.
6. **A3-S5: "That was five states in one hour."** The act names four states (Indiana, Ohio, Kentucky, Georgia)
   and the beats span 5:17 PM EDT (Monticello) to ~6:30–7:00 PM EDT (Resaca) = **1 h 13 m to 1 h 43 m**.
   Neither number is right.

### Wrong or soft numbers

8. **A3-S5: "about four hundred miles south, in Georgia."** Measured Monticello → Resaca = **438 miles**.
   "About four hundred and forty" or "more than four hundred."
9. **A3-S2b: "Across the map at that hour, fifteen tornadoes were on the ground at the same time."** The

10. **H popfig "4:45 PM" against "five tornadoes on one radar screen at once."** The fact sheet gives the

11. **H: "and the worst ones hadn't even formed yet."** At 4:45 PM, Xenia — the deadliest single tornado of

12. **A4-S3: "seven in nine hours."** 2:16 PM CDT to Guin's dissipation at 10:57 PM CDT = **8 h 41 m**, so it
