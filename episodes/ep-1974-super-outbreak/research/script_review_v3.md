## 5 REGISTER

Third-grader stumbles, quoted, with plainer rewrites:

1. **"And the corridor that took the worst of it, in north Alabama, is the fastest growing city in the state today."** A corridor is not a city, so the sentence is a category error as well as hard to follow.
   → *"And the farmland that took the worst of it, in north Alabama, is a city now. It is the fastest growing city in the state."*
2. **"The case for a radar that could see wind finally had its evidence, and the Doppler network that watches the same sky today grew out of days like this one."** 30 words, abstract subject.
   → *"It also proved the country needed a radar that could see the wind itself. That is where today's radar came from."*
3. **"The ingredients were ordinary spring weather for that part of the country, arriving on the same day."**
   → *"Warm wet air, dry air on top, and a cold front. Ordinary spring weather there. It all arrived on the same day."*
4. **"Fix your radar tonight. Bring in extra people tomorrow."** Unmarked reported speech. On audio a listener hears the narrator giving *them* orders.
   → *"He told them to fix their radars that night and bring in extra people the next day."*
5. **"That is the whole story of this channel in one county, and it is why the 1974 map still matters."** CLAUDE.md bans structure self-reference; "this channel" reads as a seam.
   → *"That is the whole story of this ground in one county, and it is why the 1974 map still matters."*
6. **"Its own report, six days later, said the warnings had saved many lives."** Fine, but "Both were true" two sentences later is a flourish that asserts nothing. Keep only if the visual holds both numbers.

**Clock times not in digits:** one — "It was not yet four o'clock." (3:50) → *"It was not yet 4 PM."*
All sixteen other clock references are digits with a zone named. Good.

**Documentary-voice flourishes:** "the pictures from that walk went around the world" (the register marks
this `[editorial]` — it is the narrator asserting cultural memory with no source); "By midnight there was
nothing left to count but the dead" (earns its place, keep).

**Style:** "neighbours" → "neighbors"; "north-east" → "northeast". US-English channel.

**Fragment load:** 21 % of sentences are five words or fewer, in long staccato runs — "Same town. One mile
to the west. One hour later. Ninety eight miles long." The repo rule is flowing connective sentences, not
chopped fragments, and pocket-TTS runs punchy prose slower (ep5: 139 wpm on that style). This tic is both
a register problem and part of the runtime shortfall.

## 7 FACT RED FLAGS

Claims stated as settled that the sheets mark unverified, plus one internal contradiction.

1. **"The college had a thousand people who moved when they were told." (2:50)** The addendum §4 says no source describes where anyone sheltered, and the fact register orders this line cut unless sourced. The sentence invents a cause for a zero-death outcome. **Highest-risk line in the script.**
2. **"Because school was out, but the buildings were not empty. Because the roads were full of people driving home." (4:13)** Addendum §5 says explicitly that no source confirms in-session vs just-dismissed and argues against "school had just let out". Two unsourced causal claims, no `[verify]` tag, used to answer the act's question.
3. **Internal contradiction.** A2-S1 (4:11): "Dayton's weather office put a tornado warning on Xenia at 4:10 PM … Thirty minutes." A6-S2 (15:50): "In 1974 fifteen minutes was the best any town got." The payoff act contradicts the script's own best-documented warning.
4. **"the researchers said why in three words. Better warnings." (14:52)** That is two words.
5. **"Three hundred and thirty people dead."** Hook states it settled; the sheet carries NCEI 335 against NOAA 330 as a contested band. Pick one, state it the same way in H and A4-S3, and put the source on the card.
6. **"A hundred and forty eight tornadoes in sixteen hours" (1:05) and "for sixteen hours" (13:05).** NCEI says a roughly 16–24 hour period. Sixteen is the low end stated as fact, twice.
7. **"President Nixon wrote to him afterward." (8:12)** The source says a special commendation. "Wrote to him" adds a document nobody has seen.
8. **"Most people that year did not even know what a tornado watch was." (1:00)** Tagged `[verify]`, but the underlying finding is qualitative and about the tornado hazard generally, not watch recognition. The register's own instruction is soften to "many" or cut.
9. **The church van (8:53).** Register: verify the girls' fate before any line implies it. Leaving it unresolved implies it.

## 9 OVERALL — 7/10, confidence 70 %

**Verdict: FIX THEN RE-GRADE.**

This is a competent script with a strong spine: the countdown works, the escalation to "It went through the
rescuers" is genuinely the best beat in any of the three versions, and the 16 → 4 payoff in A6 answers the
dominant question inside the last 35 seconds exactly as doctrine asks. The hook clears the 8 gate.

It does not ship yet, for four reasons that are all measurable rather than matters of taste. The stakes axis
in A5 is 2, which fails the stated pre-TTS gate of no chapter axis under 3, and it is the act where the
audience actually leaves. Three claims the fact sheets mark as unfound are narrated as settled, one of them
inventing a cause for Hanover's zero deaths. The payoff act contradicts the script's own Xenia timeline.
And the runtime lands at 17:56 to 18:45 against a locked 22:00 ± 1:00, so the length question has to be
settled before any TTS spend, not after.

Fix items 1 through 6 and this is an 8.5. Fix 1 alone is worth more than the other nine combined.
