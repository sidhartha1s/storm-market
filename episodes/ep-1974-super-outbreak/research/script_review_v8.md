## HOOK

**8/10.**

Sentence one — "Seven F5 tornadoes in one day, and it has never happened again." — is exactly the channel rule:
the title's payoff delivered whole, a number first, no runway, no word a third grader would stumble on, and the
"never again" clause turns a statistic into a claim the rest of the video has to defend.

Two points come off for craft the palette already forbids: "Three hundred and thirty people dead." is a
five-word verbless trailer fragment (banned outright in the Spoken voice palette, and the first of three
fragments in a script allowed one), and the same number is spent twice inside 64 words — "three hundred and
thirty" opens the second sentence and closes the fifth, so the hook's last line buys no new information when it
should be the hardest thing in the segment.

## STAKES CURVE

Rises cleanly H → A1 → A2 → A4, with one flat stretch and one real sag.

- **H to A1 (rises).** Seven F5s / 330 dead / five hooks → one F5 over empty farmland and six dead → a college
  of 1,000 with zero dead → a town of 1,700 losing 31 with fifteen minutes of warning. Escalation by human
  consequence, exactly the script-os ladder.
- **A2 (peak of the first half).** 34 dead, five named schools, a bus on the stage, 1,445 Guardsmen, Nixon. The
  strongest act in the script and the only one where every axis reads 5.
- **A3 (flat, not falling).** The information density is the highest in the episode — F6 crossed out, the 4:05
  verbatim warning, five hooks, the office going dark, Louisville, a safe through three floors, Georgia — but
  it covers four cities in 524 words with no connective stake, so the *volume* climbs while the *tension* holds
  still. A viewer can follow it; a viewer cannot feel it building.
- **A4 (emotional peak).** Two F5s on one town an hour apart, the second through the rescuers, the tape, Becky
  Todd's two sons. Correct placement per the formula: emotion banked at the end.
- **A5 (planned dip, unplanned sag).** A detective act after the impact act is right. The sag is self-inflicted:
  A5-S3's triple restatement of one mechanism, and A5-S4's two adjacent institutional lines ("weather offices got
  backup power" / "the Doppler network... grew out of days like this one") which are context, not stakes.
- **A6 (the real sag, and it is in the worst place).** The act's genuine reveal — 16 dead in 1974, 4 in 2011 on
  the same ground with 2.5× the people — lands at A6-S1b, roughly minute 19. Everything after it restates the
  thesis: "It will happen again..." then "April third can happen again..." then "What changed is the warning."
  then "Today the warning reaches you..." then "Today the warning buys you minutes." Five sentences carrying one
  idea across the final 380 words. The episode's last stakes movement is a safety instruction, and the safety
  instruction is the one ungrammatical sentence in the script.

**Where it sags:** the second half of A5 (≈17:30–18:30) and the whole of A6 after A6-S1b (≈19:15 onward).

## REGISTER

Sentences that are not plain spoken American, quoted with chapter.

**Oxford phrasing**
- A4-S1: "To this day nobody can say for certain which one killed **whom**." — "whom" is not a word a third
  grader says out loud. → "nobody can say for certain which tornado killed which people."

**Comma splices**
- A3-S3: "Dick Gilbert reported traffic for WHAS radio**,** he grew up on Pennsylvania Avenue in Crescent Hill,
  and the tornado was on his street." — two independent clauses joined by a bare comma.
- Z: "Tell me in the comments**,** I read all of them."

**Trailer fragments under six words / fragments beyond the one allowed (rule 7 allows one per script; there are three)**
- H: "Three hundred and thirty people dead." (5 words, verbless — palette-banned outright)
- A5-S1: "Ten months, more than ten thousand miles, low and slow, with every track drawn by hand onto one map."
  (verbless)
- A6-S3: "A storm shelter or an inside room on the lowest floor you can reach in that time, and a car trying to
  outrun a mile-wide tornado is the one thing you can't." (the first half has no verb, the two halves do not join;
  this will not read aloud and is the last instruction in the episode)

**Reveal-flip / balanced-contrast engine (humanise rule 7 allows ONE per script; six are present, five of them in A6)**
1. A2-S3: "because thirty minutes moves you across a room, **not** out of a city"
2. A6-S1b: "**More** houses in the path **and fewer** dead"
3. A6-S2: "In 1974 the radar saw rain, **and today** it sees the wind turning inside the storm"
4. A6-S2: "Back then the warning was a radio you had to have switched on, **and today** it's the phone in your pocket"
5. A6-S2: "Brandenburg got fifteen minutes that day and most towns got nothing at all, **and today** the average
   warning runs eight to fifteen minutes"
6. A6-S2: "**Today** the warning reaches you, **and** the houses moved into the path instead"
   — items 3, 4 and 5 are three consecutive sentences on the identical then/now frame. That is the pattern the
   audience calls AI.

**Summary tags / announcing the move (rules 4 and 7)**
- A6-S1: "Put another map on top of the 1974 tracks." — an instruction to the viewer about what the video is
  doing next, the "let me walk you through" family.
- A6-S1: "The second time, the numbers went the other way." — announces the reveal the next sentence delivers.
- A6-S2: "What changed is the warning." — a thesis tag with no fact in it.

**Decoration words / narrating the conclusion on top of the facts (rules 1, 5)**
- A2-S1: "and in 1974 thirty minutes **was a gift**" — a narrator adjective on the fact.
- A4-S2: "and **it's a rare recording** of an F5 passing directly over a house" — the conclusion narrated on top
  of the fact the previous clause already delivered.
- A5-S1: "**low and slow**" — a rhyming decoration pair.

**Rhetorical questions in the body:** none. The only question in the narration is Z's comment ask ("Where are you
watching this from, and do you have a place below ground?"), which is CTA spine and allowed. Clean.

**Anaphora runs (3+ sentences opening on the same word):** none. But sentence-initial "And" appears **eight**
times, three of them inside A5 alone (A5-S1, A5-S3, A5-S4), and two of those eight are near-identical sentence
shapes on the same subject: I's "And the corridor that took the worst of it, in north Alabama, is the fastest
growing city in the state today" and A5-S4's "And the corridor the map drew across north Alabama is not
farmland any more."

**Dangling pronoun (not on the brief's list but it breaks the read):**
- A3-S2: "and then, at 5:40 PM, **his** office went dark." No male antecedent in the segment.

**Do the palette rules hold?**

- **At most three of each per 20 minutes:** yes, comfortably. Max is 2 (repeated verb).
- **Never twice inside three minutes (same device):** yes. The two repeated-verb instances sit 9 minutes apart.
- **Never the same one in neighbouring acts:** yes. Repeated verb is in H and A3, with I, A1 and A2 between.
- **"Aim for one noticeable device every two to three minutes":** **no — this is the failure.** Five devices in
  twenty minutes is one every four minutes, and they are not spread: **A4-S1 carries the aside and the opener
  four words apart** ("...doing the rescuing. So two F5 tornadoes..."), while **A2, A5, A6 and Z carry zero
  devices between them** — that is roughly eleven straight minutes (≈4:50–7:40 and ≈15:00–20:00) of narration
  with no spoken texture at all. The palette's warning is about a device appearing in every act; the inverse
  failure is a device appearing in almost none, and it reads as written-not-spoken across the whole back half.
- **Banned items:** no "uh", no "um", no self-corrections ("about an hour, maybe a little less" — absent), no
  doubled negatives for rhythm ("not before, not since" — absent). Clean on all four.
- **Trailer fragments (also palette-banned):** three present, listed in REGISTER above. This is the only
  palette-banned category the script breaks.
- **Contractions (always-on):** present and natural throughout — "hadn't", "didn't", "couldn't", "can't",
  "they're", "it's".

**Documentation mismatch:** the stage note under the hook reads "*the shipped hook uses no device beyond the
contraction, per the palette*". It does — "He knew, and three hundred and thirty people died anyway" is the
repeated-verb device. Either the note or the line should change, because the next grader will census it too.

## FACT FLAGS

Checked against `fact_sheet.md` and `fact_sheet_addendum.md`. `[verify ...]` lines already tagged in the script
are listed as **to source** at the end, not as errors. F5 confirmed correct throughout for 1974 (Fujita scale;

**Errors and contradictions**

1. **A4-S1 — two death counts that read as a contradiction.** "Twenty eight people died along those miles, from
   Lawrence County through Limestone" (tornado 1, full track) then ~90 words later "Sixteen people died in
   Limestone County that night" (addendum: 16 = Limestone County only, from **both** F5s combined). Neither line
   names its scope, and the second number is smaller than the first, so the listener hears a correction.
   Highest-priority fact defect in the script.
2. **A2-S4 — chronology inverted.** "fifty miles away in Cincinnati the afternoon was about to get worse."
   Sayler Park was ~4:32 PM EDT (addendum: 4:05 warning + 27 minutes); central Xenia was hit at 4:40 PM EDT. The
   Cincinnati tornado came **eight minutes before** Xenia, not after.
3. **A2-S4 — distance disagrees with the script's own fact register.** Narration says "fifty miles away in
   Cincinnati"; the register entry reads "Cincinnati ~40 mi from Xenia `[measure on the plate]`". One of the two

4. **A3-S5 — interval too short.** "Four states inside two hours." The four states in that segment run Sayler
   Park 4:32 PM EDT → Sugar Valley/Resaca 6:30–7:00 PM EDT ≈ **2 h 28 m**. "Two and a half hours" is the honest
   number and costs nothing.
5. **A3-S2 — "his office went dark" has no antecedent.** The last named man is Fujita (A3-S1). Reads as though
   Fujita ran the Cincinnati office.
6. **A1-S2 — overstates the source.** "He spent that night carrying people out of the wreckage, a hundred and
   sixty nine of them **by his own count**." The source (army.mil, via fact sheet) says Haley **helped carry** 169
   dead and injured. "Carrying... by his own count" upgrades assisted work to sole work and attributes the tally

7. **A3-S3 — overstates the source.** "It tore the roof off Freedom Hall." Addendum: "tore off **part of** the
   roof of Freedom Hall."
8. **I — unsupported quantifier.** "for the next eight hours most of the ones that followed found a town."

9. **A6-S2 — two overclaims in one sentence.** "Brandenburg got fifteen minutes that day and **most towns got

11. **Attribution inconsistency on 330.** A4-S3 says "by NOAA's count"; H and A6-S2/S3 state 330 flat. The fact

12. **Rounding against humanise rule 11 (exact figures, never rounded).** I: "two thousand six hundred miles"

## TOP TEN FIXES

**1. A4-S1 — scope the two Alabama death counts so they stop contradicting each other.** (Highest: a viewer
hears a mistake.)
> Now: "Twenty eight people died along those miles, from Lawrence County through Limestone." ... "Sixteen people
> died in Limestone County that night, and about fifty in that one stretch of north Alabama."
> → "Twenty eight people died along that first tornado's fifty two miles, from Lawrence County into Limestone."
