# script_review_v1 — skeptic grade, ep-1974-super-outbreak

Reviewer: independent, did not write the script. Graded against script-os.md, the owner's standing
hook and register rules, and research/fact_sheet.md. Measured numbers here come from tool runs, not
estimates: narration is 2,775 tag-stripped words, not the 2,950 the header claims, and almost every
segment header over-claims its own word count by 15 to 40 percent (A2-S2 says 130 w, is 78 w).

## 5. REGISTER

**Sentences a third grader stumbles on, quoted, with plainer rewrites:**

- "The same wind, the same minutes, and the difference between a basement and a living room is the
  difference between a story and a name on a wall." Thirty words, two abstract "difference between"
  pairs. Rewrite: "Same wind. Same minutes. The students were in a basement. They lived."
- "That is the shape of the whole day in one hill." Rewrite: "That one hill explains the whole day."
  Then cut one of the two, because they make the same point.
- "Its target, if a thing like that has a target, was Guin, Alabama." An inversion with a clause
  buried in the middle. Rewrite: "It was heading for Guin, Alabama."
- "The problem with thirty minutes is what a town does with them." Rewrite: "Thirty minutes only
  helps if people hear the warning. School had just let out."
- "and for sixty two miles it did something almost merciful." Rewrite: "and for sixty two miles it
  stayed over empty ground."
- "So take the dominant question straight." Doctrine jargon leaking into narration. Rewrite: "So,
  could it happen again?"
- "So do the arithmetic on fifteen minutes." Rewrite: "So what can you do with fifteen minutes?"
- "Fifty years later, asked to describe the third of April, he said seven words." Participle opener,
  and it makes the viewer count. Rewrite: "Fifty years later he described that day in one sentence."
- "and read them like a doctor reading x-rays." Homograph trap: "read" is present tense here and
  pocket-TTS will say "red". Respell it "reed" in the TTS copy.

**Clock times not spoken in digits. Nine violations of an explicit owner rule:**

"A quarter to five" is the owner's own banned example, quoted back into the script. Separately, the
narration never states a time zone while the graphics carry CDT, EST and EDT, and A1-S2's "an hour
and fourteen minutes later" is measured from 2:16 CDT while the sentence before it ended at 3:19
EST. The spoken clock is not followable.

**Britishisms and documentary-voice flourishes:** "almost merciful"; "under the same sky"; "the
worst of the worst"; "if a thing like that has a target"; "in a state that does not think of itself
as tornado country"; "torn open". "Half past seven" and "a quarter of an hour" are British register,
not plain American.

**Structure references, banned by the repo because they read as a seam:** "That is where the number
in the title comes from" (A5-S1b); "So take the dominant question straight" (A6-S2); "the point of
this whole story is" (A6-S3); "That is the whole answer" (A6-S4). All four sit in the final third.

## 7. FACT RED FLAGS

**Hard errors a viewer or a graphic will catch:**

1. **"Four hundred miles to the north-east, in a town called Xenia."** Brandenburg to Xenia measures
   168 miles. Spoken once and repeated in the chapter card. The repo requires a map for every spatial
   claim, so the map will visibly contradict the voice.
2. **"And six hundred miles south, in Georgia."** Otterbein, Indiana to Sugar Valley, Georgia
   measures 424 miles.
3. **"he said seven words"** followed by "It was just a bad day", which is six words.
4. **"Seven F5 tornadoes in one afternoon"**, in the hook and again in A6-S3. The seventh was at
   night; the script's own act 4 is called "Alabama after dark".
5. **"wind he called inconceivable."** The fact sheet says Fujita concluded the F6 *rating* was
   inconceivable, not the wind. This is the A3 payoff line, the most load-bearing quote in the
   episode, and it misattributes the quoted word.
6. **"the reason the day has a name."** Fujita coined "Super Outbreak" for the whole 148-tornado
   event, not for Xenia.
7. **"Eleven hundred were hurt"** at Xenia. The sheet's range is 1,150 to 1,300, so this sits below
   the floor. Say "about twelve hundred".
8. **"Seven times he found whole streets swept to the foundations."** Contradicts the script's own
   A1-S1, where the DePauw F5 crossed six counties and "never a town", killing six people mostly in
   farmhouses. There were no whole streets on that track.

**Items the fact sheet flags as unverified but the script states as settled:**

9. "Nobody in America had seen a violent tornado live that long." The sheet explicitly says soften
   this to "one of the longest-lived" unless a primary supports the superlative.
10. "Today it averages a quarter of an hour, and a long-track storm can give half an hour", plus
    "the average lead time was measured in seconds". The sheet marks the entire lead-time comparison
    unverified and says cut the numbers unless sourced.
11. "This is the radar at 4:45 PM", the five hooks, and "at that same moment, fifteen tornadoes were
    on the ground at once". The sheet gives no timestamp for the five hooks and none for the fifteen
    simultaneous tornadoes. Three unverified precisions are stacked into one beat and then burned
    into a graphic label.
12. "Together they killed about fifty people." The sheet calls the Tanner death split unverified and
    the roughly fifty combined figure widely cited but secondary.
13. "Twenty eight dead by the official count, thirty two by the town's own." Thirty two is Tornado
    Talk's revised research count including two later hospital deaths, not a town count.
14. "Three people died there instead of thirty." The counterfactual is invented. No source supports
    a thirty-death baseline for Sayler Park.
15. "the President wrote to him afterward to say why." The sheet records a special commendation from
    Nixon and the register itself asks to verify the document. Not naming Nixon also throws away the
    only presidential noun available.
16. "the students had been sent to the basements" at Hanover, and "some had gone dark that
    afternoon" about weather offices. Neither detail appears in the fact sheet.
17. "it would go on to explain airliner crashes." Not in the fact sheet at all.
18. "thirty one of them died" reads as 31 of the 1,700 townspeople. The sheet gives 31 total, 28 in
    the Brandenburg area.

## 8. TOP TEN FIXES, ranked by expected retention gain

1. **Plant one carried question per act.** Six stretches over 90 seconds currently run with nothing
   open, about 15 of 21 minutes. Minimum version: stop paying L1 in A1-S1. End that segment on "The
   weather office had a warning out. There was nobody underneath it to hear it, and that is the last
   time that would be true." Add a question at the top of A2 ("Thirty minutes should have been
   enough. Xenia was still the worst-hit town of the day. Why?") and at the top of A4 ("The next one
   hit the same town twice, and nobody has ever agreed on which one killed whom.").
2. **Fix the A5 stakes collapse, minutes 14 to 17.5.** Swap A5-S2 and A5-S3 so the act ends on the
   microburst, and cut A5-S3 from 125 words to about 50: keep backup power and the Doppler line, cut
   the inter-agency coordination sentence and the 2007 scale update, which is already implied.
3. **Fix the post-CTA de-escalation in A3-S3.** Do not open on the relief. Land "Two people died in
   Louisville" as the last sentence of the segment and open on the jeopardy instead: "In Louisville,
   an F4 crossed the river into a city of three hundred thousand people, and the radar could not see
   it."
4. **Compress A6 so the answer lands in the last 45 seconds.** Cut A6-S3's restatement block (the
   air mass was ordinary, the front was ordinary, the ingredients still line up) to one sentence, and
   move the Gus Allen return to just before the answer rather than after it.
5. **Hook.** Cut "This is the radar at 4:45 PM" entirely, change "in one afternoon" to "in one day",
   change "if they knew" to "if he knew". Result: 55 words, inside the rule, no false claim.
6. **Cut A4-S2's roll call from four names to two.** Keep Lindley, whose tape is the retention spike,
   and Becky Todd, the only named child deaths. Cut Haney's freight-train quote, the most common
   tornado quote in the genre, and Pennington, whose beat duplicates Todd's.
7. **Delete three of the four same-sky statements.** Keep only A6-S2's "Today the warning travels.
   The houses moved into the path instead."
8. **Convert all nine clock phrasings to digits** per the table in section 5, and either add a spoken
   time zone the first time each region appears or drop the cross-region interval arithmetic in
   A1-S2 in favour of "At 3:30 PM, in Brandenburg, Kentucky, they had fifteen minutes."
9. **Delete the four structure references.** "That is where the number in the title comes from"
   becomes "Seven times, he wrote the top of his scale." "So take the dominant question straight"
   becomes "So, could it happen again?" "the point of this whole story is" becomes "and that is the
   part that should worry you". "That is the whole answer" is cut with no replacement.
10. **Fact pass before TTS.** 400 miles becomes "about a hundred and seventy miles"; 600 miles
    becomes "about four hundred miles"; "seven words" becomes "one sentence"; "wind he called
    inconceivable" becomes "a rating he decided was inconceivable"; "the reason the day has a name"
    becomes "the tornado everyone still means when they talk about that day"; "eleven hundred"
    becomes "about twelve hundred"; the Guin duration superlative is softened; "instead of thirty"
    is cut; and "read" in Z's "I read all of them" is respelled "reed" for TTS, since that is the
    exact homograph the owner flagged on 9 Sep 26 and this script reproduces it unrespelled.

## 9. OVERALL

**Script score 6 / 10. Confidence 65 percent.** Not higher because retention is decided by the
delivered voice, the graphics and the insert audio, none of which exist yet, and because two of my
largest findings, the A3 and A5 sags, could be partly rescued by a strong visual event I cannot see.

The reporting is genuinely good and several beats are excellent: the CTA hinge into "He wrote a six",
the bus on the stage, "It went through the rescuers", and the 148-tornadoes paragraph. The first
fourteen minutes work. What keeps this off a 7 is not any single leak but the pattern. Most of the
episode runs with no question open, stakes fall off a cliff in act 5, the payoff arrives 2.5 minutes
early, and there are eight measurable factual or arithmetic errors plus nine violations of an
explicit, previously stated owner rule on clock times.

**Verdict: FIX THEN RE-GRADE.**
