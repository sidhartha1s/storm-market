In the survey photos Fujita kept finding trees knocked down in straight lines, all in the same direction, like hair that had been combed. A tornado can't do that, because a tornado turns things. Nothing in the science of nineteen seventy four explained it, so a few years later he gave it a name, the microburst. It went on to explain why airliners were falling out of the sky near thunderstorms. The country used the rest of his map too, and weather offices got backup power. The case for a radar that could see wind finally had its evidence, and the Doppler network that watches the same sky today grew out of days like this one. And the corridor the map drew across north Alabama is not farmland any more.
