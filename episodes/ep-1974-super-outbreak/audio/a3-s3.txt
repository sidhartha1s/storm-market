In Louisville, Kentucky, an F four touched down inside a city of three hundred and sixty thousand people at five thirty seven P M, Eastern time, and the radar couldn't see it. What could see it was a helicopter. Dick Gilbert reported traffic for W H A S radio, he grew up on Pennsylvania Avenue in Crescent Hill, and the tornado was on his street. He stayed. He stayed in the air, followed it, and told the whole city block by block where it was and where it was going. It tore part of the roof off Freedom Hall at the fairgrounds and wrecked the Crescent Hill water station while he talked. Two people died in Louisville, and the President sent him a special commendation for it.
