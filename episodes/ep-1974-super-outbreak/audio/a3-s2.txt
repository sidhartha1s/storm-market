The radar at the Cincinnati weather office was built in nineteen sixty, and it could see rain but it couldn't see wind. A tornado showed up only as a hook on the edge of a storm, if it showed up at all, so the office was warning a city off a shape. At four oh five P M the office put out a tornado warning for Hamilton County, and the reason it gave was hook echoes on the radar. The county stayed under that warning, and the office renewed it at four forty five P M. An hour and a half after the first one went out, the hook came through Sayler Park on the west side of Cincinnati, and four people died in that county. By then the office was looking at five hooks at once, and then, at five forty P M, the office went dark.
