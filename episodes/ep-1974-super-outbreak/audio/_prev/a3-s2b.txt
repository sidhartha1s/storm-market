The Cincinnati weather office lost power at five forty P M, in the middle of the outbreak, and with it the radar, the teletype and the incoming phone lines. For three hours the office that had been warning half of Ohio had to call Cleveland on whatever phone still worked and ask Cleveland to send its warnings out. Its own report six days later said the warnings had saved many lives, and it also recorded seven dead in its area. Somewhere in that evening, fifteen tornadoes were on the ground at the same time.
