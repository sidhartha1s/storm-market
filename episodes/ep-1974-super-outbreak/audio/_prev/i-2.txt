A hundred and forty eight tornadoes in less than a day, across thirteen states and a piece of Canada, and two thousand six hundred miles of ground ripped up end to end. Seven of those tornadoes sat at the top of a scale that was only three years old. And the man who invented that scale was about to fly over every one of those miles and doubt his own numbers. And the corridor that took the worst of it, in north Alabama, is the fastest growing city in the state today. The first F five touched down at two sixteen P M over farm country, and for the next eight hours most of the ones that followed found a town.
