April third can happen again, because the ingredients were ordinary spring weather for that part of the country arriving on the same day, and every few decades they do. In nineteen seventy four the radar saw rain, and today it sees the wind turning inside the storm before the funnel exists. Back then the warning was a radio you had to have switched on, and today it's the phone in your pocket, and it will wake you up. Brandenburg got fifteen minutes that day and most towns got nothing at all, and today the average warning runs eight to fifteen minutes everywhere. Allen Pearson's forecast was right a day early, and three hundred and thirty people still died, because a warning is only as good as the radio it plays on and the basement you can reach. Today the warning reaches you, and the houses moved into the path instead.
