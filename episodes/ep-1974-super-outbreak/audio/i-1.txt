The day before, a man named Allen Pearson picked up a telephone. He ran the Severe Storms Forecast Center, and the charts for the third of April scared him. He called every weather office in the eastern half of the country and told them to fix their radar that night and bring in extra people the next day. He was right about the day and he was right about the place. The watches went out, the warnings went out, and people still died, because in nineteen seventy four a warning was a voice on the radio, and a radio has to be switched on. Nine years earlier the Weather Bureau had found that people did not know it could forecast a tornado at all. This time the surveys found the opposite. Most people knew the storms were coming, and most of them did the right thing.
