April third can happen again, because the ingredients were ordinary spring weather for that part of the country arriving on the same day. In nineteen seventy four the radar saw rain, and today it sees the wind turning inside the storm before the funnel exists. Back then the warning was a radio you had to have switched on, and today a tornado warning sets off the phone in your pocket, with a tone and a buzz that most phones will play even on silent. Brandenburg got fifteen minutes that day and most towns got nothing at all, and today the average tornado warning gives you about thirteen minutes. Allen Pearson's forecast was right a day early, and three hundred and thirty people still died, because a warning is only as good as the radio it plays on and the basement you can reach. Today the warning reaches you, and the houses moved into the path instead.
