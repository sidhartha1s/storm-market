## Description
https://paulraymond.online
📘 The book mentioned at 3:17 — The Storm-Ready Home

Right now, a tropical depression is spinning up near Guam, and Japan's biggest holiday in eleven years starts in three
days. Japan's Meteorological Agency expects the system to earn a name, Dujuan, within twelve hours. Its own longest
forecast keeps the storm moving past Japan's main islands during the same five days millions of Japanese travelers take
their first full Silver Week since 2015. Two outcomes are still open tonight: one track curves the storm into Kanto, the
region that holds Tokyo; the other slides it by, offshore, weakened but still felt.

This update covers: where JMA and the US Navy's Joint Typhoon Warning Center agree and differ on pressure, wind, and
motion; Japan's four-checkpoint forecast track through next Monday; the JTWC's stronger peak forecast and the two branches
still on the table; why this storm's timing collides with Japan's first full five-day Silver Week in eleven years and the
2026 Asian Games in Nagoya; what it means for Americans traveling in Japan and for the roughly 55,000 US active-duty
service members based at Yokosuka and Yokota; how Japan's ten-minute wind standard compares to the US one-minute
standard; and the two things still genuinely open tonight: this storm's track, and its strength.
