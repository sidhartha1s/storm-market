**Iconography of History.** First 30 s (paraphrase): Thanksgiving 1950, families at dinner, atmosphere "assembling something never seen before"; by night temperatures collapse, wind screams through mountain towns, snow in feet; roofs collapse, rivers rise, power fails, crops freeze. Lead claim: "a chain reaction. Snow, ice, wind, and flood", at least 160 deaths, billions in damage, changed how the nation saw weather. One story or two hazards: one storm, but a list of four hazards, Appalachian snow first; the coast gets one line later ("In New England, gusts topped 100 mph"), plus hurricane-force winds and surges "in the tens of feet". Not framed as a contrast of two places. Errors noticed: viewers say several photos are 1960s (cars); the uploader admits in a reply the pictures came from "wikki free images" and may be wrong. A subscriber ask sits inside the first 90 s.

**Noles Explores & Explains.** First 30 s: Thursday 23 Nov 1950, 100 million Americans east of the Mississippi at turkey dinner; unusual intensity and oddity brewing; "the most expensive [storm] in American history"; record ice, snow, wind and rainfall; "series of unintended consequences"; he is Nolles, here to "explore and explain". Lead claim: most consequential storm of the century, changed forecasting. One story or two: one storm, told as a chronological build, then region by region. The wind section is real and numbers-led: Hartford 100 mph gusts, NYC 94 mph, Allentown 88, Syracuse 90, Lock Haven 77, Mount Washington 160 ("as high as the meter went"), 44 dead in the NYC metro. So coast wind is covered, as one chapter of a long tour, not the lead. Errors noticed: none certain. Mount Washington 160 mph agrees with the NOAA NCEI article (below). Viewers dispute only the "Appalachia" label.

**Vintage America.** First 30 s: Friday after Thanksgiving 1950, sky over the Carolinas; no satellite, no model, a forecaster with a hand-drawn map; "storm of the century", 6 days; snow drifts 25 ft deep, "wind gusts of 160 mph", a million homes without power, 160+ deaths. Lead claim: the numbers did not look like anything in the record books. Closest to our angle: wind and snow both in the first 30 s, and later "These were not blizzard winds. These were hurricane force winds" with NYC 94, Newark 108, Bear Mountain 140, Mount Washington 160. It still treats 160 mph as a storm-wide figure (it is one mountaintop). Errors noticed: the 160 mph claim in the lead; Bear Mountain 140 not checked against any source; a viewer notes a 1950 opening line over a 1955 Ford image. Tiny audience (3,588 views, 8 comments), so weak proof of demand.

**Gap across all three:** none opens with two hazards at once as the contrast. Iconography leads with inland snow, Noles with the overview, Vintage with 25 ft drifts plus 160 mph (one hazard list). Our "108 mph on the coast, record snow and cold inland, at the same time" is open. Newark 108 appears only in Vintage America (no source stated).

Two unverified leads: a Noles comment says traffic stalled on US 25 in Kentucky because of a Knoxville football game and people slept in strangers' homes (2 likes); another says the Altoona ice storm left power out up to 2 weeks (1 like).

## Ruled out as lead

- "Chain reaction: snow, ice, wind, flood" (Iconography): a list with no contrast, already the rival's line.
- "Most expensive storm in US history" (Noles): superlative that needs a cost definition; not in a source checked here.
- "160 mph gusts" as headline (Vintage): one mountaintop station that Noles says maxed the meter, not a place anyone lived; copies the rival's number. Use Newark 108 only once an official source for it is in hand (Vintage names none).
- "Storm of the century" / "forgotten disaster": no viewer in 292 comments uses either; one says "never heard of this storm until now", a hint but a single comment.
