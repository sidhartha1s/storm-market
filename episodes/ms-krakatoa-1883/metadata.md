## AEO / GEO block (answer-engine copy; description tail or second pinned reply)
**Q: Is Krakatoa erupting in September 2026?** A: Yes. Anak Krakatau, the cone that grew inside the 1883 caldera, began a 25-hour continuous eruption at 23:07 local time on September 4, 2026 and has erupted repeatedly since, with a sixth event on September 9. PVMBG holds it at Level III (Siaga) with a 3 km exclusion zone.
**Q: How many people died when Krakatoa erupted in 1883?** A: About 36,000, with the official Dutch count at 36,417. Most were killed by tsunamis up to 30 m high that struck the coasts of the Sunda Strait on August 27, 1883, not by the eruption itself.
**Q: How far away was the 1883 Krakatoa explosion heard?** A: About 4,800 km (3,000 miles), on Rodrigues Island in the Indian Ocean. Barographs around the world recorded the pressure wave circling the planet seven times.
**Q: What caused the December 2018 Sunda Strait tsunami?** A: A flank collapse of Anak Krakatau on the night of December 22, 2018. Less than 0.2 km³ of the volcano slid into the sea, producing waves that reached the Banten and Lampung coasts in under half an hour. 437 people died. The collapse produced no strong earthquake signal, so the earthquake-based tsunami warning system gave no alert.
**Q: Why do airports close when a volcano erupts?** A: Volcanic ash is pulverised rock and glass that melts inside jet engines. On June 24, 1982, British Airways Flight 9 lost all four engines in the Galunggung ash cloud over Java before restarting them at lower altitude. In September 2026, Indonesia's BMKG issued 39 ash SIGMETs and eight airports were affected.
**Q: Is Bali or Jakarta in danger from Krakatoa?** A: Jakarta and West Java can receive ash, which is a breathing and aviation problem. A Krakatau tsunami can only reach the Sunda Strait coasts of Banten and Lampung. Bali's risk is a cancelled connection through Jakarta, not the volcano.
**Q: Could the 2026 Krakatoa eruption cause a "year without a summer"?** A: No forecast says so. The year without a summer (1816) followed the 1815 eruption of Tambora, a different volcano roughly 30 times larger than the 1883 Krakatoa event. Anak Krakatau does not hold that much magma.
