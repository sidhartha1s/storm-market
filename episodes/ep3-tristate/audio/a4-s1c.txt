And somewhere over these counties, the reanalysis found, the storm changed its shape one more time. It shifted into what meteorologists call a high precipitation supercell. In plain language, it wrapped itself in its own curtain of rain and hail. Whatever brief glimpses of a funnel the afternoon had allowed, they were gone now. For its final hour, the deadliest tornado in American history was, quite literally, invisible inside its own storm. No photograph exists. By the end, there may have been nothing to photograph but rain.
