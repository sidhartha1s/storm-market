Change came slowly, and then all at once. In the late nineteen thirties the Weather Bureau finally began easing the rule. In March nineteen forty-eight, two Air Force officers in Oklahoma issued the first successful tornado forecast in history. By nineteen fifty-two, the government was issuing public tornado outlooks. Then came radar, spotter networks, and the warning system we carry in our pockets today. A century ago, this storm killed six hundred ninety-five people who never heard it coming. Today, the same storm would still destroy the towns. But the people would know it was coming.
