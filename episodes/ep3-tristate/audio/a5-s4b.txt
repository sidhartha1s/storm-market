And then, for a long time, the country largely forgot. There was no famous name for this storm, no single city to anchor the memory, just a line of small towns that rebuilt quietly and carried it privately. But in those towns, it never faded. A century later, the descendants of survivors still tell the stories the way they were handed down. The sound like a thousand freight trains. The sky that turned black at midday. In southern Illinois, history still divides at March eighteenth. Before the storm, and after.
