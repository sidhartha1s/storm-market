# ep-joplin-2011 · structure.md (draft 1, 15 Oct 26; the owner reads it on the review page with the hook and intro)

**Title (owner, verbatim):** Joplin 2011: The Sirens Did Not Lie

**Claim (locked, copied from `angle.md`):** In Joplin the tornado found people indoors, in buildings with nothing below them: 135 of the 155 who lost their lives to its impact were inside a structure, and not one person the investigators could place was below ground. The town believed its sirens went off all the time; the records say about once a year.

**Dominant question:** the viewer keeps watching to find out why the people of Joplin, most of them indoors and many doing what they were told, had nothing below them, and how a town whose sirens sounded about once a year came to hear them as noise.

**Architecture:** detective, with a countdown inside it. The NIST investigators are the detectives and one master map of Joplin is the case board: the film walks it location by location (homes, stores and churches, the hospital and the nursing homes), each stop adding dots to the 135. Act 2 runs as a countdown, five oh nine to five thirty eight. The sirens come first so the viewer meets "once a year" before the 135.

**Runtime plan:** 22:00 (planned; the owner has not set it, it is a question on the review page). `word_budget.py` resizes it after the voice probe.

### A1 · Sirens: belief against record (1:45 to 4:45)
- **Purpose:** prove the belief and the record side by side, and keep the two false-alarm numbers apart.
- **Question:** where did "all the time" come from, if the record says about once a year?
- **Reveal:** the record: weekly one-minute Monday test (about fifty two a year) plus about one real sounding a year since 2007, three of them dated (S4, S6, S7). The false alarms were real, in a different set: 14 of 18 tornado warnings from 2005 to 2011 had no verified tornado, in line with the national 74.7 percent (S10, S12), and 11 of 12 in the last five years (S11, S13). People heard warnings and tests and filed them all under "sirens" (S28). Plus the belief in a "protective bubble" (S23, S36, S37, S38).
- **Visual event:** [RS] a two-row calendar 2007 to 2011: fifty two grey test ticks a year against single red marks (18 Jan 2007, 10 May 2008, 8 May 2009, then 22 May 2011). A second, separate strip for the 18 warnings, 4 verified.
- **Exit hook:** "On May twenty second the sirens sounded twice, and the people who heard the second one did not agree on what it meant."
- **Rows:** S1 to S8, S10 to S19, S23, S24, S28, S36 to S38.
- **Comments answered:** RODALCO2007 (callback, the hook's comment); "The sad part is nobody knew what really was coming that day." (824 likes).

### A2 · The evening, five oh nine to five thirty eight (4:45 to 7:45)
- **Purpose:** a countdown that shows the first siren sounded for a storm that never made a tornado, and the second came as the tornado entered the city.
- **Question:** what did the second siren mean to the people who heard it?
- **Reveal:** 5:09 warning for a different storm (E4, E5); 5:11 first siren, mainly for funnel reports in Kansas (E6, E8), heard as meant for the north (E8b); 5:17 the city-wide warning, no new siren (E9, E10); media still saying the storms were passing north (E12); rotation from 5:29 to touchdown at 5:34 (E25, E13); 5:38 second siren, four minutes after touchdown (E14, E15). Lead time: seventeen minutes to touchdown from the 5:17 warning, twenty three from the first siren (E21, E22). People read the second siren as worse, or as an all clear (S25, S26); the city had no all-clear signal (S33). For most it was the non-routine signal that sent them to shelter (E19, E37).
- **Visual event:** [RS] a clock running down the master map: polygon 30 in the north, polygon 31 over the city, the siren rings at 5:11 and 5:38, the touchdown dot at 5:34. NOAA radar image at 5:34 [verify: which NOAA radar frame is public domain and timed].

### A3 · Homes with nothing underneath (7:45 to 11:15; book ad inside)
- **Purpose:** the first and largest group on the map: 71 in houses and apartments, in a city where 82 percent of homes had no basement and no public shelter existed.
- **Question:** what was under the best place they had?
- **Reveal:** 82 percent of homes had no basement, no community shelter or safe room in city or county (H1, H3, H4). 59 in single-family homes, 54 of them in homes rated demolished, 50 in one-story homes (H9 to H11); 12 in apartments, all above ground (H19). After the ad: only 3 of the 71 had a basement they could reach, and at least two of the three were not in it when the storm hit (H22, H8). Survivors in the EF-4 zone: 11 of 27 in basements, 15 in interior rooms (H13, H14). A neighborhood of early-1980s ranch homes with basements: heavy damage, nobody lost (H15).
- **Visual event:** [RS] the 71 dots light on the map in house outlines; a cross-section of a one-story house with an empty space where a basement would be. Counter visit 1: 71 of 135, below ground 0.
- **Exit hook:** "The big stores had something the homes did not: a refuge area and staff who knew where it was."
- **Rows:** H1 to H24, H26, H27 (base notes).
- **Comments answered:** "most people that took shelter in their homes" (1,100 likes); "my step dad went to the center most closet in the house, threw everything out into the hallway" (1,600 likes); "How does anyone survive this?" (431 likes).

### A4 · Stores, churches, restaurants (11:15 to 14:45)

Walmart: fifty to sixty in the refuge area, the wall came in onto it, a D-shaped pocket (B5, B6, B10), three lost, location uncertain (B7). ### A6 · What changed and what did not (18:15 to 21:00)

Adopted: tornado loads in ASCE 7-22 (R12); ICC 500 shelters required in new schools and emergency facilities in parts of 22 states (R13, R14, R15).
