# ep-joplin-2011 · structure.md (draft 1, 15 Oct 26; the owner reads it on the review page with the hook and intro)

**Title (owner, verbatim):** Joplin 2011: The Sirens Did Not Lie

**Claim (locked, copied from `angle.md`):** In Joplin the tornado found people indoors, in buildings with nothing below them: 135 of the 155 who lost their lives to its impact were inside a structure, and not one person the investigators could place was below ground. The town believed its sirens went off all the time; the records say about once a year.

**Dominant question:** the viewer keeps watching to find out why the people of Joplin, most of them indoors and many doing what they were told, had nothing below them, and how a town whose sirens sounded about once a year came to hear them as noise.

**Architecture:** detective, with a countdown inside it. The NIST investigators are the detectives and one master map of Joplin is the case board: the film walks it location by location (homes, stores and churches, the hospital and the nursing homes), each stop adding dots to the 135. Act 2 runs as a countdown, five oh nine to five thirty eight. The sirens come first so the viewer meets "once a year" before the 135.

**Runtime plan:** 22:00 (planned; the owner has not set it, it is a question on the review page). `word_budget.py` resizes it after the voice probe.

## Chapter cards

### H1 + T1 + I1 · Hook, tail, intro (0:00 to 1:45)
- **Purpose:** Paul, as an old friend, asks the viewer what they'd do when the siren goes off, agrees with their answer (get inside), then breaks it twice (a hundred thirty-five were indoors; none below ground the investigators could find); the tail breaks a third belief (the sirens did go off, twice), and the intro breaks the fourth (about once a year outside the weekly test, against "all the time"), with a question about every 20 s.
- **Question:** if the sirens were rare, why did a town hear them as noise, and why was nobody the tornado took below ground?
- **Reveal:** once a year at most outside the weekly test, NIST from the county emergency manager (S2, S4, S5); most of the 161 inside a building, then 135 of the 155 inside a structure and zero the investigators could place below ground (T4, T3, H5, H6); 82 percent of homes had no basement (H1).
- **Visual event:** Paul full frame, no card; the master map with 135 dots inside building outlines, 19 outside (12 in vehicles), 1 not placed (location unknown), and an empty "below ground" row.
- **Exit hook:** "So if those sirens hardly ever went off for real, why\'d so many people think they never stopped?" answered with "After the storm, the NWS interviewed more than sixty survivors, and plenty of them talked about the sirens."

### A1 · Sirens: belief against record (1:45 to 4:45)
- **Purpose:** prove the belief and the record side by side, and keep the two false-alarm numbers apart.
- **Question:** where did "all the time" come from, if the record says about once a year?
- **Reveal:** the record: weekly one-minute Monday test (about fifty two a year) plus about one real sounding a year since 2007, three of them dated (S4, S6, S7). The false alarms were real, in a different set: 14 of 18 tornado warnings from 2005 to 2011 had no verified tornado, in line with the national 74.7 percent (S10, S12), and 11 of 12 in the last five years (S11, S13). People heard warnings and tests and filed them all under "sirens" (S28). Plus the belief in a "protective bubble" (S23, S36, S37, S38).
- **Visual event:** [RS] a two-row calendar 2007 to 2011: fifty two grey test ticks a year against single red marks (18 Jan 2007, 10 May 2008, 8 May 2009, then 22 May 2011). A second, separate strip for the 18 warnings, 4 verified.
- **Exit hook:** "The second time Joplin's tornado sirens went off that evening, people couldn't agree on what it meant."
- **Rows:** S1 to S8, S10 to S19, S23, S24, S28, S36 to S38.
- **Comments answered:** RODALCO2007 ("no one is paying any attention to the sirens going off", 3,000 likes; its first and only card, at A1-S3); "The sad part is nobody knew what really was coming that day." (824 likes).

### A2 · The evening, five oh nine to five thirty eight (4:45 to 7:45)
- **Purpose:** a countdown that shows the first siren sounded for a storm that never made a tornado, and the second came as the tornado entered the city.
- **Question:** what did the second siren mean to the people who heard it?
- **Reveal:** 5:09 warning for a different storm (E4, E5); 5:11 first siren, mainly for funnel reports in Kansas (E6, E8), heard as meant for the north (E8b); 5:17 the city-wide warning, no new siren (E9, E10); media still saying the storms were passing north (E12); rotation from 5:29 to touchdown at 5:34 (E25, E13); 5:38 second siren, four minutes after touchdown (E14, E15). Lead time: seventeen minutes to touchdown from the 5:17 warning, twenty three from the first siren (E21, E22). People read the second siren as worse, or as an all clear (S25, S26); the city had no all-clear signal (S33). For most it was the non-routine signal that sent them to shelter (E19, E37).
- **Visual event:** [RS] a clock running down the master map: polygon 30 in the north, polygon 31 over the city, the siren rings at 5:11 and 5:38, the touchdown dot at 5:34. Radar: NWS Assessment Appendix A, Figure 4 panel (G): KSGF 0.5 degree base reflectivity, valid 22 May 2011 22:34Z (5:34 pm CDT), credited "NOAA/NWS", no NWS logo and no third-party basemap; panel (H) storm-relative velocity at the same time if wanted; never NIST Fig. 2-7 as "NOAA" (factcheck-acts, A2 radar CONFIRMED).
- **Exit hook:** "At five thirty-eight, most people went for the best spot they had. The investigators went back later and looked at what was underneath that spot."

### A3 · Homes with nothing underneath (7:45 to 11:15; book ad inside)
- **Purpose:** the first and largest group on the map: 71 in houses and apartments, in a city where 82 percent of homes had no basement and no public shelter existed.
- **Question:** what was under the best place they had?
- **Reveal:** 82 percent of homes had no basement, no community shelter or safe room in city or county (H1, H3, H4). 59 in single-family homes, 54 of them in homes rated demolished, 50 in one-story homes (H9 to H11); 12 in apartments, all above ground (H19). After the ad: only 3 of the 71 had a basement they could reach, and at least two of the three were not in it when the storm hit (H22, H8). Survivors in the EF-4 zone: 11 of 27 in basements, 15 in interior rooms (H13, H14). A neighborhood of early-1980s ranch homes with basements: heavy damage, nobody lost (H15).
- **Visual event:** [RS] the 71 dots light on the map in house outlines; a cross-section of a one-story house with an empty space where a basement would be. Counter visit 1: 71 of 135, below ground 0.
- **Exit hook:** "Most of the big stores had something the homes didn't: a spot set aside for cover, and staff who knew where it was."
- **Rows:** H1 to H24, H26, H27 (base notes).
- **Comments answered:** "most people that took shelter in their homes" (1,100 likes); "my step dad went to the center most closet in the house, threw everything out into the hallway" (1,600 likes); "How does anyone survive this?" (431 likes).

### A4 · Stores, churches, restaurants (11:15 to 14:45)
- **Purpose:** the refuge areas that existed, and why they did not hold; plus the one cellar that did.
- **Question:** did the refuge areas protect the people inside them?
- **Reveal:** NIST found no evidence the designated refuge areas helped (B3, B4). Walmart: fifty to sixty in the refuge area, the wall came in onto it, a D-shaped pocket (B5, B6, B10), three lost, location uncertain (B7). Home Depot: thirty five in the training room survived as the panels fell outward; eight lost there, and survivors' accounts put seven of them inside, walking along the front wall (B11, B12). Pizza Hut cooler, five lost; some discussed whether the second siren meant all clear (B18, B19). Dillon's cooler: thirty five, all survived (B20). Stained Glass Theater: the only basement among the eight buildings, one person wide at the stairs (B26 to B31). Two churches, seven lost (B36 to B39). Then Macadoodles: twelve people twenty feet down in a concrete wine cellar, all came through (B33 to B35, B49). The zero was never about everyone below ground.
- **Visual event:** [RS] NIST ground photographs of the Walmart and Home Depot; the D-shaped pocket drawn over the Walmart floor plan; Macadoodles cellar as a cross-section, twelve figures below the line. Counter visit 2: 102 of 135, below ground 0.
- **Exit hook:** "One building in the path was full of patients, some of whom needed help to move, and that evening its plan started when a nurse heard a tornado siren."
- **Rows:** B1 to B50 (B39 used for churches, per CONFLICT; Home Depot 8 with its base, per CONFLICT), W14, W15, X16, X17, B48.

### A5 · St. John's, Greenbriar and the age gap (14:45 to 18:15)
- **Purpose:** the buildings full of people who needed help to move, and the finding no competitor tells: older people lost their lives at four times the rate.
- **Question:** why did buildings that stood, or had a plan, still lose people, and who were they?
- **Reveal:** St. John's towers stood but did not protect everyone: 12 impact-related, 4 in intensive care (C1), with 14 as the all-causes count (C2, per CONFLICT, each with its base). Condition Gray started when the head nurse verified sirens; she likely heard only the second round (C10, C11). Greenbriar: the center of the tornado passed over it, 19 of 95 (C17), plan to move residents to inner hallways (C19), people in the hallway buried (C20). Meadows: two, in an EF-1 wind zone (C25, C26, C27). Then Finding 47: about 8 per thousand aged 60 and over against 2 per thousand under 60, and it holds after removing every hospital and nursing home death (C29, C30).

### A6 · What changed and what did not (18:15 to 21:00)
- **Purpose:** what NIST asked for, what the national code bodies adopted, and what the supplied records do not show.
- **Question:** would a store, an apartment block or a nursing home in the path today have something below or around its people?
- **Reveal:** NIST's sixteen recommendations (R1), Recommendation 7, shelters in multi-family, mercantile, school and assembly buildings (R3, R4). Adopted: tornado loads in ASCE 7-22 (R12); ICC 500 shelters required for new schools and emergency response buildings in the highest tornado zone, Section 423 since the 2015 IBC (FEMA Recovery Advisory 1, Feb 2023; NIST news, Jul 2024), with the 2018 school-campus change (R13, R14, R15); FEMA and NIST list nothing for new stores or apartments (said as "the sources do not show", never "no code requires it"). Joplin's school district, not the city, has built 14 safe rooms since 2011 (FEMA case study, updated 30 Jul 2024). Mercy says its new hospital has a window system designed to withstand 250 mph winds (mercy.net). No national siren standard as of NIST TN 2008 (Aug 2018) and the NWS siren FAQ; in 2011 there was none, and no national all-clear protocol (S34, R24). The NWS asked for a non-routine warning and fewer perceived false alarms (R21 to R23).
- **Visual event:** a recommendation board: R7, R8, R13 ticked where adopted, open where the sources say nothing.
- **Exit hook:** "The rule that took hold covers new schools and emergency buildings. But on May twenty-second, 2011, Joplin High School and East Middle School were empty." (A6-S7; R13, B40, B42; the 135-dot map carries the rest; no mirrored callback)
- **Rows:** R1 to R26, S34.
- **Comments answered:** "By the way, Joplin is thriving as of now. They're rebuilding prettier buildings, preparing more public tornado shelters..." (3,600 likes); "I wonder why commercial buildings and residential homes like at 15:15 don't have mandatory shelters" (28 likes); "Asked a nurse why the windows looked a little weird and she told me they were rated for 250mph winds" (482 likes).
