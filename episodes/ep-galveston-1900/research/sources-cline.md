## 3. Cline's own Sept 1900 report (as reproduced by NWS Heritage; MWR original page itself not fetched)

- What he says he did Sept 8 morning | CONFIRMED | "I drove to the Gulf, where I timed the swells, and then proceeded to the office." NOTE: report says drove to the Gulf, not a ride along the beach warning people.
- Warning the public | CONFIRMED | "The public was warned, over the telephone and verbally." and "I went down on Strand street and advised some wholesale commission merchants" (the second is Cline's own named action; the first is passive voice)
- Warnings from Washington | CONFIRMED | "Warning messages were received from the Central Office at Washington on September 4, 5, 6, 7, and 8." and "with storm warning flying" on the morning of the 8th.

## 6. Flags/warnings 7-8 Sept
- Washington put Galveston under "storm warning" on 7 Sept | CONFIRMED | https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/galveston-hurricane-of-1900 | Central office "placed Galveston under a 'storm warning' on September 7" and city had been aware of storm since Sept 4.
- Cline raised hurricane flags on 7 Sept, without permission | CONFIRMED (NOAA Heritage) but contradicted | https://vlab.noaa.gov/web/nws-heritage/-/isaac-monroe-cline-the-cyclone-pioneer | "On September 7, 1900 ... raised the hurricane flags without permission, going against Weather Bureau protocol." Contradicted by Cline's own report (item 3), which says only "storm warning flying" and warning messages from Washington Sept 4-8; and by Wikipedia (noon 8 Sept). Flag date is CONFLICTING; his own report shows no self-ordered hurricane flags.
- Washington's forecast change | CONFIRMED | https://origins.osu.edu/read/galveston-1900-hurricane-storm | Officials did not change the forecast until midday Friday, "and that warning still called the storm a moderate one, not a hurricane." (summary)
- Bureau blocked Cuban reports; local offices needed central approval | CONFIRMED (Wikipedia) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1900_Galveston_hurricane | "Bureau director Willis Luther Moore had blocked telegraph reports from Cuban meteorologists" (angle 1, not this strand)

## Conflicts list (both numbers)
1. Flag date: 7 Sept (NOAA Heritage Cline page, snippet NOAA 200th) vs 8 Sept noon (Wikipedia) vs Cline's own report (storm warning flying, no hurricane-flag order claimed).
2. Beach warning: TSHA states it as fact vs Wikipedia/Larson say uncorroborated; own report says he "drove to the Gulf" and warned merchants on Strand street.
3. Surge 8-15 ft (NHC) vs 15.7 ft (NPS, TSHA) vs ~20 ft tide at his house (Cline).
4. Elevation 8.7 ft (NPS) vs "just under nine" (OSU) vs ~8 ft ("podcast", snippet) vs 5.2 ft at Cline's house.
5. Wind 145 (Wikipedia) vs ~140 (NPS) vs 150 (NPR snippet); last recorded sustained 84 mph.
6. Article year 1891 (scan metadata) vs 1892 (Texas Historical Foundation snippet).
7. Telegram wording: "Gulf rising, water covers streets of about half of city." (report) vs brief's "Gulf rising rapidly; half the city now under water" (UNSOURCED).
