## 1. The board of engineers
- Board of three: "Brigadier General Henry Martyn Robert, Alfred Noble, and H. C. Ripley, drafted plans for the Seawall and grade raising." Board sat "From September 1901 to January 1902". Rosenberg Library, fetched. https://www.rosenberg-library-museum.org/treasures/galvestons-seawall
- "A three-member board of engineers" (Henry Martyn Robert, Alfred Noble, President ASCE 1903, Henry Clay Ripley); report presented "on January 25, 1902"; recommended a seawall "rising 17 feet above mean low tide and stretching over 3 miles in length". ASCE landmark page, fetched. https://www.asce.org/about-civil-engineering/history-and-heritage/historic-landmarks/galveston-seawall-and-grade-raising
- Robert is the author of Robert's Rules of Order: NOT fetched this session; general knowledge only. Not used in the script.

## 2. First section of the seawall (1902 to 1904)
- "Building of the initial segment commenced in September 1902." Rosenberg Library, fetched (same URL as §1).
- "Built between 1902 and 1904"; Galveston County contracted J.M. O'Rourke and Company of Denver for "a 17,593-foot seawall". ASCE, fetched.
- "When completed, this first section was 3.3 miles long, 17 feet high, and measured 16 feet wide at the base and 5 feet wide on top." Rosenberg Library, fetched.
- "16 feet wide on the base at elevation 1 foot above mean low water", "5 feet wide on top at elevation 17 feet above mean low water", "40,000 pounds per foot of length"; granite riprap "extending 27 ft outward from the toe". ASCE, fetched.
- Funded by bonds (campaign button in the museum collection). Rosenberg Library, fetched.
- Finished July 1904: search-result summary only (galveston.com), NOT fetched. Rosenberg: "This July, Galveston celebrated the 120th anniversary of the Seawall" (article dated 2024, consistent with July 1904, inference).
- Today: "54,790 feet", "10 continuous miles" of sidewalk; five sections, "the last section being completed in 1962"; National Register 1977; National Historic Civil Engineering Landmark 2001. Rosenberg Library, fetched. ASCE: "extends over 10 miles".

## 3. The grade raising
- "from 1903-1911"; "quarter-mile-square sections" each enclosed by a dike; "Around 2,000 buildings were raised on hand-turned jackscrews"; sand through "a 20-foot deep, 200-foot wide, and 2.5 mile long canal using four self-loading hopper dredges"; "About 500 city blocks were raised using 16.3 million cubic yards of sand spread from a few inches to eleven feet thick." ASCE, fetched.
- "sand pumped from the Gulf floor raised the grade as much as seventeen feet"; "required advance raising of 2,146 buildings"; "The largest building raised was a 3,000-ton church"; "boosted five feet off the ground with jacks, then fill was pumped underneath; church services were held on schedule." TSHA Handbook, Galveston Hurricane of 1900, fetched. https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/galveston-hurricane-of-1900
- TSHA also: "Construction began on a six-mile-long seawall standing seventeen feet above mean low tide" (six miles conflicts with 3.3 miles for the first section; see Conflicts).
- NOAA AOML 2015: city decided "to build a sea wall ... but also to raise the entire city by 17 ft (5 m). Intact buildings were hoisted up by jacks and dredged sand was piled underneath". Fetched (curl, archived copy). https://www.aoml.noaa.gov/hurricane_blog/115th-anniversary-of-the-great-galveston-hurricane/

## 4. The 1915 test
- NWS Houston/Galveston, Upper Texas Coast Tropical Cyclones in the 1910s, fetched (curl): "made landfall 30 miles southwest of Galveston with 'fearful violence.' 275 lives were lost even though the Weather Bureau had issued warnings 24 hours in advance. This was the first real test of the Galveston seawall, which had been built after the devastating 1900 storm and proved invaluable for protection of the city." "storm surge reached 16.2' with wave crests of 21'". "The storm surge flooded parts of the city for 40 hours". "The seawall's only damage was two small chips near 39th street where the four-masted schooner 'Crockett' was blown over the wall"; "the schooner was pounded to pieces on top of the wall". Embankment eroded and pavement on top destroyed from 6th to 18th Street; "Several houses and buildings near the seawall were undermined and destroyed." Before the storm the beach extended up to 300 ft in front of the wall; "afterwards, it was completely gone." https://www.weather.gov/hgx/hurricanes_climatology_1910s
- Galveston and Texas History Center (Rosenberg Library), fetched: "The 1915 Storm gave the Seawall its first real test."; "The hurricane caused 12 deaths and an estimated $5-8 million in property damage in the city." "inundated Galveston with waters that reached a depth of 6 to 9 feet." https://www.galvestonhistorycenter.org/research/1915-storm
- Rosenberg Library: tested by "the hurricane of September 21, 1909, and again with the hurricane of August 16, 1915"; "It withstood both, though some repairs needed to be made after the 1915 storm." Fetched.
