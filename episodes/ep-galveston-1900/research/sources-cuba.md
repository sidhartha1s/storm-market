## Bottom line on "blocked their warnings"
- Holds in softened form. NOAA AOML says the Bureau "decreed that only its forecasts would be carried over the telegraph" and Belen said Gulf, Bureau said recurve. No primary order text was found; no date for the decree is fixed.
- Needs tightening: (a) "Cuban forecasters predicted Galveston/Texas" is secondary only; best-supported primary-adjacent claim is "into the Gulf, not recurve" (NOAA AOML). (b) "no warning" is false: Bureau hoisted Gulf storm warnings from 5 Sep (New Orleans) and told Galveston on 7 Sep. (c) Ban date unresolved: 1898 (Rubiera) vs 1900 (Larson letter), see Conflicts.

## Conflicts (explicit)
1. Date of the ban: 1898 (Rubiera: Belen barred from public forecasts when the Bureau opened Havana office; AOML: "following the Spanish-American War") vs. Aug 1900 (Moore letter to Eckert "On August 28" per Larson via Econlib) vs. archived "Moore to Eckert, Sept. 20, 1900" (Pietruska fn 86). Unresolved. Also Moore and Gangoiti exchanged letters in Nov-Dec 1898 and Aug 1900, so contact was not fully cut.
2. Scope: NOAA says the decree covered Bureau-only forecasts "over the telegraph"; Roker says War Department banned all weather messages on Cuban government lines plus a Havana-New Orleans cut. Only the NOAA version has an agency source.
3. Texas claim: NOAA says "northwest and into the Gulf"; Wikipedia/Larson-family say Texas/San Antonio; Rubiera says "Gulf ... threat to North American coast". Only Gulf is supported by NOAA.
4. "No warning": Spectrum/Roker imply none; MWR shows Gulf storm warnings from 5 Sep and Galveston told 4-8 Sep (Bureau-reported, self-interested). Cline's later claim of riding the beach is disputed (NOAA blog says so).
5. Wikipedia and Spectrum say Moore blocked telegraph reports "due to tensions after the Spanish-American War"; neither cites an order.
