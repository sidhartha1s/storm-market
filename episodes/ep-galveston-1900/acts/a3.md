## A3 · The turn that never came
Chapter card: Purpose · show why Washington's recurve call was a reasonable bet that failed the way Havana said it would, and that the warnings worked at sea but met a calm, confident island.
Chapter card: Question · if Galveston had its warning a day and a half ahead, why did it still sit tight on Friday night?
Chapter card: Reveal · nothing to the north came down to turn the storm (the Bureau's own September summary), it went on across the Gulf the way Belen called it, and Friday on the island showed none of the signs Cline was watching for, only a swell and a tide rising against the wind.
Chapter card: Visual event · self-made steering graphic (Atlantic high, westerly dips, the hook that never came); Captain Halsey's barometer falling on screen; the swell rolling in on a calm-sky Galveston beach.
Chapter card: Exit hook · Paul (P4): at half past three on Saturday, Isaac Cline wrote one line into the special observation for Washington; the telegraph wires were down, so Joseph phoned it to Western Union at Houston (cta-hinge).

Source line: National Hurricane Center. [VE] [RS] [loop L-A3 open] (if storms usually turn, what kept this one going west?)
> Washington's call had a real reason behind it. A hurricane doesn't steer itself. It drifts around the edge of a big dome of high pressure out over the Atlantic, and once it gets far enough north, a dip in the westerly winds usually catches it and swings it off to the northeast. The National Hurricane Center still calls that recurving. That turn was the whole bet, and Galveston only stayed safe if it came.
