## A3 · The turn that never came
Chapter card: Purpose · show why Washington's recurve call was a reasonable bet that failed the way Havana said it would, and that the warnings worked at sea but met a calm, confident island.
Chapter card: Question · if Galveston had its warning a day and a half ahead, why did it still sit tight on Friday night?
Chapter card: Reveal · the lows that month stayed mostly to the north (the Bureau's own September summary, which does not say that is why the storm held its course), it went on across the Gulf the way Belen called it, and Friday on the island showed none of the signs Cline was watching for, only a swell and a tide rising against the wind.
Chapter card: Visual event · self-made steering graphic (Atlantic high, westerly dips, the hook that never came); Captain Halsey's barometer falling on screen; the swell rolling in on a calm-sky Galveston beach.
Chapter card: Exit hook · Paul (P4): at three thirty on Saturday afternoon, Isaac Cline wrote one line into the special observation for Washington (said as three thirty on Saturday afternoon); Joseph found the telegraph wires down and phoned it to Western Union at Houston (cta-hinge).

### A3-S1 — narration
**Picture:** self-made steering graphic: a broad dome of high pressure over the Atlantic drawn as a soft orange ridge, a storm dot riding its southern and western edge; a dip in the westerly winds swings down from the north, catches the dot and flings it northeast; lower-third "NATIONAL HURRICANE CENTER"; chip "RECURVE"; last beat, a pin on Galveston stays dark as the dot curves away. Source line: National Hurricane Center. [VE] [RS] [loop L-A3 open] (which road did this storm take, Washington's or Havana's?)
> Washington's call had a real reason behind it. A hurricane doesn't steer itself. It drifts around the edge of a big dome of high pressure out over the Atlantic. Once it gets far enough north, a dip in the westerly winds usually catches it and swings it off to the northeast. The National Hurricane Center still calls that recurving. That turn was the whole bet. If it came, the storm would swing away from Texas.

Rows: Bureau expected recurve "as many storms did near this latitude": sources-cuba.md §3 (NOAA AOML). Mechanism: factcheck/a3-r1.md rows 1 to 3 (NHC al032014 discussion #4; hurricanescience.org).
