## A2 · Havana
Chapter card: Purpose · show who read this storm right: the Jesuit observatory at Belen, its record under Father Benito Viñes and Father Lorenzo Gangoiti, and how the Weather Bureau came to sit beside it in Havana and own the wire.
Chapter card: Question · if Havana had the better storm readers, how did Washington's forecast end up the only one on the telegraph?
Chapter card: Reveal · after the war the Bureau moved its hurricane warning center into Havana itself; the two offices kept writing to each other, the fight was over the wire, and nobody can pin the decree to one date (1898 vs August or September 1900).
Chapter card: Visual event · Havana map with two pins in one city (BELEN · WEATHER BUREAU); the arrow from Jamaica to Havana; three dated cards for the decree sliding into a stack that will not line up; the two arrows from the hook side by side on 4 September.
Chapter card: Exit hook · Havana said northwest, into the Gulf; Washington said it would turn (Paul, P3), and Washington thought it had good reason (loop L-A2, paid in A3).

### A2-S4 — narration
**Picture:** Caribbean map, an arrow lifts from Kingston, Jamaica, and lands on Havana; chip "1898: SPANISH-AMERICAN WAR"; chip "BUREAU WARNING CENTER · JAMAICA → HAVANA"; then a Havana close-up with two pins, "BELEN" and "US WEATHER BUREAU · STOCKMAN"; lines run from the Bureau pin to Jamaica, Haiti, Santo Domingo, Puerto Rico and ships. Source line: NOAA AOML, 2015; Monthly Weather Review, September 1900. [VE] [RS] [Q] (two offices, one wire: whose forecast travels?)
> Then came the Spanish-American War, in eighteen ninety-eight. Afterward, with the United States running Cuba, the Weather Bureau moved its hurricane warning center from Jamaica to Havana. Its man there was a forecaster named Stockman, and his office kept the islands and the ships informed. So now one city had two weather offices, and only one of them answered to Washington.

### A2-S5 — narration
**Picture:** two envelopes cross the Florida Straits on the map, one stamped "GANGOITI ↔ MOORE · NOV-DEC 1898", one "BUREAU → GANGOITI · AUG 1, 1900"; lower-third "WILLIS MOORE · CHIEF, US WEATHER BUREAU"; then the telegraph line graphic from the hook, the Washington line lit; chip "DECREE: DATE?". Source line: National Archives, Record Group 27 (cited in Pietruska, 2016); NOAA AOML, 2015. [VE] [Q] (when did the wire close?)
> They weren't strangers who never spoke. Letters went between Gangoiti and Willis Moore, the Bureau's chief, in late eighteen ninety-eight, and the Bureau was still writing to Gangoiti on the first of August, nineteen hundred. The fight, as NOAA tells it, was over the wire. And NOAA's account of the telegraph decree gives no exact date.

Source line: José Rubiera, OnCuba, 2015; Erik Larson, Isaac's Storm (via Econlib); Pietruska, 2016. [RS] [VE] [PAY] (when did the wire close: nobody agrees)
> Accounts don't agree on one either. José Rubiera, a Cuban meteorologist, puts it in eighteen ninety-eight, when Belen was barred from publishing its forecasts. Erik Larson quotes a letter from Moore to the head of Western Union, saying the Bureau in Cuba had been greatly annoyed by independent observatories. Larson dates it August twenty-eighth. An archive file lists a Moore letter to that same man on September twentieth, twelve days after Galveston. So the record can't even say whether the decree came years before this storm or in the weeks around it.

### A2-S7 — narration
**Picture:** the telegraph line graphic over a map of Cuba: the island's government lines drawn in, a stamp "WAR DEPARTMENT" lands on them, then a chip "NO WEATHER MESSAGES"; a direct line Havana to New Orleans is drawn, then cut, and the message reroutes up to Washington and back down. Source line: Al Roker, HistoryNet (secondary); NOAA AOML, 2015. [VE] [RS] [Q] (what did that leave Belen?)
> Al Roker's history of this storm fills in how the wire worked in Cuba. The War Department ran the island's government telegraph lines, and he writes that it agreed to the Bureau's request to ban weather messages from them. Moore cut one more line. His own Havana office couldn't report straight to New Orleans. Its reports went to Washington first.

Rows: all secondary only (sources-cuba.md §2, Conflicts 2: only the NOAA "decreed" version has an agency source, so this segment names Roker in the spoken line and on screen). "Blown Away" byline is Al Roker (taken from the Wikipedia ref list, not re-checked on the page) · same URL]

### A2-S8 — narration
**Picture:** back to the Havana close-up, the "BELEN" pin; the Viñes and Gangoiti cards from A2-S3 return beside it; the telegraph line from Havana toward the Gulf goes dark at the Belen pin while the Bureau pin's line stays lit; chip "OFF THE WIRE". Source line: NOAA AOML, 2015. [VE] [HUMAN]
> Put yourself in Gangoiti's chair. He had the instruments, the records and thirty years of method. Belen could still watch a storm and write down what it saw. But by NOAA's account, only the Bureau's forecasts went over the telegraph, and the telegraph was how a warning reached a port across the Gulf.

Rows: decree: sources-cuba.md §2 (AOML "decreed that only its forecasts would be carried over the telegraph"; "Only the Weather Bureau prognostication was carried over the wires", factcheck-D.md I1-S3); thirty years: A2-S3 row.
