# ep-galveston-1900 · hook options (26 Oct 26, new hook rule)

Angle paid by every option: Galveston did not ignore a warning; its own weatherman had called a damaging storm "an absurd delusion" in print, and Havana's forecast that the storm was heading into the Gulf did not go over the telegraph. The full 1891 line is spoken in Option 1 only; Options 2 and 3 carry it by paraphrase or by another sentence from the same page, with the "ABSURD DELUSION" chip on screen.

## Option 2 · Hook shape: object (2026-10-26)
The one ordinary thing is a single page of the town paper from 1891: it is where Galveston was told it was safe, it was partly written by the man who would run the weather office in 1900, and everything after it in the story answers it.

### H1-S1 — Paul on camera
**Picture:** Paul full frame at 0:00, holding a printed reproduction of the Galveston Daily News page of 16 July 1891; popfig "GALVESTON, TEXAS · SEPTEMBER 1900" inside the first second; the sound under him: newsprint unfolding, then rain on a window, no wind.
> This page of the Galveston paper, from July eighteen ninety-one, told readers no hurricane could send a storm wave big enough to badly damage their city.

Rows: sources-cline.md §1 and factcheck-E.md H1-S4 (same page: "impossible for any cyclone to create a storm wave which would materially injure the city"; "badly" accepted for "seriously/materially", factcheck-D.md H1-S2; "hurricane" for "cyclone").
