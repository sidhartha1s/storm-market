# ep-galveston-1900 · hook options (26 Oct 26, new hook rule)

Angle paid by every option: Galveston did not ignore a warning; its own weatherman had called a damaging storm "an absurd delusion" in print, and Havana's forecast that the storm was heading into the Gulf did not go over the telegraph. Break one = the city's own expert; break two = the forecast that read the storm right and was not on the wire.

## Option 1 · Hook shape: record (2026-10-26)
The strongest fact is a sentence on a newspaper page, so the hook opens on those exact words and then says what came after them.

## Option 2 · Hook shape: pair (2026-10-26)
The same man wrote both the 1891 reassurance and the 1900 count of the lives lost, two true facts in his own hand that cannot both seem true, so the hook sets them side by side and leaves them unexplained.

### H1-S1 — Paul on camera
**Picture:** Paul full frame at 0:00; popfig "GALVESTON, TEXAS · SEPTEMBER 1900" inside the first second; the sound under him: two dry telegraph-key clicks, one under each half of the line, over a low wind bed.
> One Galveston weatherman wrote both: a storm badly damaging his city was an absurd delusion, and at least six thousand of its people lost their lives.

Rows: sources-cline.md §1 (16 July 1891, "absurd delusion"; "badly damaging" accepted as fair, factcheck-D.md H1-S2); sources-cline.md §3 (Cline's own report, "at least 6,000 people", paraphrased per flag-words.md).

### H1-S2 — narration
**Picture:** split screen: left, the Galveston Daily News page, chip "JULY 16, 1891"; right, the Monthly Weather Review report byline, chip "SEPTEMBER 1900"; no motion between them. Source lines: Galveston Daily News, 16 July 1891; Isaac Cline, Monthly Weather Review, September 1900. [VE]
> The first was in the city paper in the summer of eighteen ninety-one. The second was in his report to the Weather Bureau, nine years later.

Rows: sources-cline.md §1 (page date); sources-cline.md §3 (report reprinted from MWR, Sept 1900).

### H1-S3 — narration
**Picture:** hard cut to the NOAA weather map over western Cuba; two arrows from the same point, one northwest into the Gulf labelled "HAVANA", one curving northeast labelled "WASHINGTON". Source line: NOAA AOML, 2015. [RS] [VE] (break two)
> That September there were two forecasts for the same storm. Havana said it was heading into the Gulf, and Washington expected it to curve away to the northeast.

Rows: sources-cuba.md §1 (Belen: northwest into the Gulf); sources-cuba.md §3 (USWB "expected the storm to recurve to the northeast").

### H1-S4 — narration
**Picture:** hold the two arrows; the WASHINGTON arrow lights a telegraph line, the HAVANA arrow stays dark; chip with NOAA's words "only the Weather Bureau prognostication was carried over the wires". [VE] [loop E1 open]

Rows: sources-cuba.md §2 and factcheck-B.md I1-S4 (AOML: "Only the Weather Bureau prognostication was carried over the wires.").

## Option 3 · Hook shape: drop-in (2026-10-26)
The record puts the warnings, the flags and Cline at the Gulf on the morning of the eighth, so the viewer can stand inside that Saturday before anyone explains anything.

### H1-S1 — Paul on camera
**Picture:** Paul full frame at 0:00; popfig "GALVESTON, TEXAS · SEPTEMBER 1900" inside the first second; the sound under him: gusting wind and a flag snapping on its rope, surf low behind it.
> It's Saturday morning, the storm warning flags are up, and the man running your weather office once called this kind of storm an absurd delusion.

Rows: sources-cline.md §3 (morning of 8 Sept, "with storm warning flying"); sources-cline.md §1 ("absurd delusion"; "a storm like this" accepted as fair, factcheck-C.md I1-S6).

### H1-S2 — narration
**Picture:** the Monthly Weather Review line listing the warning messages, each date ticking on as a chip: "TUE 4 · WED 5 · THU 6 · FRI 7 · SAT 8". Source line: Isaac Cline, Monthly Weather Review, September 1900. [RS] [VE] (break one lands in the open: the city was warned)
> Washington's warning messages started on Tuesday, and one has come in every day since.

Rows: sources-cline.md §3 and sources-cuba.md §3 ("Warning messages were received from the Central Office at Washington on September 4, 5, 6, 7, and 8"); 7 Sept 1900 was a Friday (factcheck-D.md I1-S5), so the fourth was a Tuesday.

### H1-S3 — narration
**Picture:** cut to the NOAA 1900 map, Havana pinned; popfig "BELEN OBSERVATORY, HAVANA"; a dark telegraph line runs from Havana and stops short of the coast; chip "ONLY BUREAU FORECASTS ON THE TELEGRAPH". Source line: NOAA AOML, 2015. [RS] [VE] (break two)
> Down in Havana, the forecasters at Belen Observatory said this storm would move into the Gulf. NOAA says the Bureau decreed only its own forecasts could travel by telegraph.

Rows: sources-cuba.md §1 (Belen: northwest into the Gulf); sources-cuba.md §2 (the decree, in NOAA's words with spoken attribution, factcheck-A.md H1-S4 and factcheck-D.md I1-S3).
