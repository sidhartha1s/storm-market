## 0. Claim
(1) Cline's "storm wave" is what forecasters now call storm surge: the mound of seawater a hurricane's wind drives ahead of it onto the land, riding on top of the normal tide. (2) The open question: at Galveston, which way would that water go?

## 1. Source
- Storm wave = Cline's usage: factcheck/a1-r1.md row 21 (CORRECTED: tie it to his usage, not to NHC).
- NHC: "Storm surge is an abnormal water level rise generated by a storm over and above the predicted astronomical tide"; "primarily caused by the strong onshore winds of a hurricane" (factcheck/a1-r1.md rows 21, 22; factcheck/a1-r2.md row 14). https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/surge/
