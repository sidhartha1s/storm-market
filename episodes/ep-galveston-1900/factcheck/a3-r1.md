| 23 | S6 | "Galveston's turn came on Friday, the seventh." | CONFIRMED | Boston Herald in MWR p.376: "on the morning of the 7th ... warned the people of Galveston"; accepted TSHA (factcheck-D). 7 Sept 1900 was a Friday |

| 26 | S7 | "the warning spread down the whole Texas coast that day" | CORRECTED | Herald: "during that day extended its warnings all along the Texas coast". "Down" is added direction |
| 27 | S8 | "Cline watched that evening for the brick-dust sky he'd seen ahead of other storms on this coast" | CORRECTED | p.372: "This feature, which has been distinctly observed in other storms that have occurred in this section, was carefully watched for, both on the evening of the 7th and the morning of the 8th." Passive: not said Cline saw it himself; also the morning of the 8th |
| 28 | S8 | "Thin high clouds drifted in from the southeast that morning, and by noon the clouds were coming from the northeast" | CONFIRMED | "cirrus clouds moving from the southeast during the forenoon of the 7th, but by noon only alto-stratus from the northeast" |

| 31 | S9 | "Joseph Cline worked the office until nearly midnight." | CONFIRMED | "He having been on duty until nearly midnight" |
