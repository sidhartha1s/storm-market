| 1 | S10 | "Cline had said the water would spread out over the low land to the north." | CONFIRMED | 1891 Galveston Daily News: "too large a territory to the north which is lower than the island, over which the water may spread" (A1-S5 rows, factcheck/a1-r1.md rows 17 to 20) |
| 2 | S10 | "On the bay side, the north side, his own report puts it as high as fifteen feet." | CORRECTED | p. 373: "The tide, however, on the bay or north side of the city did not obtain a height of more than 15 feet." The report gives a ceiling, not a reading; "as high as" reads as a measured peak. Say "puts the tide at up to fifteen feet" |
| 3 | S11 | "There was no seawall." | CONFIRMED | factcheck/a1-r1.md row 38: no seawall in 1900, first section finished 29 July 1904 (Galveston History Center) |
| 4 | S11 | "What stood between the Gulf and the town that night was the town's own wreckage." | CORRECTED | p. 373: "It is possible that there was 5 feet of backwater on the Gulf side as a result of debris accumulating four to six blocks inland. The debris is piled eight to fifteen feet in height." The report does not date the pile ("is piled", written after the storm), so "that night" is unsupported. The pile sat four to six blocks inland, so it stood between the Gulf and the rest of the town, not the whole town. Say "What stood between the Gulf and the rest of the town was its own wreckage." |
| 5 | S11 | "It piled up four to six blocks in from the beach." | CONFIRMED | p. 373: "debris accumulating four to six blocks inland"; inland from the Gulf side, so "in from the beach" is a fair gloss. Height (eight to fifteen feet) kept on the chip only |
| 6 | S9 | "Inside, his floor stood well above the street, and there were about eight inches of water on it." | CORRECTED | p. 373 gives two readings at the same moment: "about eight inches deep in my residence", then a 4 ft rise brought the water to "a stage 10 feet above the ground". So just before the rise the water outside stood about 6 ft above the ground with only 8 in on the floor: the floor was roughly five feet above the ground. Derived, consistent. The report measures from "the ground", not "the street": say "well above the ground outside" |
