## Ruling on the writer's flag (I1-S4 "Galveston was warned.")
CORRECTED, light. What is solid: Galveston was put under a storm warning on 7 Sept (TSHA "On the previous day Galveston had been placed under a storm warning"; MWR Garriott: "on the morning of the 7th, nearly thirty-six hours before the disaster, warned the people of Galveston of its coming"). What is not solid: a flat "was warned" opener, followed by "Warning messages ... every day from the fourth to the eighth", lets the ear hear five days of storm warnings. Cline's MWR sentence does say "Warning messages were received ... September 4, 5, 6,7, and 8", but MWR also uses "advisory messages on the 4th and 5th" for New Orleans, so the early messages were probably advisories. For Galveston itself no source says what the 4th to 6th messages were, so the script must not call them advisories either. Safest neutral wording: "Galveston was not left in the dark. Messages from Washington came in every day from the fourth to the eighth." The storm warning on the seventh then carries the word "warning" where the record supports it.
