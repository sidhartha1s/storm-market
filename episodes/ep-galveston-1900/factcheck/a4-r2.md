# Fact-check a4-r2: S3, S6, S10, S12 spoken lines (28 Oct 26)
Sources: MWR Sept 1900 pp. 372-373 (OCR media/candidates/intro/_frames/mwr.txt lines 160-172, 185-205, 222-252); factcheck/a3-r2.md rows 35-37; factcheck/a4-r1.md; factcheck/a1-r1.md row 27 (NPS 8.7 ft); research/sources-cline.md; acts/a5.md A5-S1. No new web fetch.

| # | segment | claim (first words) | verdict | evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | S3 | "He went back to the beach, watched again, and filed a telegram to Washington" | CONFIRMED | p. 372: "returned to the Gulf, made more detailed observations of the tide and swells, and filed the following telegram addressed to the Central Office at Washington" |
| 2 | S3 | "Unusually heavy swells from the southeast ... overflowing low places three to four blocks from the beach" | CONFIRMED | telegram: "Unusually heavy swells from the southeast, intervals one to five minutes, overflowing low places south of city three to four blocks from beach" ("south of city" dropped, no overstatement) |
| 3 | S3 | "Such high water with opposing winds, it said, had never been seen before." | CONFIRMED | telegram: "Such high water with opposing winds never observed previously." Same sense; "seen" for "observed" is a fair plain-English swap |
| 4 | S3 | "Nine years earlier, this same man had written that a storm badly damaging Galveston was an absurd delusion." | CONFIRMED | sources-cline.md §1: 16 July 1891 Galveston Daily News, "simply an absurd delusion"; subject = belief the city would be seriously damaged. 1900 minus 1891 = 9 |
| 5 | S6 | "At noon the heavy rain set in." | CONFIRMED | p. 372, heavy rain from about noon (a4-r1 row 27) |
| 6 | S6 | "Later, Isaac went home, and Joseph waded to the telegraph offices through water two to four feet deep." | CONFIRMED | p. 373: after giving the message to the observer Isaac "went home to lunch"; "Mr. J. L. Cline went to the telegraph offices through water from two to four feet deep". "Waded" is a gloss (a4-r1 row 29) |
| 7 | S6 | "One telephone wire still worked to Houston" | CONFIRMED | "the long distance telephone had one wire still working to Houston" |
| 8 | S6 | "Joseph phoned the three thirty report through to Western Union there." | CORRECTED | MWR says he "gave the message to the Western Union telegraph office at Houston". The message is the special observation (a3-r2 rows 35-37) with the 3:30 line added. The report calls the whole thing a "special report" / "special observation", not a "three thirty report"; the 3:30 stamp belongs to the added line. Use the report's word "message" |
| 9 | S10 | "Remember the ground under his house, five point two feet above the sea." | CONFIRMED | p. 373: "The ground was 5.2 feet elevation" at Rosenberg Ave (25th St) and Q St |
| 10 | S10 | "By half past seven, the water stood ten feet above that ground." | CONFIRMED | p. 373: sudden four-foot rise "about 7:30 p. m."; "The water had now reached a stage 10 feet above the ground" (the report ties the 10 ft stage to that moment) |
| 11 | S10 | "An hour later it was nearly five feet higher." | CONFIRMED | "The tide rose the next hour, between 7:30 and 8:30 p. m., nearly five feet additional" |
| 12 | S10 | "That put it about twenty feet above the sea, right at his door." | CONFIRMED | "making a total tide in that [locality] of about twenty feet"; 10 + 5.2 = 15.2, plus nearly 5 = about 20, measured from sea level. Cline stresses "these observations were carefully taken". "At his door" = that locality; bay side not above 15 ft (stays a house figure, not island-wide) |
| 13 | S10 | "The highest ground anywhere on the island stood less than half that high." | CONFIRMED | NPS: island's highest point 8.7 ft (a1-r1 row 27; OSU "just under nine feet"); 8.7 / 20 = 0.435, under half. Holds even at OSU's nine feet (0.45) |
| 14 | S12 | "And what did the daylight show? There is film of it." | CORRECTED | The film (A5-S1) was shot days after, probably 11 to 19 Sept 1900, copyright 24 Sept: men searching Broadway wreckage. It is not film of Sunday's daylight itself; "of it" overstates. A5-S1 itself says "In the days after, a film company shot..." |
