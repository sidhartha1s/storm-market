## Beat 5 · Cause / Mechanism · 445 words
Here is the mechanism most news coverage skipped. Normally, trade winds push warm Pacific surface water west, toward Asia and Indonesia. In El Niño, those trade winds weaken, and some years even reverse. Warm water that would normally pile up near Asia sloshes back east instead. It piles up near South America, which is why the Niño one plus two region runs hottest. [f5] Farther west, the wider Niño three point four region also warms, but by a smaller amount. [f4] That is the plain reason CPC's headline number, plus one point eight degrees, sits lower than the coastal reading. [f4][f5] It also explains why different trackers report different numbers this week. CPC measures a specific patch of ocean called Niño three point four. [f4] Some independent trackers use a broader relative index, or a different averaging window. That is likely why one non-agency tracker reported plus two point nine degrees today, while CPC reports plus one point eight. [f16][f4] This program treats CPC's own number as the authoritative one, until the October eighth update. [f6] The bigger mechanism is what warm ocean water does to the atmosphere above it. Warm water heats the air above it, and that heat rises. Rising air shifts the jet stream, the river of wind that steers United States weather. A shifted jet stream is the direct link between an ocean anomaly and your local forecast. That is why CPC's winter outlook tilts the northern states milder and the southern states wetter. [f19] There is also a feedback loop worth understanding. As trade winds weaken, more warm water shifts east, which weakens the trade winds further. That self-feeding cycle is part of why CPC now assigns a seventy-five percent chance of a historic reading. [f3] A historic reading, plus two point five degrees or higher, would exceed every El Niño since nineteen fifty. [f3] The stronger the ocean anomaly, the stronger the jet-stream shift, and the larger the weather swings. CNN has linked this same pattern to hurricane activity threatening Hawaii this week. [f15] The same outlet ties an altered atmospheric pattern to a quiet Atlantic hurricane season so far. [f15] CNN also connects this event to wildfires currently burning in Indonesia. [f15] None of those three links have been confirmed directly by CPC in the material reviewed for this update. They are CNN's own attributions, worth watching, not yet an agency finding. For older adults, a shifted jet stream means real changes to winter travel and heating costs. A milder north can lower heating bills, but a wetter south raises flood and storm risk. That trade-off is the practical heart of the mechanism behind today's numbers.
