### B2-S1 — narration
> The country's alert list holds five volcanoes at level three tonight, its second highest level. Krakatau is one. Semeru is another. A volcano on Flores, a volcano in Java, and a volcano in North Sumatra round out the list. None of that has changed since our last report. [s3]
> Twenty two more volcanoes sit one level down, at level two. Forty two sit at the lowest level, and none sit at the highest level in the country tonight. [s3]
> Two volcanoes are erupting hard, and a third just joined them. Ibu, at level two, erupted four separate times between last evening and just after midnight. The first came at nine forty five, with a four hundred meter column. Two more followed within the hour, each around four hundred meters. The fourth, just before one in the morning, reached six hundred meters, the tallest of the night. Instruments recorded ground shaking of twenty eight millimeters during those eruptions, each lasting thirty to forty seven seconds. Every column drifted the same direction, northeast, as gray ash. [s1]
> Semeru, at level three, erupted at least four times overnight as well. Two of those produced no visible column at all. The clearest eruption came before dawn, a column near one thousand meters, matching yesterday's peak for a second straight day. A second, smaller eruption followed within the hour, an eight hundred meter column drifting east and southeast. Above sea level, the two clearest columns reached roughly four thousand six hundred and four thousand five hundred meters. Aviation controllers held an orange warning on that plume through both eruptions. [s1, s2]
> Then, just before sunrise, a volcano further east began erupting too, a smaller column, only about one hundred meters, colored white to gray. This was not the volcano's first sign of life. A quieter, unseen eruption had already been logged there the evening before. The observer on duty said the eruption was still going when the report was filed. [s1]
