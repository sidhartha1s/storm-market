## The hard fact this round designs around
JMA's own official forecast (16 Sep 19:10 JST) keeps the system an UNNAMED tropical depression.
Its longest published lead time, +117h (21 Sep 15:00 JST), puts it at **37.0N 143.1E, Severe
Tropical Storm, 30 m/s, 975 hPa, "East of Japan"** — OFFSHORE of Tohoku/Kanto, not a confirmed
Tokyo landfall, and not yet in JMA's "Strong Typhoon" band (33-44 m/s). The press "direct hit on
Kanto" framing in circulation is simulation/speculation, not JMA's own longest advisory. Every
idea below is built to be honest TODAY and to survive BOTH outcomes:
- **Branch A** — it curves back in and makes a real approach/impact on Kanto (the outcome the
  press simulation and some outlets are already implying, still possible beyond JMA's 117h horizon).
- **Branch B** — it does what JMA's own longest forecast currently shows: curves north and passes
  offshore, and Kanto gets rain, wind, storm-surge risk, and Silver Week transport disruption
  without a direct hit. The 2024 Typhoon Ampil brush (Kanto, Obon week, minimal Tokyo damage,
  4 injured, hundreds of Shinkansen cancellations, ~250 households without power in each of
  Ibaraki/Tochigi) is the closest recent real-world precedent for Branch B.
No title or hook below promises a Tokyo hit, a "Category 2," or a Hagibis-scale outcome as fact.
