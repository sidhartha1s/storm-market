Silver Week 2026 in Japan: Dates, Crowds, and What to Do in Tokyo

Japan's first full five-day Silver Week, with crowd forecasts and the best ways to spend it in Tokyo

By Metropolis • September 10, 2026

What Is Silver Week?

Silver Week is Japan’s rare autumn counterpart to the spring Golden Week — a stretch of national holidays that, in the right year, lines up into a five-day break. It only happens when Respect for the Aged Day and the Autumnal Equinox fall exactly two days apart, turning the single weekday between them into a bonus “Citizens’ Holiday.” That alignment is uncommon: it has happened just twice before, in 2009 and 2015, and after this year it won’t happen again until 2032.

Silver Week 2026 Dates

Silver Week 2026 runs from Saturday, September 19 to Wednesday, September 23 — five consecutive days off.

Tuesday, September 22 becomes a holiday automatically under Japan’s Public Holiday Act, which turns any single working day sandwiched between two holidays into a day off. Combined with the weekend before it, that’s five straight days — and anyone who also takes September 24–25 off can stretch it into a ten-day break.

Why Silver Week 2026 Is a Bigger Deal Than Usual

This isn’t just another long weekend. It’s Japan’s first full five-day Silver Week in 11 years, and it pushes the country’s total national holidays for the year from 16 to 17. For residents, it’s the closest thing the calendar offers to a second Golden Week. For visitors, it means the two busiest domestic travel periods of the year — Golden Week and Obon — now have autumn company.

How Crowded Will It Be?

Expect Golden-Week-level congestion on transport and at popular getaways, though with a different shape:

  • Shinkansen and highways: JR Central and JR West have made the Nozomi fully reserved-seat only from September 18–23, with the normally unreserved cars 1 and 2 switched to reserved for the duration. Reserved seats open exactly one month ahead, so Silver Week travel should be booked well in advance.
  • Peak travel windows: Outbound congestion is worst on the evening of Friday, September 18, and the morning of Saturday, September 19. The return crush hits the afternoon and evening of Wednesday, September 23.
  • Tokyo itself gets quieter. A lot of residents leave the city for onsen towns and hometowns, which makes central Tokyo more pleasant than usual — but nearby day-trip favorites like Hakone, Nikko, and Kawaguchiko fill up fast.
  • Banks, post offices, and ward offices close on September 21–23, so plan around that if you need paperwork done.
  • Shops, restaurants, and attractions stay open. Unlike New Year, Silver Week doesn’t shut the city down — many venues run special events for the holiday crowd instead.

What’s Happening in Tokyo During Silver Week 2026

September is already one of Tokyo’s most event-dense months, and Silver Week lands right in the middle of it:

  • Tokyo Game Show 2026 — Makuhari Messe, September 17–21 (30th anniversary edition), with public days on the 19th, 20th, and 21st.
  • Aki Basho (Grand Sumo Tournament) — Ryogoku Kokugikan, September 13–27. Mid-week tickets tend to be the easiest of Tokyo’s three annual tournaments to get.
  • Ultra Japan — Odaiba, September 19–20.
  • Tsukimi (harvest moon viewing) — around September 25, with atmospheric spots at Nezu Shrine, the Roppongi Hills rooftop, and Tokyo Skytree’s Tembo Galleria.
  • Autumn higan — the week around the equinox (Sep 20–26), when many Tokyoites visit family graves; parks stay busy but the mood turns quieter and more reflective.
  • Red spider lilies (higanbana) — Showa Kinen Park and Kinchakuda are reliable spots as the flowers hit their early-autumn bloom.

Should You Visit Tokyo During Silver Week 2026?

If your plans are flexible, here’s the trade-off:

Go during Silver Week if you want the city at its most festive — sumo, Tokyo Game Show, tsukimi events, and a genuinely lively atmosphere — and you’re staying within Tokyo rather than day-tripping.

Avoid it if you’re planning day trips to Hakone, Nikko, or Kawaguchiko, or you want to avoid paying holiday-surge rates for hotels and transport.

Best alternative: the week immediately after Silver Week (roughly September 24–October 1) is one of the best windows on the Japanese calendar — the heat has broken, the holiday crowds have cleared out, and prices drop back to normal within about 48 hours of the holiday ending.

Silver Week 2026 FAQ

When exactly is Silver Week 2026? 

Saturday, September 19 through Wednesday, September 23, 2026 — five consecutive days off.

Why is September 22 a holiday if it’s not one of the official ones?

Japan’s Public Holiday Act automatically converts any single working day squeezed between two national holidays into a “Citizens’ Holiday.” In 2026, that rule turns the Tuesday between Respect for the Aged Day (Sep 21) and Autumnal Equinox Day (Sep 23) into a bonus day off.

Is Silver Week as busy as Golden Week? 

It’s close. Trains, highways, and popular resort towns see a Golden-Week-style surge, though Tokyo proper tends to feel less crowded than during Golden Week since more residents leave the city.

When is the next Silver Week after 2026?

Not until 2032, and even then it will only be a four-day version (September 18–22). The next full five-day Silver Week isn’t until 2037.

Do shops and restaurants close during Silver Week?

No — unlike the New Year holidays, most shops, restaurants, and attractions stay open throughout Silver Week, though banks, post offices, and government offices do close on the public holidays (September 21, 22, and 23).
