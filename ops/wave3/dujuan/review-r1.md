## Per-idea verdicts

### 1. A Depression Near Guam Is Aiming at Japan's Big Holiday
**Verdict: DIES.** TD-stage coverage even from JP outlets barely moves (fact pack: TBS's own depression video 94K vs. the naming+Silver-Week cluster at 500K+). The Guam/Navy stake tested at near-zero (see "us navy yokosuka" query — nothing relevant above 10K). Thumbnail can't show a depression; a 55-year-old in Ohio doesn't click "TD near Guam."
**Fatal flaws:** no US stake demonstrated by data; too early in the cycle; duplicate of idea 2's naming beat once merged.
**FIX:** don't ship standalone. Fold as the cold-open 15 seconds of idea 2/4, not its own video.

### 2. It Has a Name: Typhoon Dujuan Forms Near the Marianas
**Verdict: MAYBE.** Naming alone isn't the view driver — the big numbers (552K/548K/151K) belong to "naming + Silver Week" combined, not naming in isolation.
**Fatal flaws:** title doesn't carry the stake that's actually driving views.
**FIX:** merge the naming beat into the Silver Week angle (idea 4) rather than a standalone "it just got a name" video; if shipped separately, the title must include the Silver Week collision, not just "Typhoon No. 25 forms."

### 3. Dujuan's Track Just Jumped East, Straight Toward Tokyo
**Verdict: MAYBE.** No isolated search evidence for "track shift" content, but this is the exact update JP outlets are already mining for hundreds of thousands of views (Silver Week cluster). Real service-journalism value.
**Fatal flaws:** depends on a track shift that may not happen before publish; thumbnail plan (before/after map) is sound but needs Tokyo named on-frame, not implied.
**FIX:** name Tokyo/Kanto explicitly in title and thumbnail text; tie the hook to a US base (Yokosuka/Narita) sitting inside the new cone for the American-stake test.

### 4. Silver Week Collision: Japan's Biggest Holiday Meets a Typhoon
**Verdict: BLOWS UP.** Directly matches the single largest evidence cluster found: JP outlets are racking up 552K, 548K, 151K, 45.7K, 33.6K THIS WEEK on exactly this angle (typhoon forming, hitting during Silver Week). This is the best-validated idea in the round.
**Audience test:** passes both — a 55-year-old Ohioan reads "storm hits during the exact days millions just took a five-day holiday" as a relatable vacation-ruined story; a Japanese viewer has direct travel stakes.
**FIX:** none needed structurally; ship as the flagship of the series, publish window as planned.
