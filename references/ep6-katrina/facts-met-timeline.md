# Hurricane Katrina — Meteorology, Forecast & Timeline Facts
Scope: meteorology, forecast/warning, evacuation timeline, Gulf-coast-wide impact. Primary sources only (NHC/NOAA/NWS/govt). All times given as stated in the source (UTC vs local noted).

## 1. Storm chronology, Aug 23–30

- TD-12 (Katrina) formed as a tropical depression by **1800 UTC 23 August 2005**, over the southeastern Bahamas, about 175 n mi southeast of Nassau — source: NHC Tropical Cyclone Report AL122005 (Knabb/Rhome/Brown) — https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/data/tcr/AL122005_Katrina.pdf — confidence: HIGH
- Florida landfall: **25 August 2005, ~2230 UTC**, Category 1, max sustained winds 70 kt (81 mph). Six direct fatalities in Florida. — source: NHC TCR AL122005 — https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/data/tcr/AL122005_Katrina.pdf — confidence: HIGH
- Rapid intensification to Category 5 occurred "in less than 12 h" on the morning of **28 August**; peak intensity reached at **1800 UTC 28 August**: max sustained winds **150 kt (173 mph)**, minimum central pressure **902 mb**. — source: NHC TCR AL122005 — confidence: HIGH
- Hurricane watch for southeast Louisiana, including metropolitan New Orleans, issued **10:00 AM CDT, 27 August 2005**; storm surge forecast at that point 15–20 ft, locally to 25 ft. NHC 11 PM EDT 27 August advisory placed Mississippi in the forecast cone 32 hours before Louisiana landfall. — source: NWS Service Assessment, "Hurricane Katrina, August 23–31, 2005" — https://www.weather.gov/media/publications/assessments/Katrina.pdf — confidence: HIGH
- NHC Advisory #26, issued **4:00 AM CDT / 0900 UTC, 29 August 2005**: "A HURRICANE WARNING IS IN EFFECT FOR THE NORTH CENTRAL GULF COAST FROM MORGAN CITY LOUISIANA EASTWARD TO THE ALABAMA/FLORIDA BORDER...INCLUDING THE CITY OF NEW ORLEANS AND LAKE PONTCHARTRAIN," forecasting onshore movement "JUST EAST OF GRAND ISLE THIS MORNING" and reaching the LA-MS border area "THIS AFTERNOON." — source: NHC public advisory archive — https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/archive/2005/pub/al122005.public.026.shtml — confidence: HIGH
- First (final Louisiana) landfall: **29 August 2005, 1110 UTC (6:10 AM CDT)**, near Buras-Triumph, Plaquemines Parish, LA. Category 3, max sustained winds 110 kt (127 mph), central pressure 920 mb. — source: NHC TCR AL122005 — confidence: HIGH
- Second landfall: **29 August 2005, 1445 UTC (9:45 AM CDT)**, near the Louisiana/Mississippi border. Category 3, max sustained winds 105 kt (121 mph), central pressure 928 mb. — source: NHC TCR AL122005 — confidence: HIGH

## 2. NHC forecast quality

- "The official track forecasts for Katrina issued within about two and a half days of landfall...were exceptionally accurate and consistent." — source: NHC TCR AL122005 — confidence: HIGH
- Official track forecast errors issued 12, 24, 36, and 48 hours prior to 1200 UTC 29 August (final approach) were only 19, 24, 32, and 56 n mi respectively. — source: NHC TCR AL122005 (as summarized in NWS Service Assessment) — confidence: HIGH
- NWS Service Assessment: "NHC forecasts afforded emergency management and the public 56 hours to implement their hurricane plans and make evacuation decisions along the north central Gulf Coast," based on the forecast issued 11 PM EDT 26 August showing a major-hurricane landfall in Louisiana. — source: NWS Service Assessment — https://www.weather.gov/media/publications/assessments/Katrina.pdf — confidence: HIGH
- Track forecast errors for Katrina were "considerably less than the average official Atlantic track errors for the 10-year period 1995–2004." — source: NWS Service Assessment — confidence: HIGH
- NWS Director (testimony) stated forecasts "were more accurate than usual, with all of the forecast tracks during the last forty-eight hours lining up almost directly on top of the actual track." — source: White House "Hurricane Katrina: Lessons Learned," Chapter 3 (citing NWS Director Johnson testimony) — https://georgewbush-whitehouse.archives.gov/reports/katrina-lessons-learned/chapter3.html — confidence: HIGH

## 3. Robert Ricks / NWS Slidell bulletin, 28 August 2005 — FULL VERBATIM TEXT

Issued **1011 AM CDT SUN AUG 28 2005**, NWS Slidell/New Orleans-Baton Rouge Weather Forecast Office (product NPW — Inland Hurricane Wind Warning w/ embedded special statement). Public domain text, captured verbatim below from the primary source page.

Primary source: https://www.weather.gov/lix/NPW_28_1011 — confidence: HIGH (verified against raw HTML `<pre>` content, not paraphrase)

> URGENT - WEATHER MESSAGE
> NATIONAL WEATHER SERVICE NEW ORLEANS LA
> 1011 AM CDT SUN AUG 28 2005
>
> ...DEVASTATING DAMAGE EXPECTED...
>
> .HURRICANE KATRINA...A MOST POWERFUL HURRICANE WITH UNPRECEDENTED
> STRENGTH...RIVALING THE INTENSITY OF HURRICANE CAMILLE OF 1969.
>
> MOST OF THE AREA WILL BE UNINHABITABLE FOR WEEKS...PERHAPS LONGER. AT
> LEAST ONE HALF OF WELL CONSTRUCTED HOMES WILL HAVE ROOF AND WALL
> FAILURE. ALL GABLED ROOFS WILL FAIL...LEAVING THOSE HOMES SEVERELY
> DAMAGED OR DESTROYED.
>
> THE MAJORITY OF INDUSTRIAL BUILDINGS WILL BECOME NON FUNCTIONAL.
> PARTIAL TO COMPLETE WALL AND ROOF FAILURE IS EXPECTED. ALL WOOD
> FRAMED LOW RISING APARTMENT BUILDINGS WILL BE DESTROYED. CONCRETE
> BLOCK LOW RISE APARTMENTS WILL SUSTAIN MAJOR DAMAGE...INCLUDING SOME
> WALL AND ROOF FAILURE.
>
> HIGH RISE OFFICE AND APARTMENT BUILDINGS WILL SWAY DANGEROUSLY...A
> FEW TO THE POINT OF TOTAL COLLAPSE. ALL WINDOWS WILL BLOW OUT.
>
> AIRBORNE DEBRIS WILL BE WIDESPREAD...AND MAY INCLUDE HEAVY ITEMS SUCH
> AS HOUSEHOLD APPLIANCES AND EVEN LIGHT VEHICLES. SPORT UTILITY
> VEHICLES AND LIGHT TRUCKS WILL BE MOVED. THE BLOWN DEBRIS WILL CREATE
> ADDITIONAL DESTRUCTION. PERSONS...PETS...AND LIVESTOCK EXPOSED TO THE
> WINDS WILL FACE CERTAIN DEATH IF STRUCK.
>
> POWER OUTAGES WILL LAST FOR WEEKS...AS MOST POWER POLES WILL BE DOWN
> AND TRANSFORMERS DESTROYED. WATER SHORTAGES WILL MAKE HUMAN SUFFERING
> INCREDIBLE BY MODERN STANDARDS.
>
> THE VAST MAJORITY OF NATIVE TREES WILL BE SNAPPED OR UPROOTED. ONLY
> THE HEARTIEST WILL REMAIN STANDING...BUT BE TOTALLY DEFOLIATED. FEW
> CROPS WILL REMAIN. LIVESTOCK LEFT EXPOSED TO THE WINDS WILL BE
> KILLED.
>
> AN INLAND HURRICANE WIND WARNING IS ISSUED WHEN SUSTAINED WINDS NEAR
> HURRICANE FORCE...OR FREQUENT GUSTS AT OR ABOVE HURRICANE FORCE...ARE
> CERTAIN WITHIN THE NEXT 12 TO 24 HOURS.
>
> ONCE TROPICAL STORM AND HURRICANE FORCE WINDS ONSET...DO NOT VENTURE
> OUTSIDE!

(The same product then repeats parish-specific Inland Hurricane Wind Warning segments for Mississippi and Louisiana zones with area-specific timing, e.g. "MAXIMUM SUSTAINED WINDS IN EXCESS OF 140 MPH CAN LAST FOR 2 TO 3 HOURS" for lower Plaquemines/Orleans/St. Bernard/St. Tammany etc. Full segment text available in the same product if needed.)

- Backstory: forecaster **Robert Ricks Jr.**, a native of New Orleans's Ninth Ward, wrote the bulletin drawing on his own family's experience with Hurricanes Betsy (1965) and Camille (1969). Quoted after the fact: "I was trying to find things to actually take out" (i.e., editing for restraint, not exaggeration). — source: NBC Nightly News interview, cited via nola.com retirement profile — https://www.nola.com/news/weather/forecaster-who-wrote-dire-hurricane-katrina-warning-retires-from-national-weather-service/article_a1404242-4567-11eb-b572-eb041dbce88d.html — confidence: MED (backstory is a journalistic account, not a government document; the bulletin text itself is HIGH/primary)
- The NWS's own retrospective assessment/technical paper called the bulletin's "unprecedented detail and foreboding nature" a "significant moment for the NWS during Katrina," noting its language was based on a warning template originally designed by the NWS Tampa Bay office in the 1990s. — source: "From the 'Statement Heard Around the World' to Hurricane Threats and..." NWS technical paper — https://www.weather.gov/media/bro/research/pdf/J8.4StatementHeardRoundWorld_KatrinaSymp_manuscript.pdf — confidence: MED (paper referenced via search only, not fully fetched/verified line-by-line — see OPEN ITEMS)

## 4. Evacuation timeline

- Gov. Kathleen Blanco declared a Louisiana state of emergency on **26 August 2005**. — source: White House "Hurricane Katrina: Lessons Learned," Chapter 3 — https://georgewbush-whitehouse.archives.gov/reports/katrina-lessons-learned/chapter3.html — confidence: HIGH
- Mayor Ray Nagin called for **voluntary evacuation** at **5:00 PM CDT, 27 August 2005**. — source: same, Chapter 3 — confidence: HIGH
- Contraflow (lane reversal on major highways) implemented by Louisiana and Mississippi at **4:00 PM CDT, 27 August 2005**. — source: same, Chapter 3 — confidence: HIGH
- Mayor Nagin ordered **mandatory evacuation** of New Orleans on **Sunday, 28 August 2005, at approximately 11:00 AM Central time — roughly 19 hours before projected landfall**. This time is now cross-corroborated by two independent government documents. NWS Service Assessment: "11 a.m. CDT," explicitly called "the first-ever mandatory evacuation of the city." House Select Bipartisan Committee, "A Failure of Initiative" (Evacuation chapter), verbatim: "Finally, on Sunday morning at around 11:00 a.m. central time — 19 hours before projected landfall, Nagin announced the issuance of a mandatory evacuation order." (RESOLVED — earlier secondary press accounts citing ~9:30 a.m. appear to be loose/rounded; the two government-document citations agree on ~11 a.m. and are authoritative.) — sources: NWS Service Assessment (https://www.weather.gov/media/publications/assessments/Katrina.pdf); House Select Bipartisan Committee, "A Failure of Initiative," Evacuation chapter (https://biotech.law.lsu.edu/katrina/govdocs/109-377/evac.pdf) — confidence: HIGH
- On the "first-ever mandatory evacuation" claim specifically: the NWS Service Assessment states this explicitly; the House report does not repeat the "first-ever" framing verbatim but does note that New Orleans and Jefferson Parish "declared mandatory evacuations late or not at all" relative to Mississippi and Alabama's earlier declarations — consistent with, but not an independent confirmation of, "first-ever." — confidence: MED
- Gov. Blanco stated President Bush had called her shortly before the press conference urging a mandatory evacuation order. — source: search-surfaced press coverage of the joint Nagin/Blanco news conference (needs primary transcript confirmation — see OPEN ITEMS) — confidence: MED
- Evacuation scope figures (multiple, differently scoped — do not conflate):
  - Statewide (Louisiana): Gov. Blanco estimated **~1.2 million people, ~92% of the at-risk population**, evacuated before landfall; "tens of thousands" remained, "many...the region's most vulnerable." — source: White House Lessons Learned, Ch. 3 — confidence: HIGH
  - New Orleans city: NWS Service Assessment states an evacuation rate of **approximately 80%** of the New Orleans population. — source: NWS Service Assessment — confidence: HIGH
  - Jefferson Parish specifically: **83% evacuation rate (375,000 of 450,000 residents)**. — source: NWS Service Assessment — confidence: HIGH
- Superdome as "refuge of last resort" — full population progression, RESOLVED with dated figures from the House Select Bipartisan Committee, "A Failure of Initiative" (Evacuation chapter, https://biotech.law.lsu.edu/katrina/govdocs/109-377/evac.pdf):
  - **Opening announced**: at the Nagin/Blanco press conference, 28 August 2005: "At noon today, the Superdome will then be opened up as a refuge of last resort, where we will start to take citizens that cannot evacuate."
  - **At landfall** (6:10 AM CDT, 29 August): "approximately 10-12,000 people were sheltered in the Superdome."
  - **HSOC Spot Report #30**, 2:00 AM CDT, Wednesday, 31 August: 12,000–15,000 people at the Superdome.
  - **Peak, per the Wednesday-noon (31 August) video teleconference**: population had "swollen to approximately 23,000." This is the best-sourced peak figure — treat ~23,000 (Wed. 31 Aug, noon) as the authoritative peak population, not the commonly-repeated 20,000–30,000 secondary-source range.
  - confidence: HIGH (RESOLVED)

## 5. Mississippi coast impact

- Maximum observed storm surge (high-water mark): **27.8 ft at Pass Christian, MS** (immediate Gulf coast, just east of St. Louis Bay). — source: NHC TCR AL122005 — https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/data/tcr/AL122005_Katrina.pdf — confidence: HIGH
- A storm tide of approximately 28 ft was measured at the Hancock County EOC (storm tide = surge + astronomical tide, a related but distinct measurement from the 27.8 ft surge high-water mark). — source: NHC TCR AL122005 (as reflected in secondary summary — recommend direct page-cite confirmation, see OPEN ITEMS) — confidence: MED
- Storm surge of **17–22 ft** along the eastern Mississippi coast, roughly Gulfport to Pascagoula. — source: NHC TCR AL122005 — confidence: HIGH
- Mississippi death toll (2023 NOAA/NHC reanalysis, direct fatalities only): **172 direct deaths in Mississippi**. — source: NHC TCR AL122005, 2023 update (per NOAA/NHC reanalysis reporting) — confidence: HIGH
- Waveland, Bay St. Louis, Gulfport, Biloxi destruction: extensive, described in NHC TCR and NWS Mobile "Hurricane Katrina — August 2005" page; specific per-town damage superlatives (e.g., "X% of structures destroyed") not independently verified to a single-number primary citation in this pass — see OPEN ITEMS. — source pointer: https://www.weather.gov/mob/katrina — confidence: LOW (pointer only, not yet fetched/verified)

## 6. KLIX (Slidell) radar during the storm

- The Slidell, LA WSR-88D radar (KLIX) remained in continuous operation through the eyewall's approach; NWS LIX's own 20th-anniversary retrospective page shows KLIX radar images captured at 5:54 AM, 8:16 AM, and 10:10 AM CDT on 29 August 2005, indicating the radar was actively producing images through landfall. — source: NWS New Orleans/Baton Rouge 20th Anniversary of Hurricane Katrina page — https://www.weather.gov/lix/katrina_anniversary — confidence: HIGH
- KLIX's **last full volume scan/reflectivity sweep transmitted to NCDC (National Climatic Data Center)** was at **1354 UTC (8:54 AM CDT), 29 August 2005** — i.e., shortly before the second (LA/MS border) landfall at 1445 UTC — as the most intense eyewall winds passed directly over the radar site. This is characterized in the source as a loss of data transmission, not necessarily physical destruction of the radar. — source: AMS (American Meteorological Society) conference paper on Katrina's radar-observed structure, citing NCDC data — https://ams.confex.com/ams/pdfpapers/108456.pdf — confidence: MED (this is a peer-reviewed conference paper referencing NCDC data, not a direct NHC/NWS statement; NWS's own official Tropical Cyclone Report and LIX Post-Storm Report do not themselves mention the radar's operational status — see OPEN ITEMS)

## 7. Superlatives — verified with scope

- **Costliest US hurricane on record** (unadjusted): ~**$125 billion** in damage (2005 dollars), NHC 90% confidence interval $97.4B–$145.5B. — source: NHC TCR AL122005 — confidence: HIGH
- **Costliest US tropical cyclone, inflation-adjusted**: NOAA NCEI figures cited in 20th-anniversary coverage put Katrina at **~$201.3 billion (adjusted)**, versus Hurricane Harvey (2017) at **~$160.0 billion (adjusted)** — Katrina remains #1, Harvey #2, not a tie, when adjusted. (Unadjusted, both are commonly quoted near $125B, which is the likely source of the "tie" framing — but NOAA's adjusted ranking keeps Katrina ahead.) — source pointer: NOAA NCEI "Costliest U.S. Tropical Cyclones" — https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/billions/dcmi.pdf — confidence: MED (NCEI is the correct primary source; exact figures should be directly re-confirmed against the linked PDF table rather than secondary summary — see OPEN ITEMS)
- **3rd deadliest US hurricane** (i.e., deadliest since 1928): NHC's own "Hurricanes in History" outreach page states Katrina "is the deadliest hurricane to strike the United States since the Palm Beach–Lake Okeechobee hurricane of September 1928" — meaning Katrina ranks behind only the 1900 Galveston hurricane (deadliest ever) and the 1928 Okeechobee hurricane, i.e., 3rd deadliest overall. — source: NHC "Hurricanes in History" — https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/outreach/history/ — confidence: HIGH
- **Minimum pressure**: 902 mb at peak intensity (1800 UTC 28 August, over the Gulf); 920 mb at first (Buras) landfall — one of the lowest landfalling pressures on record for a US hurricane. — source: NHC TCR AL122005 — confidence: HIGH
- **Storm size**: tropical-storm-force winds extended out to **200 n mi from the center** — an exceptionally large wind field. — source: NHC TCR AL122005 — confidence: HIGH

## 8. Death toll — current official figure (2023 NOAA/NHC reanalysis)

- **Total: 1,392 combined direct and indirect fatalities** (this is the current, revised, official NHC/NOAA figure, down from the long-cited "1,800+" figure). — source: NHC TCR AL122005, 2023 update — confidence: HIGH
- Breakdown:
  - **520 direct deaths**: 341 Louisiana, 172 Mississippi, 6 Florida, 1 Georgia.
  - **565 indirect deaths** (majority — 318 — attributed to cardiovascular causes).
  - **307 deaths of undetermined direct/indirect classification** (cause of death not specific enough to classify).
  - 520 + 565 + 307 = 1,392.
- Basis for the reanalysis: review of medical logs for **over 1,000 victims**, provided by Louisiana and Mississippi state officials, informing two academic papers that the NHC's 2023 report update incorporated. — source: nola.com reporting on the NHC/NOAA 2023 update — https://www.nola.com/news/hurricane/how-many-people-died-in-katrina-toll-reduced-17-years-on/article_e3009e46-91ed-11ed-8f2a-a7b11e1e8d34.html — confidence: HIGH (figures corroborated independently by the NHC TCR fetch itself, see Section 1/7 sourcing — cross-checked HIGH)

## 9. Hour-by-hour, 29 August 2005, New Orleans area

- **~6:10 AM CDT (1110 UTC)**: First Louisiana landfall near Buras-Triumph, Plaquemines Parish (Category 3, 127 mph sustained). — source: NHC TCR AL122005 — confidence: HIGH
- **8:38 AM CDT**: NWS Doppler radar detected the leading edge of the eyewall approaching southern Pearl River County, MS; tornado warning issued 8:42 AM CDT citing imminent "destructive winds with gusts of 100 to 120 mph," effective until 9:45 AM CDT. — source: NWS New Orleans/Baton Rouge Tornado (Eye Wall Wind) Warning — https://www.weather.gov/lix/TOR_29_0842 — confidence: HIGH
- **8:54 AM CDT (1354 UTC)**: Last full-volume scan transmitted by the KLIX (Slidell) WSR-88D radar before data transmission was lost as the eyewall passed directly over the radar site. — source: AMS confex paper citing NCDC data — confidence: MED
- **~9:45 AM CDT (1445 UTC)**: Second landfall near the Louisiana/Mississippi border (Category 3, 121 mph sustained). — source: NHC TCR AL122005 — confidence: HIGH
- **"Dodged the bullet" media narrative — VERDICT: the literal phrase is NOT found in a same-day broadcast/wire artifact, but an equivalent, verbatim, dated broadcast artifact carrying the identical misdirection framing WAS found and is citable.**
  - **Verbatim artifact found (this is the recommended hook pull target):** NPR, program **"Day to Day,"** story published/aired **30 August 2005, 12:00 AM ET**, reported by **Phillip Davis**, hosted by **Alex Chadwick**. Title/deck (verbatim): **"New Orleans Spared the Worst, But Still Reeling."** Deck text (verbatim): *"New Orleans was spared the worst of Hurricane Katrina, but the city is still suffering from extensive wind and flood damage and the streets linking an estimated 80 percent of the metro area are still flooded."* Note the irony baked into the same segment: host Chadwick, in the same broadcast, says "In New Orleans, two large breaches in levees continue to pour water from Lake Pontchartrain into the city" — i.e., NPR published the "spared" framing in the same story that already described the levee breaches, which is exactly the misdirection beat. — source: NPR, https://www.npr.org/2005/08/30/4823849/new-orleans-spared-the-worst-but-still-reeling — confidence: HIGH (verbatim transcript text fetched directly)
  - **Corroborating primary-adjacent detail**: NHC Advisory #27 (29 Aug 2005) reported the storm center passing the LA/MS state line "35 miles away from the city" around 11 AM, which is the meteorological fact the "spared" framing was hung on — the storm's core missing downtown New Orleans by that margin. — source pointer via FactCheck.org's contemporaneous (Sept. 2005) timeline citing "Hurricane Katrina Advisory Number 27," National Hurricane Center — confidence: MED (the NHC advisory itself should be pulled directly from the archive at https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/archive/2005/pub/ for a fully primary cite if this becomes the hook's factual anchor)
  - **Counter-evidence the narrative had already reversed by evening**: a Wayback Machine capture of the CNN.com homepage timestamped **"Updated: 5:55 p.m. EDT (21:55 GMT), August 29, 2005"** shows headlines already reading "Life-threatening flooding next fear," "Katrina hit Mississippi 'like a ton of bricks,'" "Parts of six Louisiana parishes 'devastated,'" and "New Orleans: No sewage, water, power or gas" — i.e., by early evening the same day, CNN's own homepage had moved past any "spared" framing into disaster-scale coverage. This is directly screenshot-able/citable. — source: Wayback Machine capture, http://web.archive.org/web/20050829215759/http://www.cnn.com:80/ — confidence: HIGH (raw HTML fetched directly, not paraphrased)
  - **The literal phrase "dodged the bullet"**: still NOT confirmed to any same-day (29 August 2005) on-the-record broadcast or wire source in this pass. Retrospective accounts (e.g., Sally Forman, Nagin's communications director, quoted years later) attribute the sentiment to the immediate post-landfall period, and a 2005 Media Matters analysis argues officials (Bush, Chertoff) used the phrase afterward but disputes it was ever a genuine, non-refuted press narrative on 29-30 August itself — no front page in a 46-country/477-newspaper archive dated 30 August carried a "dodged the bullet" headline. **Recommendation: use the NPR "spared the worst" artifact as the documentary's on-screen broadcast pull (it is verbatim, dated, and sourced) rather than the folk-memory phrase "dodged the bullet," which cannot be attributed to a specific same-day broadcast.**
