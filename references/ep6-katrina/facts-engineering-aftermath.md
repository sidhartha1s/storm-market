## 1. THE BREACHES

- **17th Street Canal breach** — floodwall failed when canal water reached ~10.6 ft above mean sea level (well below the wall's ~14 ft top elevation) — NO overtopping occurred; failure was a foundation/deflection failure, not overtopping — source: IPET Volume V (Performance — Levees and Floodwalls) https://biotech.law.lsu.edu/katrina/ipet/FINAL%20Vol%20V%20The%20Performance%20-%20Levees%20and%20Floodwalls%20-%20maintext%20-%2001Jun07.pdf — confidence HIGH
- **17th St mechanism (IPET)**: unbalanced hydrostatic loading caused the I-wall to rotate/deflect outward, opening a gap on the inboard (landside) face between the wall and the soil; water filled this gap and drove pressure to the base, shearing a weak soil plug — a thin layer of sensitive marsh/organic clay beneath the levee — allowing a foundation failure without the wall ever being overtopped — source: IPET Vol V, ASCE External Review Panel report https://biotech.law.lsu.edu/katrina/reports/erpreport.pdf — confidence HIGH
- **17th St sheet-pile depth discrepancy (net-info-gain candidate)**: 1993 design drawings specified sheet piling driven to 17.5 ft below sea level; November 2005 sonar soundings of the failed wall found the actual sheet piles reached only ~10 ft below sea level — roughly 7 ft (2.1 m) shallower than the design called for. Root cause traced to USACE's own misreading of a mid-1980s sheet-pile load test (the "E-99" study): the Corps concluded piles need not go past ~17 ft, when the underlying test data implied 31–46 ft was needed for the soft clays present — source: sonar findings widely reported, see Wikipedia 17th Street Canal (pointer) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17th_Street_Canal ; IPET's official position is that the as-built depth "met contemporary standards" for 1960s design — this is a live dispute IPET does not fully resolve — confidence MED (depth numbers well-sourced; IPET's contemporary-standards defense is their position, not an independent finding)
- **London Avenue Canal — east breach** (near Mirabeau/5000 Warrington Drive): occurred approximately 6–7 a.m. Aug 29, 2005 — source: New Orleans Historical https://neworleanshistorical.org/items/show/276 (secondary/pointer) — confidence MED
- **London Avenue Canal — west breach** (Lake Vista/6100 Pratt Drive): occurred approximately 7–9:30 a.m. Aug 29 (sources vary: some say 7-8am, other accounts place the two 30-ft monolith failures closer to 9:30am) — flag as disputed timing — source: New Orleans Historical (pointer), IPET Vol V for engineering detail — confidence MED (mechanism), LOW-MED (exact time)
- **London Avenue mechanism**: similar I-wall/foundation failure to 17th Street — underseepage through a buried sand layer beneath the levee raised pore pressure until the foundation failed and the wall lurched, again below the design flood elevation — source: IPET Vol V — confidence HIGH
- **Industrial Canal (Inner Harbor Navigation Canal) — first/upper breach**: near the junction with MRGO, reported to have failed during the storm's peak surge, morning of Aug 29 — source: IPET Vol V; New Orleans Historical (Lower Ninth Ward Levee Breaches) https://neworleanshistorical.org/items/show/288 — confidence MED-HIGH
- **Industrial Canal — second/lower breach**: approximately 7:45 a.m., described by witnesses as an "explosive sound," widened rapidly to roughly a 1,000-ft gap; combined floodwaters from the two Industrial Canal breaches submerged the entire Lower Ninth Ward — source: New Orleans Historical (pointer); GAO and IPET timeline references — confidence MED-HIGH
- **Industrial Canal mechanism**: unlike the drainage-canal I-walls, IPET/ILIT found the Industrial Canal floodwalls were subject to significant overtopping and surge forces consistent with the storm's actual intensity in that area — a materially different failure mode from the 17th St/London Ave "failed below design load" story; the ING 4727 barge (see item 10) was long blamed by residents/litigants as the cause of the breach, but the Berkeley-led ILIT investigation concluded the barge passed *through* an already-open breach caused by foundation failure — it did not cause the breach — source: ILIT Ch. 3 report https://biotech.law.lsu.edu/katrina/ILIT/report/CH_3.pdf ; nola.com trial coverage https://www.nola.com/news/crime_police/trial-to-decide-whether-barge-broke-industrial-canal-floodwall-during-hurricane-katrina-begins/article_77c03069-5291-5a1f-ad7e-e7b44075817c.html — confidence HIGH (ILIT conclusion); 2008 federal court in New Orleans found Ingram Barge not liable — confidence HIGH
- **Overtopping vs. breach — overall scale, RESOLVED against IPET directly**: IPET's own investigation enumerates **50 total breaches** system-wide. Of those, **4 breaches were foundation failures** — the wall failed BEFORE water reached the top/crest (this is the 17th St Canal / London Ave Canal failure class — "failed below design load"); the remaining **46 breaches were overtopping-induced** — water cascaded over the crest first, causing scour/erosion that then breached the structure (this is the dominant Industrial Canal / MRGO / Plaquemines failure class) — source: IPET Vol V (Performance — Levees and Floodwalls) https://biotech.law.lsu.edu/katrina/ipet/FINAL%20Vol%20V%20The%20Performance%20-%20Levees%20and%20Floodwalls%20-%20maintext%20-%2001Jun07.pdf ; corroborated by National Academies IPET summary brief https://nap.nationalacademies.org/resource/12647/IPET_brief_final.pdf — confidence HIGH
- **MRGO levee**: overtopped and breached in almost two dozen places, flooding all of St. Bernard Parish, half of Plaquemines Parish, and much of Orleans Parish including the Lower Ninth Ward — confidence MED
- **Plaquemines Parish**: levees overtopped and damaged in many locations, producing about a dozen full breaches — confidence MED

## 2. THE ENGINEERING VERDICTS

- **IPET's core conclusion**: "the hurricane protection system in New Orleans did not perform as a system" — the system failed not solely from Katrina's severity but from design shortfalls, incomplete construction, and lack of redundancy — source: IPET Final Report summary (paraphrase widely used; verify exact page in IPET Vol I executive summary before quoting verbatim on air) — confidence MED (concept is IPET's, exact phrasing needs primary-doc verification — see OPEN ITEMS)
- **USACE's own report language**: the flood defense system "was compromised by the incompleteness of the system, the inconsistency in levels of protection, and the lack of redundancy" — source: NBC News coverage of Corps report release https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna13089236 — confidence MED (direct quote reported by NBC from the Corps' own report; verify against primary IPET/USACE document text)
- **Lt. Gen. Carl Strock (USACE Chief Engineer) admission**: "This is the first time the Corps has had to stand up and say we had a catastrophic failure of one of our projects." — reported June 1–2, 2006, at the release of the Corps' internal review — source: NBC News https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna13089236 , corroborated by Spokesman-Review https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2006/jun/02/army-corps-takes-blame-for-failure-of-levees/ — confidence HIGH
- **ASCE characterization**: American Society of Civil Engineers, in an April 2007 review, called the New Orleans flooding "the worst engineering catastrophe in US history" — source: ASCE External Review Panel report https://biotech.law.lsu.edu/katrina/reports/erpreport.pdf ; ASCE "Lessons of Katrina" https://www.asce.org/publications-and-news/civil-engineering-source/civil-engineering-magazine/article/2015/07/the-lessons-of-katrina — confidence HIGH
- **ASCE's two named design mistakes**: (1) overestimating the soil's shear-strength/resistance beneath the floodwalls, (2) failing to account for the water-filled gap that formed at the base of the I-walls under load — source: ASCE ERP report (as above) — confidence HIGH
- **ILIT (Berkeley-led team) conclusion**: the levees failed because of design and construction errors compounded by insufficient funding and inadequate oversight by federal, state, and local agencies — released July 31, 2006, funded by the National Science Foundation — source: ILIT Final Report https://biotech.law.lsu.edu/katrina/ILIT/index.html ; UC Berkeley News release https://newsarchive.berkeley.edu/news/media/releases/2006/05/24_leveereport.shtml — confidence HIGH
- **RESOLVED — verbatim quote found**: "the system did not perform as a system," ... "The hurricane protection in New Orleans . . . was a system in name only." — attributed to the Army Corps' own post-mortem (i.e., the IPET investigation) — source: City Journal, "Katrina's Real Lesson" https://www.city-journal.org/article/katrinas-real-lesson (secondary outlet quoting the Corps' post-mortem directly; the article does not itself give a page/volume citation) — confidence MED-HIGH (quote wording confirmed; exact IPET volume/page number NOT independently pinned — repeated direct-fetch attempts on IPET's own PDFs (IPET_Summary_USGS.pdf, IPET Vol V) returned HTTP 403/unreadable-binary in this pass). ## 3. HURRICANE PAM EXERCISE (July 2004)

- **Who ran it**: FEMA convened the exercise; the simulation itself was designed and run by Baton Rouge-based contractor Innovative Emergency Management, Inc. (IEM) — source: Congress.gov Senate Hearing S.Hrg. 109-403 https://www.congress.gov/event/109th-congress/senate-event/LC11307/text ; IEM's own account https://iem.com/planning-exercise-results-in-usable-plans-the-fictional-hurricane-pam/ — confidence HIGH
- **When/scale**: ran July 16–23, 2004, bringing together roughly 300 local, state, and federal emergency officials — source: House "A Failure of Initiative" report, Hurricane Pam appendix https://biotech.law.lsu.edu/katrina/govdocs/109-377/pam.pdf — confidence HIGH
- **Scenario simulated**: a hypothetical Category 3 storm named "Pam" with sustained winds of 120 mph, up to 20 inches of rain in parts of southeast Louisiana, and storm surge that topped the levees — source: House report Pam appendix (as above) — confidence HIGH
- **Predicted numbers**: more than 1 million residents evacuated; 500,000–600,000 buildings destroyed; ~175,000 injured; 200,000 made sick; more than 60,000 killed — source: House "A Failure of Initiative," Pam appendix (as above) — confidence HIGH
- **Which predictions came true**: Pam did NOT predict the levees would actually break (it modeled overtopping, not breach) — that possibility appeared instead in the Times-Picayune's 2002 "Washing Away" series, not in Pam. Where Pam proved eerily accurate: overcrowded shelters short of food/water/essentials, blocked highways stranding evacuees in floodwater, hospitals overwhelmed and running out of generator fuel — source: House report Pam appendix; NPR retrospective https://www.npr.org/2005/08/31/4827395/media-sounded-early-warnings-of-the-worst — confidence HIGH
- **Why follow-up stalled**: the Pam exercise was meant to be the first of a multi-phase planning process to turn the tabletop scenario into a fully executable catastrophic hurricane plan for southeast Louisiana; that follow-on work was incomplete at the time Katrina hit because of funding shortfalls and the exercise's later planning phases not being finished/funded — source: Government Executive retrospective https://www.govexec.com/defense/2006/11/emergency-preparedness-exercises-remain-an-imperfect-science/23196/ ; House report Pam appendix — confidence MED-HIGH (funding-stall narrative is well-attested across sources; get exact appropriations figure from the House report text directly for an on-air stat — see OPEN ITEMS)

## 4. DECADES OF PRIOR WARNINGS

- **Lake Pontchartrain & Vicinity Hurricane Protection Project**: authorized in 1965 (post–Hurricane Betsy) to protect the Lake Pontchartrain basin against a "Standard Project Hurricane" — source: GAO-06-244T, "History of the Lake Pontchartrain and Vicinity Hurricane Protection Project" https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-06-244t — confidence HIGH
- **% complete by 2005**: reported at approximately **65 percent complete** as of 2005, some 40 years after authorization — source: GAO-05-1050T / GAO-06-244T (GAO congressional testimony) https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-05-1050t — confidence HIGH
- **Caveat on that 65%**: GAO also notes the completion percentage doesn't capture that many "completed" reaches were already below their design grade due to datum/surveying errors made during construction and ongoing regional land subsidence — source: GAO-06-244T (as above) — confidence HIGH
- **Times-Picayune "Washing Away" series — RESOLVED (dates + direct URLs + verbatim quotes)**: six-part series (published across 5 days) by reporters John McQuaid and Mark Schleifstein, running **June 23–26, 2002** — direct URLs: series landing page https://www.nola.com/environment/page/washing_away_2002.html ; "The Big One" https://www.nola.com/news/environment/article_668440a9-4e07-50a3-b42b-5e1ad45b7221.html ; "Left Behind" https://www.nola.com/news/environment/left-behind/article_a1cbd344-e506-5eec-9d90-fc720c8fac08.html — confidence HIGH (dates cross-checked against academic archive copy at https://msuweb.montclair.edu/~furrg/essays/tpnoseries02.html)
  - Verbatim opening warning: **"It's only a matter of time before South Louisiana takes a direct hit from a major hurricane."**
  - Verbatim death-toll prediction: **"A likely death toll would be between 25,000 and 100,000 people, dwarfing estimated death tolls for other natural disasters."**
  - Verbatim flood-mechanics warning: **"The water will flow through the city and stop only when it reaches the next higher thing. The most continuous barrier is the south levee, along the river. That's 25 feet high, so you'll see the water pile up on the river levee."**
  - This series, not the 2004 Pam exercise, was the primary-source document that explicitly floated levee breach/failure as a mechanism (Pam only modeled overtopping) — confidence HIGH
- **MRGO "funnel" claims**: post-Katrina engineering modeling found MRGO intensified the initial storm surge by about 20%, raised the surge height by roughly 3 feet, and increased surge velocity from about 3 ft/s to 8 ft/s at the funnel-shaped junction with the Gulf Intracoastal Waterway — source: URS Corporation study, "The Direct Impact of the Mississippi River Gulf Outlet on Hurricane Storm Surge" https://biotech.law.lsu.edu/katrina/URS/index.htm — confidence MED-HIGH (note: at least one other study found MRGO did not significantly affect peak surge because surge conveyance was dominated by flow across the full width of the coastal lakes/marsh — flag as a genuine scientific disagreement, not settled)
- **MRGO litigation (In re Katrina Canal Breaches Litigation)**: 2009 ruling by U.S. District Judge Stanwood Duval found the Army Corps liable for negligent maintenance of MRGO (calling the Corps' conduct "insouciance, myopia and shortsightedness"), awarding roughly $719,000 to four plaintiffs from the Lower Ninth Ward/St. Bernard Parish — crucially, Duval held that the drainage-canal levee failures (17th St, London Ave, Industrial Canal) were shielded by sovereign immunity under the 1928 Flood Control Act, but MRGO — being a navigation channel, not a flood-control structure — was NOT shielded — source: Center for Progressive Reform https://progressivereform.org/cpr-blog/katrina-ruling-breaches-sovereign-immunity/ ; CS Monitor https://www.csmonitor.com/USA/2009/1119/p02s20-usgn.html — confidence HIGH
- **Appeal outcome**: the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals overturned Duval's MRGO liability ruling, reinstating government immunity and shielding the Corps from damages after all — source: FindLaw case record, "IN RE: KATRINA CANAL BREACHES LITIGATION" (2012) https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-5th-circuit/1611308.html — confidence HIGH

## 5. FLOODING EXTENT

- **% of New Orleans flooded**: approximately 80 percent of the city was submerged — source: widely corroborated across NPR, NASA Earth Observatory https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/15445/hurricane-katrina-floods-new-orleans and CNN retrospective reporting — confidence HIGH
- **St. Bernard Parish**: approximately 95 percent flooded — source: History.com summary of ASCE/IPET findings (secondary, pointer) — confidence MED
- **How long water stayed / dewatering**: most floodwater was pumped from the main New Orleans basins in about 11 days — far faster than the ~3 months many initially predicted — though the lowest-lying parts of eastern New Orleans took additional weeks. Total dewatering was declared complete October 11, 2005 — 43 days after landfall — despite Hurricane Rita reflooding roughly 40% of the city in late September — source: nola.com retrospective by Richard Campanella https://www.nola.com/entertainment_life/flooding-katrina-swb-campanella/article_44b6a98e-0bde-42b1-bdd0-860167fcf165.html ; NPR "Heroic Efforts Empty New Orleans of Floodwaters" https://www.npr.org/2005/09/26/4864743/heroic-efforts-empty-new-orleans-of-floodwaters — confidence HIGH
- **Pumping rate (net-info-gain candidate)**: at peak, roughly 380 cubic meters of water per second were being pumped out of the city by the Sewerage and Water Board, supplemented by the Army Corps' Task Force Unwatering — source: nola.com/Campanella retrospective (as above) — confidence MED-HIGH

## 6. THE WEEK AFTER

- **Superdome — population**: served as a shelter of last resort for approximately 30,000 evacuees at its peak (some accounts range 25,000–30,000 — pin the number used to a single primary/near-primary cite before air) — source: NOLA.com Katrina archive retrospective https://www.nola.com/news/katrina/katrina-archives-rape-murder-gunfights-at-the-superdome-it-never-happened/article_0e2fab7b-3865-4f71-a8b7-914f77c28e68.html — confidence MED-HIGH
- **Superdome — verified deaths (myth vs. record)**: the OFFICIAL verified death count inside the Superdome was six — four from natural causes, one from an apparent drug overdose, one an apparent suicide. This is in sharp contrast to unverified rumors at the time of as many as 200 bodies. NPR's 2015 retrospective on the "shelter of last resort" documents how the initial atrocity narrative (murders, rapes) was subsequently found unsupported by evidence — source: NPR "At A Shelter Of Last Resort, Decency Prevailed Over Depravity" https://www.npr.org/2015/08/25/431909047/at-a-shelter-of-last-resort-decency-prevailed-over-depravity ; NOLA.com Katrina archive (as above) — confidence HIGH
- **Superdome full-evacuation date — RESOLVED**: the last approximately 300 remaining evacuees departed by bus on **Saturday, September 3, 2005**, described by NBC News as leaving behind "a darkened and stinking arena strewn with trash" — the Superdome was declared fully evacuated by **September 4, 2005** — source: NBC News, "Superdome evacuation completed" https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna9175611 — confidence MED-HIGH (content confirmed via search-engine text extraction of the NBC article; direct WebFetch of the URL was blocked with HTTP 403 in this pass, so the exact byline/dateline was not independently re-verified against the live page). Closed for repairs for roughly 13 months afterward — confidence MED (secondary sourcing only for the repair-duration figure)
- **Convention Center — when people gathered**: evacuees began congregating at the Ernest N. Morial Convention Center starting August 31, 2005, on the direction of New Orleans law enforcement — even though the city had not designated it an official shelter and it had no food, water, or security staffing — source: White House "Hurricane Katrina: Lessons Learned," Chapter 4 https://georgewbush-whitehouse.archives.gov/reports/katrina-lessons-learned/chapter4.html — confidence HIGH
- **Convention Center — when officials learned**: federal officials, including DHS Secretary Michael Chertoff and FEMA Director Michael Brown, publicly claimed on/around September 1–2 that they had only just learned of the crowds at the Convention Center — despite reports having reached authorities days earlier; estimates of the crowd there ran as high as 25,000 — source: White House Lessons Learned Ch. 4 (as above) — confidence HIGH
- **Convention Center — when relief arrived**: a caravan of at least three dozen National Guard trucks and buses arrived roughly four-plus days after landfall (i.e., around September 2, 2005) to evacuate the crowd — source: White House Lessons Learned Ch. 4 (as above) — confidence HIGH
- **Communications collapse specifics**: Katrina knocked out roughly 3 million customer phone lines, seriously damaged telephone switching centers, and disabled 1,477 cell towers across the Gulf Coast; New Orleans Police Department officers lost the ability to communicate with each other, and NOPD headquarters itself had to be evacuated — source: White House "Lessons Learned" report, Ch. 4 (as above) — confidence HIGH

- **Displaced population**: more than 1 million people were displaced across the Gulf Coast region; as many as 600,000 households remained displaced a full month after landfall — source: Displacement after Hurricane Katrina (secondary/pointer, cites Data Center/Brookings-style figures) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displacement_after_Hurricane_Katrina ; Data Center "Facts for Features" https://www.datacenterresearch.org/data-resources/katrina/facts-for-impact/ — confidence MED-HIGH (need to pin down the primary Census Bureau or Brookings displacement study directly for an exact citation — see OPEN ITEMS)

- **Houston Astrodome**: housed approximately 15,000 evacuees at its peak — source: history/retrospective reporting (secondary/pointer) — confidence MED

- **Elderly toll — LA Dept. of Health data (strong on-air stat)**: **49% of Louisiana Katrina victims were age 75 or older** — a group that made up less than 6% of the greater New Orleans/Louisiana population — making this age cohort dramatically, statistically overrepresented among the dead (p < .0001). Nearly 60% of all Louisiana fatalities were over age 65. Mean victim age was 69.0 years (95% CI 67.8–70.2). Leading causes of death: drowning (40%), injury/trauma (25%), heart conditions (11%) — source: LDH "Hurricane Katrina Deaths, Louisiana, 2005" report (as above) — confidence HIGH

## 9. ACCOUNTABILITY OUTCOMES

- **House "A Failure of Initiative" report**: final report of the Select Bipartisan Committee to Investigate the Preparation for and Response to Hurricane Katrina, issued February 15, 2006 — source: full text at govinfo.gov https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRPT-109hrpt377/html/CRPT-109hrpt377.htm — confidence HIGH
- **Key finding quotes**:
  - On the levees: "There was a failure of initiative to get beyond design and organizational compromises to improve the level of protection afforded," attributing the failure to "some combination of nature's wrath ... and man's folly (an assumption that the design, construction, and maintenance of the levees would be flawless)."
  - On command/control: "No one is in charge," per a scientist's testimony to investigators, describing "a complete breakdown in communications that paralyzed command and control."
  - Overall verdict: "Our report is a litany of mistakes, misjudgments, lapses, and absurdities all cascading together."
  - Source for all three: House Report 109-377 full text (as above) — confidence HIGH (verify exact page numbers against the govinfo.gov text before air-quoting)
- **Sovereign immunity outcome**: because the drainage-canal levees (17th St, London Ave, Industrial Canal) were built and operated as part of a federally authorized FLOOD CONTROL project, the Flood Control Act of 1928's sovereign-immunity provision shielded the U.S. government/Army Corps from liability for their failure — meaning, despite IPET's, ILIT's, and the Corps' OWN admissions of design and construction fault, no plaintiff was able to collect damages for the drainage-canal breaches. The one exception (MRGO, a navigation channel, not immune) was itself later overturned on appeal (see Section 4) — source: Center for Progressive Reform analysis https://progressivereform.org/cpr-blog/katrina-ruling-breaches-sovereign-immunity/ ; Fifth Circuit ruling (FindLaw, as above) — confidence HIGH
- **HSDRRS rebuild**: the $14.5 billion Hurricane & Storm Damage Risk Reduction System — 133 miles of perimeter levees/floodwalls, canal closure structures with pumps, including the Permanent Canal Closures and Pumps system — was formally completed and handed over to the state of Louisiana on **May 27, 2022**, designed to provide a 100-year level of storm-surge risk reduction (i.e., protection against a surge event with a 1% annual chance of occurring/being exceeded) — source: nola.com photo feature on handover https://www.nola.com/multimedia/photos/photos-the-storm-damage-risk-reduction-system-built-to-protect-new-orleans-is-handed-over/collection_d43ffb2e-ddeb-11ec-8ef0-af594bc60bb5.html ; Defense Media Network https://www.defensemedianetwork.com/stories/usace-delivers-hurricane-and-storm-damage-risk-reduction-system-hsdrrs/ — confidence HIGH

## 10. NET-INFO-GAIN CANDIDATES (best bets, flagged)

1. **★ BEST: The 7-foot sheet-pile shortfall at 17th Street Canal.** Design drawings called for sheet piling driven to 17.5 ft below sea level; post-failure sonar found it only reached ~10 ft — and the root cause traces to the Corps misreading its OWN 1980s load-test data (the "E-99" study), concluding 17 ft was adequate when the underlying data implied 31–46 ft was actually needed. This is a specific, vivid, verifiable engineering-negligence detail that most competitor videos gloss over as "the levees were badly built" — this is the receipt. Needs one more primary-source pass (IPET's own discussion of the E-99 misinterpretation) before quoting the 31-46ft figure verbatim on air.
2. **★ STRONG: The Hurricane Pam exercise's eerie specificity.** A full-scale FEMA/IEM tabletop simulation just 13 months before Katrina predicted 1M+ evacuees, 500,000-600,000 destroyed structures, and — critically — the EXACT failure modes that actually played out (overwhelmed shelters, stranded highway evacuees, hospitals out of generator fuel) — yet the follow-up planning to turn Pam into an executable real plan was never finished/funded before Katrina hit. The gap between "we literally ran this simulation" and "we were still unprepared" is a strong narrative beat.
3. **STRONG: The Superdome myth-vs-record gap.** Rumors during the crisis put the Superdome body count as high as 200; the verified official count was 6 (four natural causes, one overdose, one suicide) — none violent. This is a well-documented "the truth was worse in a different way" beat (systemic failure, not mob violence) that reframes the popular narrative.
4. **GOOD: The barge ING 4727.** Widely used as a visual icon of Lower Ninth Ward devastation (a barge washed into a residential street), but the actual engineering/legal record (ILIT + 2008 federal court ruling) found the barge did NOT cause the Industrial Canal breach — it drifted through an already-failed floodwall. A good "correct the popular image" beat if the show wants a myth-bust moment tied to a striking visual.
