- **[11:50–12:21]** Blackhawk-assisted rescue on Janette [Jeannette] Street — army picking up sick/injured one at a time.
- **[12:27–13:38]** Levee-failure explainer: narration states NONE of New Orleans's earthen levees failed — failure was in floodwalls (17th Street Canal) only 2 feet thick, designed in 1965 for a Category 3 storm; Katrina (Cat 4) overtopped them. **Al Naomi**, Army Corps of Engineers flood-control manager, says a billion dollars and 20 years' lead time would have been needed to Cat-4-proof the walls; "I don't think it could have been done in time."
- **[14:21–15:20]** President Bush visits; shares a shower aboard Air Force One with Nagin (his first hot water in 5 days). Nagin recounts the exchange: Bush allegedly said "I know we could have done a better job and we're going to fix it."
- **[16:00–17:05]** Nagin previews grim body-count math: ~80% evacuated of ~pre-storm population, ~50,000 "unaccounted for," predicts "significant" casualties. UNTRUSTED-COMPETITOR-CLAIM.

### Chapter 3 — "Order Out of Chaos" (17:05–30:42, orig. aired Sept 11, 2005)
- **[17:06–17:50]** Scene-set: two weeks post-Katrina; 80% of NOPD officers lost their homes; "nearly a third" of cops still missing/unaccounted; ~1,200 stayed on the job.
- **[17:50–23:10] Superintendent Eddie Compass** (NOPD chief), filmed riding in back of a National Guard truck through flooded streets. Describes: holding the city together with no comms/resources/ammo; switching between search-and-rescue and tactical response because people were shooting at fire trucks and rescue teams; an attempted kidnapping of himself at the convention center by a crowd hoping to trade him for a bus; calls officers who abandoned their posts "cowards" — "less than a coward."
- **[23:10–23:44]** Compass swears in Police Academy cadets after only 10 weeks training, wherever he finds them.
- **[23:44–24:03] Veteran officer Bruce Adams** — cops wearing the same shirt for 10 days, no ID gear.
- **[24:00–27:04]** City divided into search grids ("everything in red" = still underwater). Ride-along on a rescue mission with National Guardsmen + NOPD vice cops; a family at "8:42 Algo [Argonne?] Street" persuaded to evacuate, one member with an untreated cut; a retired cop, his wife, and two others rescued from a home; an elderly woman across the street ("I'm 70 years old... Thank you, Jesus") evacuated — her first time outside since the storm and first sight of the destruction.
- **[27:04–29:31]** Officers' personal losses: **Lt. David Benelli** (head of NOPD sex crimes division) and wife **Becky Benelli** (sergeant, crime lab) turn their spared house into "Camp Benelli," housing 24 homeless cops. **Capt. Tammy Brassette** lost her house. **Officer Shauna Pichon** found her mother but still searching for son/father/brother. **Officer Daryl Gordon** — wife and 3 kids relocating to Houston while he stays on the job, paying $70/day for a hotel for them.
- **[29:31–30:42]** Compass closes: "every police officer that stayed and worked and protected life is a hero."

### Chapter 4 — "The Bridge to Gretna" (30:42–44:16, orig. aired Dec 18, 2005)
Framed ~4 months after Katrina, investigating the Gretna Police Department blocking evacuees from crossing the Crescent City Connection bridge.
- **[30:44–33:27]** Setup: bridge links New Orleans to the (mostly white, 17,500-pop, 35% Black minority) West Bank suburb of Gretna. 3 days post-Katrina, ~6,000 people crossed and were bused out; the next day a second wave — tourists from the French Quarter plus hundreds of locals — was turned back at gunpoint.
- **[33:27–36:00] Eyewitnesses interviewed:** **Kathy Golden** (evacuee, now lives in Boston, was visiting hometown with her daughter/son/friends); **Larry Bradshaw** and **Lorie Beth Slonsky** (paramedics from San Francisco, in town for a convention, stranded when their hotel lost power/plumbing after 4 days); **Chiron Hollowman** (evacuee) — all describe Gretna police racking shotguns and firing a warning shot over the crowd. Bradshaw: police told them "we're not going to have any super domes over here and this is not New Orleans" — which he read as coded racial language. A Gretna police cruiser later blared "get the [expletive] off the bridge" over its loudspeaker and allegedly pointed a gun at evacuees; a helicopter's downdraft scattered their makeshift highway encampment.
- **[35:55–37:00]** Golden/Hollowman: crowd was "95% African-American"; they believe race was the reason, though acknowledge no direct evidence beyond appearance and the officers being white.
- **[35:55–43:00] Gretna Mayor Ronnie Harris** defends the decision: Gretna itself had no power/shelter/medical services/city services, was "completely on its own," and was alarmed by media reports of looting, arson, and rape in New Orleans; feared being overwhelmed. **Gretna Police Chief Arthur Lawson** declined a fresh interview but is shown in archival CBS News footage defending the bridge closure as protecting his own residents; when pressed on firing a shotgun to turn back "old folks and children," calls it a necessary "dead end" seal given no available shelter.
- **[40:55–43:00] Oliver Thomas** (President, New Orleans City Council) blasts the decision as inhumane, noting it was a state-owned bridge and the crowd was nonviolent, "one calculated step at a time." Notes Gretna's own city council later unanimously passed a resolution defending the closure, and residents posted "thank you" yard signs for the police chief.
- **[44:02–44:16]** Closer: the Louisiana Attorney General's office was investigating possible civil-rights violations. UNTRUSTED-COMPETITOR-CLAIM (no resolution given in this program).

### Chapter 5 — "One Year Later" (44:16–56:48, orig. aired Aug 27, 2006)
Correspondent: **Byron Pitts** (transcribed "Byron Pittz").
- **[44:17–46:10]** Katrina killed "more than 1,300" people; new levee walls now built to 20 ft (vs. 12–13 ft pre-Katrina) with anti-scouring reinforcement; 220 miles of levees repaired by Army Corps of Engineers.
- **[46:10–48:35] Nagin interview:** claims New Orleans is "ready for another hurricane like Katrina"; admits his delay in ordering mandatory evacuation "almost certainly cost lives" and says he privately blames himself; describes a revised evacuation plan — no shelter of last resort, no Superdome, no convention center, everyone leaves via bus/train/plane for Cat-3+ storms. Confirms ~600 dead in New Orleans specifically.
- **[48:35–49:16]** Neighborhood devastation still visible a year on; 6 of 9 hospitals still closed; school enrollment down from ~65,000 to a projected 21,000; thousands still in government trailers; Nagin defensive about slow debris cleanup ("you guys in New York can't get a hole in the ground fixed... five years later").
- **[49:16–51:32] Leonard Moore**, LSU professor of African-American history, writing a book critical of Nagin titled *"An Oreo in Chocolate City,"* says bluntly Nagin is "too damaged to be an effective politician," challenges him to "grab hold of the reins" for Black residents.
- **[51:32–52:00] Oliver Thomas** (City Council President) again — more sympathetic, says the city is "sinking or swimming" with Nagin for better or worse.
- **[52:00–53:16]** National Guard (300 troops) still deployed to fight a murder spike; Nagin: "if I could deputize King Kong and Godzilla, I'd do it tomorrow" to fight drug-turf violence.
- **[53:16–56:00]** Structural-inequality thread: half the city's population still displaced, disproportionately poor and Black; public housing projects slated for demolition (~13,000 residents pre-storm); Trump International Tower condo development confirmed; **Jim Carville** (longtime political advisor to NOLA mayors since 1969, worked for Nagin) warns of the city becoming "a playground for the rich," predicts "the Las Vegas of the South." Debutante balls (white society) went ahead as scheduled just 4 months after Katrina while Black social clubs canceled — Carville frames this as "the white establishment" seizing a "musical chairs" moment as Black residents were displaced.
- **[56:00–56:48]** Nagin insists a "new" inclusive New Orleans is his goal; closes on $15B in federal aid allocated but barely delivered.

### Chapter 6 — "Memorial" / "Was It Murder?" (56:48–70:07, orig. aired Sept 24, 2006)
- **[56:53–58:56]** Setup: 1,000+ bodies recovered citywide post-Katrina; 34 patient deaths at **Memorial Medical Center**, stranded in 10 ft of water/no power for 4 days; Louisiana Attorney General **Charles Foti** (transcribed "Charles Foty"/"Fod") charged that 4 of those deaths were homicides, not natural/environmental. **Dr. Anna Pou** (head/neck cancer surgeon, 15+ years practicing) and nurses **Cheri Landry** ("Sher Landry") and **Lori Budo** ("Lorie Budo") were arrested, accused of injecting patients with a "lethal cocktail" of two drugs.
- **[58:56–59:39]** Dr. Pou, first on-camera: "It is unbelievably shocking for me... I am not a murderer... we are not murderers."
- **[59:39–1:00:29]** Timeline: Pou chose to stay with patients after being offered a chance to leave; by early Tuesday levees failing, water rising ~1 ft/hour.
- **[1:00:29–1:02:37]** Conditions: 10 ft floodwater, no power, no ventilators, no phones, 110°F heat ("relentless... suffocating"), human-waste smell, authorities told Memorial it was not a rescue priority because rooftop-stranded residents came first. Nurses Landry/Budo: "Where are the boats? Where are the helicopters? Why isn't anybody coming?" — both say they stayed until every patient/visitor/family member was evacuated first. The 7th floor (Life Care acute-care unit, a separate company operating inside Memorial) was the most critical; its own doctor never showed, so Pou and others improvised care. Patients carried down/up as many as 7 flights of stairs to a helipad; several died mid-evacuation. By Wednesday night, doctors call the hospital "pitch black... a death trap," "a battlefield."
- **[1:02:37–1:03:24] Dr. John Cookamore** (name likely garbled by auto-caption, needs verification against original credits — possibly Dr. John Kokemor / Thiele) — describes examining patients by flashlight; recounts word spreading Thursday morning that no organized rescue was coming — "we were dumbfounded and in a bit of shock... we were being abandoned"; says the only alternative would have been abandoning patients, which was "unacceptable... to virtually all of our doctors."
- **[1:03:52–1:04:51]** Per AG Foti's affidavit: doctors waded into floodwaters trying to commandeer boats; witnesses allege Dr. Pou told 7th-floor staff there wasn't much time, a decision had been made to give patients (too sick to move) lethal doses, and that she "took full responsibility"; staff then allegedly seen entering rooms with syringes/vials.
- **[1:04:51–1:06:04] Attorney General Foti** on camera: "this is not euthanasia. This is plain and simple homicide." Says his office investigated 10.5 months before charging; cites post-mortem toxicology showing high levels of morphine (painkiller) and Versed (sedative) in the 4 patients (ages 61–90) — but says that underlying evidence had not yet been publicly released at time of broadcast. Foti, pressed on DNR status of the patients: "Do not resuscitate does not mean do not rescue."
- **[1:06:04–1:07:00]** Pou's lawyers wouldn't let her discuss specifics (state hadn't shared its evidence); she flatly denies murdering anyone: "Why would I suddenly start murdering people? It doesn't make sense." Denies believing in euthanasia, distinguishes it from "comfort care" and the "double effect" doctrine (pain medication that may incidentally shorten life is standard end-of-life ethics).
- **[1:07:00–1:07:22]** Ironic/tragic detail: help (chartered helicopters arranged by Memorial's corporate owner) was already on the way when the alleged injections occurred — but no one inside the hospital knew that. 34 total dead at Memorial.
- **[1:07:22–1:09:52]** Closing: case in the hands of the New Orleans DA re: grand jury; Pou faces potential life in prison and loss of her medical license — "that is the truest thing that is truly the most painful for me... it is the best thing about my life," ending on her visibly emotional.

## D. STORY THREADS RANKED (by emotional/documentary value for a 30-min retrospective)

1. **The Bridge to Gretna** (chapter 4) — Synopsis: Desperate, mostly Black evacuees trying to walk out of a drowned city on foot are turned back at gunpoint by a mostly-white suburban police force, sparking a still-unresolved argument about whether it was self-preservation or racism. Sharpest moral conflict in the whole program; built-in "both sides" tension (Bradshaw/Golden/Hollowman vs. Harris/Lawson) that plays well as a documentary act. **Ethical flag: none major — all named adults, on the record, no children imaged in distress close-up needed.**

2. **Memorial Hospital / "Was It Murder?"** (chapter 6) — Synopsis: A respected cancer surgeon and two nurses, trapped for four sweltering days without power in a flooding hospital, are accused of murdering four patients with lethal drug doses — raising the hardest ethical question of the whole disaster: euthanasia vs. mercy vs. abandonment. **Ethical-sensitivity flag: HIGH.** This involves specific named patient-death allegations and an active criminal matter (as of the 2006 broadcast); do not use patient imagery/identities, do not editorialize on Dr. Pou's guilt/innocence, present as an open/contested legal question only, and note this thread requires independent verification of the eventual outcome (the case was later no-billed by a grand jury in 2007 — VERIFY independently before stating in-script; do not rely on this program alone).

3. **NOPD collapse and Chief Eddie Compass** (chapter 3) — Synopsis: A police department that lost 80% of its officers' homes, faced armed thugs shooting at its own rescue boats, endured an attempted kidnapping of its chief, and split bitterly over who stayed and who "abandoned" the badge. Strong hero/villain internal-conflict narrative (Compass's "cowards" quote) plus humanizing "Camp Benelli" makeshift-shelter detail.

4. **Superdome/city-in-freefall + the Nagin-Honoré rescue thread** (chapter 2) — Synopsis: The mayor and the general who became the public faces of the emergency response, flying over a drowned city, arguing about triage, discovering the levee-vs-floodwall distinction live on camera. Good hook material (present-tense stat/found footage energy) but heavily overlaps with better-known/more-covered ground — use for connective tissue/context rather than a standalone act.

5. **One Year Later — the two New Orleanses** (chapter 5) — Synopsis: A year on, the city is still gutted; Mayor Nagin insists on progress while a professor calls him "too damaged" to lead, and political insider Jim Carville warns the rebuilding is quietly reshaping the city into "a playground for the rich" — debutante balls proceeding on schedule while Black public-housing residents face demolished homes. Strong "who gets to come home" equity angle for a longer-arc/legacy segment.

6. **Charity Hospital / rooftop deaths** (chapter 2, ~7:50–9:47) — Synopsis: A doctor's account of an elderly woman fanned by her husband for days who died on a hospital rooftop helipad waiting for a helicopter that never came in time — smaller, self-contained, extremely potent single-scene emotional beat, good as a cold-open or mid-episode gut-punch vignette rather than a full act.
