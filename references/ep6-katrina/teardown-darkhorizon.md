## c. COVERAGE LIST

**What it covers:**
- New Orleans' below-sea-level geography and the levee/floodwall/drainage-canal system
- The 2004 FEMA-funded "Hurricane Pam" simulation exercise and its ignored warnings
- Full storm timeline: Aug 23 tropical depression → Aug 25 Florida landfall (cat 1, 14 dead) → Gulf re-intensification → Aug 28 cat 5 peak (902 mb) → weakening before final landfall
- Aug 29 Louisiana landfall at Buras/Plaquemines Parish (cat 3, 920 mb, 125 mph)
- Mayor Ray Nagin's mandatory evacuation order and NHC's "human suffering will be incredible" bulletin
- Levee/floodwall breaches (Industrial Canal named specifically; MRGO referenced by function, not by name in narration)
- Lower Ninth Ward flooding, attic escapes, ax marks in roofs
- Superdome and Convention Center shelter crisis (food/water/toilets failing, roof damage)
- Stranded elderly on highway overpasses
- One named survivor vignette (Doris Comardelle/Commerdell, 93, Chalmette/St. Bernard Parish)
- Death toll (~1,400 revised 2023), damage ($125B), housing units destroyed, population decline (485,000 → 230,000)
- Army Corps of Engineers' 2006 admission of faulty design/construction; ASCE finding that 2/3 of flooding was preventable
- $14.3B post-Katrina levee/floodgate rebuild
- Katrina's name retirement (2006) and a present-day New Orleans coda

- Hospital and nursing-home deaths (Memorial Medical Center controversy, Charity Hospital losing power) — a major, well-documented Katrina story, entirely absent
- Prisoners abandoned in Orleans Parish Prison
- Civilian "Cajun Navy"-style volunteer boat rescues
- Race and class dynamics of the response and media coverage (no mention of the "looter" framing controversy or the broader racial-justice narrative)
- Any historical comparison to Hurricane Betsy (1965) or Camille (1969) — no regional hurricane history for context
- MRGO ("Mississippi River Gulf Outlet") named as a contributing engineering cause, or coastal wetland loss as a surge amplifier
- Political fallout beyond the Army Corps (no Bush administration flyover photo, no FEMA director Michael Brown "heckuva job" moment)
- Levee-fund corruption/embezzlement allegations
- Long-term recovery mechanics (Road Home program, school system overhaul, gentrification/displacement debates)
- Environmental contamination (oil spills in St. Bernard Parish, toxic floodwater)
- More than one named/personalized survivor story — the human-interest layer is thin relative to the scale of the disaster

## e. RETENTION MECHANICS

- **Cut rate:** Fast in the hook and storm-timeline acts (sub-2s cuts on satellite/news montages), noticeably slower in Act 1 (map/diagram graphics held 4-6s each) and in the toll-statistics act (data cards held long enough to read).
- **Flash-forward re-hook device:** The Nagin "Ladies and gentlemen, I wish I had better news" quote is shown TWICE — once out of order in the 0:24-0:35 hook, then again in full chronological context at 6:24. This is a deliberate open-loop: the hook plants a mystery ("why does this official sound so grim?") that only resolves 6 minutes later.
- **Open loops:** The central open loop is planted at ~1:06-1:31 ("What destroyed the city had been quietly failing for 40 years... they wrote reports, they ran simulations... the results were catastrophic and nothing was done") — this teases the levee-failure explanation that doesn't fully pay off until Act 3 (8:19+) and isn't confirmed/closed until the Army Corps admission at ~16:08.
- **Micro re-hooks:** Roughly every 45-90s there's a hard tonal or visual escalation (satellite imagery jump, a real archival TV clip, a stat spike, an on-screen document/bulletin). This roughly matches our own "rehook every 45-60s" rule.
- **Ending:** No survivor-callback close and no cliffhanger/teaser for a next episode — it resolves cleanly ("The storm didn't destroy New Orleans. The system did.") then cuts straight to a direct subscribe/support ask over generic B-roll. No recap, but also no forward-looking hook to another video.
- **Pacing weakness:** Act 1 (1:45-4:17, the levee/Hurricane Pam explainer) is the most graphics-heavy, slowest-cut segment of the video — the "expert quote" card is held for ~8 seconds twice in a row (identical text repeated on two consecutive tile-sheet frames), suggesting either a slow zoom/hold or a genuine pacing lull right after the hook's energy peak.
