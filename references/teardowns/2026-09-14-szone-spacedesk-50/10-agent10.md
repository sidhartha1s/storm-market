**(1) What I'd click and what I'd skip.** Sitting in my chair with the clicker, eight I'd have hit: "America's Deadliest Volcano Is Waking Up — But Something Is Different" (St. Helens is MY mountain, I remember 1980, and the thumbnail arrow pointing at real fresh lava on a snow cone got me before I read the words). "This U.S. State Is About To Face Its Worst Storm Since 1992" — a named year I lived through beats any made-up superlative. "What This Record Heat Will Do To The U.S." — Phoenix, a real city, a real number. **(3) The host.** The Szone avatar bothers me more than I expected — not because it looks fake, because it looks the SAME in every single video, same jacket, same tone, whether he's talking volcanoes or football scores, and that's exactly what the top comment on the St. Helens video said: "the A.I. is hallucinating... Mount St. Helens is currently at normal background activity" — somebody fact-checked him in his own comments and got 8 likes doing it. On Sakurajima the top comment, 15 likes, just asks "Who is this guy? He's everywhere... I'm thinking AI." That's not anger, that's suspicion, and suspicion is worse for trust than anger. SpaceDesk's voice never drew a single one of those comments in the four transcripts I read — it just sounds like a guy reading a wire report, no theatrics, and the top comment on the moon-crash video was a kid's memory of a light flash on the moon in 1958 — that's a channel that earned a personal story, not a "who is this guy."

**(4) Copy word for word, and what would beat them.** Copy: opening on a bare number before any adjective ("423 earthquakes," "100 degrees," "17 million cubic meters a second") — my ear perks up for a number, not for "terrifying." Copy the named source every time — USGS, NWS Miami, JMA, an actual scientist's name and university. Copy the "here is what changes my mind" checklist at the close instead of a subscribe pitch. What would beat them both: never repeat the SAME face or SAME voice tics every day (the "here's the thing," the "stay with me," the reveal-flip sentence — I counted these and they're the tell everyone's fact-checking against). Use a plainer voice with no personality quirks, and when you're wrong, say so on camera next episode — nobody in 50 videos ever said "I was wrong about Tuesday."

**(5) Building a daily one for a man like me.** 12-15 minutes, no more — the Miami video held me at 20 only because it never repeated itself; most can't do that daily. Open with the number, not a question. Split roughly 40% what's happening right now, 30% the history (give me a real year — 1997, 1983, whatever locks it to something I lived), 30% what to actually do or watch for — I don't want doom, I want an evacuation zone or a watch-vs-warning distinction. I'd watch every night if it never recycled yesterday's tape, the way Sakurajima's second video did to me.
