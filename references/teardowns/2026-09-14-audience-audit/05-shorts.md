# Corpus analysis: six outliers (23,305 w) vs Warning Hour ep2 + Krakatau #4 (5,929 narration w)
Rates = per 1000 words, comp aggregate / ours aggregate (ep2, krak in brackets where they split). Counted by `count.py` + `c2.py` in `C:\Users\Sidhartha\AppData\Local\Temp\claude\channel-review\` (regex classes; ±10%). Sentence stats: theirs median 12-18 w, 8-30% of sentences ≥25 w; ep2 median 14, 13% ≥25; krak median 11, **0.6% ≥25 w** (162 sentences, one over 25). Krak is the outlier of the eight files on short-sentence monotony.

## (a) Address
- you/your 3.1 / 4.0 (ep2 2.4, krak 7.4); we/our/us 2.1 / 1.0 (krak **0.0**); people 3.3 / 7.4 (ep2 9.0); I/me 0.2 / 0.7 (krak 2.1: "I reed all the comments", "Before I tell you").
- Theirs: "you" is second-person *cognition* ("By the end of this, you will understand the threat"; "That is the part of this story that should stop you cold"; "If that means nothing to you, here is the translation"; "what has scientists genuinely alarmed. Stay with me"; "Nothing about it, standing in 1979, and looking up at that perfect white peak"). "We" is the species/nation ("We are getting better at watching these mountains. We are not yet good at surviving what they do"; "the systems we depend on"). Two lowest performers (St Helens 1.4, Krakatau 0.9 you/1000) barely use it, per the 09-13 review.
- Ours: "you" in ep2 is instruction ("Learn what yours sounds like, and pick your safe place now"); krak's 7.4 is inflated by CTA and quiz ("guess how tall… A hundred feet? Three hundred?"). "People" at 9.0 is the death-count noun; theirs names the group ("pilgrims, traders, workers", "the elderly, the isolated, people who work outdoors").
- Adopt: one direct-address promise inside 45 s, "we" = Americans/the country 2-3× per script, replace half of "people" with the specific group. Refuse: "you" as quiz or "picture yourself" (humanise rule 2), "I".

## (b) Authority attribution
- scientists/researchers/geologists 2.2 / 0.3; forecasters/meteorologists 0.2 / 1.8 (ep2 2.7); agency names 1.7 / 2.5; said/warned/study/published 2.0 / 2.7; "nobody knows / honest answer / cannot predict" 1.0 / 0.2.
- Theirs put a source on every hedge and every scare: "the USGS was careful to say something important about that number"; "A team led by Brian Stone Jr. at Georgia Tech modeled exactly that, and published it in the journal Environmental Science and Technology"; "Stone himself called a blackout during a heatwave, and this is a direct quote, probably the greatest climate-related hazard we can imagine"; "She was careful with her words in a way that is worth paying attention to. 'The repeated eruptions indicate a continued supply of magma…'"; "Every geologist who studies it says so without qualification."
- Ours: ep2 has good agency attribution (SPC, NWS, "the government's own investigators") but zero named scientists; krak has "the disaster agency says", "the weather agency's own words" without a person. Neither script frames uncertainty as the scientists' own ("Nobody can tell you the day" is narrator-owned).
- Adopt: one named researcher + institution + venue per mechanism chapter; "X said, and this is a direct quote"; put "nobody knows" in a scientist's mouth. Refuse: "many experts believe" (rule 6), "the scientists are not saying everything is fine. They are saying the opposite" (reveal-flip).

## (c) Time anchoring
- now/today/this week/yesterday 3.8 / 2.7 (ep2 1.5, krak 5.3); next week/coming days/what happens next **1.4 / 0.0**; the last time/ago/since 1.4 / 1.5; still/already 2.1 / 5.2 (ours doubles theirs; owner's phrase-lift confirms: still 2.6, already 2.0).
- Theirs stamp the calendar with day-names and relative days: "3 days ago on Wednesday morning"; "Six days from today, at 34 minutes and 32.9 seconds past 6:00"; "As of yesterday morning, the National Weather Service had 431 active alerts… up 78 in a single day from 353"; "Keep your eye on that region over the next several days"; "the same stretch of the calendar we are walking into right now". Every one ends on a forward window: "We will be tracking every one of those signposts as this develops."
- Ours: ep2 is fully past-tense archival; its only present is the cold open. Krak says "this week" 4× but never "next week / by Friday". The **future window is our zero column**: nothing in either script tells the viewer what to watch for by a date.
- Adopt: "as of this morning", day-of-week stamps, a dated "watch for" list at the close (El Niño piece: "the next CPC update lands Thursday the 9th"). Refuse: "still/already" as drama glue; cap at 1.5/1000.

## (d) Place anchoring
- US state names 2.6 / 6.0 (ep2 8.3, krak 1.1); US cities 2.2 / 3.0; "the United States / the country / the Northeast / the Southwest" 2.5 / 2.2 (krak 1.6).
- Theirs, even on foreign events, convert to US geography: "That is further than the distance from New York City to Detroit"; "which is the distance from London to Tehran"; "For viewers in North America, the timing translates to 2:34 in the morning Eastern"; "40 miles from Times Square, the ground moved"; "Spokane, 250 mi to the east, went dark at midday." Heat dome names 12 states + 5 NWS offices in 4 minutes.
- Ours: ep2 is dense (Alabama towns, streets) and it is the hit. Krak names Sunda Strait, Banten, Anyer, Carita, Tanjung Lesung, Merak, Bakauheni, Lampung (8 Indonesian toponyms a US 60-year-old cannot place) and reaches America only at minute 7 (Lituya) and via a denial ("A Krakatau wave cannot reach America either").
- Adopt: every foreign distance/height restated in a US landmark; the US state in the first 60 s; regional nouns ("the Southwest", "the Gulf Coast", "the corn belt"). Refuse: >3 unfamiliar foreign place names per script.

## (e) Scale language
- ever recorded/in history/first time 1.4 / 1.3; "one of the (most|largest…)" 0.8 / 0.3; hundreds/thousands/millions of 1.2 / 1.0; bare superlatives 1.7 / 3.0 (ep2 3.9).
- Theirs stack superlative + comparator + unit conversion: "the hottest month ever recorded in any city in the United States"; "It was the largest landslide ever recorded in human history. 2/3 of a cubic mile"; "four times larger than the most powerful nuclear weapon ever detonated by any country in history"; "That is not a gentle upward drift. That is a hazard that has exploded in frequency within a single human lifetime"; "one of the most precise appointments in the history of spaceflight".
- Ours: ep2 already superlative-heavy ("deadliest single American tornado since nineteen fifty-five", "costliest tornado in American history at that moment") and it worked. Krak: "tallest wave ever measured", "loudest" absent (they use it; we say "heard four thousand eight hundred kilometres away" with no US comparator).
- Adopt: "one of the most X on Earth/in the United States" 2-3× per script; pair every big number with a relatable comparator (three stories, football field, NYC-Detroit). Refuse: "unprecedented" (rule 5), superlative + "That is not X. That is Y" (flip).

## (f) Certainty and hedging
- hedges (could/might/roughly/about/nearly/estimated) 8.8 / 7.9 (krak 4.2); "nobody knows"-class 1.0 / 0.2; certainty markers (exactly/precisely/will/that is physics) 5.2 / 3.3; genuinely/actually/simply 2.0 / 1.5.
- Theirs hedge the future loudly and assert the past and the physics flatly: "The honest scientific answer about what the Mountainville fault is capable of is that nobody knows yet"; "The modelers say, 'Watch.' The predictor says, 'Maybe.' Nobody on Earth knows"; "That is not an exaggeration. That is physics. That is geology"; "Mount St. Helens will erupt again. Every geologist who studies it says so without qualification"; "Nobody watching this volcano right now can tell you which of those three paths it will take. That honest uncertainty is not a failure of science."
- Ours: krak is the least-hedged file of eight (4.2) while making the most forward claims ("the same shape that failed… is being rebuilt"). Ep2 hedges numbers ("about", "roughly") but never states a flat law ("That is physics").
- Adopt: split explicitly: flat declaratives for mechanism and record, attributed uncertainty for the future, with the three-path form ("If A, X. If B, Y. If C, Z. Nobody can tell you which"). Refuse: "genuinely/actually/truly" as intensifiers (rule 10; theirs run 2.0, we should stay ≤1), "That honest uncertainty is not a failure of science. It is the reality" (flip).

## (g) Transitions and section headers
- "here is/here's" 1.0 / 0.7; sentence-initial "So," 0.8 / 1.8 (krak 4.8); "Now," 0.4 / 0.3; "to understand why / you have to understand / think about / consider" 1.5 / 0.7; question marks 0.6 / 1.7 (krak 4.8, 9 questions in 1,884 w vs Nepal 2 in 3,839).
- Their seams are cause-chains and time-cuts, not questions: "To understand why this flood was so lethal, you have to understand how fast the water rose"; "Here is where this story stops being a disaster that already happened and becomes something worse, something still unfolding"; "Yesterday the situation reached its turning point"; "Then there is the opposite kind of warning. The Pacific Northwest in the summer of 2021"; "Before the finish, deal with the claim spreading online that this impact is deliberate." Five of six carry a debunk section ("two claims start circulating online, and they are worth addressing head-on") at 60-75%.
- Ours: krak seams are rhetorical questions ("So did Indonesia fix it?", "Then where?", "So what would have to happen…") banned by rule 8; ep2 uses cinematic time cuts ("At half past two that afternoon, in Mississippi, the second act began") which is their pattern and compliant.
- Adopt: time-stamp or place-stamp seams; a 60-90 s debunk block on the live-hazard videos (El Niño: "HAARP / faked records"), stated as "The problem with that story is…". Refuse: "here's the thing/here is why", "to understand X you have to understand Y" (rule 4 announcing the move), question headers.

## (h) Sentence rhythm
- Median 12-18 w theirs; ≥25-word sentences 8-30% theirs vs krak 0.6%; ≤5-word fragments 5-18% theirs, krak 19.1%, ep2 9.1%. Long-then-kicker pairs (≥18 w then ≤6 w) 0.9-4.6 theirs, ep2 3.4, krak 2.6: parity.
- Their long sentence is a chain of clauses carrying 3-4 facts, then a kicker: "By late February of this year, 1,053 observations had piled up from observatories in Mississippi, Utah, Beijing, and England, plus surveys and backyard telescopes." / "It swept away the Rasawagatti border post… It took out bridges. It tore away long stretches of the only overland road connecting Catmandeue to Tibet" / "Five times." / "It fails when it fails." / "Watch the limb."
- Ours: krak's "sentences under 24 words" rule (header line of the script) removed the long carrier sentence entirely; the result is 162 sentences of near-uniform 8-14 w, the flattest rhythm in the sample and the opposite of rule 13. Ep2 is fine.
- Adopt: 10-15% of sentences at 25-35 w (fact-chains with and/then/which), one ≤4-word kicker per 150 w. Refuse: drama-stack fragments (>1 per script), kickers that are flips ("Nepal is not the exception. Nepal is the warning").

## (i) Vocabulary level
- 10+-letter words 36-79/1000 theirs (East Coast 78.5, St Helens 53.7) vs ep2 31.8, krak 25.9. Definition moves 2.0 / 1.8 (parity in count, not form).
- They keep the jargon and define it in a clause, then reuse it as a character: "It is a landslide damned or avalanche damned barrier lake"; "Upper air height is a direct measurement of how warm and how tall the column of atmosphere is. And a 600 decameter value at the 500 millibar level is among the highest ever measured"; "They called it immature. Not young in human terms, but immature in the geological sense"; "friatic explosions, the mountain clearing its throat"; "solar radiation pressure, the faint constant push of light itself".
- Ours: ep2 defines well ("helicity", "dual polarization") but krak deletes terms ("tilt" for tiltmeter/inflation, never "flank collapse", never "lateral spreading"); the 45-75 audience tolerates more Latinate vocabulary than our TTS-simplification allows.
- Adopt: name the term, define in ≤12 words, reuse the term 3+ times; one "in plain terms" translation per number-dense minute. Refuse: banned list words (rule 5), metaphors that could paste into another script ("keg of dynamite", "battering ram", rule 3).

## (j) Emotional register
- fear-class (terrifying/deadly/killed/catastrophic…) 4.9 / 8.4 (ep2 10.5, krak 3.7); reassurance-class 2.0 / 2.5.
- Theirs spend fear words sparingly and put them in a source's mouth or a fact: "This is the kind of hazard that scientists lose sleep over"; "Here is the scenario that keeps heat researchers up at night"; "an almost unimaginable reading for that latitude, and then burned to the ground the very next day"; reassurance is always conditional and sourced: "The moon will be fine"; "The good news is that domes do break… That is a real possibility, and it is worth holding on to. But the forecast, as it stands today, is for persistence."
- Ours: ep2's 10.5 is mostly the verb "killed/died" doing counting duty, which is fine; the adjectives ("terrible", "cruel piece of irony", "textbook nightmare", "mercifully") are narrator emotion. Krak has near-zero fear vocabulary and no named victim (namedpeople 0 vs ep2 5, theirs 1-6).
- Adopt: "keeps [named group] up at night" attribution, one named person per tragedy, reassurance as a sourced conditional. Refuse: narrator adjectives ("terrible", "cruel", "mercifully"), "as if the whole graveyard had looked away" (rule 2 personification), aphorism endings ("Nepal is the warning").
