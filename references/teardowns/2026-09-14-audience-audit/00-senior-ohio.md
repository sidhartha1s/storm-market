## 1. Three schools, read at 300 px on a TV grid

**A) Name+Year documentary card** ("APRIL 27, 2011 / FOUR EF5s" over an AI wedge tornado, per metadata.md §Thumbnail).
- 65-year-old sees: a dark storm picture and a date. The date is only meaningful if he already knows the event. For Super Outbreak he does (75% US, 34-75; he watched Spann live), which is why it worked: 7.5% CTR is recognition, not curiosity.
- Trust high, curiosity low. It promises "the record of a thing you remember."
- Repeatable: yes, but it only fires on famous events. Tri-State 1925 (141 views) is the proof: nobody alive remembers it, so the year sells nothing.
- Looks like: every tornado-archive channel. Zero distinctiveness, but that lane is also where Suggested pulls from, so the sameness is partly a feature.

**B) TheSpaceDesk photograph + 2-4 bold words** (top row: "IT HAS STARTED", "6 DAYS TO IMPACT", "THE SOUTHWEST IS TRAPPED").
- 65-year-old sees: one huge legible phrase (white first line, red payoff word), one clean subject filling 60% of the frame, no clutter. Text is 25-30% of frame height; ours ("6+ ERRUPTIONS IN A WEEK") is thinner and italic, which smears at 300 px.
- Trust medium, curiosity high. The phrase is a present-tense verdict, not a label. "THE SOUTHWEST IS TRAPPED" names the viewer's region; that is why 260k.
- Repeatable: very. Same type stack, same two-colour rule, new subject each time. Series identity comes free.
- Risk: it is not the archive-channel look, so it reads "sensational" to a doc viewer unless the image is a real agency photo. The credibility comes from the photograph looking like NOAA/NASA, not AI.

**C) Breaking-news chyron/map/arrow** (Szone middle row; our Texas, 8% CTR).
- 65-year-old sees: the TV news frame he trusts (red BREAKING NEWS bar, white lower-third caps, satellite map with his city names, red arrow). It needs no reading at all; the grammar is learned from 40 years of local news.
- Trust high (borrowed), curiosity high for live events. Place names on the map do the targeting: "NEW YORK / NEW JERSEY", "SEATTLE / TACOMA". Our Texas thumb has a flag but no map and no city name, and still hit 8%.
- Repeatable: yes for anything live. Dead on a 1925 story (a "BREAKING" bar over a 100-year-old event is a lie a senior spots instantly).
- Risk: looks like every Szone clone, and YouTube may treat a fake CNN bug as misleading. Never use a real network logo. Our own "THE WARNING HOUR" bug in the same slot is the fix.

## 2. When to use which
- Historical doc (Joplin, Tri-State, banked four): A's photograph + B's type. Real archive photo (NOAA/NWS survey, newspaper front page), name of the place in the frame, the year small. Text is the human-scale fact, not the date: "158 DEAD IN 32 MINUTES" beats "MAY 22, 2011". Date goes in the title only.
- Live-wave mini (El Nino this week, hurricane peak, a named storm): C, with our own bug, a map with 3-4 city labels, one red arrow, 2-4 word chyron. Present tense, a state or region name in the text.
- Forecast explainer (the 20-min El Nino doc, "what this winter does to your state"): B, using an official NOAA/CPC map or satellite composite as the photograph. Map+arrow allowed, no BREAKING bar. The words are the viewer's stake: region + verdict.
