# Adversarial audit: The Warning Hour (2026-09-14)

Data used: ep2 narration 4,045 words / 28:12 = 143 wpm, median sentence 14 words, 32 sentences >24 words, 1 question mark, 8 "you". Krakatau #4 narration 1,794 words / 11:52 = 151 wpm, median 11 words, 9 question marks, 11 "you". Competitor transcripts 3.4–4.3k words at 182–200 wpm. Ebook CTA: ep2 at 8:26 = 30% of runtime; Krakatau #4 at 5:15 = 44%.

## 1. Distribution (why Krakatau got 50 impressions and ep2 got Suggested)

1. [S5] Krakatau is a Search-only video with no Search title. Uploaded title "How Krakatoa Made a Tsunami Without an Earthquake" contains neither the trending query ("Anak Krakatau eruption", "Krakatau 2026") nor a year. Szone's title carries the place name and "ERUPTS" in the thumb. 50 impressions = the algorithm never found a cohort to test it on; it tested on searchers and they did not click.
2. [S5] Suggested goes to videos the system can pair with an existing cluster. Ep2 sits beside a dense, evergreen "2011 Super Outbreak / James Spann / Tuscaloosa tornado" cluster with hundreds of hours of watch history. Krakatau 2026 was a 5-day news cluster owned by 400k-sub channels that published 09-05 to 09-09; we shipped #3 on 09-10 and #4 on 09-12, after the wave had peaked (Critical Latitude 492k on 09-09). The 2018 tsunami anchoring in #4 is neither the live story nor an evergreen search term.
3. [S4] A 0-to-1k channel gets no cold-start audience, so every video must ride an existing evergreen cluster (Name + Year event) until subs exceed ~5k. Evidence: your three evergreen docs = 2,159 of 2,362 total views (91%). The 400k channel can ride breaking waves because it has a returning-viewer base that gives the first 1,000 impressions; you do not.
4. [S4] No playlist, no end-screen chain, no series continuity. metadata.md still reads "[EP1 LINK — OWNER TO FILL]" and "[SOCIALS — OWNER TO FILL]" two weeks after upload. Ep2 viewers (AVD 11.5 min) had nowhere to go; Joplin at 489 views is where they should have gone. Fix: "US Tornado Disasters" playlist with Joplin, ep2, Tri-State, banked docs; end screen on every doc pointing at the next doc, not at the ebook.
5. [S3] Cadence: 7 uploads in 20 days across 3 formats (doc, El Nino mini, Krakatau mini). The algorithm builds a channel profile from the first ~10 uploads; yours reads as three channels. Pick one format per month. For US 45–75, publish Thu–Sat, 6–8 AM Eastern (retirees watch mornings and after 7 PM ET on TV); test two slots and hold each for 4 videos before judging.
6. [S3] Ebook CTA at 30–44% on a 150-view video earns nothing and costs retention. At 150 views × 2% click = 3 clicks. Ep2 retention was 41%; a 16-s cut to a book ad at 30% is where a Suggested viewer who came for the tornado leaves. Move the CTA to a pinned comment and description until the channel has 10k subs, or make it a 5-s end-card mention only.
7. [S3] Description SEO is strong on ep2 (chapters, AEO block, tags) and it worked; Krakatau #4 has no chapters listed in edit_script.md and a title with no keywords. Consistency: every video needs 12+ chapters with searchable place names; a search hit on "Hackleburg tornado" landing on 10:27 is real traffic.
8. [S2] Tri-State (35 min, 141 views) shows length alone does not earn Suggested. 1925 has a thin cluster (few competitor videos, little watch history). Bank picks should be scored by cluster density (count videos >100k on the topic) before production, not by how good the story is.

## 2. Packaging

9. [S4] Title cutoffs: desktop Suggested sidebar truncates at ~55–60 characters, mobile home feed at ~45–50 on two lines, TV app at ~40. Your recommended ep2 title "Four EF5 Tornadoes in One Day — The 2011 Super Outbreak" is 56 chars; on TV the viewer sees "Four EF5 Tornadoes in One Day — The 2011" with the year cut. Put the searchable Name+Year first: "2011 Super Outbreak: Four EF5 Tornadoes in One Day" (50 chars, survives all three).
10. [S4] Devices for 45–75: place, year, specific number, plain verb, no slang, no ALL-CAPS-shouting in the title (thumb is where caps go). Seniors click "The Day Alabama Lost 240 People" over "IT'S COMING, TEXAS". Teens click urgency; seniors click recognition (an event they lived through) and stakes to their own state. Never use "Impossible", "Insane", clickbait adjectives; use the date.
11. [S4] Thumbnail styles, when each: (a) Name+Year documentary (archival frame, 2–3 words, date) for evergreen history docs; that is your working lane and matches the audience. (b) SpaceDesk NASA-shot + 2-word bold caps ("IT HAS STARTED") for breaking science with a global hook. (c) News-chyron (BREAKING NEWS bar, map, red arrow, place labels) for live US-region hazards. In the grid, all five competitor breaking thumbs have either a map with place labels + red arrow or a two-word verdict; ours have neither a place label nor a map. "THE IMPOSSIBLE TSUNAMI" tells a US senior nothing about where.
12. [S5] Our middle bottom thumb reads "6+ ERRUPTIONS IN A WEEK". "Eruptions" is misspelled. On a channel with 50 impressions, a typo in the only text a viewer sees is a hard no-click and reads as spam to a 60-year-old.
13. [S3] Ep2's thumbnail plan in metadata.md was an AI-generated tornado ("cinematic wide shot... photorealistic") with "FOUR EF5s" in red, while the fallback list had the real Tuscaloosa live shot @17:42. For evergreen docs the real archival frame plus a date wins with this audience; AI storms read as "fake" to viewers who watched the 2011 coverage live. FORMAT.md itself says audiences call out AI inserts.
14. [S3] The Texas mini thumb ("IT'S COMING, TEXAS" + BREAKING NEWS + Texas flag on cracked ground) is the closest to the winning chyron grammar and got your best mini CTR (6–8%) and some Suggested. That is the template for minis; the Krakatau thumbs abandoned it.

## 3. Format strategy

15. [S5] Kill the mini lane for now. Long docs: 41% AVD, 11.5 min absolute, earned Suggested. Minis: 30% AVD, 2.5–3.5 min absolute. Suggested ranks on absolute watch time contributed per impression; a 28-min doc at 41% gives 4x the watch minutes of a 10-min mini at 30%. Two Krakatau minis produced 6 views total for two builds.
16. [S4] Minis at 9–12 min are the worst of both: too long to finish (30% AVD), too short to bank watch time. Outliers in the review are 18–23 min at 190 wpm = 3.4–4.3k words. If a breaking video is made at all, it should be 18–22 min on a US-stake hazard inside 48 hours of the trigger, or not made.
17. [S3] The breaking lane needs a returning audience to seed the first-hour test, which you do not have. Reopen it at 5k subs, and only for US-region hazards (heat dome, hurricane, tornado outbreak day), never Indonesia.

## 4. Script strategy (read both scripts)

18. [S4] Pace: ep2 143 wpm, Krakatau 151 wpm vs competitors 182–200. For a 45–75 audience 150 is defensible for a documentary; it is wrong for a breaking mini, where the viewer compares you to the 190-wpm channels in the same feed. Do not speed the docs to 190; do speed anything labeled BREAKING to 165–175 via atempo.
19. [S4] Word count: ep2 4,045 words for 28 min. A competitor gets 4,000 words into 20 min. Your 28 min carries the information density of their 20; the extra 8 minutes is pauses, music beds and b-roll holds. Either cut the docs to 20–22 min at the same word count or add 1,500 words of content.
20. [S4] Promise placement: ep2's opening 110 words have 4 numbers and a scene ("It is late afternoon..."). Every competitor opener is a number or date in sentence one (Nepal: "489 people are confirmed dead"; East Coast: "On April 5th, 2024, at exactly 10:23"). Ep2's explicit promise ("This is the story of...") lands in paragraph two, ~0:55; it works for docs but is a "thesis opener" your own humanise-rules.md rule 7 now bans, so the house rules would forbid the one hook that has proven itself.
21. [S4] Rule conflict, surface it: humanise-rules.md bans rhetorical questions (rule 8) and the "this is the story of" thesis opener; script-os.md and FORMAT.md build the whole retention engine on chapter-exit questions ("Then where?", "So did they fix it?"). Krakatau #4 has 9 question marks and complied with FORMAT, ep2 has 1 and hit. The outliers use 3–7 questions as section headers. Verdict: keep chapter-exit questions (3–5 per video), ban only filler questions mid-paragraph. Rewrite rule 8 to say that.
22. [S3] The 24-word cap: ep2 has 32 sentences over 24 words and was your best-retained video; Krakatau #4 has 0 and flopped. The cap is a TTS artefact, not a retention rule. Competitors run a long mechanism sentence then a 2–6 word kicker. Keep the cap only where the TTS actually stumbles.
23. [S3] US-stake: Krakatau #4 reaches Alaska (Lituya, Barry Arm) at chapter 5–6, roughly minute 7 of 12, in violation of FORMAT.md rule 8 ("And you by the second minute"). A US senior hears West Java, Carita, Tanjung Lesung, Merak for six minutes first. Ep2 never needs the rule because the whole story is Alabama.
24. [S3] Krakatau #4 hook ends "Is history going to repeat? Let's find out." then "Comment down where you are watching this from. I reed all the comments." inside the first 75 s. A 60-year-old US viewer of a disaster doc does not comment where they are from at 1:10; that beat is a Gen-Z engagement tic and a leave point. Competitors' only direct address is the promise ("By the end of this, you will understand").
25. [S2] Ep2 spells every number in words for TTS ("two thousand eleven", "three hundred and sixty"). That is right for TTS but the doc-string in Krakatau's header (TTS notes, "reed") leaked into narration lines, and edit_script.md mixes spec lines and narration under the same "> " marker; a QC pass counting narration words is off by ~100.

## 5. Production

26. [S4] Over-investment: Krakatau #4 had a "retention pass (owner + GPT)", an 11-rule architecture, popfigs, MG clock cards, presenter circle, 12 sources, draft + full renders, and shipped to 2 views. None of that touches the first gate, which is impressions. A video that gets 50 impressions cannot be fixed downstream of the thumbnail and title.
27. [S3] QC gates (retention scorecard per minute, cut filter per sentence, visual audit, AVD pass, humanise pass) are five text passes on a 1,800-word script. At 50 impressions the ROI of the fifth pass is zero. Budget by lane: docs get the full stack (they earn Suggested); anything else gets title + thumb + hook only until it proves impressions.
28. [S3] The motion-graphics rule "MG carries dates so the voice does not" is right for retention but does not move impressions and costs TAT (memory: "MG time is the TAT bottleneck"). Spend that time on 3 thumbnail variants and A/B them in Studio's Test & Compare, which is free and moves the only metric that matters at this size.
29. [S2] Publish hygiene beats polish: "OWNER TO FILL" placeholders shipped in ep2's description, a misspelled thumbnail shipped on Krakatau #3. Add a 2-minute pre-publish checklist (spelling on thumb, no placeholders, playlist assigned, end screen set) and enforce it before any render gate.

## 6. The El Nino plan

30. [S3] Right wave, right lane. It is a multi-month US-wide story (not a 5-day Indonesian one), it is evergreen for the whole winter, and the 1982–83 and 1997–98 winters are inside the lived memory of a 45–75 audience, which is your proven click device (recognition). Both existing El Nino minis out-performed both Krakatau minis 10–40x with weaker packaging.
31. [S4] The historical-winters angle is right but must be framed as the viewer's own state ("what happened to your winter"), not as climatology. Ep2 proved this audience stays for place-by-place consequence, not mechanism. Structure: forecast in 90 s, then 6–8 state blocks (California floods 1982–83 and 1997–98, Texas/Gulf rain and tornado outbreaks, Florida Feb 1998 tornadoes 42 dead, Pacific NW/Midwest warm dry winter, Northeast snow drought), then "what to do by November".
32. [S3] Titles (all ≤52 chars, Name+Year first, state-level stake): "Super El Niño 2026: What Happened Last Time" (43) / "The 1997 Super El Niño Winter, State by State" (46) / "El Niño Is Coming: The Winters of 1983 and 1998" (47). Thumb: US map with red/blue winter bands and 2 words ("YOUR WINTER" or "IT'S BACK"), real satellite SST loop frame, no AI storm.
33. [S4] Risk 1: timing. CPC advisory is already out; the 400k channels will publish "super El Niño" pieces this week. Your 20-min doc must be public within 7 days or it lands after the cluster forms around their videos. Risk 2: forecasting a winter that has not happened invites the "fear-mongering" comment wall and a 2-month-later "wrong" verdict; anchor every claim on NOAA/CPC probabilities and name the source in narration (the outliers hedge by source every time). Risk 3: the 10-min hurricane mini splits the week's effort into the format that has never worked; fold the "silent Atlantic peak" into the El Nino doc as one 3-minute chapter (El Nino suppresses Atlantic hurricanes is the strongest US-stake mechanism you have) and skip the mini.

## Top three actions
A. Stop the mini lane; ship the El Nino doc at 20–22 min, ~3,600 words, inside 7 days, Name+Year title ≤52 chars, map thumb, real frames.
B. Fix distribution plumbing today: playlist, end screens doc-to-doc, fill placeholders, CTA to pinned comment and end card.
C. Resolve the house-rule conflict: allow 3–5 chapter-exit questions and the proven "this is the story of" doc opener; keep humanise rules 1–7, 9.
