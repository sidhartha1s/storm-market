Sat down with all twelve, plus everything EarthDesk's put up, and both our Krakatoa scripts side by side. Here's what I've got.

**1. That EarthDesk pace.** 143-148 words a minute, 22 to 25 minutes, and it still hit 196,000 views this week ("Anak Krakatau Is Still Erupting... And Now Three More Volcanoes Just Woke Up"). That pace suits me fine — it's close to how a person actually talks when they're explaining something serious, not selling it. Where would I stop? Honestly, not at 22 minutes if the story's carrying its own weight — I stopped on the Nepal one (z_vo0DCEKAE, 1.25 million views) right after "the very same thing is now taking shape in valleys far from Nepal" because I wanted the payoff, not because the clock ran out. I'd bail earlier — 12, 14 minutes — the moment a video starts repeating itself to fill air. Our scripts run 152-158 wpm. That's a touch fast for a bedtime disaster doc. Slow it down five percent and it'd sit better next to a coffee.

**2. The two EarthDesk Krakatau videos vs our two.** EarthDesk's Sep 11 ("Anak Krakatoa Is About To Erupt Again... And This Time It Could Be Worse," 173,143 views) opens: "Right now, as you're watching this, a volcano in Indonesia is sitting at the second highest alert level." That's a man telling me something is happening to me right now. Our 1883 script opens: "The volcano that killed thirty six thousand people in a single morning has erupted six times this week, and eight airports have already lost flights." That's a good sentence — better number, honestly — but our title ("Will Krakatoa KILL Another 36,000 People?") oversells what the script itself walks back by the end ("nobody is forecasting an 1883"). I'd click EarthDesk's thumb — plain lava photo, "INDONESIA ON HGIH ALERT" typo and all — over ours ("6+ ERRUPTIONS IN A WEEK," also misspelled) because theirs reads like a wire photo and ours reads like a stock render with a caption slapped on. I'd finish EarthDesk's because the promise ("what the mountain is doing right now, and exactly what to watch for") pays off with named agency people. I'd finish ours too, but only because I already trust the channel — a stranger wouldn't have the same reason to stay past minute two.

**3. Trust.** All three earn it the same way: a real name attached to a real quote. Rita Susilawati at PVMBG saying "each volcano has its own magma system, its own kitchen." David Johnston's actual radio call on the St. Helens one. USGS being careful to say Nepal "was not an earthquake" when it would've been easy to let that slide. That specificity is what I believe. What I don't believe: a title asking me a question the video already knows the answer to, and "here's the thing" dressed up as insight. Our sirens script opens with "Is history going to repeat? Let's find out" — that's the exact rhetorical-question move your own rules ban, and it's sitting in our own script. I'd cut it. Also noticed two of our three thumbnails on the bottom row misspell "eruptions." A guy who spent 30 years fixing other people's typos in ledgers notices that, and it costs you the same way "HGIH ALERT" costs EarthDesk in their own comments.

**4. Tomorrow's video.** This week's actual news is sitting right there: three more Indonesian volcanoes woke up Friday alongside Anak Krakatau (EarthDesk's own Sep 14 hook). I'd want ours short, 10 to 12 minutes, not the full 22 EarthDesk ran, because most of that story is "here are three more names and PVMBG says they're unrelated" — that doesn't need 25 minutes, it needs a map and five numbers. Opening line, my way: "Three more mountains in Indonesia started shaking on Friday, and the people who watch volcanoes for a living say it's a coincidence. Here's how they know."

**5. Three fixes for a friend.** One, drop the rhetorical-question opener from the sirens script — it breaks your own rule and it's the weakest line in either script. Two, proofread the thumbnail text before it ships — "ERRUPTIONS" undercuts every fact in the video under it. Three, match the title's promise to the script's own ending — "Will Krakatoa KILL 36,000 People" sets up a bet the script itself says isn't in play; a title like EarthDesk's, naming what's actually happening plus a real stake, doesn't have that gap.
