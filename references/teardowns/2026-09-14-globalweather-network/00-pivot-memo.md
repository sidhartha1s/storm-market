# Memo: Should The Warning Hour adopt the GWN footage-first format?

## 1. What actually drives their hits vs flops

- **Single dominant visual beats footage volume.** The four hits (1.63M, 1.44M, 807k, 597k views) each hinge on one continuously-shot, recognizable disaster image (ash column, submerged streets, hurricane wreckage, tornado funnels) narrated at 44-52% of runtime. That band matches our planned 60-90s-clip-then-explain rhythm. The lowest-view "flops" among real events (Minnesota 1,433; Ohio 1,877) are correctly formatted and footage-forward but simply cap out on a minor, no-death, no-injury story: packaging cannot manufacture stakes that aren't there.
- **Foreign vs US stake is not the swing factor; magnitude and clarity of stakes is.** Japan (foreign) took the top two spots on timing and name recognition; Hawaii (US) took #3 on Cat-5 + a hard number (35,000+). Ohio (US) still flopped because the hook split its promise across two states (Ohio flood + Florida creek), diluting the payoff.
- **Length: 5-11 min wins, 20+ min only survives on cut density.** Hits 1, 2, 4, 5 sit at 5:54-11:55. The 22:50 Hawaii video only sustained views via 39 cuts averaging 35s; the 17:05 and 25:53 Sakurajima/flood sequels at slower pacing decayed hard.
- **Title formula only works when the number is genuinely dramatic and singular.** "X HIT by Y - NUMBER (Latest Updates)" drove Atlanta's "14 INCHES" to 12x its number-formula sibling (Ohio "7 INCHES," 1,877 views) — but the same superlative also drew the highest complaint volume in the sample (17 "fake/lies" comments), a credibility tax on the exact mechanism driving clicks.
- **Publish latency is the biggest single lever.** Same-event sequels lose 90%+ views day-over-day: Sakurajima went 1.63M to 95.9k to 81.7k across three videos in three days. First-mover posting, not story quality, explains most of the delta.
- **El Nino's 1,395 views is the format's hard boundary.** It is 94% narrator voiceover with disconnected generic b-roll, because the event hasn't happened yet — no footage exists to cut to. This confirms footage-first cannot carry forecast/explainer topics; those stay in another lane.

## 2. Risk ledger

| Reused-content monetization policy | Thin analysis (template phrases like "so what caused this"), no spoken source credit anywhere | HIGH for them | LOW — our rules already mandate original narration + a named-source explanation chunk, which is exactly what YouTube's reused-content policy requires |
| Content ID claims vs strikes | Zero of 8 transcripts name a clip's actual uploader/handle; raw phone/streamer audio used uncredited | MEDIUM-HIGH for them | LOW-MEDIUM — accepting Content ID claims is fine per our rules; the strike risk only appears if we skip licensing, which our storm-chaser/streamer ban already forecloses |

| Copycat wave | Their own second/third videos on one event cannibalize each other (90%+ drop); a broader field of near-identical channels is forming | MEDIUM (market, not policy, risk) | MEDIUM — differentiation via Paul, verified sourcing, and the what-to-do chunk is a competitive requirement, not optional polish |

## 3. Verdict: adopt as a third lane, not a pivot

Real, already-occurred events with one strong visual and clear stakes (national or major-metro) reliably draw views in this format; forecast/explainer topics do not, so our existing explainer lane stays. GWN's actual policy exposure comes from shortcuts our footage rules already forbid (uncredited streamer clips, no spoken sourcing, thin analysis, misleading thumbnails) — so the upside is reachable without inheriting the downside. At 4 subscribers with an unproven footage-sourcing and licensing pipeline, a full pivot is not justified; run it as a third lane alongside the existing explainer and mini-storms lanes, gated on the sourcing recipe below actually working on one video before scaling cadence.

## 4. Adaptation recipe

- **Sourcing (legal, within 24h):** US-gov PD spine (NWS, NOAA, FEMA, USGS, state/county EMAs) as the default; agency/broadcast pool footage narrated over short with Content ID claims accepted; social clips only with explicit creator license/permission plus on-screen AND spoken credit. Never: storm-chaser/streamer clips scraped without a license, never a fabricated thumbnail frame.
- **Structure (12-20 min):** 60-90s licensed/PD footage cold open → 2-3 min plain sourced explanation (named agency, confirmed facts) → 60-90s footage block → Paul corner-circle analysis chunk (citations spoken) → repeat footage/explain 2-3x → US-stake chunk → what-to-do chunk (official guidance, named agency). Target ~50-55% footage / 45-50% narration, matching the hit cluster.
- **Narration rules:** plain declarative sentences, present tense for live events, agency named by proper noun every time (never "the weather agency"), no rhetorical questions, no "here's the thing," no reveal-flips, no drama stacks, no em dashes, state unresolved facts plainly rather than teasing them.
- **Title formula:** "[Place] hit by [event] - [verified, singular, significant number] ([status])" — only when the number is real and dramatic; do not force a number onto a minor or split-location story (Ohio's 2-state dilution is the negative case).
- **Thumbnail:** a real frame from the licensed footage, place name overlaid, caption matches actual severity (no "DESTROYED" for a power outage).
- **Paul's corner circle:** appears only during explanation/analysis chunks, never overlaid on raw footage, keeping attribution unambiguous.
- **What-to-do chunk:** last block before close, pulled verbatim from the named official source.
- **Publish latency target:** same-day as the breaking event; if same-day is missed, reposition as a distinct "one week later" follow-up angle rather than a same-story rehash (which the data shows loses 90%+ of the first video's views).
- **Per-video time budget (one person):** licensing/sourcing check 30-45 min, script draft 45-60 min, humanise + TTS pass 20 min, footage edit + Paul overlay + captions 60-90 min, thumbnail 15 min, QC 15 min = roughly 3-4 hours, meaning 1-2 videos/day max versus GWN's 3-6/day team output.
