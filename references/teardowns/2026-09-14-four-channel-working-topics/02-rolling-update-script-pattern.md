## Cross-pattern findings
- **GWN (the winning "Latest Updates" format) runs 65.9–77.8 wpm** (avg ~73); **EarthDesk runs 143–148 wpm** — over 2x faster. This is not caption-gap dilution (checked: 0 gaps >2s in the 3 vtt-timed GWN files); it is genuinely slow, weighted delivery — long pauses inside continuous captioned speech.
- Interview/on-scene-sound cutaways (`>>`, `[music]`, `[screaming]`) appear ONLY in the two biggest-hit videos (the Nepal pair, 1.7M views each); the other 3 GWN hits and both EarthDesk videos are pure single-VO with zero ambient tags — footage-with-natural-sound is a hit-amplifier, not the format baseline.
- Universal order of information across all 7: **current status/what's-happening-now → hard numbers → where/who's hit → cause or agency response → unresolved what's-next**, never a resolved ending.
- Universal framing verbs: "here is where things stand," "as of [day/time-of-day]," "officials have/have not confirmed," never a plain past-tense headline lead.
- Universal sourcing convention: `[Country/Region]'s [agency full name]` on first mention, short-form/acronym after; named individuals only when quoted directly, always with title.
- Universal close: an unresolved tracking line ("has not been counted yet," "we will keep updating you"), never a resolution — no video ends on a solved crisis.

## GENERIC 8-BEAT SKELETON — 12 min rolling update, OUR voice, 165 wpm delivered (1.10×150)
Present tense throughout. One named-agency quote on screen. Close = unresolved tracking line, then one soft comment/subscribe line. No rhetorical questions, no "here's the thing," no reveal-flips, no em dashes.
