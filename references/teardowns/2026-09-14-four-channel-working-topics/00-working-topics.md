1. What works: a named foreign catastrophe with a rising death/missing toll (Nepal flash flood: 34 videos, 8.9M total views, top 1.7M) beats everything else on the channel.
2. One iconic single visual event tops per-video views even at short runtime: the Sakurajima eruption clip is only 5:55 and still hit 1.6M, GWN's 3rd-biggest video.
3. Proper-noun recognition drives spikes inside otherwise-dead formats: a generic earthquake-swarm story averages under 10,000 views, but naming "San Andreas" on the same format hit 43,000.
4. Rolling "Latest Updates"/numeric-toll titles (X dead, Y missing) on a live event consistently outperform "evergreen" reflective framings of the same disaster (compare Nepal live-event videos, several 300k-1.7M, against the evergreen "What Just Happened in Nepal" think-piece at 1.2M — still strong only because SpaceDesk paired it with a proper noun + a promise of a "terrifying truth").
5. What never works: local/domestic wildfire containment updates (9 videos, none over 18,000) — no death toll, no iconic visual, no proper-noun recognition beyond a US city name.
6. State-level tornado and minor earthquake-cluster stories die at 700-1,500 views; these are the "minor US events" the task flagged, distinct from a channel's few genuine tornado wins (Tornado Alley trend piece, 390,000; Brazil multi-hazard, 608,000).
7. Generic forecast/explainer content ("Worst Heat Is Still Ahead", "No One Is Ready for This Winter") clusters in the 100k-260k band when paired with dramatic phrasing, but has no floor — the same lane also produces sub-2,000-view entries once the phrasing goes flat.
8. US vs. foreign split by views: foreign-located disasters = 15,259,491 views across 83 videos (avg ~183,850/video); US-located = 9,192,456 across 193 videos (avg ~47,630/video); global/mixed earthquake stories = 2,713,489 across 55 videos (avg ~49,336/video) — foreign disaster coverage earns ~3.9x the per-video views of domestic content despite domestic videos outnumbering foreign ones over 2:1.
