## Synthesis

**Shared formula across all four:**
1. Zero presenter on camera in all four — 100% screen-capture of agency/model websites (JMA, KMA, ECMWF, GFS, ICON, JTWC, windy.com, weathernerds.org) cut together with voiceover; the only "presenter" moment anywhere is a static branded bumper card (video 3, 0:40).
2. Motion comes from cutting between successive static, source-watermarked frames re-stamped with new dates — not custom camera pans/zooms or built animation.
3. The credibility engine in every video is multi-agency/multi-model cross-verification: 2 sources minimum (video 4's KMA|JMA, ECMWF|GFS splits), up to 6 (video 1's ECMWF/GFS/ICON/JTWC/GEFS roundup), each carrying a visible source chip or watermark.
4. Uncertainty is shown two ways in combination: a drawn cone/probability disc (KMA's official yellow cone, videos 2-4) AND an ensemble spaghetti plot (ECMWF/GEFS/JMA, all four videos) — plus it's stated verbally ("reliability is low," "30% probability outside the circle," "spread narrowing").
5. Text overlays are short (2-9 words), no paragraphs, flat-color or no background (no glass/blur anywhere), agency-native labels dominate over custom-designed cards.
6. All three Dr Soh videos (1-3) bury the title's core payoff — storm number/name/mechanism not confirmed until 55-80% into an 8-9 minute runtime — and pad with unrelated daily-habit segments (KMA temperature leaderboards, basin-wide hurricane stat cards) that have nothing to do with the titled storm. Video 4 (umbrellaman) is tighter but still delays its payoff to ~75-80% of its much shorter 3:20 runtime and assumes prior-episode knowledge.
7. No music evidence in any of the four source files — flagged as unconfirmed everywhere, not claimed.

**Top 3 copyable devices:**
1. **Multi-agency/multi-model same-screen roundup with visible source chips** (NHC/ECMWF/GFS equivalent, each labeled and watermarked) — the single most consistent device across all four videos; reads as rigor/cross-verification to a skeptical 40+ audience.
2. **One reusable "mechanism" explainer graphic** (video 3's North Pacific-High steering-wheel: one orange base map, arrows and 2-word labels added incrementally) — teaches the "why" cheaply and can be reused across an entire episode sequence.
3. **Explicit uncertainty framing paired with a narrative arc** (video 4's "spread narrowing = payoff," video 3's "70% probability" disc) — stating the uncertainty out loud, then visually showing it tighten over the runtime, creates a satisfying resolution beat.

**Top 2 avoids:**
1. **Delaying the title's actual promise to the back half of the video** — all four bury the storm-name/number/mechanism confirmation past the halfway mark (as late as 66-80%). A US 40+ audience clicking a specific title expects the threat named with real numbers in the first 60-90 seconds; anything less reads as bait-and-switch against weather-channel pacing norms.
2. **Unrelated daily-habit filler and un-recapped serial continuity** — KMA temperature leaderboards, basin-wide hurricane stat detours, and "as discussed in the last video" callbacks are loyal-audience padding/serialization devices that read as confusing scope drift to a cold, title-driven US viewer with no channel history.
