## 1. THE DAM DECISION (Addicks & Barker dams)

- **History/purpose**: Addicks and Barker are earthen flood-control dams with normally-dry
  reservoirs west of Houston, built by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers (USACE) in the 1940s
  after Houston's catastrophic 1935 flood, specifically to protect downtown Houston by holding
  back stormwater that would otherwise overwhelm Buffalo Bayou — source: U.S. Army, "What are
  the Addicks and Barker Dams and Reservoirs?" https://www.army.mil/article/86814/What_are_the_Addicks_and_Barker_Dams_and_Reservoirs_/ ; USACE Galveston District dam safety fact sheet https://www.swd.usace.army.mil/Portals/42/docs/civilworks/Fact%20Sheets/Galveston/FY13%20Buffalo%20Bayou%20and%20Tribs,%20Addicks%20and%20Barker%20Dam%20Safety,%20TX.pdf — confidence HIGH (general history/purpose); exact completion years (commonly cited as Barker 1945 / Addicks 1948) were NOT independently re-confirmed against a primary USACE document with a page-pinned date in this pass — confidence MED on the specific years, flag as OPEN ITEM.

- **Pre-Harvey condition — RESOLVED, exact classification and year**: In 2009, following a
  USACE-wide dam safety review, Addicks and Barker were designated **Dam Safety Action
  Classification I ("DSAC I") — "extremely high risk."** DSAC I signifies authorities should
  "take immediate action to avoid failure." Of the hundreds of dams the Corps operates
  nationwide, only **six** received a DSAC I rating following the 2005-launched safety review;
  of those six, only Wolf Creek Dam (Kentucky) has since been downgraded (to DSAC III) after
  extensive repairs — Addicks and Barker were still DSAC I as of Harvey. Source: Weather
  Channel investigative reporting citing USACE's own internal risk documents,
  "Documents Show Army Corps Worried About Houston Dams in 2009, 8 Years Before Harvey Hit"
  https://weather.com/storms/hurricane/news/houston-dams-harvey-addicks-barker ; corroborated
  by Zehl Law summary of the same USACE materials
  https://www.zehllaw.com/years-before-hurricane-harvey-addicks-and-barker-dams-rated-among-the-most-dangerous-in-the-nation-by-u-s-army-corps-of-engineers/
  — confidence MED-HIGH (the 2009/DSAC I/six-dams facts are corroborated across two
  independent write-ups of the underlying USACE documents, but this pass did not directly open
  a raw USACE PDF stating the classification — recommend one more pass at
  https://www.swg-wc.usace.army.mil/damsafety/ before an on-air verbatim citation).

- **Pool levels during Harvey vs. design capacity — RESOLVED**: Addicks Reservoir set its
  all-time record pool elevation of **109.08 feet on August 30, 2017** — roughly 2.4 feet below
  the 111.5-foot Addicks spillway crest; early that Tuesday morning, Addicks water did flow over
  its **auxiliary** spillway structure (not the main embankment). Barker Reservoir peaked at
  **101.5 feet**, about 3.5 feet below its 105-foot spillway crest, and did not reach its
  spillway. Source: local TV aggregation of USACE/HCFCD gauge data, KHOU "Buffalo Bayou to
  remain at record level; Barker, Addicks reservoirs have peaked"
  https://www.khou.com/article/weather/hurricanes/hurricane-harvey/buffalo-bayou-to-remain-at-record-level-barker-addicks-reservoirs-have-peaked/285-468348109
  ; USACE HEC-RESSIM technical blog on Houston-area reservoir pool elevations
  https://hec-ressim.blogspot.com/2017/09/location-and-pool-elevations-of-addicks.html — confidence MED-HIGH (figures are internally consistent across two independent sources; not yet cross-checked against a raw USACE gauge-data table).

- **The controlled-release decision — timing is DISPUTED, "1:40 AM Aug 28" could NOT be
  verified**: The one primary-source document located (the USACE Galveston District's own
  press release, mirrored on the Army's DVIDS system, posted **08.28.2017 at 10:27 a.m.**)
  states only that releases were "starting immediately" at the time of that post — it does NOT
  give a minute-level timestamp for when gates actually opened. Secondary reporting disagrees
  by a matter of hours: Houstonia Magazine reports the original plan was to start Addicks
  releases around 2:00 a.m. Monday Aug. 28 and Barker around 11:00 a.m. that day, but that the
  Corps moved up the timeline and began releasing from both reservoirs **"just before midnight"
  on Sunday, August 27** because of heavier-than-expected rain (Houstonia, "The Deliberate
  Flooding of West Houston"
  https://www.houstoniamag.com/news-and-city-life/2017/10/barker-addicks-reservoirs-release-west-houston-memorial-energy-corridor-hurricane-harvey
  ); other outlets describe the release beginning "around 1 a.m." or "around 2 a.m." Monday —
  none of the sources checked in this pass state "1:40 a.m." specifically. **VERDICT: the
  commonly-cited "1:40 AM, August 28, 2017" figure is UNVERIFIED against any primary source
  found in this pass — the safest on-air framing is "in the middle of the night between August
  27 and 28," not a to-the-minute time.** — confidence LOW on the specific minute; MED-HIGH that
  releases began sometime in the midnight-to-2am window.

- **Who decided/announced, and the stated alternative — RESOLVED, verbatim**: The decision was
  made and announced by **Col. Lars Zetterstrom, USACE Galveston District Commander**. Verbatim

  better to release the water through the gates directly into Buffalo Bayou as opposed to
  letting it go around the end and through additional neighborhoods."** Source: USACE Galveston
  District press release "Corps releases at Addicks and Barker Dams to begin," mirrored on
  DVIDS https://www.dvidshub.net/news/246255/corps-releases-addicks-and-barker-dams-begin
  (original https://www.swg.usace.army.mil/Media/News-Releases/Article/1291369/corps-releases-at-addicks-and-barker-dams-to-begin/
  returned HTTP 403 in this pass; DVIDS mirror used instead) — confidence HIGH (primary source,
  direct quote).

- **Upstream flooding — homes affected, RESOLVED**: Federal Circuit appellate reporting on the
  upstream takings case states Harvey-driven reservoir pooling caused major flooding to
  **more than 10,000 upstream homes and businesses** — source: Houston Public Media coverage of
  the December 2025 Federal Circuit ruling https://www.houstonpublicmedia.org/articles/news/flooding/2025/12/23/539585/hurricane-harvey-flooding-appeals-court-us-government/
  — confidence MED (reporting on the court record, not the opinion text itself, which returned
  403 in this pass). Separately, ProPublica/Texas Tribune reporting found **53 subdivisions in
  the Addicks watershed and 40 in the Barker watershed** listed by local officials as at high
  risk of flooding from the reservoirs — source: ProPublica, "Everyone knew Houston's
  reservoirs would flood — except for the people who bought homes inside them"
  https://projects.propublica.org/graphics/harvey-reservoirs — confidence MED-HIGH (this is

- **Downstream flooding — homes affected, RESOLVED, kept DISTINCT from the upstream figure**:

  Buffalo Bayou downstream of the dams were inundated by the releases themselves (many had never
  flooded before Harvey and did not flood during the storm's rainfall — only after the

  specifically attributable to the controlled release) — source: Zehl Law timeline page (as
  above) — confidence MED (figure not independently traced to a HCFCD/USACE table in this pass).

## 2. THE COURT RULINGS — the episode's hook

### Upstream case — In re Upstream Addicks & Barker (Texas) Flood-Control Reservoirs, No. 17-9001L, U.S. Court of Federal Claims

- **Liability ruling, Dec. 17, 2019 — RESOLVED**: Following a ten-day trial in Houston that
  began May 6, 2019 (covering thirteen "test" properties), **Senior Judge Charles F. Lettow**
  held that the government's construction, maintenance, and operation of the Addicks and Barker
  Dams to intentionally store stormwater on private upstream land during Harvey — protecting
  downtown Houston as designed — **"constituted a taking of a flowage easement under the Fifth
  Amendment."** This was a **LIABILITY-ONLY** ruling; it did not set damages. Source: Justia
  case archive, In re Upstream Addicks & Barker, Fed. Cl. Dec. 17, 2019
  https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/federal-claims/cofce/1:2017cv09001/35865/260/
  ; CourtListener docket https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8436218/in-re-upstream-addicks-and-barker-texas-flood-control-reservoirs-v/
  — confidence HIGH (holding language corroborated across multiple independent case summaries).
- **Damages ruling, October 2022**: The Court of Federal Claims (same judge) awarded
  **$484,166.44 in just compensation, plus interest (roughly $550,000 total), to six of the
  thirteen test-case properties** — source: Reduce Flooding litigation tracker
  https://reduceflooding.com/2024/12/09/addicks-barker-litigation-update/ — confidence MED-HIGH
  (secondary litigation-tracking source, consistent across multiple law-firm summaries; raw
  court order not independently opened in this pass).
- **Appeal — RESOLVED, Dec. 22, 2025**: The government appealed (Dec. 29, 2022); oral arguments
  were held Nov. 8, 2024. On **December 22, 2025**, a three-judge panel of the **U.S. Court of
  Appeals for the Federal Circuit** (case *Ablan v. United States*, No. 23-1363) **unanimously
  affirmed** the finding of government liability, holding the government "knew, or should have
  known" the dams would flood upstream private property, and — importantly — held this is a
  **permanent** taking because hurricane-level storms **"would inevitably recur without
  identifiable end."** The court also affirmed the trial court's denial of class certification.
  On damages, the outcome was **mixed**: the panel affirmed the awards for leasehold advantage,
  damaged personal property, and the offset for FEMA relief already paid, but **vacated** the
  awards for lost rent, displacement costs, and one property's flowage-easement valuation,
  remanding those specific issues for further proceedings. Source: CAFC opinion PDF
  https://www.cafc.uscourts.gov/opinions-orders/23-1363.OPINION.12-22-2025_2622469.pdf ;
  Houston Public Media https://www.houstonpublicmedia.org/articles/news/flooding/2025/12/23/539585/hurricane-harvey-flooding-appeals-court-us-government/
  ; Burns Charest law-firm summary https://burnscharest.com/headlines/2025/12/24/federal-circuit-affirms-government-liability-for-upstream-hurricane-harvey-flooding
  — confidence HIGH (opinion PDF URL located and cross-corroborated by reporting; opinion PDF
  itself was not fetched line-by-line in this pass, so exact page numbers for the "inevitably
  recur" quote are unconfirmed — flag as residual OPEN ITEM).
- **Current status (as of Sept. 2026)**: Liability is now finally affirmed on appeal; SOME
  damages categories remain on remand at the trial court, meaning **not every upstream
  plaintiff's dollar figure is final** — there is no single, final, total-dollar upstream
  settlement figure as of this writing. No broader class-wide payout exists (class certification
  was denied and affirmed denied) — each property's damages are litigated individually via test
  cases and subsequent claims.

### Downstream case — In re Downstream Addicks and Barker (Texas) Flood-Control Reservoirs, No. 17-9002L, U.S. Court of Federal Claims

- **THIS SECTION UPDATES THE TASK BRIEF'S ASSUMPTION — the downstream case was NOT simply
  "dismissed" as a final outcome; it was dismissed, then reversed on appeal, retried, and as of
  April 2026 the downstream plaintiffs ALSO won on liability. Full timeline below, flagged
  precisely by date so the script can choose which point in the timeline it needs.**
- **Original dismissal, Feb. 19, 2020**: The Court of Federal Claims initially ruled AGAINST
  downstream plaintiffs, holding they lacked "a cognizable property interest in perfect flood
  control," and separately reasoning that the plaintiffs' causation theory ignored that the
  gates were initially kept closed to protect downstream property, that this mitigation failed
  only because inflows exceeded the reservoirs' controllable capacity, and that **Hurricane
  Harvey itself — not the government's release decision — was the sole and proximate cause of
  the downstream floodwaters.** This is the "combined-cause"/causation dismissal the task brief
  anticipated. Source: Texas Tribune, "Federal judge dismisses suit against Army Corps for
  post-Harvey flooding" https://www.texastribune.org/2020/02/19/federal-judge-dismisses-against-army-corps-post-harvey-flooding/
  — confidence HIGH.
- **Appeal — REVERSED, June 2, 2022**: Downstream plaintiffs appealed. The Federal Circuit
  (case *Milton v. United States*, No. 21-1131) **reversed** the trial court, holding plaintiffs
  DID allege a cognizable property interest — specifically in flowage easements — and remanded
  for fact-finding on three questions: (1) whether a temporary taking occurred under the
  flooding-cases test; (2) whether plaintiffs can establish causation given the government's
  specific actions (as distinct from the storm); (3) whether the government can invoke a
  "necessity doctrine" defense (that the release was justified by actual, imminent danger to
  the dams). Source: CAFC opinion PDF https://www.cafc.uscourts.gov/opinions-orders/21-1131.OPINION.6-2-2022_1959808.pdf
  ; Reduce Flooding summary https://reduceflooding.com/2022/06/02/appeals-court-revives-addicks-barker-downstream-takings-cases/
  — confidence HIGH.
- **Retrial**: A limited retrial on the remanded questions began **October 25, 2024**, in
  Houston. Plaintiff's expert testified there was "no emergency involved in opening the flood
  gates" and that most downstream properties would not have flooded (or would have flooded
  less severely/for less time) had the gates stayed shut. Closing arguments were held **March 6,
  2025**. Source: Reduce Flooding litigation tracker https://reduceflooding.com/2024/12/09/addicks-barker-litigation-update/
  ; https://reduceflooding.com/2025/04/10/court-of-federal-claims-hears-final-arguments-in-addicks-barker-downstream-case/
  — confidence MED-HIGH (litigation-tracking source; testimony quotes not independently
  verified against a trial transcript).
- **Ruling, April 22, 2026 — RESOLVED, this is the current state of the law as of this
  episode's writing**: In a 48-page opinion, **Senior Judge Loren A. Smith** (a DIFFERENT judge
  than the upstream case's Judge Lettow) held for a twelve-plaintiff test case that the
  government's actions constituted a **compensable taking under the Fifth Amendment.** The
  court REJECTED the government's necessity, relative-benefits, and police-powers defenses,
  finding the Corps invoked its Water Control Manual's emergency provision (§7-05(b)) **before**
  an actual emergency posed imminent danger to the dams' structural integrity — i.e., the release
  was found to be a foreseeable, intentional decision rather than an unavoidable emergency
  response to imminent dam failure. The court concluded the "induced surcharge releases
  foreseeably and intentionally flooded downstream homes and businesses for weeks." Source:
  Houston Public Media, "Judge rules federal operator caused excessive damage to downstream
  homeowners after Hurricane Harvey" https://www.houstonpublicmedia.org/articles/news/flooding/2026/04/23/549912/judge-rules-federal-operator-caused-excessive-damage-to-downstream-homeowners-after-hurricane-harvey/
  ; Beck Redden case summary https://beckredden.com/case_studies/u-s-court-of-federal-claims-finds-downstream-addicks-and-barker-flooding-was-a-compensable-taking/
  ; Marzulla Law summary https://marzulla.com/blog/court-of-federal-claims-finds-takings-liability-for-downstream-flooding-from-addicks-barker-releases/
  — confidence MED-HIGH (multiple independent secondary summaries agree closely on holding and
  reasoning; the raw 48-page opinion itself was not fetched in this pass — recommend one more
  pass before an exact-page on-air quote).
- **Current status (Sept. 2026)**: Liability found; the case is now in the **damages phase only
  — NO dollar figure has been set for downstream plaintiffs as of this writing.** Status reports
  and conferences on damages were pending through mid-2026 per the litigation tracker. Roughly
  **8,000 downstream property owners** are estimated to be part of this litigation (a
  plaintiff-side/law-firm estimate, not a court-certified class count — class certification
  dynamics for this case were not independently confirmed in this pass). One plaintiff-side law
  firm (Fleming Nolen & Jez) has publicly estimated total downstream + upstream exposure at
  **$6.5 billion to $13 billion** — this is an ADVOCATE ESTIMATE, not a court-adopted figure —
  source: https://fnj-law.com/hurricane-harvey-victims-score-big-win-following-u-s-court-of-appeals-dismissal-reversal/
  — confidence LOW-MED on the dollar range specifically (plaintiff-side estimate); confidence
  HIGH on the ~8,000-owner figure being widely repeated across independent outlets covering the
  case.

### The hook-line precision check

**How far "a federal court ruled the United States government flooded Houston" can be pushed,
stated exactly:**

- It is factually SUPPORTABLE, as of April 2026, to say: *"Two separate federal court rulings —
  one in 2019 (affirmed on appeal in 2025) and one in 2026 — found that the United States
  government, through the Army Corps of Engineers' construction and operation of the Addicks
  and Barker Dams, is liable under the Fifth Amendment's takings clause for specific,
  identifiable flooding of both upstream and downstream private property during Hurricane
  Harvey."* This is accurate and can be pushed hard as the episode's hook — this is a genuinely
  extraordinary, low-awareness fact.
- It is **NOT SUPPORTABLE** to say the government was found liable for "flooding Houston"
  broadly, for causing Hurricane Harvey's rainfall, or for the CITYWIDE bayou/street flooding
  that had nothing to do with the Addicks/Barker reservoir system — the rulings are scoped
  narrowly to the mechanism of the two reservoirs: (a) UPSTREAM = the reservoirs functioning
  exactly as designed, permanently storing floodwater on private upstream land specifically so
  that downtown Houston would NOT flood — a "taking" of a flowage easement, not a mistake or an
  accident; (b) DOWNSTREAM = the Corps' SPECIFIC decision to invoke its emergency-release
  provision before an actual imminent-failure emergency existed, which the court found
  foreseeably and intentionally sent floodwater into homes that would not otherwise have
  flooded (or flooded as badly).
- It is NOT SUPPORTABLE to say the government has "paid" or been forced to pay any specific
  dollar amount for the downstream case, or a final/total dollar amount for the upstream case —
  liability is established in both, but the downstream case is in the DAMAGES PHASE with no
  figure set, and part of the upstream damages award is on remand. A script line claiming
  "the government owes billions" should be sourced explicitly as an ADVOCATE ESTIMATE, not a
  court-ordered sum.
- Suggested precise script framing: *"In 2019, a federal judge ruled that the government had, in
  effect, used homes upstream of the dams as a reservoir — flooding them on purpose to keep
  downtown Houston dry. It took until 2026 for a second federal judge to rule that the
  government did the same thing in reverse: that opening the dam gates flooded thousands of
  homes downstream that didn't have to flood."*

## 3. THE MAPS/DEVELOPMENT (REPORTING allowed here — every claim labeled REPORTING vs RECORD)

- **REPORTING**: ProPublica and The Texas Tribune's investigative series "Boomtown, Flood Town"
  (Dec. 2016) found that at least **4,000 more homes had been built inside the Addicks and
  Barker reservoirs' government-owned flood-storage land since Tropical Storm Allison (2001)**,
  and that, countywide, **more than 7,000 residential buildings and 1,600+ nonresidential
  buildings had been built since 2010 in the 100-year floodplain** in Harris County, per a
  Tribune/ProPublica analysis of local appraisal data — source: full text at ProPublica
  https://www.propublica.org/article/boomtown-flood-town-text and Texas Tribune
  https://www.texastribune.org/2016/12/06/houston-flooding-boomtown-flood-town-plain-text/ —
  confidence HIGH (direct read of the investigative piece's own reported figures).
- **REPORTING**: A separate, later ProPublica piece ("Everyone knew Houston's reservoirs would
  flood — except for the people who bought homes inside them") reports that local officials had
  listed **53 subdivisions in the Addicks watershed and 40 in the Barker watershed** as at high
  risk of reservoir flooding — source: https://projects.propublica.org/graphics/harvey-reservoirs
  — confidence MED-HIGH.
- **REPORTING — developer disclosure**: "Boomtown, Flood Town" quotes a longtime Houston-area
  homebuilder who built inside the 100-year floodplain acknowledging he is **not legally
  required** to tell buyers about flood risk, but says he does so anyway "to avoid lawsuits" —
  the clear implication of the reporting is that flood-risk disclosure to buyers was
  inconsistent and not mandated — source: ProPublica/Texas Tribune (as above) — confidence
  MED-HIGH (this is the reporters' characterization/quote, not a regulatory finding).
- **REPORTING — FEMA maps understated risk**: the same investigation found that **more than a
  third of the properties that flooded in Houston's 2015 Memorial Day flood and 2016 Tax Day
  flood were OUTSIDE the areas FEMA's maps designated as high flood risk** — meaning the
  official flood-zone maps (which determine mandatory flood-insurance requirements) were
  systematically under-drawing the real risk footprint even before Harvey — source:
  ProPublica/Texas Tribune "Boomtown, Flood Town" (as above) — confidence HIGH. A specific,
  named "Zone X" designation tied to a specific subdivision (e.g., Cinco Ranch/Canyon Gate) was
  searched for but **NOT found and confirmed in this pass** — flag as UNVERIFIED; do not name a
  specific subdivision's FEMA zone designation on air without a direct citation.
- **REPORTING — who knew**: ProPublica/Texas Tribune's "Hell and High Water" series (which won
  the 2017 Knight-Risser Prize for environmental journalism) establishes that Houston-area
  scientists had warned for years about the region's flood vulnerability, and that government
  at multiple levels (state, county, city) was slow to act on those warnings — the series
  frames this as institutional unwillingness to constrain growth, not a single smoking-gun
  memo — source: https://projects.propublica.org/houston/ ; Stanford's write-up of the prize
  https://west.stanford.edu/news/prophetic-report-houstons-flood-vulnerability-wins-2017-knight-risser-prize
  — confidence MED-HIGH (characterization of a large investigative series; not a single
  document citation).
- **UNVERIFIED**: specific named subdivisions built directly inside the dams' government-owned

## 4. THE PAVEMENT

- **Academic finding — Houston's own urbanization worsened Harvey, RESOLVED**: A peer-reviewed
  study (Zhang, Villarini, Vecchi & Smith, published in *Nature*, Nov. 2018) modeled Harvey
  with and without Houston's actual land-use/urbanization and found the city's development
  measurably increased the storm's rainfall and flood risk; Princeton's press summary of the
  same study frames Houston's risk of extreme flooding as **21 times greater** because of
  urbanization — source: *Nature*, "Urbanization exacerbated the rainfall and flooding caused by
  hurricane Harvey in Houston" https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0676-z ; Princeton
  press release https://www.princeton.edu/news/2018/11/15/houstons-urban-sprawl-increased-rainfall-flooding-during-hurricane-harvey
  — confidence HIGH (peer-reviewed primary study; the "21 times" figure is the university
  press office's paraphrase of the study's modeled risk increase, not a verbatim number from the
  paper's abstract — treat the multiple as an illustrative paraphrase, not a precise stat to
  quote to a decimal).
- **USGS — impervious cover and flood magnitude (national, not Houston-specific), RESOLVED**: A
  USGS-authored study (Blum, Ferraro, Archfield & Ryberg, *Geophysical Research Letters*, 2020),
  analyzing 39 years of data from 280 U.S. stream gauges, found that **a one-percentage-point
  increase in a basin's impervious cover causes, on average, a 3.3% increase in annual flood
  magnitude** (a larger 2,109-gauge dataset that includes basins with upstream regulation found
  a bigger 4.6% effect) — source: USGS publication page
  https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70217011 ; USGS "Causal effect of impervious cover on annual
  flood magnitude for the United States" https://www.usgs.gov/publications/causal-effect-impervious-cover-annual-flood-magnitude-united-states
  — confidence HIGH (this is a national relationship, NOT a Houston-specific number — do not
  imply it was measured in Houston specifically). One Houston-specific watershed data point
  found in secondary science reporting: impervious surface in a studied Houston-area watershed
  increased by **15 percent**, driving increased runoff — source: ScienceDaily coverage of
  Houston urbanization research https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2018/11/181114131957.htm
  — confidence MED (secondary science-journalism summary, not the underlying dataset itself).
- **Prairie loss — RESOLVED**: The Houston region's coastal prairie has been reduced from a
  historic scale to a small fraction of its original extent; the Katy Prairie specifically has
  shrunk from roughly **600,000 acres to about 200,000 acres** due to urbanization (a ~67% loss),
  and the broader Gulf Coastal Prairie ecosystem (from South Texas to Southwest Louisiana) has
  lost all but about **1% of its original ~20 million acres**, making it one of the most
  endangered ecosystems in North America — source: Coastal Prairie Conservancy (formerly Katy
  Prairie Conservancy) mission page https://www.coastalprairieconservancy.org/mission-history ;
  corroborating conservation-org reporting https://landtrustalliance.org/blog/grasslands-protected-on-historic-katy-prairie
  — confidence MED-HIGH (figures come from the conservation nonprofit's own mission materials,
  not an independent peer-reviewed land-cover study — treat as a well-sourced advocacy-org
  figure, not a government dataset; no exact single-year cutoff or peer-reviewed citation was
  independently verified in this pass).
- **HCFCD design-storm standard vs. what Harvey delivered — RESOLVED, with an important
  nuance**: Harris County's drainage system is NOT designed to a single storm standard —
  storm SEWERS (underground pipes) are sized for a **2-year** rainfall event, while the overall
  system (sewers + streets + open channels/bayous together) is intended to prevent structural
  flooding up through a **100-year** event — i.e., the "2-year vs. 100-year" framing in the task
  brief is not an either/or; it describes two different layers of the same system — source:
  Halff Associates engineering summary of Harris County/City of Houston drainage design
  https://halff.com/news-insights/insights/examining-atlas-14s-impact-on-future-development-in-the-houston-area/
  ; City of Houston Infrastructure Design Manual, Chapter 9 (Stormwater Design)
  https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/media/7596/download — confidence MED-HIGH (design
  standard language corroborated across an engineering-firm summary and the city's own design
  manual; NOAA later updated the underlying rainfall-frequency tables). Harvey vastly exceeded

## 8. NET-INFO-GAIN FACTS

1. **★ BEST: the downstream case flipped in April 2026 — a documentary made even a year
   earlier would have this wrong.** The popular narrative (still repeated in most retrospectives
   as of 2025) is "the upstream homeowners won, the downstream homeowners lost, case closed."
   That was true from 2020 to 2022. It stopped being true when the Federal Circuit reversed the
   dismissal in 2022, and it became actively FALSE on April 22, 2026, when Judge Loren A. Smith
   ruled the downstream releases were ALSO a compensable Fifth Amendment taking — rejecting the
   government's argument that opening the gates was an unavoidable emergency response. This is
   the freshest, least-known fact available for this episode and directly serves the "villains"
   thesis: the SAME legal question (did the government's dam decisions, not the storm, cause
   specific flooding) now has the SAME answer — yes — on both sides of the dams.
2. **★ STRONG: Col. Zetterstrom's own quote is an on-record admission of trade-off, not
   accident.** The USACE press release announcing the release does not describe an
   uncontrollable catastrophe — it describes a choice: *"It's going to be better to release the
   water through the gates directly into Buffalo Bayou as opposed to letting it go around the
   end and through additional neighborhoods."* That is the Corps' own commanding officer, in an
   official press release, explaining that some neighborhoods were chosen to flood so that
   others would not. Few competitor videos quote this line directly from the primary press
   release rather than paraphrasing it.
3. **STRONG: the DSAC I classification put Addicks/Barker in a six-dam club, nationwide, for
   eight years before Harvey hit.** Only six of the Army Corps' hundreds of dams nationwide
   carried the "extremely high risk"/DSAC I rating after the Corps' 2005 safety-classification
   overhaul; Addicks and Barker were rated into that group in 2009. Only ONE of those six dams
   (Wolf Creek, Kentucky) has since been repaired enough to be downgraded — Addicks and Barker
   were still in the worst-risk tier when Harvey arrived in 2017.
4. **GOOD: Harris County's drainage system was never really designed for a "100-year storm" in
   the way that phrase implies.** NOAA's post-Harvey Atlas 14 rainfall update found that what
   the region's engineers had been calling a "100-year rainstorm" for decades was actually
   closer to a 1-in-25-year event under NOAA's corrected, Harvey-informed rainfall-frequency
   data — meaning the infrastructure built to the "100-year" standard was quietly under-built
   relative to that label the entire time, independent of Harvey's own extremity.
