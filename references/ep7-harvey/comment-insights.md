# Comment Mining: Hurricane Harvey competitor videos

Sources: Dark Horizon "The Day That Almost Ended Texas - Harvey 2017" (400 comments pulled, top 150 by like-count reviewed, `r9QOU_W7cWI/comments_top.txt`) and StormForge "Harvey: The Storm That Changed Texas Forever" (362 comments pulled, top 150 reviewed, `9leAbS5XvKU/comments_top.txt`). All insights below are net-info-gain targets for our own Harvey episode — content these videos left on the table, or complaints their audiences made loudly and publicly.

## 1. The Addicks/Barker reservoir release is the #1 thing viewers bring up unprompted — and it's a live wound, not abstract history

Even on the video that barely explains it (Dark Horizon), the **single most-liked comment on the entire video (431 likes)** is a first-person account of being flooded out specifically by the reservoir release:

- @janesmith7331 (431 likes, Dark Horizon): *"We were under mandatory evacuation and fled, but the place we fled to started to flood... Our last place was a hotel but it was right smack dab in the area where they released all the water from Addicks, that was the scariest because there was no where left for us to go and we were way up high on like the 17th floor and the power went out... For almost an entire year after Harvey my entire family would get anxiety every time it started to rain."*
- @firecwby1999 (511 likes, StormForge): *"Fun fact: when I-10 was originally built, they had a plan on the table to run underground storm drains from the reservoirs direct to the ship channel. They didn't. They figured 'it costs too much'. Famous last words."*
- @B-rabTX (4 likes, StormForge): *"My grandfather lost 600 acres in the 40s to the Barker Reservoir. Was supposed to be government land, imminent domain all that. Fast forward 60 years and they started selling it and letting idiots build houses IN A LITERAL RESERVOIR that they built."*
- @kristymekeel2172 (3 likes, StormForge): a secondhand but detailed and verifiable-feeling account — a woman in labor with twins during Harvey, whose Addicks-area home survived the storm itself but was flooded by the reservoir release while she was stuck at the hospital; no flood insurance because the home wasn't in the mapped flood plain.
- @charlesjonesjr.864 (2 likes, StormForge) draws an unprompted parallel to Katrina: *"Watching this let's me know for certain that the Army Corps of Engineers flooded parts of New Orleans after Katrina intentionally."* — a cross-disaster accountability thread worth naming explicitly if we want a callback to our own Katrina episode's audience.
- @IratePuffin (2 likes, StormForge) flags this is an ONGOING practice, not history: *"Now, every time we have heavy rain like this, they release water from the Conroe dam and flood everyone who lives downstream on the San Jacinto river."*

**Net-info-gain confirmation:** Dark Horizon (the bigger channel, 1.08M views) essentially skips this story and its own top comment is people correcting that omission. StormForge (516K views) already tells this story well — see #6 below for exactly where it still falls short.

## 2. QC/factual-accuracy lesson: locals will correct your hook's geography

- @ss_whole (15 likes, StormForge): *"Kellywood isn't downstream from the dam, it's off Fry road in Katy, west of the dam."* This directly contradicts the video's own cold-open framing (the hook is built entirely on Kellywood being "3 miles downstream from the Barker Reservoir"). **Lesson for our pipeline:** verify any hook-anchoring geographic claim against an actual map before locking the script — a factual error in the opening 20 seconds undermines the entire accountability narrative that follows.

## 3. Untold human-interest angles neither video used

Both competitor videos are structurally thin on named, personal survivor stories — Dark Horizon uses zero (only anonymous vox-pop-style quotes), and StormForge names specific deaths ("three children," "a woman," "an elderly man") only in aggregate third-person narration, never as a followed vignette. Commenters volunteered far more vivid material than either video used:

- @oblivion85 (325 likes, StormForge) — the single most emotionally specific unused story in the entire comment set: *"I had a coworker who died during Harvey. Her car got stuck in an area that was flooding quickly. She, along with her young daughter, tried to escape on foot but were swept away. By some miracle, when rescuers found them, the mother who had already drowned, found the daughter alive, still holding on to her mom. I remember that one of the mother's biggest fears was water."*
- @MichaelConstantine92 (17 likes, StormForge) — a devastating, multi-layered systemic-failure account that goes well beyond what either video covers: *"My insurance did not pay. We all got sick. My lender sold my loan to a predatory servicer. They ruined my credit and refused payments. The house foreclosed five years later. Help never came. We lost everything. My wife died at 27 two days after the storm."* This single comment contains three distinct untold angles: insurance denial, predatory mortgage servicing post-disaster, and a death StormForge's own toll-counting narration would have excluded (indirect, delayed, uncounted).
- @maddykelly537 (1 like, Dark Horizon) — a detailed account naming a specific death not in either video's toll: a graphic-design teacher who evacuated by flying out but suffered a fatal medical episode from storm-related stress, with students only learning of his death via a school email weeks later.
- @erinshaw6266 (40 likes, StormForge) — sharp, specific, unwarned-flooding account: *"I live in the NE corner of Harris County. The part where they flooded our neighborhoods without warning. People woke up that morning and stepped out of bed into ankle deep water... Boats going up and down our streets, helicopters constantly flying around."*

**Net-info-gain opportunity:** a documentary that surfaces 2–3 named, specific survivor stories (not aggregate third-person tolls) would out-perform both competitor videos' thin human layer. The drowned-mother-and-daughter story and the predatory-mortgage-servicing story are both genuinely unique, verifiable-feeling angles no competitor video used.

## 4. Renters vs. landlords / disaster-profiteering — untouched by either video

- @saracook7807 (70 likes, StormForge): *"Thousands of people renting in homes and apartments lost everything while owners collected government money. So many vulture contractors!"*
- @debramoreno9796 (2 likes, StormForge): *"After the storm, investors came to the island in Port Aransas and took advantage of all the people who lost everything. You go there today, it's now a tourist trap full of rentals. So sad."*

**Net-info-gain opportunity:** neither video mentions disaster-capitalism dynamics (predatory contractors, absentee landlords collecting FEMA/insurance money while tenants got nothing, post-storm real-estate flipping). This is a clean, uncovered accountability angle that fits directly into our "villains" spine alongside the dam-release and lawsuit material.

## 5. Coastal Rockport/Mississippi-Gulf-Coast-style complaint: "you only talk about Houston"

Both videos are Houston-flood-centric; the coastal wind/surge impact zone gets comparatively thin treatment, and viewers noticed (an echo of the exact complaint the Katrina teardown found about the Mississippi Gulf Coast being ignored in favor of New Orleans):

- @redsammy7789 (9 likes, StormForge): *"No Mention of Rockport, where the storm came in and did major damage."* (Rockport IS mentioned as scene-setting/backstory in StormForge, but gets no sustained coverage — the complaint is fair relative to its actual screen time.)
- @adrianaguirre2965 (1 like, StormForge): *"I feel like when people talk about this hurricane they always just focus on Houston when us in south Texas were hit really hard."*
- On the Dark Horizon video, several commenters (e.g. @yellowrose1984, 10 likes) noted frustration that the region "barely mentions us to the rest of the country," echoing a broader "coastal Texas gets overshadowed" sentiment even on the video that DOES lead with Rockport's coastal destruction.

## 6. Where StormForge's accountability coverage still falls short (our actual differentiation opportunity)

Since StormForge already covers the dam-release, flood-pool development, and 2019 court ruling in real depth (see `teardown-stormforge.md`), our differentiation has to be sharper, not just present:
- No specific case name, damages figure, or appeal history for the 2019 ruling — room to go deeper and more precisely (In re Downstream Addicks and Barker (Texas) Flood-Control Reservoirs, US Court of Federal Claims — verify independently before use).
- No filmed/personalized survivor vignette anywhere in either video — every death and hardship is delivered as aggregate narration.
- The renter/landlord and predatory-contractor angle (see #4) is completely absent.
- The Kellywood geography error (see #2) is a credibility crack we can avoid by verifying our own hook's location claims against a real map.

## 7. Title/thumbnail and AI-content backlash signals

  - @jamesadams893 (1 like): *"'Almost ended Texas' what an absolutely asinine and completely shitty title for a video about Harvey. It would take a million Harvies to make a dent in TEXAS."*
  - @dannycortez4201 (3 likes): *"Almost ended Texas?!?! Please stop with the bull shit click bait... to say this hurricane almost ended Texas is fkn insane."*
  - @KB-ke3fi, @phatphlyer, @omax444, @exene2881 — four more independent "didn't end Texas" complaints in the same comment set.
  - Two scattered "AI thumbnail/AI script" complaints on Dark Horizon (@jimmer411 "bullshit AI channel", @dustinredeagle9465 "clickbait title with ai thumbnail", @pj4795 "Holy AI script") — smaller in volume than the Katrina video's AI-backlash cluster but present.
- StormForge drew zero clickbait/AI complaints in the top 150 comments reviewed — its title ("The Storm That Changed Texas Forever") is more measured and its content matches the claim, which may explain the lower complaint rate despite lower view count. One lone thumbnail complaint (@brucehearn2621, 1 like: "Your thumbnail is atrociously bad") and one pacing complaint about repeated phrasing in the first four minutes.

## Summary of biggest net-info-gain targets, ranked

1. **The dam-release decision is confirmed as the emotional center of gravity for this disaster's audience** — it produced the single most-liked comment across both videos combined, and it is under-told by the market-leading competitor (Dark Horizon). Our episode should lead with it, not bury it.
2. **StormForge already built our exact planned spine** (dam release + flood-pool development + court ruling) at 516K views — we are not first, so our execution needs to out-produce it on named human vignettes, sharper legal specifics, and a stronger hook (verify the geography this time).
3. Renter/landlord exploitation and predatory post-disaster contractors/mortgage servicing — untouched by either competitor, directly fits our "real villains" framing.
4. Named, personal survivor stories (the drowned mother/daughter, the predatory-servicer/wife's-death account) — both competitors are structurally weak here; this is our biggest human-layer opportunity.
5. A titling caution: hyperbolic "almost ended Texas"-style claims draw sustained, specific audience pushback — our own title should make a claim the episode can actually defend (StormForge's more measured title drew zero such complaints).
