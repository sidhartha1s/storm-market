Go bag checklist — packed for a real evacuation, not a fantasy one.

A go bag needs enough for seventy-two hours away from home: water and a way to purify more, cash, your documents, a medication buffer, real shoes, and a light. Print the checklist below and pack while nothing is happening — the store will be stripped of exactly what you need by the time a warning tells you to go.

Go bag checklist

  • Water and purification tabletsA day or two of bottled water per person, plus purification tablets rather than bleach — tablets don't degrade the way an opened bleach bottle does, which is exactly why they belong in a go-bag rather than under the sink.
  • Cash, in small billsIn the days after a disaster, cash frequently becomes the only functioning payment method — card networks and ATMs can be down for days.
  • Copies of key documentsID, insurance policy numbers, a photo inventory of your home. See our emergency binder for the full document set — a condensed copy belongs in the bag.
  • A few days of medicationA buffer beyond your usual refill schedule, since pharmacies and prescriptions are frequently disrupted for days after an event.
  • Real shoes and a headlampNot the shoes you sleep in. Debris, broken glass, and downed material make bare feet or slippers dangerous — and a light you have to search for in the dark is a light you don't have.
  • A battery-powered or hand-crank radioA NOAA weather radio with battery backup keeps working when cell networks and power don't.
  • Phone chargers and a battery bankPhones fail fastest exactly when everyone reaches for them at once — carry your own power.
  • A basic first-aid kitEnough for cuts, sprains, and the injuries that happen while clearing debris.
  • A change of clothes per person, weather-appropriateLayered for the season — you may be sleeping somewhere without climate control.
  • Your written trigger planA copy of your family emergency plan, so the household's agreed decisions travel with you.

The kids' go bag sub-list

Pack a small, separate bag per child, or a labeled section of the household bag, so a child old enough to carry something has their own kit and a stressed adult doesn't have to remember every child's specifics under pressure.

  • An info cardName, birthdate, parent/guardian contact numbers, any allergies or medical conditions, written on a card that stays in the bag.
  • Any daily medication, with dosage written downInclude the same medication-buffer logic as the adult list — a few days beyond routine.
  • A comfort itemA small stuffed animal or familiar object. It costs nothing and matters more than it looks like it should during a stressful evacuation.
  • A quiet activityA book, cards, or a small toy that needs no power and no screen.
  • A change of clothes and diapers or wipes if neededSized for the child, not scaled down from the adult list.

What a go bag doesn't cover

A go bag is built for seventy-two hours on the move — it is not your household's full supply. Your home's fourteen-day water and food store, your document vault, and your insurance records belong in the emergency binder, not the bag on your shoulder.

What should be in a go bag?

Water and purification tablets, cash, copies of key documents, a few days of medication, real shoes and a headlamp, a battery or hand-crank radio, phone chargers, basic first aid, a change of clothes, and a copy of your household's trigger plan.

What goes in a kid's go bag?

An info card with contact numbers and any medical needs, any daily medication with dosage noted, a comfort item, a quiet screen-free activity, and a change of clothes sized to the child.

How much water should go in a go bag?

Plan for a day or two of bottled water per person for portability, plus purification tablets so you can treat more water on the move. Your home's larger water store is a separate, much bigger figure — see the water chapter guidance in the book.
