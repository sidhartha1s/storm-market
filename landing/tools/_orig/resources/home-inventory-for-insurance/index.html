Home inventory for insurance: the free checklist and why it pays off.

A home inventory for insurance is a dated, itemized record of what you own — photos or video, receipts where you have them, and serial numbers for anything valuable — made before a loss, not after. It's the single piece of paperwork that decides how much of a claim or disaster-assistance award you can actually prove.

Federal disaster assistance illustrates the stakes plainly. The average FEMA Individual and Household Program award is roughly $3,029, against a statutory maximum of $43,600 for housing assistance alone — and only about 10.8% of recipients ever receive the maximum. That gap isn't primarily about need; awards are determined by verified loss, insurance status, and eligibility. What a home inventory removes is the loss you cannot prove, which is the part of that gap you actually control.

How to build a home inventory for insurance

  • Record a twenty-minute video walkthrough of every roomNarrate what's in each room as you film — closets, drawers, the garage, the attic. This single free action is one of the highest-value protections a household can take.
  • Photograph anything valuable individuallyElectronics, jewelry, tools, appliances — close enough to read a model or serial number where one exists.
  • Keep receipts you already haveDon't go hunting for old ones. Start with what you can find in five minutes and add to it going forward.
  • Date everythingA dated video or photo set proves the inventory existed before a loss, not after.
  • Store a copy off-site or in the cloudA home inventory that burns or floods with the house it documents proves nothing.
  • Update it once a yearAttach the check to something you already do — a birthday, the clock change — so it doesn't go stale.

Photograph your mitigation work too

Your inventory isn't only about contents. Photograph anything that proves mitigation work was done to standard — a wind-pressure rating label inside a garage door, a sealed-roof-deck certificate, an engineer's certificate for flood vents. Several states legally require insurers to offer a discount for a documented, mitigated home; the credit is carrier-specific, and the paperwork above is exactly what your insurer will ask for before applying it.

What documentation cannot do

This is not a promise that documentation moves you from three thousand dollars to forty-three thousand. Awards are determined by verified loss, insurance status, and eligibility, and most households will never approach the statutory maximum. What documentation removes is the loss you cannot prove — which is the only part of the gap you control.

What is a home inventory for insurance?

A dated, itemized record of your home's contents — a video walkthrough, close-up photos of valuables, and any receipts you have — made before a loss so you can prove what you owned after one.

Is there a free home inventory template?

Yes, the checklist above is a free starting version. The book's full binder includes a pre-formatted home inventory form as one of its 41 fillable pages.

Does a home inventory actually help with an insurance claim?

It changes what you can prove, which is the part you control. A household with a dated video walkthrough and photographed valuables is in a different conversation with an adjuster than one relying on memory.

Where should I store my home inventory?

Off-site or in the cloud — a copy that only exists inside the house it documents can be destroyed along with the house.
