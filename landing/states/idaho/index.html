Idaho: The Storm-Ready Checklist

Idaho's biggest weather risks are hail and derecho (May–Aug), blizzard and ice storm (Oct–Dec) and extreme cold (Oct–Jan). This page collects the state's emergency contacts, shelter and mitigation money, and the rules worth knowing before the next warning — every fact linked to its source below.

The hazards Idaho actually faces

  • Hail and derecho

    Peak season: May–Aug

    Hail and derecho winds arrive inside the same thunderstorm complex, often with less lead time than a tornado warning because the threat is the whole storm line, not a single funnel. Large hail totals more property-damage claims nationally than any other severe-storm hazard, and derecho wind gusts can match hurricane-force winds hundreds of miles from open water. Vehicles under cover and loose yard items secured before the line arrives are the cheap fix.

  • Blizzard and ice storm

    Peak season: Oct–Dec

    Ice storms cause more prolonged power outages than almost any other winter hazard, because ice loads snap tree limbs and power lines that snow alone would not touch — outages can run days in rural areas. Blizzards add near-zero visibility and windchill to the mix. A household's real blizzard plan is a heat source that doesn't depend on grid power and a stocked pantry that doesn't need refrigeration.

  • Extreme cold

    Peak season: Oct–Jan

    Extreme cold turns ordinary failures — a stalled car, a lost power connection, a wet coat — into emergencies far faster than people expect, and hypothermia risk starts well above freezing once wind and moisture are factored in. Frozen and burst pipes are the most common property-damage claim tied to cold snaps, and they are largely preventable with basic insulation and a known shutoff valve.

Rules worth knowing

  • Winter utility shutoff protection: Winter Payment Plan, Nov 1 - Mar 31: regulated gas/electric utilities cannot shut off service in Dec/Jan/Feb to a household with a child, a senior (62+) or a seriously ill member that cannot pay, if enrolled in the plan (Idaho PUC).
