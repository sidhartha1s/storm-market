Hazards

The hazards California actually faces

  • Wildfire

    Peak season: Oct–Jan. Wildfire risk to a home is decided mostly in the first five feet around the structure (the "home ignition zone") where embers carried well ahead of the flame front land on roofs, gutters, and anything flammable pressed against the wall. A go-bag and a named evacuation trigger matter because wildfire evacuation orders can arrive with far less notice than a hurricane or even a tornado watch.

  • Extreme heat

    Peak season: Jun–Aug. Extreme heat kills more people in an average US year than any other weather hazard, and most of those deaths are preventable: the risk concentrates in the first heat wave of the season, before bodies acclimate, and in households without reliable cooling. Checking on elderly or isolated neighbors during a heat advisory does more than any single piece of equipment.

  • Hail and derecho

    Peak season: Oct–Mar. Hail and derecho winds arrive inside the same thunderstorm complex, often with less lead time than a tornado warning because the threat is the whole storm line, not a single funnel. Large hail totals more property-damage claims nationally than any other severe-storm hazard, and derecho wind gusts can match hurricane-force winds hundreds of miles from open water. Vehicles under cover and loose yard items secured before the line arrives are the cheap fix.

Know Before You Need It

Rules worth knowing

  • Emergency prescription refills

    During a proclaimed state of emergency, a pharmacist may refill a prescription without the prescriber's authorization if the prescriber is unavailable after a reasonable effort to contact them; the statute sets no fixed days-supply cap for this provision specifically (Bus. & Prof. Code § 4064).

  • Summer utility shutoff protection

    CPUC bars electric disconnection for nonpayment when the CalHeatScore heat-index forecast crosses a set threshold (lowered in 2026 from 100°F to 90°F-equivalent), with utilities required to consult the index before any shutoff (California PUC).
