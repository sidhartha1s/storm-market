# Senior survival investigation: "The Storm-Ready Home"

Evidence: the four prep logs here; book checked by grep against `ebook/manuscript/`. Timelines are modeled on the real events from memory of the public record, times approximate; outcomes are judgments. Cost bands are retail estimates. Any number proposed for the book carries «CITE».

**[BOOK HAD IT]** = covered (file, protection), skipped or misread. **[BOOK GAP]** = not covered, or only for able-bodied, car-owning smartphone readers.

## 1. Persona investigations

### 1.1 Dorothy Kowalski, 78. Hurricane Ian-type Cat 4, Fort Myers Beach, late September

**Timeline**
- **Day -4 to -3.** Track points at Tampa; Lee County is in the cone, not the center.
- **Day -2 (Mon).** Hurricane watch. Her sentence fires: "call Rita and we leave." Rita, 70: "it's going to Tampa, let's wait for the order." The Joplin verification behavior, done by her driver. Daughter books an Orlando hotel; no ride-hail takes a walker and a cat. Night: track shifts south, hurricane warning.
- **Day -1 (Tue) morning.** Lee County orders Zone A out, later than neighboring counties. Her registry form was never finished; she is not on the transport list. County line busy an hour.
- **Day -1, 2 pm.** Rita, now frightened, drives her to the general shelter (special-needs shelter needs pre-registration). Biscuit goes to a separate pet site.
- **Day 0, ~3 pm.** Landfall. Surge roughly 12 to 15 feet. Her ground-floor condo floods to the ceiling.
- **Day +1 to +3.** Insulin fine: cooler, thermometer, frozen bottles (P60). Food not: cans she "cannot judge for my sugar," meals at shelter times. Day +2 before dawn: a low, the walker in the dark between cots, a fall. Bruised hip, ER.
- **Day +4 on.** Home gone. Emergency refill works (P59). Cat back day +6.

**Outcome: survived, injured.** Counterfactual: had Rita left with her own family, Dorothy stays on a ground floor six blocks from the Gulf, the profile of most Lee County Ian deaths (older, at home, drowned). **One missing person away from death.**

**Decisive moments and cause chain**
1. Trigger owned by a driver who never agreed to it. [BOOK GAP] P95 assumes the people who agree the trigger drive; P4-C1 asks for one "named individual," singular.
2. Registry half-enrolled. [BOOK HAD IT, P111, P4-C1] Never says "it counts only once they confirm," or that transport runs only after an order.
3. P95's hurricane-watch example says "cars fuelled... garage braced," not "Zone A without a car: leave." [BOOK HAD IT in P3-02, mis-taught by P95's example]
4. Zone unconfirmed; pet separation. [BOOK HAD IT, P91, P109] No phone route to the zone; no "write the pet-friendly shelter's name."
5. Diabetic food and a night fall in a shelter. [BOOK GAP] P13 has no diabetic line; P43 is home-only.

### 1.2 Harold, 74 (COPD, oxygen) and Marge, 72 (dementia). Camp Fire-type, Paradise ridge, November

**Timeline**
- **Day -1.** Red Flag Warning on the radio. Harold does exactly what P3-05 says: car fueled, go-bags and two full tanks in the car. **Best single piece of prep among the four.**
- **6:30 am.** Ignition upwind. Harold asleep on the concentrator. The weather radio is silent: in the modeled event, evacuation orders go by county call system, not the radio (verify for Butte 2018). [BOOK GAP: never says the radio may not carry evacuation orders.]
- **~7:15 am.** Orders go out. His landline is not registered; the flip phone gets no wireless alert. [BOOK GAP]
- **7:40 am.** Brown sky, smoke smell. Power dies as lines burn; concentrator stops; he goes to a tank.
- **7:50 am.** Son in Sacramento, watching TV, reaches the landline: "Dad, go now."
- **7:50-8:05.** Marge, woken into darkness, refuses the car and walks toward the trees. Harold follows on oxygen. Fifteen minutes lost. [BOOK GAP]
- **8:05-10:30.** One paved road into the main route. Gridlock, fire both sides by 8:30, embers on the car, an oxygen cylinder inside. Two hours in smoke; COPD flares; he turns the flow up and the second tank runs low.
- **10:45 am.** Out. Chico ER: COPD exacerbation, smoke inhalation, three days admitted. Marge distressed, unhurt. Dogs unhurt. House lost.

**Outcome: near-death (Harold); Marge survived unharmed.**

**Decisive moments and cause chain**
1. Overnight onset, no alert path for a flip phone, landline or sleeper. [BOOK GAP] P85 assumes a smartphone; "landline" and "flip phone" have 0 grep hits.
2. Red Flag readiness bought about 20 minutes. [BOOK HAD IT, P3-05 During, P91] Credit the book.
3. Dementia refusal at the car. [BOOK GAP] One bullet in P4-C1.
4. One road through fuel. [BOOK HAD IT, partly, P106, P3-05] "Not an escape route," then nothing: no earlier trigger, no trapped-in-car steps.
5. Oxygen in a car near fire; tank hours at real flow. [BOOK GAP] P61 says only "cylinders are the outage answer." N95 with COPD: P52 silent.

### 1.3 Earl Dawson, 82, alone. Winter Storm Uri-type, Austin, February

**Timeline**
- **Day 1.** Hard freeze warning. His trigger fires: tub filled, power station charged, back bedroom, taps dripping. **Good.**
- **Day 2, 2 am.** Rolling outages become total; single digits outside. Aids out, CPAP stops; he wakes at 4 am short of breath, moves it to the power station. By morning it is flat; no car or solar to recharge. [BOOK HAD IT, P38; he could not do watts x hours.]
- **Day 3.** Bedroom about 45°F. Soup on the gas stovetop, window cracked. New CO alarms, but with aids out he would not hear one. [BOOK HAD IT, P48 bed-shaker; absent from P47, which he read.] Boil-water notice; a crawl-space pipe splits; he shuts the main (P35, good).
- **Day 3, 9 pm.** The 15-year-old generator (old fuel never drained, P40 partly) will not start. He tinkers in the dark on ice with neuropathic feet, falls, hits his head. **He is on warfarin.** Headache, bed. [BOOK GAP: "warfarin", "anticoag" 0 hits.]
- **Day 4.** Phone dead; niece in Houston, also blacked out, cannot reach him; no neighbor knows him. Drowsy, confused, not eating. No leave-temperature written. [BOOK HAD IT, P3-11 "agree an indoor temperature," no number.]
- **Day 4, 4 pm.** Niece calls 911. Iced roads; the welfare check reaches him Day 5 morning, Duke barking at the door.
- **Day 5.** Hypothermia and a slow head bleed. ICU.

**Outcome: near-death.** Texas counted 246 storm deaths, hypothermia leading, CO poisoning a major share «CITE: Texas DSHS, February 2021 Winter Storm-Related Deaths report». Earl fits the core profile: old, alone, no neighbor, power-dependent.

**Decisive moments and cause chain**
1. No named person nearby. [BOOK HAD IT, P4-C1] No script for asking a stranger.
2. No leave-temperature number; no night transport to a warming center. [BOOK HAD IT without the number; transport BOOK GAP]
3. Solo generator repair at night. [BOOK GAP] P40 never says "if it will not start, you leave."
4. Fall on a blood thinner. [BOOK GAP]
5. CPAP sized by guess; CO alarm he cannot hear. [BOOK HAD IT, P38 without a worked example; P48/P4-C1 bed-shaker, not linked from P47]

### 1.4 Bev Tanner, 67, Ray, 70 (cane), grandkids 4 and 8, Peanut. Moore May 20, 2013-type EF5

**Timeline**
- **~1:10 pm.** Tornado watch. The book says a manufactured home leaves now (P4-C2, P3-01). Bev decided she will not. Kids arrive ~2:30.
- **2:40 pm.** Warning. She does not look outside. [BOOK HAD IT, P95] Good.
- **2:40-2:50.** Kids' helmets on; Ray needs four minutes to the car; the 4-year-old wants a toy; Peanut hides under the trailer. Ten minutes gone.
- **2:56 pm.** Touchdown to the west. On the road; traffic thick; hail.
- **3:02 pm.** Church locked. No second place. [BOOK GAP] Ray falls on wet steps trying the side door.
- **3:05-3:20.** Back in the car in the open lot, kids on the floor under Bev. The tornado passes about a mile south; their park is in the path; the single-wide is gone.

**Outcome: survived, injured (Ray wrist fracture, Bev hail cuts). Survived by geography, not by plan.** A mile north, a car in an open lot is among the worst places in an EF5; at home, the trailer was destroyed.

**Decisive moments and cause chain**
1. Watch trigger rejected. [BOOK HAD IT, P4-C2] "Accept you will do this several times for nothing" is right and lost her; no middle version (high-risk days, kid afternoons).
2. One unconfirmed shelter, no backup. [BOOK GAP] Nothing for "voicemail," a neighbor's in-ground shelter, a city registry, or the park owner.
3. Movement time never measured. [BOOK HAD IT, P4-C2 "time the journey"]
4. Parked car vs. trailer as last resort. [BOOK HAD IT, partly, P3-01 table "In a car"]; adult helmets skipped [BOOK HAD IT, P25].

## 3. Comprehension failures

**3.1 Confirmed cross-reference bug.** `P5-C1-warning-literacy.md` line 235, Protection 91 "What this does not do": "The decision is the leave-early trigger in **Protection 112**." Verified by grep: 112 is "The out-of-area contact" (`P5-C3-evacuation.md` line 185); 95 is "The trigger plan" (`P5-C1` line 321). Fix: change 112 to 95. Two of four readers (Dorothy, Harold) flagged it independently. Recommend a script check that every "Protection N" sentence names a protection whose title matches its claim.

**3.2 The same word used two ways.** "Torch" means flashlight in P0, P43 and P107 (32 hits book-wide), and P3-11 says someone burns a house down "thawing a pipe with a torch" (blowtorch). To a US reader, "put a torch under every bed" reads as an open flame. Replace every "torch" with "flashlight"; keep "blowtorch" in P3-11 (P3-09 already says "blowtorch").

**3.3 British English throughout** (grep counts): neighbour 45, programme 40, torch 32, enrol 16, organis- 13, petrol 6, stabiliser 6, tyres 5, colour 4, fuelled 4, nappies 2. Dorothy named five of them. One sweep to US English.

**3.4 Number-chains instead of instructions.** "37, 42, 38, 39, 32, 47, 40, 41..." (P3-17 Before) made Earl flip back each time; Bev asked "what 25 means." Rule: name first, number second ("helmets (25)").

**3.5 Jargon before explanation.** "SAME" (all three radio buyers stalled), "watt-hours" (Harold, Earl), "Tornado Alley rank 8. Owners only" (Bev), "equipment's tolerance" (Harold), "gas to pilot or off at the valve" (Dorothy).

**3.6 Density.** Header block plus 4-5 paragraphs per protection. Dorothy stops at 20 minutes; P1-C1 (5,500 words) was abandoned or skimmed by all four. Fix: a 3-line "Do this" box atop each protection.

**3.7 Car, smartphone and two able adults assumed.** P85 "every phone," P106 "drive both routes," P110 half-tank, P95 "cars fuelled," P108 "every adult gets an area," P107 three days of water. Each reader hit one and concluded "this part isn't for me."

**3.8 A statistic that frightens without directing.** The P4-C1 Dosa study (evacuation roughly doubling death risk, nursing-home residents) made Dorothy ask whether she should stay. See R11.

**3.9 No start line.** 130 protections, 41 forms; all four asked for "my three."

**3.10 Pictures asked for:** big-print card per hazard (4 of 4), radio buttons and county code (3), zone lookup (2), CPAP/oxygen power chart (2), generator yard (1), insulin cooler (1), mobile home in EF-1 wind (1).

### R2. New "Start here if you are over 65" page (1 page)
Target: `P0-front-matter.md`, immediately after "## The fifteen-minute start" (before "Find your region"). Draft:

> **If you are over 65, or you care for someone who is, start here.**
> This book has 130 protections. You need about ten. Here they are, in order.
>
> 1. **Two people, not one.** Two people who will come for you or call you daily in a storm. One near, one far. Tell them. (112; "Older adults," Part Four)
> 2. **One sentence on the fridge.** "When ___ happens, I leave, and I call ___ and ___." In a hurricane zone, wildfire area or mobile home, the blank is the *watch* or *Red Flag Warning*, not the order. (95)
> 3. **An alarm that wakes you.** Weather radio by the bed. Flip phone or landline: sign the landline up for county calls. Hearing aids out at night: add a bed-shaker. (85, 86, 87)
> 4. **Your machine's backup.** Oxygen, CPAP or insulin: make the three calls in Protection 42 this week.
> 5. **Your registry.** Ask the county to list you for help leaving. It counts only when they confirm you are on it.
> 6. **Thirty days of pills,** and a card listing each one, including any blood thinner. (59, Form 2)
> 7. **Flashlight, shoes and whistle at the bed.** (43)
> 8. **Your "leave" temperature,** written down. (P3-11, R7)
>
> **What this does not do:** this page is not the whole book. It keeps you alive long enough for the rest to matter.

Set in 14-pt minimum. No diagram (D8 card sits opposite).

### R3. New protection: "Leaving with no car, or with someone who cannot move fast" (2 pages)
Target: `P5-C3-evacuation.md`, new block after "### 111 · Where you are going, specifically" (renumber as 111A or append as 131 to keep numbering permanent). Draft:

> ### 131 · Leaving without a car, or with someone slow
> **Prevents:** being left behind because your only ride waited.
> **Cost:** $0 · **Time:** two afternoons · **Materials:** phone, this page · **Regions:** all five.
>
> If you do not drive, your trigger is your driver's trigger. They must agree to it out loud. If they want to "wait for the order," you need a different driver.
>
> Name **two drivers**. Ask both: "If a hurricane watch is issued for our county, will you take me that day?" Numbers on the fridge.
>
> Ask your county emergency office four questions and write the answers: Am I on your list? When does transport come (usually only after an order)? Where to? Can my pet and walker come?
>
> Time yourself from "go" to seated in the car with bag, medicine and pet. Over ten minutes, your trigger moves earlier.
>
> Pack a bag you can carry or roll. If three days of water is too heavy, carry one day.
>
> **What this does not do:** a registry is not a ride on your schedule. Your drivers are the plan; the registry is the backup.

Diagram D3 (timeline strip).

### R4. New protection: "Your medical device in an outage and on the road" (2 pages)
Target: `P2-C6-medical.md`, after "### 61 · Dialysis, oxygen, and infusion contingency". Draft:

> ### 61A · Oxygen, CPAP and insulin, worked out on paper
> **Prevents:** a machine or a medicine running out in a car, a shelter or a cold house.
>
> **Oxygen.** Ask your supplier: "How many hours does one full tank last at my flow rate, and at the higher rate I use when I am short of breath?" Write both numbers. Carry enough for the drive, plus a delay. In a car, tanks ride upright and secured. Never smoke or let anyone smoke near them. «CITE: FDA / oxygen-supplier guidance on cylinder transport and fire risk»
> **CPAP.** Find the watts on the power brick label. Multiply by the hours you sleep. That is the size of battery you need. Worked example on the facing page. «CITE: one manufacturer spec sheet for a common CPAP, with and without humidifier»
> **Insulin.** Ask your pharmacist how many days your insulin is good out of the fridge. For many insulins it is weeks, not hours. «CITE: manufacturer labeling (e.g. FDA package insert) for in-use room-temperature limits» Never put ice directly on pens.
> **Blood thinners.** If you take warfarin or another blood thinner, any fall that hits your head is an emergency even if you feel fine. Ask your doctor now what to do if you cannot get a blood test during a long outage. «CITE: AHA/NIH patient guidance on head injury while anticoagulated»
>
> **What this does not do:** it does not tell you your dose or settings. Those come from your doctor. It tells you which numbers to ask for before the day you need them.

Diagram D2 (CPAP/oxygen power sizing) and D5 (insulin cooler).

### R5. Manufactured-home tornado plan with a backup (1.5 pages)
Target: `P4-C2-places.md`, inside "## The manufactured or mobile home", after the paragraph "**Then set the trigger at the watch, not the warning**". Draft:

> **If leaving at every watch is not possible**, do this, in this order:
> - Always leave on a watch the forecasters label high-risk or "particularly dangerous." «CITE: SPC risk categories, PDS watches, and share of violent-tornado deaths on those days»
> - With grandchildren or a slow walker in the house, leave at the watch, no exceptions.
> - Have **three** places, each with a drive time: a neighbor's in-ground shelter (ask now), a public building confirmed open, your city's shelter registry if it has one. Ask the park manager in writing about a park shelter.
> - Caught at home in a warning with nowhere to go: «CITE: NWS last-resort guidance for mobile-home residents (vehicle vs. ditch vs. home)».
> - Time everyone to the car. Over five minutes, a warning is too late for you.
>
> **What this does not do:** none of this makes a mobile home safe. It gives you somewhere else to be.

Diagram D4 (three-shelter map). Illustration I2.

### R6. Alerts for flip-phone, landline and hard-of-hearing users (1 page)
Target: `P5-C1-warning-literacy.md`, inside "### 85 · Two independent alert paths", after the body and before "What this does not do". Draft:

> **If you have a flip phone or a landline.** Call your county emergency office and ask to register your landline for emergency calls. Do not assume it is listed. Some flip phones receive alerts; test it on the county's test day. Ask a relative to call you when an alert reaches them, and write it into your trigger.
> **A weather radio may not tell you to evacuate.** It always carries weather warnings. It carries local evacuation orders only if your county arranges it. Ask your county.
> **If you take hearing aids out at night,** your alarms must shake the bed or flash. That includes the carbon monoxide alarm (Protection 47).
